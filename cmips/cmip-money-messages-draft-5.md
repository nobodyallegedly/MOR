## In plain words (new in draft 5)

*Two or three people can now share a work, a vow or a post under one deal, signed in Edubba: its split service divides every payment for it. A buyer's or a tipper's app finds the deal from what the work names, says in one line before paying that it is shared, and pays the deal's own payee, whose pointer leads to the service. A tip to such a work goes to the deal, not to whoever posted it. And whoever shares in a sale, a member of a collective or a party of a deal, can read the buyer's claim of it, so their app shows the buyer's contest beside the sale.*

## Changes to draft 4

### 4.4. A payee whose agreement names a split service (changed in draft 5)

4.4. *New in draft 4 (F357).* **A payee whose agreement names a split service.** Where the payee's agreement in force names a split service (Agreements terms field 14: in a collective, its grant; the grant's grantee is the service), the payer sends the invoice request **to the service**: its `to` the service, sealed to the service's own encryption key, left at the service's inbox route. The commitment is unchanged: **the payee in it stays the collective**, `paid-to` its pointer in force (whose addresses are the service's, Agreements rule 16). The service, the payee's side, answers (section 5), signed with its own key, with an invoice from its own node, and later signs the receipt with the collective's grant key (Agreements rule 16; Money draft 9, rule 7). The payer's money app reads field 14 from the agreement in force it already reads for the pointer check (Agreements rule 16), and says before paying that the service answers for the payee. *A payer's app that does not read Agreements sends to the payee, as before: the request reaches no one who can open it, and the payment waits, unknown, a stated cost.* A request to the service whose commitment's payee does not name the service in field 14 of its agreement in force is refused by the service, saying why.

4.4.1. *New in draft 5.* **A deal's payee.** Where the payee is a party of a deal (an agreement without a key grammar) whose field 14 lists a grant signed by that payee, the same holds, read for the deal: the payee's agreement is **the version the payment follows**: the offer's field 0, for a purchase; the deal's version itself, for a tip (4.4.2); the service is the grantee of **the payee's own grant** in that version's field 14 (Agreements rule 16: "in a deal, one grant per payee"); the payee stays the commitment's payee, `paid-to` its own pointer in force; the service receipts with that payee's grant key (Agreements, "What a split service's grant key may sign": "a purchase naming the grantor's own claim … or a payment following it (field 5)"). *How Edubba writes such a deal (its own payee, a party holding no stake, whose pointer names the service's rails): `docs/splits-reading.md`, "The one thing to know first".*

4.4.2. *New in draft 5 (decided: "with a tip IF there is a split the target is the agreement not the act").* **A tip to a work shared under a deal** follows the deal: its commitment's `fulfils` (the receipt's field 5, the claim's field 4) is the deal's version (8.2), its payee the deal's payee, its purchase absent. It is no purchase (Agreements rule 12: a payment is a purchase "only if its receipt or claim names the claim it pays under"); nothing is owed back (rule 12's refund reaches "a payment for a standing offer" and "a payment for a publication carrying a work some agreement claims", neither of which a payment following the deal itself is); the service splits it as any incoming receipt (Agreements rule 20). *A reading the MOR lead may confirm in commentary: `docs/splits-reading.md`, question 5.*

4.4.3. *New in draft 5.* **A tip drafted in another app** (a draft, section 2, what 0) is for a shared work where its `refs` name a post under a deal (8.1) and its payee (field 1) is that deal's payee: the money app then makes the payment follow the deal (4.4.2). The draft's format is unchanged. A draft copied by hand, which carries no `refs`, cannot: an app refuses to prepare one by hand for a shared work, saying so.

### 7.2.3. A collective's sale, and a deal's (changed in draft 5)

7.2.3. *New in draft 4 (mechanic, this draft's, following F357; a reading, to confirm).* **A collective's sale.** Where the seller is a collective whose split service signed the receipt (4.4), the service tells each party of the collective's agreement in force: a sale sealed to each, signed by the service, its fields as above, field 0 the collective's offer (made under its agreement, Agreements rule 11). A member's app holding the work's key (sealed to every member, Agreements rule 57) delivers it to the buyer, signed as the collective. *Every member learns of every sale: a collective keeps no secret from its members (rule 57).*

7.2.4. *New in draft 5.* **A deal's sale.** Where the payee is a deal's payee (4.4.1), the service tells **every party of the version the purchase names** (its terms' field 0, the deal's payee included): a sale sealed to each, its fields as above, field 0 the offer (made under that version). The work's key is the publication's signer's to deliver (the offer's side, Agreements rule 11: "The seller's side delivers the content key"): a buyer's app takes a key delivery from the publication's signer, as before. *Every party learns of every sale: the stated cost the parties accepted, each receiving the split anyway (Agreements rule 20).*

7.2.5. *New in draft 5.* **Field 3 names what the offer sells**: a publication, or, for an offer selling a vow (`[3, vow]`), the vow by its name. A reader takes it as the act the sale is about; an app delivers a key only for a publication.

## 8. A work shared under a deal (new in draft 5)

8.1. **How an app finds that a work is shared under a deal** (the MOR lead's open question, `docs/splits-reading.md`, question 3). From the post that shows it, the acts its `refs` name, at the places the app already looks:

- a deal's terms (Agreements type 0, no key grammar): a plain post under the deal. *A text act cannot name the deal in `objects` (Text rule 14), so it names it in `refs`; the post then does not "belong to" the deal (Agreements glossary): it only points the tipper's app at it, and the tip belongs to the deal through its field 5.*
- a standing offer whose field 0 names a deal's version: a protected file or a vow sold under the deal.

The version read is **the one named**. *Client conformance, a strong SHOULD (Agreements rule 12):* a buyer's app looks for a later version, a clone naming it as chain (`objects` `[[parent, act]]`), at the places of every party (each signs every version), and pays under the latest it finds, warning where what it shows is outdated. *This draft's apps read the version named; no later version is made by them yet (the report's costs).*

8.2. **What a tip follows**: the version found in 8.1 (for a post naming an offer, the offer's field 0).

8.3. **The one line before paying** (decided, the round's prompt: "This work is shared: X, Y, Z. The split service answers for it."): before signing an invoice request for a purchase or a tip under a deal, the payer's app says, in one line, who shares in it, each with their share of the stake (Agreements terms field 7), by the names the payer gives them, and which split service answers. *The deal's own payee is not one of them: it holds no stake.*

8.4. **A tip to a person** (a post naming no deal) is unchanged: the person's own pointer, as before (Money rule 17: "for a tip, the version of the payee pointer it follows").

8.5. **What a reader does with a post naming something that is not a deal** (a collective's agreement, an act that is no terms): nothing here: the tip goes to the post's signer, as before. *A post may name another person's deal: tips then go to that deal's owners, never to whoever wrote the post; the line before paying (8.3) shows who they are. Nobody gains by it, as nobody gains by a false `for` (Envelopes).*

## 9. A purchase's claim, readable by every member or party (new in draft 5)

*Following the MOR lead's core line of client conformance (decided with the MOR lead, 23 October 2026), in its words: "where the payee of a purchase is a collective or an agreement with several parties, the buyer's client makes its claim readable by every member of the collective's agreement in force, or every party of the version the purchase names (sealed to each, or its key delivered to each)". Until that line is on main, this section follows it by those words; where the line says otherwise once written, the line decides.*

9.1. **Who**: for a purchase (Money claim field 9) whose agreement is a collective's, every party of the collective's agreement in force as the payer's app read it for the purchase's line; whose agreement is a deal of several parties, every party of the version the purchase names, the deal's payee included. *The parties are the version's: this cMIP addresses them, it does not choose them (Agreements: the parties of the version the purchase names).* A purchase under a lone seller's offer, or a deal of one party, adds no one.

9.2. **How: sealed to each, in the same container** (mechanic, this draft's). The payer's claim of the payment (Money type 3), signed once, its `to` naming its first recipient (the payee, or the split service where 4.4 sends it there) and then every reader of 9.1; one sealed container whose content key is wrapped to each one's current encryption key (Envelopes, the sealed container); left at the first recipient's inbox route, then at each reader's. *Why not the claim sealed to the service with its key delivered to each: a second act per reader, and the service's cooperation; sealing to each needs neither, and the claim's bytes are the same for everyone.*

9.3. **When, and in what order**: at payment, as the claim is written (Agreements rule 22's client conformance: "the claim written at payment"); the first recipient first, so the service has the referral before it splits (rule 22), then every reader. A reader not found, or publishing no encryption key or inbox, is not reached: its app says the buyer's claim was not found, "unknown", never "none".

9.4. **What a reader does with it**: its app (Edubba's Sales) opens the claim from its inbox, holds it with the parties' chains and public acts, and shows the buyer's contests of the purchase beside the sale (Agreements, "Contest (type 14)"), as the core reads them; where the core cannot say, "unknown". *A contest shows beside a purchase only: where the core reads the payment as no purchase, the app says so, with the core's words (the report's point 2 for the MOR lead: a collective's sale whose agreement stakes the collective itself and not the work).*

## Costs, stated (new in draft 5, beside draft 4's)

- **Every party or member sees who bought** (9), the decided cost, and every party of a deal learns of every sale (7.2.4).
- **A deal's own payee is held by its proposer** (4.4.1): whoever holds its keys could stop the deal's income (revoking its grant) or point it elsewhere (a bypass every Agreements client shows, rule 16). *As the texts stand, a deal's payee points its whole flow at the service; the deal's own payee keeps every person's own pointer theirs (the report's point 1 for the MOR lead).*
- **A tip from an app that does not read Agreements** follows the deal's payee's pointer, not the deal: it arrives at the service, which cannot receipt it under the deal (its grant key signs only under the deal): it waits, unreceipted.
- **A draft by hand cannot tip a shared work** (4.4.3): use the lane or Ask Damgar.
- **A later version of a deal is not looked for yet** (8.1): the apps pay under the version the work names.
