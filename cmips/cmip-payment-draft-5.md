## Reassessment (new in draft 5)

*In plain words: the payment cMIP still has all of its job; the redesign made its one part about sales simpler.*

**What it still does.** *It is the common language every rail speaks: the payment's fingerprint (the commitment), which the payee's side binds on the rail before money moves, and the check anyone runs afterwards from a receipt or a claim. It says how rail Modules plug in, how a payer chooses where to pay, and how a transfer to one's own vault is told apart. Money hands this task to a cMIP on purpose, and nothing in the redesign took it back.*

**What the redesign changed for it.** *Only what a purchase is, and who receives it. Before, a purchase named the claim it paid under, and the money could go to a collective's split service signing with the collective's grant key, or to a deal's payee through a service holding its grant key; draft 2 had several paragraphs on who may issue an address for whom. Now a buyer pays a price tag; under a publication, the tag's payee is its split service, which receives in its own name with its own key, and the purchase names the ownership agreement and version whose shares the service pays. So item 4 is one short paragraph, and the words "offer" and "claim it pays under" go.*

**Should it retire?** *No. Without it no receipt or claim carries a checkable proof, and every amount on MOR would rest on one party's word.*

## In plain words

*When one identity pays another, two records are signed: the payee's receipt ("I received this") and the payer's claim ("I paid this"). Both carry the same proof from the payment system that moved the money, the rail. This cMIP is the common language for that proof. It does not move money, and it signs nothing.*

*Its central idea is the payment commitment: before money moves, the payee's side commits, on the rail itself, to one fingerprint of what the payment is: who is paid, by whom, how much, in what unit, for what, and where. On Lightning the payee's node signs that fingerprint inside the invoice. Afterwards, anyone holding the receipt or the claim recomputes the fingerprint from what the act says, and the rail's own rule checks that the rail carried exactly that payment. The payer cannot invent it, because only the payee's declared node can sign the invoice; the payee cannot deny it, because the payer holds the proof that the invoice was paid.*

*Each rail (Lightning, on-chain bitcoin, and others later) is a rail Module under this cMIP: it says how its addresses look, what its proof is, and how the proof is checked.*

## Purpose

This cMIP fills the Money MIR's task 6, payment (Development, task table; Money, "Tasks", F112). It defines:

- how rail Modules plug in under it;
- the payment commitment, by which a payment on a rail carries what the receipt and claim say, including the id of the act it follows: an obligation, an agreement, a price tag, a publication, a bake or a payee pointer (Money, "What a payment fulfils"; **new in draft 5**: a price tag, a publication and a bake in place of draft 4's "offer");
- the proof a receipt or claim carries in its field 1;
- how a receipt or claim is verified;
- how a payer chooses where to pay, and what it does when it cannot (Money rules 14a, 14b and 16).

An agreement or a publication names it for task 6 (Agreements, terms field 2; publication field 9; **new in draft 5**: a publication too). A payment that follows only a payee pointer, or a price tag on a bare bake, under no agreement, may use it too.

Identity, Envelopes, Text and Money.

## Rail Modules

A rail Module is a specification of kind 1 (Module) whose field 5 ("implements") names this cMIP. **Its specification carries field 11, the rail's kind, and field 13, the set its proof binds** (new in draft 3, F301; Development Q6, taken): both are required on a Module whose field 5 names this cMIP, and a specification without them is invalid (Development draft 10, Part three), so no specification at all (F323): no client can have adopted it, and a receipt or claim naming it is unknown (below, "Verifying", step 1). Its field 6, where present, names its verification rule as a program under Development rule 22; a rail Module whose rule is given only as text and a reference implementation carries none (new in draft 3, F326). Its text MUST define:

1. **Its rail address**: the bytes of a payee pointer's `rail` entry `address` for this Module, and the unit each address carries.
2. **Its vault source**: the bytes of a vault entry's `source`, and how a fresh receiving address is obtained under it for each payment (derived from it, or issued by a node it names).
3. **How it carries the commitment**: where, on its rail, the payment commitment (below) is bound to the payment by the payee's side, so that the rail's proof shows it; and **whether it is a request rail or a push rail** (F128, W4), declared in field 11 of the Module's specification, 0 a request rail, 1 a push rail, which a client reads from the specification, never from the Module's code and never set by hand (F140; new in draft 3, F301; Development Q6, taken). On a **request rail** the payee's side issues each receiving address for one commitment, before the payment: the price tag, and the ownership agreement's version, a purchase names are then those the seller's request committed to (Agreements rule 12; **new in draft 5**, where draft 4 said "the claim a purchase names"). A request rail binds payee and purpose: wherever field 11 is 0, field 13 holds 0 and 1, or the specification is no specification (Development draft 10, Part three; FR11; new in draft 3, F328, Q5). On a **push rail** the payer pays an address the payee published once, with no request: the Module says where the payer binds the commitment (for instance in data the payment carries), and **the split service settles a purchase on its own chain** (Agreements rule 12; **new in draft 5**: the service is the one receiver, where draft 4 had each holder settle on its own), among its receipts naming the tag and version the payer committed to: **the payment decides** (F131, IT3): a receipt naming another tag or ownership version recomputes another commitment than the one the payment carries, so the rule answers invalid, and Agreements show it as a wrong receipt, counting for nothing. *Every rail Module written under this draft (the Lightning and on-chain rail Modules) is a request rail.* *Their drafts written before F301 declared it in their text and code only; they are republished with fields 11 and 13 (new in draft 3, F301).*
3a. **What its proof binds** (new in draft 3, F301; Development Q6, taken): which of the payee, the purpose and the previous hop the rail's proof binds, declared in field 13 of the Module's specification, ascending, from 0 payee, 1 purpose, 2 previous hop, and read by a client from the specification, never from the Module's code. *It decides which act settles a disagreement in payee or purpose between a receipt and a claim (Money draft 7, rule 11; F151): where the rail binds them, the commitment decides. Where it binds part of the set, element by element: the commitment decides for what field 13 holds, the payer's claim for the rest, a contrary receipt shown as a dispute, and where the payee is unbound, the payment address decides whose payment it is (Money draft 8, rule 11; new in draft 3, F328, Q4). Draft 2 left this to each Module's text and code ("format open", F140 item 1).*
4. **Its rail proof**: the bytes of `rail-proof` (below).
5. **Its verification rule**: given the commitment, the amount, the address or source the payee signed, and the rail proof (and any data the rule names that the client supplies, such as a chain's headers, Development rule 12, F204), it answers valid, invalid, pending or unknown, and names the trusted party it relied on, if any (Money rules 2 and 3). **A reference implementation** (new in draft 3, F326): a rail Module whose text gives its rule as text and a reference implementation says so in its text: its rule is a reference implementation, not a program published under Development rule 22, so no reader takes two clients to have been checked against one machine.
6. **What "the same payment" is** (F200): from a rail proof, the bytes that identify the payment it shows, the same in every proof of that payment whatever else differs (on Lightning the payment hash; on-chain the output the transaction created), or none where the proof shows no payment yet. A verifier reads it as `payment = [ rail, the Module's bytes ]`, and Money's rules that tell payments apart (8a, 10, 15) compare it, never the proof's bytes. *A Module chooses it so that one payment is never two (a reorganisation, a second encoding) and two payments are never one; the commitment does not serve, since a payer may pay one commitment twice, and a pending claim naming a commitment would then cover a payment made later (Fable's review of the on-chain rail, finding 2).*

A rail Module signs nothing, holds no key and moves no money: the payee's software issues what the rail needs, the payee signs the receipt and the payer signs the claim (F112).

## The payment commitment

```cddl
commitment = [
  rail: hash,              ; the rail Module
  payee: hash,             ; the identity paid
  amount,                  ; Money's amount: unit and value
  fulfils: hash,           ; the act the payment follows: an obligation, agreement, price tag, publication, bake or payee pointer (new in draft 5);
                           ;   for a transfer to oneself, the genesis or rotation declaring the vault paid to (item 6; new in draft 4, F355)
  payer: hash / signing-key / null,  ; the payer's identity; an anonymous payer's bare key (Money, F113); or null
  paid-to,
  salt: bstr .size 16,     ; chosen by the payer, fresh for each payment
  ? purchase               ; a purchase only: Money's `purchase`, the ownership agreement and its version (F126; new in draft 5)
]

paid-to = [ 0, pointer: hash, rail: uint ]       ; the payee's flow pointer act, and the index of its rail
        / [ 1, declared-by: hash, entry: uint ]  ; the genesis or rotation declaring the payee's vault in force, and the index of its entry
        / [ 2, claim: hash ]                     ; a claim of the payee's naming a refund rail (key 7): a refund owed on that claim's payment, paid there (RQ1)
```

The commitment hash is `tagged_hash("MOR/cmip/payment/commitment", commitment)`, over the commitment encoded in deterministic CBOR.

1. **Before paying**, the payer computes the commitment and asks the payee's side for a receiving address committing to it, as the rail Module defines. The payee's side MUST issue one only for a commitment naming the payee, and a `paid-to` that is the payee's own pointer or vault, or a claim the payee signed naming a refund rail (for a refund owed to it), with the address or source that `paid-to` names.
2. **The payer checks** the address against the payee's own pointer or vault before paying: a conforming wallet runs the rail Module's rule on it, without the completing part of the proof, and pays only on the answer the rule gives an unpaid payment (pending, for Lightning).
3. **The salt** keeps the commitment from being guessed by anyone who sees the rail: on Lightning, the invoice is visible to every node on its route.
4. **A purchase** (F126; rewritten in draft 5 for Agreements rule 12 and Money rule 18). A payment naming a price tag a publication counts puts Money's `purchase`, **the ownership agreement the publication names, by its founding version, and the version on its line at which the payer's client read it current**, as the commitment's last element; the payee's side commits to it like the rest: on a request rail, that is the version the purchase pays, the seller's own request (F128, W4). A payment that is no purchase (a tip through a publication, a tip to a bare bake, a lone seller's purchase, whose receipt carries no field 9, Agreements rule 11) leaves it out, so its commitment is the seven elements above. **The payee's side of a purchase is the publication's split service, the tag's payee, receiving in its own name** (Agreements rule 16): it issues a receiving address only for a commitment naming itself as payee, its own pointer or vault in `paid-to`, a tag of a publication it serves (or a backup of, field 11) in `fulfils`, and, for a purchase, the ownership agreement that publication names; it signs the receipt with its own key. *Client conformance:* a split service whose publication has ended issues no receiving address for any commitment under it (Agreements rule 12). *Draft 4's paragraphs on a collective's split service signing with the collective's grant key (F130, H7), and on a deal's split service holding each payee's grant key (F129, H4, H5; F130, H6), are withdrawn: no grant key names a service any more, and the service is the payee. A payout the service makes to an owner or partner is a payment by the service, whose receipt the owner or partner signs with its own key, as before.* *No element of the commitment changes.*
5. **An anonymous payer** who wants a refund to stay claimable puts a fresh bare signing key of its own in `payer` (Money, F113), and, before paying, recomputes the commitment from what the payee's side signed, so the payee's side cannot swap the key. A payer that commits `null` can never be refunded: nobody can claim for it.
6. **A transfer to oneself** (new in draft 4, F355). An identity moving its own money into its own vault commits with `payer` and `payee` both its own identity, `paid-to` `[1, declared-by, entry]` (its vault in force, and the entry paid to), and **`fulfils` the same act as `declared-by`**: the genesis or rotation declaring that vault. It carries no purchase. Such a commitment is a transfer to oneself and nothing else: a commitment whose `fulfils` is its own `paid-to`'s `declared-by` but whose payer is another identity, a bare key or null, or which carries a purchase, is not a transfer to oneself, and the vault's side MUST NOT issue a receiving address for it. *A transfer to oneself owes nothing to anyone and buys nothing: the vault declaration is the one act it can honestly be said to follow ("into the vault this act declares"). Naming it needs no payee pointer, which a person who only keeps a vault may not have, and names no flow for a payment that goes to the vault. A payment from another into one's vault names what it pays (an obligation, a price tag, the payee's pointer; **new in draft 5**: a price tag where draft 4 said an offer), as before.* *A transfer made under draft 3's reading (`fulfils` naming one's own payee pointer in force) stays valid: this draft refuses nothing draft 3 allowed; a client writing a new transfer to oneself names the vault declaration.*

*This answers Money's open parameter for every rail at once: what the payment follows (`fulfils`) is inside the commitment, which the rail carries; each rail Module says only where.*

## The proof

A receipt's or claim's field 1 holds, encoded in deterministic CBOR:

```cddl
proof = [ paid-to, salt: bstr .size 16, rail-proof: bstr ]
```

`rail-proof` is defined by the rail Module named in field 0. Every other part of the commitment is read from the act itself: field 0 (rail), the payee (receipt 3, claim 2), the amount (receipt 4, claim 3), `fulfils` (receipt 5, claim 4), the purchase where the act names one (receipt 9, claim 9), and the payer: a receipt's field 2 (an identity or a bare key), or null where it is absent; for a claim, the key in its field 8 where present (Money, F113), otherwise the claim's signer.

## Verifying a receipt or claim

A verifier first checks the act as Money requires (its signer: the receipt's payee, F112; the claim's payer). Then:

1. **The rail Module.** If the verifier has not adopted the rail Module named in field 0, the answer is unknown (Development rule 9). *A rail Module whose specification lacks field 11 or field 13 is no specification (new in draft 3, F301, F323), so no verifier has adopted it: the answer is unknown, never invalid.*
2. **The proof** decodes as above, or the answer is invalid.
3. **Where it was paid, and on a rail the payee accepts** (Money rule 12a, F115). For `[0, pointer, rail]`: the verifier holds that payee-pointer act, valid and signed by the payee, or the answer is unknown; its payee is the act's payee, its rail at that index names this rail Module, and the address carries the amount's unit, or the answer is invalid. For `[1, declared-by, entry]`: the verifier holds that genesis or rotation, counting on the payee's identity chain, declaring a vault, or the answer is unknown; it is the payee's, and the entry at that index names this rail Module and the amount's unit, or the answer is invalid. For `[2, claim]` (RQ1): the verifier holds that claim, valid, or the answer is unknown; it is signed by its payer as Money requires (an anonymous payer's key signing it, key 8), its signer is the payee, and its refund rail (key 7) names this rail Module and its address carries the amount's unit, or the answer is invalid. *The payee of a refund paid there is the claim's signer: the payer itself, or the one-time identity an anonymous payer signed with, F113; the refund is owed to the key all the same (Money rule 10a).*
4. **The commitment** is recomputed from the act and the proof.
4a. **A transfer to oneself** (new in draft 4, F355). Where `fulfils` (receipt 5, claim 4) is the same hash as `paid-to`'s `declared-by` (`[1, declared-by, entry]`), the act is a transfer to oneself: its payer (receipt 2, or the claim's signer) is the identity of its payee, it is signed by that identity, and it names no purchase, or the answer is invalid (decided by Nobody, allegedly, 21 October 2026: invalid, not an ordinary payment that fulfils nothing). *Checked from the act alone: the verifier compares two hashes it already holds, and step 3 has already found the vault declaration counting on the payee's chain.*
5. **An anonymous claim.** A claim carrying field 8 is checked as Money requires: the signature verifies under the key it names, over the claim's fields, or the answer is invalid. A claim's signer that is not the committed payer (an identity where the commitment names a key, or a key where it names an identity) recomputes a different commitment, which the rail's rule then refuses: *a routing node that learnt the preimage cannot claim the payment, nor its refund.*
6. **The rule.** The rail Module's rule is run on the commitment hash, the amount, the address or source found in step 3, and the rail proof. Its answer, with the rail Module's hash and the trusted party it relied on, is the verification answer.
7. **Under an agreement or a publication** (F115; **new in draft 5**: a publication too). Where the payment falls under an agreement, or under a publication (a purchase, or a tip through it), the verifier also checks that the rail Module implements this cMIP (its specification's field 5 names this cMIP's hash); a rail Module that does not counts for nothing there, whatever its rule answers. A verifier that does not hold the rail Module's specification answers unknown.

**A reference implementation** (new in draft 3, F326). The steps above are given as text and as a reference implementation (`cmips/payment`), as each rail Module's rule is (item 5): they are a reference implementation, not a program published under Development rule 22.

**The same payment** (F200). A verifier that hands receipts and claims to Money's rules states, for each one whose answer is valid or pending, the payment its rail Module says it is (item 6); two acts of one payment then count once, whatever their proofs' bytes. *A verifier that states none compares proofs' bytes, as before, and a payment mined again after a reorganisation counts twice for it.*

**The rail's kind and binding** (new in draft 3, F301; Development Q6, taken). A verifier that hands receipts and claims to Money's rules states, for each one, the rail's kind and the set its proof binds as fields 11 and 13 of the rail Module's specification named in field 0 give them, never as the Module's code or the verifier's settings say: Money rule 18 (Money draft 7) reads the kind (whether a push-rail payment two holders received is a purchase or a refund, F128 W4), and Money rule 11 (Money draft 7) the binding (which act decides a disagreement in payee or purpose, F151). *Two verifiers holding the same acts and the same specifications then read the same kind and binding, whatever builds of a Module they run (Fable's Money review, M2).*

**A transfer to oneself, judged** (new in draft 4, F355). A transfer to oneself that verifies shows the money moved into the identity's own vault, under the entry named. It discharges no obligation, follows no agreement, publication or price tag (**new in draft 5**: draft 4 said "offer"), and pays no one else: its `fulfils` names a declaration, not a debt. A client shows it as "a transfer to your vault" (Damgar's Transfer to vault, H46), with the vault and its entry, never as a payment to someone. *The money it moved is spent only with the chain key and the transfer's own record, commitment and salt included (F355): a client keeps that record for its owner, sealed to them.*

**What verification does not decide.** Whether a payment to `[2, claim]` repays a refund owed (the claim being the payer's own on the payment refunded, Money rules 10a and 14), Agreements judge from the same acts: the core reads it as paid at that claim. Whether the payment discharges an obligation (Money rule 7), and whether it followed the vault (rules 14a and 15), and whether the pointer it was paid to was in force for it (rules 12 and 14), are judged by the verifier from the same acts, beside the answer. A payment to the flow that the vault in force did not allow (rule 14a) can be valid on its rail and still not protected by good faith (rule 15).

## Choosing where to pay

A payer's wallet:

1. reads the payee's payee pointer in force (Money rule 12) and vault in force;
2. applies Money rule 14a to the amount: to the flow, to the vault (under any entry of the unit), or nowhere;
3. picks the first rail, of the flow pointer in order of preference or of the vault's entries for the unit, whose rail Module it has adopted and whose address carries the unit;
4. if there is none, or rule 14a leaves nowhere, refuses, and SHOULD send the payee an ordinary text message to its inbox route saying what it tried to pay, in which unit, for what, and why (Money rule 14b, F111). *For example: "A payment to you was not sent. 539ec793 tried to pay you 500 in unit a652d37a for act 2fb21fe5, and its wallet refused: the payee's vault has no entry for this unit, so it cannot be paid to the flow (Money rule 14a). The debt stays open (Money rule 16): it can be paid once you can receive it, for this unit by adding it to your vault in a rotation."*

## Delivery

The payee signs the receipt once the rail shows the payment complete, and delivers it privately to the payer, where the payer is named (Money rule 8). The payer signs the claim when it chooses to, and delivers it privately to the payee, or to whom it chooses (Money rule 10). Both are private Money acts; each travels in a sealed container to the counterparty's inbox route (Envelopes, F99).

## Reasoning

- **One commitment, every rail.** *Each rail has its own way to attach data to a payment (an invoice's description hash, an on-chain contract, a bank reference). If each rail attached the receipt's fields its own way, every rail Module would re-invent the binding and get it wrong differently. One hash, carried however the rail allows, keeps the check the same everywhere.*
- **The payee's side commits, the payer's side proves.** *Pattern 1: no record of money should rest on one party's word. The payee's declared node commits before payment; only payment yields the completing proof (a preimage on Lightning). The payer cannot make the commitment; the payee cannot make the payer's claim.*
- **The payer is in the commitment.** *Otherwise anyone who learns the rail proof could claim to have paid. An anonymous payer is in it too, as a key nobody else holds, so a refund cannot be taken by whoever saw the proof (F113).*
- **The payee names its rails.** *A rail Module is chosen by the payee in its own pointer or vault, not by the payer or the agreement: an upgrade to a rail never needs a clone of an agreement (F115).*
- **No act types.** *Receipts and claims are Money's acts; this cMIP adds nothing a Money-only client cannot read. A client without it shows a receipt's proof as bytes it cannot check, and its answer as unknown.*

## Open

- **The verification rule's RV32IM program** (Development rule 12), **rewritten in draft 3 (F326)**. Draft 2 said the profile was not yet published and step 15 or 16 would compile the rules for it. Now: this cMIP's verification steps (1 to 7, above) and each rail Module's rule are given as text and as a reference implementation (`cmips/payment`, `modules/lightning`, `modules/onchain`), and **are a reference implementation, not a program published under Development rule 22**: no client has been checked against another on one machine. *The machine's profile is published after the freeze as a specification of its own, named by hash, MOR 1's first post-freeze technical specification (F326), which anyone may adopt by choice.*
- **Batches** (Money rule 8a) and **conversion hops** (receipt fields 6 and 7): not yet defined here; a batch's commitment and a forward's binding are for a later draft.
