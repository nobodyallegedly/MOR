# Founding Modules

Specifications above the core that MOR V1 builds on. Not core: they compete like any other Module.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `module-airgap-chain-key-signer-draft-6.md` | **Dubsar Air-Gapped Chain-Key Signer**, draft 6, for Nobody, allegedly, to approve: draft 5 with a fresh seed at every change of keys as the rule (rule 3.3, F361), always, with no exception (Nobody, allegedly: "At each rotation, two set of keys are created, always."); the new chain-key seed's backup confirmed before the rotation is signed, the new signing seed's after (rules 3.3 and 3.7, F362); the key index across a fresh seed (key *n* of the seed drawn for the rotation at position *n*). Its code (`airgap/`) still implements draft 5's rule 3.3; Kišib follows draft 6. Draft 5 kept beside it. |
| `module-airgap-chain-key-signer-draft-5.md` | How an online device and an offline signer exchange chain-key commitments and rotations, and how a collective's split chain key is dealt. Draft 5 (code after step 15, part 1) adds rule 29's escape out of a contested self-hosted position and the second signature after an invalid rotation, each on bytes the device checks itself. Draft 4 is kept beside it. The genesis client implements it. |
| `module-chain-key-seed-words-draft-2.md` | **Dubsar Words**, draft 2: draft 1 with its Purpose made true under the air-gapped Module's draft 6 (each seed gives the one key that counts until the next change, not every key) and the key index across a fresh seed; backups and keys unchanged. For Nobody, allegedly, to approve. |
| `module-chain-key-seed-hex-draft-2.md` | **Dubsar Hex**, draft 2: the same two sentences as the words Module's draft 2; backups and keys unchanged. For Nobody, allegedly, to approve. |
| `module-chain-key-seed-words-draft-1.md` | A seed Module: the chain-key seed written as 24 words, and how its keys are derived. |
| `module-chain-key-seed-hex-draft-1.md` | A seed Module: the chain-key seed written as 72 hexadecimal characters, and how its keys are derived. |
| `module-lightning-rail-draft-3.md` | Draft 3 (code after step 15, part 2): draft 2 with fields 11 and 13 of its specification (a request rail, binding payee and purpose; F301) and its rule said to be a reference implementation (F326). Draft 2 kept beside it. |
| `module-lightning-rail-draft-2.md` | A rail Module under the payment cMIP: a BOLT 11 invoice signed by the node the payee declared, committing to the payment, and its preimage, as the proof of a Lightning payment (roadmap step 12). Draft 2: **experimental**, its costs stated (F117), an instrument for testing Money, never for real money. Not approved. |
| `module-onchain-rail-draft-3.md` | Draft 3 (code after step 15, part 2): draft 2 with fields 11 and 13 (a request rail, binding payee and purpose; F301) and its rule said to be a reference implementation (F326). Draft 2 kept beside it. |
| `module-onchain-rail-draft-2.md` | A rail Module under the payment cMIP: pay-to-contract, a fresh Taproot address tweaked by the payment commitment, signed for by the payee's request key, and the confirmed transaction at its one canonical position, checked against the headers of the chain the verifier follows, as the proof of an on-chain payment (roadmap step 12a; F200 to F205). Draft 2: **experimental**, its costs stated; a request rail. Not approved. |
| `module-bitcoin-clock-draft-3.md` | Draft 3 (code after step 15, part 2): draft 2 with its rules said to be a reference implementation, not programs published under Development rule 22 (F326). Draft 2 kept beside it. |
| `module-bitcoin-clock-draft-2.md` | A clock Module under the anchoring cMIP (`cmips/cmip-anchoring-draft-2.md`, now draft 4): a point on Bitcoin is the block; an on-chain payment's proof is that payment's anchor (F201, F202); a batch anchor, the anchoring cMIP's batch with its root committed by pay-to-contract, checked by a sibling of the rail's rule (the same tweak and header checks against the chain followed, no amount, six confirmations), roadmap step 14a. Draft 2: **experimental, not approved**; draft 1 (`module-bitcoin-clock-draft-1.md`) kept, superseded. Code: `onchain/src/clock.rs` (crate `mor-onchain`). |
| `module-clock-timestamp-draft-2.md` | Draft 2 (code after step 15, part 2): draft 1 with its rules said to be a reference implementation, not programs published under Development rule 22 (F326). Draft 1 kept beside it. |
| `module-clock-timestamp-draft-1.md` (approved by Nobody, allegedly, 13 October 2026) | A clock Module under the anchoring cMIP: the simplest clock there is, an ordinary identity signing stamps, "I held these acts at this time", in seconds since 1970 (UTC); an act placed at its earliest stamp, and a reader placing an act by the earliest stamp of any act naming it; what it proves (its operator's word only) and does not, stated; for ordering what protects nothing, such as a feed (website cMIP draft 8). Draft 1: **experimental, not approved**. Code: `timestamp/` (`mor-timestamp`). |
| `units-bitcoin-draft-1.md` | Four units: the satoshi, and the satoshis of testnet, signet and regtest, which are not satoshis. Not yet approved. |
| `module-identity-carry-words-draft-2.md` | Draft 2 (F332): the words carry the identity's encryption key too, derived from the signing seed, so an identity brought into an app opens its own private messages; the stated cost, whoever holds the words can post as the identity and read its private messages; the words hidden before three are asked back (H3); the signing key's words and the chain key's always labelled; after a rotation made elsewhere, the app takes the new words and renews the encryption key it published. Draft 1 kept beside it. **Experimental, not approved.** |
| `module-identity-carry-words-draft-1.md` | An Identity Module: an identity carried from one app to another by its ID and its signing seed written as 24 words (the words Module's encoding). The chain key never travels; each app keeps its own line, with rule 17's warning before any rotation (roadmap step 9b). Draft 1, **experimental, not approved**. |
| `module-jpeg-draft-1.md` | A media type (task 5): a JPEG file as a picture, which way up and in what colours, and how a posting client strips it to the picture alone (roadmap step 9). |
| `module-audio-draft-1.md` | **Dubsar Audio**, draft 1 (decided by Nobody, allegedly, 21 October 2026: "let's have an audio module", M4A): a media type (task 5): one M4A file, the MP4 container with exactly one AAC-LC sound track, mono or stereo, and no picture track; what a client checks before playing, what it refuses (in plain words), its limits (64 MiB less 16 bytes, two hours, 48,000 Hz), and how a publishing client strips it to the sound alone, byte for byte. No app uses it yet. **Not yet approved.** |
| `module-video-draft-1.md` | A media type (task 5): an MP4 file with H.264 pictures and optional AAC sound as a film; what a client checks before playing, what it refuses, its limits, and how a publishing client strips it to the film alone (F243). Draft 1, **not yet approved**. |

| Code | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `airgap/` | The three chain-key Modules above in Rust (crate `mor-airgap`), with `mor-signer`, the command-line signer for an offline laptop. See its README. |
| `lightning/` | The Lightning rail Module in Rust (crate `mor-lightning`): the BOLT 11 decoder and the rule, a client for lnd (feature `lnd`), and the regtest network for the end-to-end test. See its README. |
| `onchain/` | The on-chain rail Module in Rust (crate `mor-onchain`): the tweak, the transaction and header decoder, the rule, and a btcd client (feature `btcd`) for the end-to-end test on regtest. See its README. |
| `carry/` | The identity carry Module in TypeScript (`mor-carry`): the words, read and refused, the encryption key they carry (draft 2), checked against the Rust code's vectors (`airgap/src/carry.rs`, feature `carry`). See its README. |
| `jpeg/` | The JPEG Module in TypeScript (`mor-jpeg`): reading and stripping, with test pictures and vectors checked by Pillow. See its README. |
| `timestamp/` | The timestamp clock Module in TypeScript (`mor-timestamp`): the stamp's format, reading a clock's stamps judged through its chain, and the clock service, `mor-clock`, stamping the acts it is given (an open HTTP form) and those it finds at the relays it follows. The site's display client reads stamps with the same code. |
| `video/` | The video Module in TypeScript (`mor-video`): checking and stripping, with synthetic test films and vectors checked by ffprobe and ffmpeg. See its README. |
| `audio/` | The audio Module in TypeScript (`mor-audio`): checking and stripping an M4A file with the video Module's own code (boxes, the track, AAC, stripping) and its own rules (brands, one sound track, limits, words), with synthetic test recordings and vectors checked by ffprobe and ffmpeg. See its README. |
