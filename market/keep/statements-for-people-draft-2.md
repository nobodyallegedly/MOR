*Each statement is tested by The Back Row's member test 4: people tell it back in their own words, right after setup and a week later, and eight in ten must answer rightly both times. A question that fails changes the setup, not the person.*

## 1. For a member

> Your name on MOR has two keys. One lives on your phone and signs what you do every day. A spare is kept somewhere safe, away from any screen, in pieces held by people you chose. Only the spare can replace a key.
>
> **If you lose your phone, or someone steals it,** the people holding the pieces of your spare come together and replace the key on your phone. Your name, your history and what you paid for stay yours. Do it quickly: until you do, whoever holds your phone can act in your name. **Then your app anchors the change. Until it is anchored, money people send following your old details counts as paid to you, even if a thief took it.** *(New in draft 2; Postmark's line.)*
>
> **If you lose the spare but still have your phone,** your name keeps working, but its keys can never be replaced again. You can no longer sell a share you own. **You can still leave a group with your phone alone, keeping your share, so it can split or close without waiting for you, unless its rules count by share; then it waits for its rule on absent members.** If you set it up beforehand, the person you named to follow you can take over your followers and be proposed for your seats, and your shares can pass to the people you named in each agreement. If you didn't, none of that is possible. **Set it up on the first day.** *(Changed in draft 2: draft 1 said you could no longer sign to split or close a group, which was true only until you leave it.)*
>
> **If you lose the spare and your phone is stolen,** whoever holds your phone can act in your name, and nobody can stop them, Keep included. Your successor, if you named one, is where your followers can go.
>
> **If you lose both,** nobody can ever sign in your name again. What you published stays readable, but you would have to start a new name.
>
> **If enough of the people holding the pieces of your spare act together without you,** they can replace your keys and take your name. Choose them as you would choose who holds a copy of your house keys, and never give enough pieces to one household. *(The Back Row: "never give enough pieces to one household" quoted as the line it will reuse.)*
>
> **If too few of them are left,** because they died, moved away or lost their piece, your spare is gone, and you may not know until the day you need it. Once a year, your app asks each of them to check their piece. It never brings the pieces together to do it. *(The Back Row's case.)*
>
> **If Keep disappears,** nothing changes for you. We never held any of it.

### The yearly check, as the member sees it

*(The Back Row's wording, with the case it leads to. The numbers follow the member's own split. Neither message is shown only once: it stays until the member acts.)*

> **Two of your five holders didn't check this year. Your spare still works, but only just. Ask the three who checked to help you make a new spare now, while they still can.**
>
> **Three of your five holders didn't check this year. Your spare no longer works, and can't be remade. Your name keeps working on your phone; protect your phone. If you named someone to take over, they still can.**

### The successor, as the member sees it

> **You named [name] to take over your name. They haven't confirmed, and that's as it should be:** they confirm when the time comes, even after you're gone, and need nothing from anyone to do it. Make sure they know how. **If [name] can no longer take over, because they died or lost their own keys, name someone else with your spare.** *(Last sentence: The Back Row.)*
>
> **[Name] has confirmed.** People who follow you may now start following them instead. If you didn't expect this, tell them, and change your successor with your spare.

*The setup names a successor, or records that the member chose none, in a change of keys made during the setup itself, while the spare is in hand (Identity rule 37: a successor is named only in a change of keys).*

## 2. For a subscriber

*Beside the four cases of the audience keys Module (draft 2, section 6), quoted from there.*

> **Your paper is a key to your money and to your privacy.** Whoever sees it can collect refunds owed to you, and link every period you ever paid, in every bundle you use. Keep it out of sight. *(The Back Row: the money first. Until the audience keys Module's draft 3, this reads "a key to your privacy".)*
>
> **What the support line can do:** help you type your paper on a new phone and get your past periods back.
> **What it will never do:** see your screen, ask you to read your words aloud, or send you a link to type them into. You type them only on your own phone, in the app you installed yourself. Anyone who asks otherwise is not us. *(The Back Row: a rule to hold to, not only a warning.)*
> **What it cannot do:** bring back a paper you lost, see what you paid for, or act in your name.

## 3. For a person holding a piece of someone's spare

> **You hold one piece of someone's spare.** On its own, your piece does nothing. With enough other pieces, it can change their keys, and so act as them. They chose you because they trust you.
>
> **Never** show your piece to anyone, type it anywhere but the app they set up for this, or give it to someone else to hold. Never bring it together with other pieces unless the person who chose you asks, in person or by a way you've agreed in advance.
>
> **Once a year** your app will ask you to check your piece. It checks it alone, and tells the person only that it is still good.
>
> **If they ask you to help them,** check it is really them, the way you agreed when you took the piece. Then follow the app.
>
> **If anyone else asks,** whatever they say, even that the person is ill or gone: don't. Tell the person, if you can.
>
> **If the person who chose you dies,** whoever they named to take over while alive needs nothing from you: what passes to them passes without your piece. If someone asks for your piece after their death, don't, unless the person who chose you told you, in the instructions they gave you with your piece, to help a named person, and your app shows that person as the one they named. Without that, do nothing. Nothing can be done, and nothing should be. *("While alive": The Back Row.)*
>
> **If you no longer want to hold it,** tell them. They'll make a new spare and deal new pieces to people who will hold them. Until they do, keep yours safe; it may be one of too few. Don't destroy it without telling them, and don't hand it on. *(The Back Row's question.)*
>
> **If you lose it,** tell them at once. That is not a failure; not telling them is. *(Kept exactly, as The Back Row asked.)*

## 4. The questions people are tested on

The Back Row's three, for members:

1. "Your phone and your spare are both gone. What happens to your account, and what can you still do?"
2. "Who could take your name without you, and how would you stop that happening?"
3. "Someone calls saying they are support and asks for your words. What do you do?"

And a fourth, for members who named a successor (Keep's, agreed by The Back Row):

4. "What happens if the person you named confirms tomorrow?"

For subscribers, the same questions are asked of the paper rather than the spare.
