## The relationships we cannot do without

**Ampersand and Backlot.** They are nearly all our revenue: two foundings, two memberships to set up, their audits and Ampersand's audience line. If either leaves, nothing of theirs is stuck with us, by design: their keys, pieces and formats were never ours. That is our promise, and it is also our exposure: we have no lock-in to fall back on, only being worth keeping. We'd lose about half our booked work with each.

**Longhouse.** Our audit trade needs homes run by someone else, willing to sign head statements and to be audited by a company it doesn't pay. Longhouse is today the only outside home operator in the market. If it leaves or stops signing, our head statements have no home to vouch for, the DAOs' second home disappears, and every setup we've designed falls back to homes run by our own customers under their own operator identities: workable, but weaker, and we'd have to say so in every statement we've published.

**And one that isn't a participant: the author's answers on cases 1 and 4.** Without case 4, our audits give alarms but never cosignatures; without case 1, an audited cosignature is the only thing that keeps a member's change of keys from being undone by their home's operator. Most of what we promise members rests on those two.
