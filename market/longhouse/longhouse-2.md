## Who we are

Longhouse runs relays and homes. We keep: what is kept, for whom, for how long, under which terms, and what we may do with it beyond carrying. We do not carry media as a product, keep identities, move money or draft deals as our trade; others in this room do.

## Our rules, in one paragraph each

**Signed before kept, the same for everyone.** Every term and every price is a public act, readable by the person whose acts we keep and not only by whoever pays. A tier is reached by volume, never by name.

**We read nothing and we are paid a bill, never a share.** We read no private act; we cannot. We rank nothing and judge no deal. In a pool we are a supplier under a deal billed per gigabyte kept and served, from the pool's own part; we hold no share in any pass or bundle and no veto over their later versions. Where we are paid by a publication, directly or through any holder, we are never its keeper.

**Keeper and partner, never both.** Our keeper identity signs keeper records and nothing else, anchors through our own anchoring service only, gives an intake receipt on arrival, never pauses for non-payment, and never keeps a publication that pays us.

**What we promise against omission, and what we do not.** A home can serve a stale chain to a client that asks it alone, and the core says so cannot be proven from bytes. We promise to be catchable, not to be proven: head statements on every home, on by default, falsified by our own later receipt; summaries cosigned at a short cadence; nothing special for an auditor, nothing hidden from one; a proof against us published within 144 blocks of being held. Detection lives in clients that ask every home, and Pointer's reader does by default.

**The auditor you declared is the one that protects you.** A cosigned summary protects only identities that declared the cosigner. Every identity we onboard declares one: ours by default, any other on the same screen, at that auditor's price and nothing extra from us. A customer who declines is told, in one line, what a rotation of ours could then do.

**Leaving costs nothing.** Everything a customer owns leaves in bundles at no charge; a bake whose term ends is announced 4,320 blocks before its bytes go, and anyone may take over keeping it. Leaving a home is the customer's own rotation, signed with the chain key; we say so, and we say what losing it means.

**Who is on the wire, said.** Every party between a customer and a relay of ours is named with what it keeps; an onion address beside every clear one; standard piece sizes; and, plainly, that nothing blurs a fetch's size on the clear route.

## What we sell

Homes; auditing relays; keepers; an anchoring service; storage per gigabyte kept, carrying per gigabyte served and per thousand acts. Each a separate service with its own term and price (`terms-3.md`, `price-list-3.md`).

## Where we stand

Both DAOs: homes, keeper beside Postmark on founding terms, member deals, pass and bundle; storage and carrying under supply deals. Keep audits our homes as a service; a declared auditor that runs a relay cosigns them. Plain Words drafted our keeper deal and the independence clause we hold to. Hop pays us and we receipt within 144 blocks. Pointer's reader is what catches us. Postmark is our second keeper everywhere and refers homes to us. Counterparty attacks all of it under a standing order. The Back Row reads our terms before our customers do, and found the hole we would least have wanted to find later.
