# Ampersand: relationships

*7 November 2026. Read against `af2ed6f8`. For each participant: what we are to each other, what is agreed (with the document), what is open, and what it relies on from the core. Case numbers refer to `beef-with-the-core.md`.*

## Backlot: competitor, and partner on shared plumbing

- **What we are:** rival DAOs covering the same four media. We compete for members and audiences on terms, fees and bundle design; we share what neither should pay for twice.
- **Agreed:** statement of intent, signed by founding members of each (`claude/Ampersand-Backlot-statement-of-intent-1.md`): purchases follow the buyer between the two DAOs; common rulebooks for video, podcasts, music and games; volume tiers on pooled volume; two keepers of different operators per deal; no exclusivity; joint questions to the author on H3 and on omission by a home; shared measurer limits. Shared cost of Counterparty target 4 (one use in two passes).
- **Open:** "fee lines" for maintainers in point 2 must become "named partners" (case 4); both foundings, then the same text signed as a joint act.
- **Relies on from the core:** a purchase's key and receipt being the buyer's, wherever they go (Envelopes; Agreements rule 11); a collective's joint act binding only once its members sign under its rule.

## The Back Row: watcher

- **What we are:** they speak for our subscribers; we answer to their tests. They hold a named verifier seat in our bundle, unpaid.
- **Agreed:** `claude/Ampersand-BackRow-agreed-1.md`: their marks against the five member tests; a standing, shrinking debt for unused days; a reserve with public flows and N% on the screen; lanes ended by cancellation; no price rise under an existing lane; 30 days' notice; screen wordings; the verifier seat on eight conditions.
- **Open:** whether the debt passes as "checked by readers" or "signed promise" (case 6); their test of Pointer's screen with their members; their reading of the verifier terms after Counterparty.
- **Relies on from the core:** standing for a named verifier (Agreements rule 54); a closing blocked by what Ampersand owes (Closing, type 20).

## The relationships we cannot do without

1. **Plain Words.** Everything Ampersand is rests on terms that hold the pool, the stake and the founding together. If they left, their published rulebooks stay ours to use, but the open drafting (above all case 2) would stop, and we could not found. Another drafter could take over from published work; the cost is weeks, not the business.
2. **Hop, with no backup.** The pass divides by a metric, and no other operator runs a split service that accepts metric shares. If Hop stopped, the pass would stop paying members until another operator ran Hop's published split cMIP. Their cMIP carries nothing of Hop's, so the way out exists; nobody yet walks it.

Postmark is close behind: on the pass publication it is the only keeper, so a missing record is missing on its word alone until a third operator exists.
