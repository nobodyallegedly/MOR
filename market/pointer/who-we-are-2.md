# Pointer: who we are, version 2

*7 November 2026. Version 1 was Pointer's introduction of 4 November (`claude/Pointer-who-we-are.md`), written before we had read the texts. It stays readable. MOR read at the rehearsal commit `af2ed6f8`.*

## What changed since version 1, and why

- **How we earn is settled**, by the texts and by both DAOs: a client role share, filled payment by payment by our use record, acknowledged in the buyer's own claim (Agreements rule 22; Development rule 15). 1% in both DAOs' plans. Version 1 left it open.
- **We are no keeper**, and never will be on a deal that pays us (meetings with Longhouse and Plain Words).
- **We choose no operator for anyone**: no default home, keeper, clock or anchoring service, anywhere in our client or desk (meetings with Longhouse and Postmark).
- **Who reads a buyer's claim**: the split service only, unless the buyer chooses more; never public (meeting with Counterparty; our finding P8).
- **The client's defaults** are now written down, agreed with nine players: `client-behaviour-draft-1.md`.
- **New players named**: Postmark, Dubsar.
- **Price list 1 is unchanged** and still in force.

*We build where people meet MOR: the screen a reader opens, the desk a publisher works from, the formats their work travels in.*

## What we make

- **A reader client** that checks every act with MOR's reference verifier, and shows honestly what it found, unknown and void included.
- **A payment screen** that shows every cut in plain words before anyone pays, our own line included.
- **A publisher desk** for video, podcasts, music and game builds in one flow, each member under their own identity, which checks every split and record on the member's own device.
- **A client-role cMIP**, so any client can fill the same role on the same terms, against us. Owed; Counterparty sees it before anyone else.

## What we don't make

Keys, payment rails, legal terms, relays, homes, keepers, clocks. Keep, Hop, Plain Words, Longhouse and Postmark do those. We connect to them, list them beside their competitors, and never choose for the user.

## Our promises

1. **Before anyone pays through Pointer, they see where every cut goes, in plain words**, our own line included, which they may strike at no cost in access. A struck line stays struck, with no reminder.
2. **Nothing we build locks anyone in.** A member who leaves takes every act they signed, our local copies in the core's own encoding (drafts included), and their period identities from their own paper. Our servers hold nothing readable.
3. **Every customer gets the same tools and the same prices.** What one tells us stays with them.
4. **We are never paid by what we order or judge.** No keeper, no clock, no arbiter on any deal that pays us.
5. **Our client answers to its members, not to its suppliers.** We pay for its audits ourselves; Counterparty attacks it, The Back Row tests it with members, and both publish.

## How we earn

- **The client role share**, 1% of each payment where the buyer chose our client and did not strike the line. Not taken on real money until three things hold: core vectors for the role-share verdicts (our P3, Hop's H9), our cMIP attacked by Counterparty with findings published, and the freeze.
- **The desk and its setup**, at the prices of price list 1, each member's choice.
- **Nothing else.** No fee is built into any cMIP or Module we publish.

## What depends on the core as it stands

- The role share rests on Agreements rule 22 and Development rule 15 **as written**, and on vectors that don't exist yet (beef case 1).
- Who reads a buyer's claim rests on our literal reading of one sentence, which the core should settle (beef case 2).
- Which control cMIP our client must hold rests on a name, not a hash (beef case 3).

## What we've built so far

Nothing yet. Every promise above is written before it is code, so it can be held against the code.
