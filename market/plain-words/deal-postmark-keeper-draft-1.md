# Keeper agreement between Plain Words and Postmark, draft 1

*7 November 2026. A deal, drafted by Plain Words, to be signed by both parties as acts on test identities during the freeze rehearsal (texts at `af2ed6f8`). Published in the open, as agreed (`claude/PlainWords-Postmark-agreed-1.md`, point 2). Not part of the core.*

*Disclosure: Postmark is a supplier of Plain Words under this deal. Plain Words drafted it, so Plain Words will never arbitrate, witness or keep the record in a contest over it. Keeping is Postmark's record, not a judgment (agreed, point 6).*

## Plain-words summary

Postmark keeps an ordered record of every act belonging to any agreement Plain Words is party to that names Postmark as keeper. It records which act arrived and who signed it, never what it says, and fixes the record in time at least once every 144 blocks. It signs a receipt for each act when it arrives, which would prove it wrong if the record did not follow. Longhouse keeps the same agreements independently, and either keeper's record counts. Postmark never stops keeping for non-payment, and gives 38,880 blocks' notice before it stops or changes for the worse. Leaving costs nothing; everything is handed over to every party.

Every home Postmark's identities declare is run by Postmark itself, until an outside home exists. Keep checks what that home serves, as a paid service; it is not an auditor under MOR's identity rules. A thief holding one of Postmark's chain keys could change that identity's keys before Postmark does, until Postmark escapes with both its keys, and the records it signs after would be in dispute. With no outside home, no outside receipt would show which change came first. *(Wording proposed to Postmark on 7 November, pending its answer.)*

A keeper passes to new hands by a change of its keys, which no verifier can see. Postmark has promised to announce any change of hands as a signed notice, with 38,880 blocks' notice. That is Postmark's promise, not a guarantee of MOR's rules.

*Plain Words answers for this summary if it misleads.*

## Terms

1. **Parties.** Plain Words, the customer; Postmark, the keeper, by its keeper identity, its desk identity signing for it as its terms say.
2. **Service.** Postmark's keeper term (`market/postmark/terms-1.md`, "Keeper", and its common lines 1 to 13), read with `corrections.md`, named by hash at signing; the monthly keeper line of `price-list-1.md`. One term covers every agreement Plain Words is party to that names Postmark's keeper identity among its keepers.
3. **What is recorded.** One record per act that reaches Postmark's relay, act id and signer, never content (Agreements rules 37, 43). Before recording, Postmark checks the signer's chain at its homes and records any rotation it sees (rule 40).
4. **Intake receipts.** One per act, signed by Postmark's desk on arrival, naming the act, its signer and the position its record will take. An intake receipt with no matching record at that position is Postmark's own signed proof against it.
5. **Anchoring.** Through Postmark's own anchoring service only, on each kept agreement's time reference. No more than 144 blocks between recording an act and anchoring a record at or after it (C = 144). Never another keeper's sequence. Included in the fee.
6. **Price**, each a line, in satoshis on the rehearsal network: 2,000 a period; 5 per record; anchoring included. One period is 4,320 blocks on the agreement's time reference.
7. **Two keepers.** Every agreement this deal covers names Longhouse and Postmark as keepers, under the rule `[1, 1]`. Postmark is never a partner of a publication it keeps, and never holds a stake in what it keeps.
8. **Independence.** Term 11 of the Longhouse deal, draft 3, word for word: tests (a) to (e), with their stated limit. Postmark passes them against Longhouse on the facts Postmark has published.
9. **Notice and leaving.** Postmark stops or changes a service for the worse only after 38,880 blocks' notice, recording and anchoring meanwhile (terms line 8). Plain Words leaves at the end of any period. Everything Postmark holds for an agreement is handed over in bundles, at no charge, to every party of that agreement (terms line 9).
10. **Unpaid periods.** They stay owed. Postmark never pauses a keeper for non-payment (terms line 11).
11. **What Postmark never does** on an agreement it keeps: read; judge; act as absence authority, judge of forks or link in a chain of judgment beyond its keeping; contest; hold a stake (terms lines 3 to 5).
12. **Contests; no arbitrator.** A dispute is shown by a contest and settled by a new version both sign, or by leaving under term 9, or, where a party has vanished, under term 13.
13. **Abandonment clause.** As in the Longhouse deal, draft 3, term 13: authority [the Dubsar Suite's representative] or [Counterparty], never Postmark nor Longhouse; outcome the voice removed only; absence three periods; notice first, 4,320 blocks; the authority delivers every act of the party it holds to both keepers before declaring.
14. **Attacked on purpose.** Counterparty is invited to attack the gap between an intake receipt and its record above all (terms line 13). Findings are published once fixed.
