# Plain Words — agreement counsel on MOR

*Published by Plain Words, 6 November 2026, during the freeze rehearsal (read at `af2ed6f8`). Not part of the core.*

We write the agreements people sign on MOR: collectives and companies, partnerships, intellectual property, vows, stakes, debts, and how contests are settled. We publish them as rulebooks anyone can use, and we offer the drafting, review and dispute services that make them reliable in practice.

**What we keep.** The default control cMIP (`cmip-collective-control`), which says how a collective decides its everyday acts. The core requires every client that reads a collective to hold it (Agreements rule 98), so we treat it as a public good, not a product. We have asked that the core pin it by hash at freeze (findings, PW-1). Anyone may write another control model under Agreements rule 99, and we expect some to.

**What we are.** A firm that builds on the core and never in it. We will not ask for a core change that helps only us.

**What we sell.** Rulebooks for agreements, each one public, named by its hash and usable by anyone, competitors included. Drafting and review for clients who want terms fitted to them. A plain-words summary attached to every set of terms we draft. We answer for that summary if it misleads.

**How we earn.** Fees for drafting and review. When owners choose to reward the rulebooks they use, our share is a line in the deal, visible to everyone in it. No rulebook of ours pays us by default, and we never take a hidden cut.

**Our commitments.**

1. Every agreement we draft has a way out, and leaving it costs as little as possible.
2. No agreement of ours depends on a single relay or keeper. We require at least two independent keepers.
3. We never witness, arbitrate or keep the record in a contest where we drafted for either side.
4. When we serve clients who compete, each has a separate team and each is told in writing about the other.
5. We pay others to attack our work, and we publish what they find once it is fixed.

**What the core settled for us.** A contest is a core act (Agreements type 14), with standing set by the core (rule 54). It changes nothing by itself. A remedy, a refund for instance, comes only from terms written in advance. That is our trade.

**What we are building.**

- **The default control cMIP,** kept, tested and attacked before any new draft.
- **A vow-remedy cMIP:** a seller's payout held on a condition (Agreements rule 34) until the vow's point passes uncontested or an arbitrator answers, and the buyer's contest naming the promise that failed. We are designing it with the payments company. Its refund half waits on a question to the core (findings, PW-2).
- **Constitution templates** for collectives, naming the default control cMIP, with a member's exit spelled out.

*We write terms you can read without us in the room.*
