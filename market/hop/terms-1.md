# Hop: terms of the split service, draft 1

*7 November 2026. The terms Hop asks every publication it serves to carry, beside its split cMIP. To be signed as public acts once Hop's identity exists on regtest; until then they are text. Nothing is updated: a change is terms-2. Not core.*

**Where they come from.** Each term names the meeting that set it. The records: `Hop-Backlot-meeting-1`, `Ampersand-Hop-agreed-1`, `Hop-Counterparty-meeting-1`, `Longhouse-Hop-meeting-1`, `Hop-Postmark-agreed-1`, `Keep-Hop-agreed-1`, and The Back Row's record of its meeting with Hop.

**What waits on the core.** Term 5 exists because of our H7 (a present split service can refuse any new version). Term 7 exists because of our H1 (a split cannot pay a vow's refund). Term 4's delivery follows our H10's broad reading. If the core answers those, these terms shrink.

## 1. What the publication carries for Hop

- Hop as split service (field 3), signing every price tag.
- Hop as a partner (field 2), at the line of our price list in force when the publication is first signed.
- Our split cMIP, draft 1 or later (field 9, task 8, or plan key 1).
- An abandonment clause covering the split service and every partner, authority a named identity, outcome the voice removed only (Plain Words).
- At least two independent keepers of the ownership agreement, or a successor named and kept from the first day (Postmark's F-PM-4).
- The three custody limits below.

## 2. Custody limits (The Back Row, Keep)

Signed by every party, set per publication; Hop cannot raise any of them alone:
- the most Hop may hold outside its vault for the publication;
- the most Hop may hold in its vault for it;
- the longest any amount may wait before it moves.

At nine tenths of what Hop may hold, Hop refuses requests for new passes; at the full amount, renewals too, saying why. No limit is raised to fit a publication: if a period's money plus the record deadline's does not fit, Hop does not carry it until it does.

## 3. How Hop holds the money (Keep; Hop's README, commitment 11)

One identity, not a collective. Chain key two of three among Hop's people. Homes: Longhouse's and one Hop runs under a separate operator identity. The Bitcoin clock, each lock change anchored through two independent services. A hot node for receiving and paying out; an on-chain vault for anything that waits, two of three of Hop's people to spend. **Hop has three people today:** the vault's signers and the chain key's holders are the same three until Hop has six, and the bounds that hold meanwhile are written in our README.

## 4. What every party sees

- Every receipt Hop signs, sealed to the payer.
- Every split, delivered to every holder of the stake, paid or not, and to every receiver; deposited where delivery fails (Counterparty, Postmark).
- Each period, a public custody statement: what Hop held, where it waited, each movement and its rail fee, each pointing to its rail proof.
- What Hop holds is computable from its receipts, splits and payout receipts by the publication's parties and any verifier they name; those acts are private to the parties they concern, so a buyer checks through them (The Back Row).

## 5. Signing new versions (H7; Longhouse's wording)

Counted on the agreement's time reference from a version offered to Hop and recorded by a keeper of the agreement:
- within **1,008 blocks**, Hop signs any version that replaces Hop, or that neither adds to its duties nor lowers its line;
- within **288 blocks**, any version replacing a measurer after a proven lie.
This is Hop's promise, not a guarantee of MOR's rules: a split service that stays present can refuse any new version.

## 6. What Hop reads

The identity each payment names, because the payment's commitment includes it; the measurer's records, because Hop divides by them; nothing else. With a fresh identity each period, Hop cannot tell a subscriber's months apart.

## 7. Refunds

Hop pays every refund the core owes from what it holds: a payment racing the publication's ending, a payment that received neither its key nor a receipt naming the tag (Agreements rule 11), to the payer the commitment names, at the refund rail its claim names. **Hop pays no refund for a broken vow:** a split service cannot (H1). The publication's summary says so in plain words.

## 8. Leaving

The publication's parties replace Hop by a new version (term 5). Hop still owes payouts on everything it received before (Agreements rule 31), and delivers every receipt and split it signed for the publication to the new service, at no charge.
