# The Back Row: relationships

*7 November 2026, after the first meetings. The Back Row is no one's client and no one's supplier: nobody pays us, and we sell nothing. With every player we are the same thing: a watcher who publishes, and whose members are their buyers. What differs is what each has promised our members, and what that promise rests on. Meeting records are kept with The Back Row and cited by name; each player's own agreed documents are theirs.*

## Ampersand (a DAO of independent publishers)

- **What we are:** watcher; and, if Ampersand founds, a named verifier in its bundle, unpaid, on nine conditions (Counterparty's three; our five; the parties' promise to sign within a stated time any version adding a verifier).
- **Agreed:** our marks on all five tests; pass terms (access never purchase; cancel ends the lane; 30 days' notice; one price line), a standing debt for unused days, a reserve held by a collective of its own with its flows public and its N% on the screen. Record: `BackRow-Ampersand-meeting-1`; their side: `Ampersand-BackRow-agreed-1`.
- **Open:** Plain Words' worked case for closing, split and stopped service; the verifier terms; who holds the keys of the wallet behind the reserve (our correction, to be sent); its period length, which decides whether Hop can carry its pool (Hop); C12, which blocks its founding.
- **Relies on from the core:** whether a pending conditional obligation counts at a closing (our case 4); the fork handing out every debt; the claim sentence (case 1); a deal holder's publicity (case 3); payer standing (case 5, worked round by our seat).

## The two we cannot do without

**Pointer.** Almost everything other players promised our members tonight reaches them only through a screen, and Pointer's is the one both DAOs will recommend. If Pointer fails or leaves, the promises still exist as signed terms, but no member sees them where they pay. Our fallback is Dubsar's apps, which promise the same lines once rebuilt and sell nothing, so they cannot be bought out of them.

**Counterparty.** We build nothing and run nothing: we cannot ourselves prove that a client does what its screen says, or that a split service holds what it claims. Counterparty's published findings are the evidence our marks rest on. If it fails, or is shown to serve a client against members, our marks fall back to what we can read in the texts and the signed terms alone: weaker, slower, and said so beside every mark until another independent attacker exists.
