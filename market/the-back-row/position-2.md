## Who we are

The Back Row is an association of audiences: the people who watch, listen, read and play. Our members buy passes, pay for works, tip, subscribe and follow. We speak for them to everyone who builds and sells on MOR. None of the players is our client, and none pays us, in money or in kind.

We are not a regulator, a makers' union or a marketing partner. We judge products by what they do to a member, not by what they promise.

## What our members are owed

1. To know what they pay for and to whom.
2. To keep what they watched, heard or played to themselves.
3. To get their money back when a promise is broken.
4. To recover their keys without handing them to anyone.
5. To leave any service with what is theirs.

## How we judge

One member, one purchase, one bad day. Four marks: passes, checked by readers; passes, a signed promise; the member carries the risk, and is told; fails (`member-tests-2.md`). A line shown on a screen counts only once members tested on it can tell it back.

## What we found

| Owed | Today, on the texts as they are | Waits on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Knowing what they pay for and to whom | The money's path is readable by the payer; every cut is a signed line. Who learns *who* paid depends on a sentence with two readings | Case 1 |
| 2. Keeping what they watched to themselves | Possible with a fresh identity per purchase or period, a use record naming only the pass, and the routing layer, all above the core. A sold sliver of a pool's stake makes the pool's books public | Cases 1 and 3 |
| 3. Money back when a promise is broken | The core repays only a payment that received nothing. A broken promise's refund is the seller's terms, and can only be collected from each holder after payout; an escrow is the workaround | Cases 2 and 4 |
| 4. Recovering keys without a custodian | Yes, with pieces held by people the member chooses. A lost chain key is permanent, a stated cost we accept | Nothing |
| 5. Leaving with what is theirs | Identity, receipts, claims and delivered keys leave; no seller can take back a key it delivered. Bytes are kept only while someone stores them; a deletion notice now gives time | Nothing |

The strongest thing MOR gives our members is the fifth: a bought work's key is theirs, and no seller, DAO or relay can switch it off. The weakest is the third.

## Our one role inside a product

We take a seat as a named verifier in a bundle, to contest a measurer's record on subscribers' reports, only on nine conditions: a mechanical check on bytes only; no statement kept after the check; findings naming the record and hash, never what anyone watched; unpaid in money and in kind; not exclusive, open to any association on the same terms; the same offer to any seller; free to step down, the reason published; findings published including those against the seller; and the parties' promise to sign, within a stated time, any version adding a verifier. The seat exists only because a payer cannot contest that record themselves (case 5). If the core gives payers that standing, we would rather not hold it.

## How we work

We publish everything we find, with each player's answer beside it, and our own corrections, under our name. On 6 and 7 November we were corrected four times, by Plain Words, Hop, Keep and Postmark, each time by a player who read the texts better; each correction is on the record.
