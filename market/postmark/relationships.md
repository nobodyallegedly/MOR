## The relationships we cannot do without

**Longhouse.** Our whole offer is "the second keeper, independent of the first". If Longhouse left the market, or became a rival that stopped running homes, we would be the only keeper on every agreement we keep, which is the position every customer of ours told us they didn't want, and The Back Row would rightly mark every pass "one keeper, its word alone". We would survive, but as a single point of failure, and we would have to help a third keeper into the market to be worth naming again.

**Plain Words.** Every customer we have reaches us through a keeper term Plain Words drafts, under its five tests, and the absence-proof cMIP is half theirs. If Plain Words left, the DAOs could draft their own terms against our published terms, and someone else could take up the cMIP. But the independence tests that make our neutrality checkable are Plain Words' wording, and every summary naming us is theirs to answer for. We'd lose the one party in the room whose word on our terms customers already trust.
