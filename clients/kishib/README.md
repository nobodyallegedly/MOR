# Kišib (mor-kishib)

**Kišib** (𒈩, Sumerian kišib, "the cylinder seal": a person's mark of authority, serious to lose and serious to replace; F335): the identity hub: the one place where a chain key is used, from paper, and then forgotten; and, in the program (Mac and Linux, never the web client), the **Dubsar runner**, which signs for the apps you approve. On the Mac and Linux, a program and its page, as Dub; the same page as a web client, **for testing only**, to be served at `kishib.dubsar.org`, with a one-line banner (its "?" opens the risks) on every screen. **Rebuilt on the Dubsar Suite's kit (`clients/kit`) on 22 October 2026**, as the redesign drew it (`docs/design/suite-redesign.md`, "Kišib"; the mockups `docs/design/mockups/kishib-*`; the session's report, `docs/kishib-runner-report.md`): dominantly black, the Identity layer's glacier blue in pastel fills (`src/page/view.ts`, `TINT`).

**Test identities only, until MOR is frozen.** Written "Kishib" in file and folder names.

## In plain words

**Four tiles**, each with its danger marks (decided on the mockups): **Identities** (one bar), **Runner** (two; in the program only, never on the web, so the web client shows three tiles), **Paper** (three), **Collectives** (three). A tile opens with its column on the left; the tabs across the top move between them; on a phone, the column comes first and folds away once a part is chosen, with a back arrow. Top right, what Kišib holds: "Holds nothing · runner off" while the runner is off; **"Runner locked"** while it waits for its passphrase; with it on, the identities it keeps (decided: "the only place where an ID can be held is the new runner").

- **Identities**: **Make an identity** (H48: its homes, its relays, a chain key on a new paper whose words are shown once and three asked back before anything is signed, then its signing key's words once its homes take it; Kišib keeps nothing); **Read a record** (any ID: homes, receipts, keys, names, lines, any contest or succession); **Confirm a succession** (moved here from Paper: the successor confirms with its signing key's words, not paper). With the runner on, **Held here** comes first: the identities the runner keeps, each with its record and **Remove**, and the form to give it one.
- **Paper**, behind **the quiet door** ("Have your paper ready. Kišib uses the words for one act and forgets them.", Open Paper or Not now, nothing else on the screen): **Check the paper**, **Change keys**, **Change the vault**, **Name a successor**, and, last and set apart, **Escape a contested position**. A change of keys shows its steps: the words › read back › new chain paper › signed › new signing words.
- **Collectives**, behind the same door: **Waiting for you** (give your share to a rotation another member started), **Start a rotation**, **In the same room**, **Check a share** (new: a paper share, its number and 24 words, checked against the collective: written right and whose it is; with as many papers as rebuild the key, whether they still rebuild it; nothing signed).
- **Runner** (the program only; below).

An identity in MOR has two keys that matter here. The **signing key** signs everyday acts; Dub and Edubba hold it, and carry it by its words. The **chain key** exists only on paper: 24 words, written when the identity was made, and written anew at every change of its keys. It is used only to change the signing key, for example when a phone is lost or stolen. Kišib is where that paper is typed, used for the act at hand, and forgotten. **Every change of keys makes two new papers** (H79): a new chain key's words, for the next change, and a new signing key's words, for the apps. The old chain-key paper is then spent.

**It holds nothing between uses** (but the runner's keys, while it is on or locked, encrypted under its passphrase, below). The words typed are turned into keys for one act, then overwritten. Nothing is written to the program's folder (which holds only which browsers are paired with it) or to the browser's storage (the web client opens no database and writes nothing to local or session storage). A review left unconfirmed is dropped, with its words, after fifteen minutes. Close Kišib, and nothing of your words remains.

What it does:

- **Change an identity's keys** (F335). Give its ID, one of its homes, and its chain key's 24 words. Kišib reads the identity from every home its chain names and every relay its routes name, finds **every line of its acts**, the ones Dub made, Edubba made and the web client made, and says each in plain words (Identity rule 35): a line left out would have its acts become void. **A line holding private acts is placed by its card** (H80's question 2, F373): paste each app's card whole, as its identity window shows it (latest act, position, summary, then how many of its acts Kišib cannot read, and the app's name). Each card must name an act the homes hold; when the apps' counts add up to the acts the homes hold that Kišib cannot read, those acts lie on the cards' lines and are kept with them, nothing to tick. **Where a line cannot be placed** (a card whose latest act is not at the homes, an app saying acts of its line are missing, counts that fall short), the review says so **in red** before anything is signed: whose line, what lives on it (a member key, notes, a list), and that it may stop counting as if left out; nothing is signed until the box under it is ticked. **Since F376 each app keeps two lines**, its public acts on one (Kišib reads it itself, and so can anyone after the change) and its private acts on the other (kept by its card); its window shows a card per line, each naming its app and its line (public, private, or the line from before, public and private acts together), joined by “; ”: paste them whole, and Kišib says each line by name. *Cost, stated (accepted by Nobody, allegedly, 21 October 2026, F376):* the counts are each app's own word; Kišib cannot check them. A count changes only what Kišib says, never what the change of keys keeps; an act on no line given makes the counts fall short and is said in red. Everything is read back before anything is signed. Confirmed, **Kišib first shows the new chain key's words**, a new paper made at that moment (a fresh seed: the air-gapped Module's rule 3.3, the words seed Module's derivation, key *n* of the new paper for the rotation at position *n*): you write them down, they are hidden, three are asked back, and Kišib forgets them. **Only then** does it sign one rotation, with the old paper's chain key, committing the new paper's key, and send it to every home. Said before the words: this paper becomes the one that counts once the homes accept the change; until then the old paper still works, but only to send this same change again. If the homes stall, **"Send it again" sends the exact same bytes**, never a second rotation (Identity rule 29). Once the homes' receipts are in, it shows **the new signing key's words** (written, hidden, three asked back, forgotten); the old paper is then spent and can be destroyed. Carry the signing words to Dub (identity window, its "renew"), Edubba (where it says the key changed, or the identity's record: "Take the new words"), Damgar and Ummia; keep the chain words apart, for Kišib only. The old paper given again is refused, said plainly ("an earlier chain-key paper of this identity"); the new paper makes the next change.
- **Rotate a collective** (F337), completing a member change proposed in Edubba and signed by every member. Remote by default: the member who proposed it starts it in Kišib with their paper share and their signing key's words. Kišib first says, on a screen they must read and tick, what this device will hold and why it matters. It then announces the rotation in each member's thread (Dub shows it), with its fingerprint. Each other member opens their own Kišib, reads the rotation in plain words, and gives their paper share there. The share goes sealed to a one-time key that expires after an hour. Once exactly the threshold is in, the starter's Kišib does it all in one sitting: rebuilds the key, checks it against the commitment the collective's chain holds, signs, deals the new shares (each sealed to the one-time key its member's Dub made when it signed the change, as at the founding; the starter's own shown here once) and overwrites the key. In the same room instead: the papers are typed one after another into one Kišib, nothing is sent, and every new share is shown on that screen once. Afterwards the collective's new signing key's words are shown once, for the member who holds it (Edubba takes them).
- **Escape a contested position** (F299, Identity section 11): when someone else has changed your keys with your stolen chain-key paper, or two rotations contest one position of a self-hosted identity. With both papers (the chain key's words, and the signing key's words of the key you held before), Kišib signs a homeless rotation to new homes and an escape endorsement listing every rotation served at that position as abandoned. The escape commits a key of a new paper too (above all here, where the old one was stolen), its words written before the escape and its endorsement are signed, the new signing key's after, as at a change of keys: the stolen paper makes nothing that counts after it.
- **Name or confirm a successor** (F300). The identity names its successor in a rotation, signed with its chain key's words. The successor confirms it with its own signing key's words: one act naming that rotation. Until then clients show it as declared and follow nobody. Each side's screen says what each signs.

What it may do: **check paper words** without signing anything ("these are the right words", or not, and which key of the words they give); **show an identity's record** (homes, receipts, lines, any contest, any succession).


**Change the vault (H40, round 2).** A door of its own on the first screen opens the change of keys at The vault, saying first, in plain words, that changing the vault or its limit is a change of keys: the chain-key paper needed, and two new papers written (the chain key's and the signing key's), as at any change of keys. An identity's record shows its ID written for people (`mid1…`) and its acts as `mact1…`, the hexadecimal beside; IDs are taken in either form (F349).

## The Dubsar runner (the program only, never on the web)

*Decided by Nobody, allegedly (the redesign, "The Dubsar runner cMIP"; draft 2's five answers, 22 October 2026); its text: `cmips/cmip-runner-draft-2.md` (draft 1, `cmip-runner-draft-1.md`, kept).* A second way for an app to sign: you give an identity's **signing** words to Kišib once, on this computer, and the apps you approve ask Kišib to sign for them. The app holds no key. Inspired by Amber and nsecbunker. In Kišib's program on the Mac and on Linux; never in the web client (decided: "not on web").

- **Off by default, and nothing kept while off.** The Runner tile's **On or off** says the risk first: Kišib keeps the signing keys you give it on this computer, encrypted under a passphrase you choose; whoever gets into the computer while Kišib is open and unlocked can sign as those identities through Kišib, within what you allowed; the folder alone gives nothing without the passphrase; **still at risk**: someone who saw your passphrase typed, or guesses it, and a computer taken, or a program running as you, while Kišib is open and unlocked; a forgotten passphrase means turning it off and giving the words again; a malicious app could cover the panel or trick a click; money, agreements, relying on another's act, and keys always ask; an app you let open what is sealed reads private messages without asking each time; turning off erases everything. **Turn it on** only once "I have read the risk" is ticked and **a passphrase is chosen, typed twice** (at least 8 characters). The risk is optional: it may stay off for good.
- **Encrypted at rest** (draft 2, decided: "Agreed, it needs to be protected"). Everything the runner keeps (the keys, the apps paired, what it signed and opened) is in `runner.json`, sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from the passphrase with scrypt (128 MiB): never in the clear. The passphrase, and the key derived from it, live in the program's memory only.
- **Locked, and off: two states** (draft 2, decided: "Yes, agreed"). Quit Kišib (its program: `node --import tsx src/cli.ts stop` in this folder), restart the computer, or press **Lock it now** (Runner › On or off), with the runner on, and it is **locked**: everything stays, encrypted. *Closing Kišib's window alone does not lock it: its program keeps running, unlocked; said where the runner is on.* Reopening Kišib, its first screen asks **the runner's passphrase** (and top right says **Runner locked**); given, everything is back, and no app pairs again. A wrong passphrase opens nothing. While locked, an app asking is told plainly that Kišib is locked. **Turn it off** (Runner › On or off) **erases everything**: keys, pairings, the log; turned on again, it starts from nothing. That is also the way out of a forgotten passphrase: off, on with a new one, the signing words given again, each app paired again.
- **Held here** (Identities): give an identity by its ID, a home and its signing key's words; Kišib checks they give the key its homes serve, and refuses a chain key's words. The chain key is never kept.
- **Pairing, started in the app, approved here.** In Dub: the identity panel (or the first screen), **+ Add › Use one from Kišib**, Ask Kišib to pair: Dub shows a code. In Kišib, **Runner › Apps** shows "Dub asks to pair" with the same code and its origin: choose which identities it may use, and whether **Dub may open what is sealed to this identity** (off unless ticked), **Approve** (or Refuse). Dub then lists them, and uses one.
- **The panel inside the app's window.** Each act Dub would sign goes to Kišib, which reads it from its exact bytes and draws its panel in Dub's slot (the kit's `openSlot`): "Dub asks Kišib to sign a post", as whom, the act in plain words, on which line and position, **Sign it** or **Decline**. The frame is Kišib's page, sandboxed: Dub can neither read nor change it, and Kišib serves it only into a frame of the pairing's own origin. Signed, Dub sends the act as any act it signs.
- **Standing yeses**, per app and kind (posts and replies, private messages, display names, the contacts list): "Always yes for Dub's posts" in the panel, or Ask / Yes in **Apps**. **Money, agreements, relying on another's act, and keys always ask** (relying, decided again in draft 2: "Ok, agreed"), and so does anything Kišib cannot read.
- **Opening what is sealed** (draft 2, decided: "Yes"). An app approved with "may open what is sealed to this identity" (shown, and changed, as No / Yes in its line under **Apps**) sends Kišib the sealed containers it finds addressed to the identity; Kišib opens them with the encryption key the signing words carry and returns the act inside, never the identity's key. No panel (an opening signs nothing); **each opening listed in Signed**, saying which app and what kind of act, and how many times. Revoking the app ends it. Dub then reads the replies to its private messages and keeps its contacts list sealed to the identity.
- **Waiting**: requests not yet answered, in case a panel was closed; answered here, the app that waits has its answer. Ten minutes each; closing Kišib drops them, nothing signed.
- **Signed**: what the runner signed, and what it opened, newest first, each a card with the kit's two menus (Copy ID, Copy ID link, Open profile, the record; Copy act ID, Why it's valid, its standing read at the identity's homes and relays; for an opening, that nothing was signed). Kept, encrypted, until the runner is turned off.
- **Revoke** in Apps: the app signs and opens through Kišib no more.
- **The key check (H80)**: before signing for any app, the runner reads the identity from its homes; after a change of keys from paper it refuses, says so in Held here, and asks for the new signing key's words there.
- **Lines**: the runner signs on the app's own lines (public and private, F376), which the app keeps as for any identity; at a change of keys, **Change keys › Lines another app made › Add the cards of the lines Kišib's runner signed** writes the cards of the lines it signed (the same as the app shows in its Line and homes).
- **A draft 1 file** (its keys in the clear, from Kišib before draft 2): Kišib starts with the runner locked and asks a passphrase to be chosen, typed twice; then it is sealed under it.

## Readings

*Where the texts are silent, Kišib takes these readings. To be confirmed by Nobody, allegedly.*

1. **A new paper at every change of keys, confirmed before signing** (H79, reopened; F361, F362). *Decided by Nobody, allegedly, 21 October 2026:* "No, it does not stay the same. Unsure why sessions believe so. A rotation generates two new sets of keys, chain and signing." And, the same day, on when each is shown: "From a safety point of view, confirming the new chain key comes before signing the rotation, confirming the new signing key can come after." The rotation at position *n* is signed with the current paper's key *n* − 1 and commits key *n* of a fresh seed (the air-gapped Module's rule 3.3, "from a fresh seed", the device showing "that the new seed must be backed up" before inserting it; its key index, which holds "even across a fresh seed"; the words seed Module's derivation). The fresh seed is drawn, its words shown, written and three typed back, and only then is the rotation signed and sent; the new signing key's words come once the homes' receipts are in. The new paper counts once the homes accept the change; until then the old paper still works, but only to send that same change again (reading 3). Once it counts, the old paper is spent: given again, it is refused, and "Check paper words" says it is spent. *Replaced:* until 21 October 2026 this reading was "the same paper for every rotation", marked *to be confirmed by Nobody, allegedly*, never confirmed, and H79 was closed as designed on the strength of it: a mistake. *An identity whose last change reused its paper* (the author's T, sitting 5) changes once more from that old paper, said on Kišib's screen (`LAST_OF_OLD_PAPER_SAY`); that change commits the new paper's key. *Cost, stated:* a stolen paper is spent at the next change of keys; a paper written for a change that was never sent is worthless (reading 3); and a change that never counts leaves a written new paper that waits on it (the old paper still sends that same change again).
2. **What a sitting holds, and how long, in memory only:** the words, from the review until it is confirmed or dropped (fifteen minutes at most), then overwritten; once confirmed, the old paper's key (and, for an escape, the old signing key) until the new paper is typed back and the change signed, then overwritten; the new chain key's seed, until three of its words are typed back; the signed rotation's bytes, to send again, until its new words are typed back; the new signing key, until three of its words are typed back. Strings the shared code keeps (a signing key in an identity held for one act) cannot be overwritten in JavaScript; they are dropped, never stored.
3. **No memory between uses, and rule 29.** Kišib does not remember what it signed once closed. Before signing it reads the homes: a rotation already served at the next position is never signed again. Given the old paper, Kišib then offers only **"Send the same change again"**: it signs nothing and sends that rotation's exact bytes, as the homes and relays serve them; if it was not yours, the escape. If Kišib is closed after the new paper was confirmed and the change sent, before its signing key's words were shown, that change installs a signing key nobody holds, but the new paper was written before it was signed: once the change counts, the next change, from the new paper, installs a key you hold (said on the screen while it waits). If Kišib is closed after the new paper was shown but before the change was sent, nothing was signed: the next sitting starts over with another new paper, and the unused one is worthless (said before the words, and by "Check paper words" when it is given).
4. **The new signing key's words are shown only once the rotation counts**, so they are never the words of a key that does not count. The new chain key's words are shown before signing (reading 1): a paper whose change never counts is either sent again from the old paper, or worthless if the change was never sent.
5. **A collective's request leaves out the keys the rotation creates** (the next chain key, the new signing key), as the air-gapped Module's request hash leaves out the next commitment: members consent to everything else, shown with its fingerprint; the new keys are created at the moment of signing, in the same sitting as the rebuild.
6. **The request travels as media at the collective's relays, found by its SHA-256** (the fingerprint), unsigned: it decides nothing, and a member's Kišib checks it against the collective's chain (its previous act, its expiry) before showing it.
7. **A member's share is sealed in a private Envelopes publication signed by the member**, its media tagged `CONTRIBUTION_MEDIA` (a test value of Kišib's own), the share's number, value and the request's fingerprint inside. MOR seals only signed acts; the act also records consent. *Cost:* it starts a new line of the member's acts, which nobody else can read; leaving it out of the member's next rotation voids nothing of value.
8. **Exactly the threshold**: Kišib takes no more shares than the collective's agreement needs, and one per member; a share numbered for another member is taken and said, the rebuild decides.
9. **The hour runs on each device's clock**: MOR has no clock. A member's Kišib refuses a request past its expiry; the starter's Kišib destroys the one-time key at expiry, after which no sealed share can be opened.
10. **The escape's new homes must not be old homes** (an old home holds the rival and refuses any other rotation revealing the same key, rule 30). The escape is also left at the old homes and relays, where a reader meets it (an old home keeps it as evidence, rule 48) and asks the new homes for its receipts.
11. **A succession is confirmed by a link confirmation** (Identity type 7) naming the rotation, signed by the successor (F300 allows a witness act too).
12. **An identity that declares a clock** (Money rule 15): Kišib does not anchor a rotation's quorum, and says so in the review; the genesis client anchors.
13. **Reading a self-hosted identity:** a homes-only reader (the genesis client's lookup) does not see a rival served only at a relay; Kišib reads the identity's relays too, as the gauntlet's reader does.
14. **Making an identity (H48)** as Dub makes one: its chain key key 0 of a new paper under scheme 3 (the words seed Module), its routes naming its relays as outbox and inbox, its encryption key the one its signing words carry (the carry Module, draft 2), so every app bringing it opens its messages. The paper's words come first and three are typed back before anything is signed, as at a change of keys; its signing words once its homes' receipts are in. Its routes and encryption key make a short public line no app continues: read by Kišib itself at the next change of keys, kept with nothing to give.
15. **Checking a share** reads one paper's words (their checksum) and its number among the holders of the collective's agreement in force; it cannot check one paper against the key, since its blinding is not on paper, and says so; given as many papers as rebuild the key, it rebuilds it, compares it with what the collective's chain commits to now, and overwrites it at once. Nothing is signed.
16. **The runner's readings** are its cMIP's, marked "mechanic, this draft's" there (`cmips/cmip-runner-draft-2.md`): the pairing code and token; a request's fingerprint; the kinds' edges (the key delivered to the identity itself signed with its act); the panel's single-use value and its frame; ten minutes for a request; the key check refusing when no home answers; the cards it gives at a change of keys; draft 2's scrypt and AES-256-GCM, their parameters, a passphrase of at least 8 characters; at most 100 containers opened at once; a draft 1 file sealed under a passphrase chosen.

## Costs, stated

- **A stolen chain-key paper is spent at the next change of keys** (H79): each change commits a key of a new paper, so the old paper derives nothing that counts after it.
- **A new paper written for a change never sent is worthless** (Kišib closed between the words and the signing): the next sitting makes another (reading 3).
- **Closing Kišib after a change is sent, before its signing key's words are shown**, loses that signing key: the new paper, already written, makes the next change.
- **The words are typed into an online device.** The air-gapped way (an offline device, the bytes by QR code, F335 (c)) is the gold standard and comes later; the web client is for testing only.
- **A collective's rotating device holds the whole old key and creates the new one** (F337, the Module's F97): if it were compromised or dishonest, it could keep the new key and later act alone. Said on the must-read screen.
- **The starter types their signing key's words** to open the members' answers and sign the announcement and the sealed shares; those acts start a new line of theirs. Kišib leaves them, and a member's share, off the identity's homes (F373): no app continues their line, so they would only show up at the member's next change of keys as acts on no line.
- **Each member types their signing key's words** beside their paper share.
- **The runner keeps signing keys on this computer, encrypted under its passphrase, while on or locked** (decided, with the risk stated). **Still at risk:** someone who saw the passphrase typed, or guesses it (`runner.json` can be tried offline; scrypt slows each guess: a long passphrase is the defence); and, while Kišib is open and unlocked, whoever gets into the computer, or any program running as you, can sign through Kišib within what you allowed, read the program's memory, or answer the panel itself.
- **A forgotten passphrase cannot be found again**: the runner is turned off (everything erased) and the words given again.
- **The click can be covered or tricked** by a malicious app (decided, accepted). What was signed is listed in Runner › Signed.
- **An app allowed to open reads every private message sealed to the identity**, without a panel each time (decided); each opening is listed in Signed, and revoking the app ends it.
- **The runner must be running and unlocked** for its apps to sign and open: Kišib open, its passphrase given.

## The vault (F338)

A change of keys may keep, declare anew or remove the identity's **vault** (Money rules 2 and 3): where a payment above a limit must go, per unit, and the one place a thief holding only the signing key cannot point payments. It changes only with the chain key, so only here (decided by Nobody, allegedly: "its setting belongs in Kišib"); Damgar shows it. The declaration is read back with the rotation and included in what is confirmed (`vaultPlan`, `VaultChange`).

## Using it

1. Once, in Terminal, from the MOR folder: `git pull`, then `cd clients/genesis && npm install && npm run wasm`, then `npm install` in `clients/desk`, `clients/dub` and `clients/kishib`, then in `clients/kishib`: `sh scripts/make-app.sh` (on Linux: `sh scripts/make-desktop.sh`).
2. Double-click **Kišib** in `~/Applications` (or choose it in the applications menu). The page opens, paired, on its four tiles.
3. **The runner, to try it** (test identities only): Runner › On or off, read the risk, tick it, **choose a passphrase and type it again**, **Turn it on**. Identities › Held here: give an identity its ID, a home and its **signing** words (Dub: the identity's menu › Words). In Dub (rebuilt as its README says, so it knows Kišib's folder): the identity panel, **+ Add › Use one from Kišib**, Ask Kišib to pair; in Kišib, Runner › Apps, compare the code, tick **Dub may open what is sealed to this identity** if Dub should read its private messages, **Approve**; in Dub, I approved it in Kišib, **Use it**. Post: Kišib's panel appears inside Dub's window; **Sign it**. Tick "Always yes for Dub's posts" once to post without a panel. Revoke in Apps to stop one app.
4. **Closing and reopening**: quit Kišib's program (in Terminal, from `clients/kishib`: `node --import tsx src/cli.ts stop`; closing its window alone leaves it running, unlocked), or restart the computer, or press Runner › **Lock it now**. Open it again (double-click Kišib): top right says **Runner locked**, and its first screen asks the passphrase; give it, and the runner signs for Dub again, with no new pairing. **Turn it off** (Runner › On or off) erases everything: keys, pairings, the log.
