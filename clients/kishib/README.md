# Kišib (mor-kishib)

**Kišib** (𒈩, Sumerian kišib, "the cylinder seal": a person's mark of authority, serious to lose and serious to replace; F335): the identity hub: the one place where a chain key is used, from paper, and then forgotten; and, in the program (Mac and Linux, never the web client), the **Dubsar runner**, which signs for the apps you approve. On the Mac and Linux, a program and its page, as Dub; the same page as a web client, **for testing only**, to be served at `kishib.dubsar.org`, with a one-line banner (its "?" opens the risks) on every screen. **Rebuilt on the Dubsar Suite's kit (`clients/kit`) on 22 October 2026**, as the redesign drew it (`docs/design/suite-redesign.md`, "Kišib"; the mockups `docs/design/mockups/kishib-*`; the session's report, `docs/kishib-runner-report.md`): dominantly black, the Identity layer's glacier blue in pastel fills (`src/page/view.ts`, `TINT`).

**Test identities only, until MOR is frozen.** Written "Kishib" in file and folder names.

## In plain words

**Four tiles**, each with its danger marks (decided on the mockups): **Identities** (one bar), **Runner** (two; in the program only, never on the web, so the web client shows three tiles), **Paper** (three), **Collectives** (three). A tile opens with its column on the left; the tabs across the top move between them; on a phone, the column comes first and folds away once a part is chosen, with a back arrow. Top right, what Kišib holds: "Holds nothing · runner off" while the runner is off; **"Runner locked"** while it waits for its passphrase; with it on, the identities it keeps (decided: "the only place where an ID can be held is the new runner").

- **Identities**: **Make an identity** (H48: its homes, its relays, a chain key on a new paper whose words are shown once and three asked back before anything is signed, then its signing key's words once its homes take it; Kišib keeps nothing); **Make one with Nakišib** (its chain-key paper drawn on the offline Mac, only its commitment brought here: below); **Read a record** (any ID: homes, receipts, keys, names, lines, any contest or succession); **Confirm a succession** (moved here from Paper: the successor confirms with its signing key's words, not paper). With the runner on, **Held here** comes first: the identities the runner keeps, each with its record and **Remove**, and the form to give it one.
- **Paper**, behind **the quiet door** ("Have your paper ready. Kišib uses the words for one act and forgets them.", Open Paper or Not now, nothing else on the screen): **Check the paper**, **Change keys**, **Change keys with Nakišib** (below), **Change the vault**, **Name a successor**, and, last and set apart, **Escape a contested position**. A change of keys shows its steps: the words › read back › new chain paper › signed › new signing words.
- **Collectives**, behind the same door: **Waiting for you** (give your share to a rotation another member started), **Start a rotation**, **In the same room**, **Check a share** (new: a paper share, its number and 24 words, checked against the collective: written right and whose it is; with as many papers as rebuild the key, whether they still rebuild it; nothing signed).
- **Runner** (the program only; below).

An identity in MOR has two keys that matter here. The **signing key** signs everyday acts; Dub and Edubba hold it, and carry it by its words. The **chain key** exists only on paper: 24 words, written when the identity was made, and written anew at every change of its keys. It is used only to change the signing key, for example when a phone is lost or stolen. Kišib is where that paper is typed, used for the act at hand, and forgotten. **Every change of keys makes two new papers** (H79): a new chain key's words, for the next change, and a new signing key's words, for the apps. The old chain-key paper is then spent.

**It holds nothing between uses** (but the runner's keys, while it is on or locked, encrypted under its passphrase, below). The words typed are turned into keys for one act, then overwritten. Nothing is written to the program's folder (which holds only which browsers are paired with it) or to the browser's storage (the web client opens no database and writes nothing to local or session storage). A review left unconfirmed is dropped, with its words, after fifteen minutes. Close Kišib, and nothing of your words remains.

What it does:

- **Change an identity's keys** (F335). Give its ID, one of its homes, and its chain key's 24 words. Kišib reads the identity from every home its chain names and every relay its routes name, finds **every line of its acts**, the ones Dub made, Edubba made and the web client made, and says each in plain words (Identity rule 35): a line left out would have its acts become void. **A line holding private acts is placed by its card** (H80's question 2, F373): paste each app's card whole, as its identity window shows it (latest act, position, summary, then how many of its acts Kišib cannot read, and the app's name). Each card must name an act the homes hold; when the apps' counts add up to the acts the homes hold that Kišib cannot read, those acts lie on the cards' lines and are kept with them, nothing to tick. **Where a line cannot be placed** (a card whose latest act is not at the homes, an app saying acts of its line are missing, counts that fall short), the review says so **in red** before anything is signed: whose line, what lives on it (notes, a list), and that it may stop counting as if left out; nothing is signed until the box under it is ticked. **Since F376 each app keeps two lines**, its public acts on one (Kišib reads it itself, and so can anyone after the change) and its private acts on the other (kept by its card); its window shows a card per line, each naming its app and its line (public, private, or the line from before, public and private acts together), joined by “; ”: paste them whole, and Kišib says each line by name. *Cost, stated (accepted by Nobody, allegedly, 21 October 2026, F376):* the counts are each app's own word; Kišib cannot check them. A count changes only what Kišib says, never what the change of keys keeps; an act on no line given makes the counts fall short and is said in red. Everything is read back before anything is signed. Confirmed, **Kišib first shows the new chain key's words**, a new paper made at that moment (a fresh seed: the air-gapped Module's rule 3.3, the words seed Module's derivation, key *n* of the new paper for the rotation at position *n*): you write them down, they are hidden, three are asked back, and Kišib forgets them. **Only then** does it sign one rotation, with the old paper's chain key, committing the new paper's key, and send it to every home. Said before the words: this paper becomes the one that counts once the homes accept the change; until then the old paper still works, but only to send this same change again. If the homes stall, **"Send it again" sends the exact same bytes**, never a second rotation (Identity rule 29). Once the homes' receipts are in, it shows **the new signing key's words** (written, hidden, three asked back, forgotten); the old paper is then spent and can be destroyed. Carry the signing words to Dub (identity window, its "renew"), Edubba (where it says the key changed, or the identity's record: "Take the new words"), Damgar and Ummia; keep the chain words apart, for Kišib only. The old paper given again is refused, said plainly ("an earlier chain-key paper of this identity"); the new paper makes the next change.
- **Rotate a collective** (F337), completing a member change proposed in Edubba and signed by every member. Remote by default: the member who proposed it starts it in Kišib with their paper share and their signing key's words. Kišib first says, on a screen they must read and tick, what this device will hold and why it matters. It then announces the rotation in each member's thread (Dub shows it), with its fingerprint. Each other member opens their own Kišib, reads the rotation in plain words, and gives their paper share there. The share goes sealed to a one-time key that expires after an hour. Once exactly the threshold is in, the starter's Kišib does it all in one sitting: rebuilds the key, checks it against the commitment the collective's chain holds, signs, deals the new shares (each sealed to the one-time key its member's Dub made when it signed the change, as at the founding; the starter's own shown here once) and overwrites the key. In the same room instead: the papers are typed one after another into one Kišib, nothing is sent, and every new share is shown on that screen once. **A collective has no signing key** (round 8, the redesign): the rotation installs none and keeps no line of acts, so no signing words follow it; once it counts, only the new shares remain to be shown. Edubba follows the rotation from the collective's homes, with no words.
- **Escape a contested position** (F299, Identity section 11): when someone else has changed your keys with your stolen chain-key paper, or two rotations contest one position of a self-hosted identity. With both papers (the chain key's words, and the signing key's words of the key you held before), Kišib signs a homeless rotation to new homes and an escape endorsement listing every rotation served at that position as abandoned. The escape commits a key of a new paper too (above all here, where the old one was stolen), its words written before the escape and its endorsement are signed, the new signing key's after, as at a change of keys: the stolen paper makes nothing that counts after it.
- **Name or confirm a successor** (F300). The identity names its successor in a rotation, signed with its chain key's words. The successor confirms it with its own signing key's words: one act naming that rotation. Until then clients show it as declared and follow nobody. Each side's screen says what each signs.

What it may do: **check paper words** without signing anything ("these are the right words", or not, and which key of the words they give); **show an identity's record** (homes, receipts, lines, any contest, any succession).


**Change the vault (H40, round 2).** A door of its own on the first screen opens the change of keys at The vault, saying first, in plain words, that changing the vault or its limit is a change of keys: the chain-key paper needed, and two new papers written (the chain key's and the signing key's), as at any change of keys. An identity's record shows its ID written for people (`mid1…`) and its acts as `mact1…`, the hexadecimal beside; IDs are taken in either form (F349).

## With Nakišib, its offline companion (optional; decided 23 October 2026)

*Decided by Nobody, allegedly: "an offline companion to kišib is the right approach, since the rotation begins online"; and "Kišib can still do all it does, for user who wish for an extra layer of safety there is now a compagnon app". Nakišib: `clients/nakisib`, its README; the round's report, `docs/nakisib-report.md` (F394).* **Nakišib** runs on an old Mac wiped once and never connected again; the chain key's paper is typed there, never into a connected device. Kišib does its half of the conversation, beside its own way, which is unchanged. Said beside each: what the offline way protects (the chain key never touches a connected device), and that it is optional.

- **Change keys with Nakišib** (Paper): the same reading as **Change keys** (every line of the identity's acts, the cards, the vault, a successor), everything read back, **no paper typed**. Confirmed, Kišib makes the new signing key and **writes the request for Nakišib** (the air-gapped Module's pending rotation, made by the Module's own `online` code) to a file you choose, with the new signing key's fingerprint, which Nakišib shows too. **Read Nakišib's answer**: the signed rotation is checked to be exactly the change asked (the Module's rule 3.9, by the Module's own code), refused in plain words otherwise, nothing sent; then sent to every home, and **Send it again** sends the same bytes; once the homes accept it, the new signing key's words, as at any change of keys. Kišib closed between the request and the answer: give both files with the identity and a home; the answer is checked and sent, and Kišib says the signing key it made with the request is gone (the next change, from Nakišib's new paper, installs a key you hold).
- **Make one with Nakišib** (Identities): Nakišib's **commitment** (its first paper, drawn, shown and three of its words typed back on the offline Mac) read from its file by the Module's own code, shown to compare with Nakišib's screen; the identity made from it as **Make an identity** makes one (its homes, its relays, its signing key, its routes and encryption key), its signing key's words once its homes take it.
- **Kišib's own WebAssembly** (`airgap/`, `scripts/build-airgap-wasm.sh`): the Module's `online` side (`pendingFor`, `acceptAnswer`, `readCommitment`, `signingFingerprint`), built to a small WebAssembly of Kišib's own, loaded only when a way with Nakišib is used. The suite's shared WebAssembly (`clients/genesis`, crate `mor-wasm`) is not touched. The web client serves it beside its page (`kishib_airgap_bg.wasm`).

## The Dubsar runner (the program only, never on the web)

*Decided by Nobody, allegedly (the redesign, "The Dubsar runner cMIP"; draft 2's five answers, 22 October 2026; draft 3's, the same day); its text: `cmips/cmip-runner-draft-3.md` (drafts 1 and 2 kept); since round 8, `cmips/cmip-runner-draft-5.md` (not yet approved): no member keys.* A second way for an app to sign: you give an identity's **signing** words to Kišib once, on this computer, and the apps you approve ask Kišib to sign for them. The app holds no key. Inspired by Amber and nsecbunker. In Kišib's program on the Mac and on Linux; never in the web client (decided: "not on web").

- **Off by default, and nothing kept while off.** The Runner tile's **On or off** says the risk first: Kišib keeps the signing keys you give it on this computer, encrypted under a passphrase you choose; whoever gets into the computer while Kišib is open and unlocked can sign as those identities through Kišib, within what you allowed; the folder alone gives nothing without the passphrase; **still at risk**: someone who saw your passphrase typed, or guesses it, and a computer taken, or a program running as you, while Kišib is open and unlocked; a forgotten passphrase means turning it off and giving the words again; a malicious app could cover the panel or trick a click; money, agreements, relying on another's act, and keys always ask; an app you let open what is sealed reads private messages without asking each time; turning off erases everything. **Turn it on** only once "I have read the risk" is ticked and **a passphrase is chosen, typed twice** (at least 8 characters). The risk is optional: it may stay off for good.
- **Encrypted at rest** (draft 2, decided: "Agreed, it needs to be protected"). Everything the runner keeps (the keys, the apps paired, what it signed and opened) is in `runner.json`, sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from the passphrase with scrypt (128 MiB): never in the clear. The passphrase, and the key derived from it, live in the program's memory only.
- **Locked, and off: two states** (draft 2, decided: "Yes, agreed"). **Quit Kišib** (Runner › On or off, or the seal in the menu bar; draft 3), restart the computer, or press **Lock now** (the same two places), with the runner on, and it is **locked**: everything stays, encrypted. *Closing Kišib's window does not end it: its program keeps running, unlocked; said where the runner is on.* Reopening Kišib, its first screen asks **the runner's passphrase** (and top right says **Runner locked**); given, everything is back, and no app pairs again. A wrong passphrase opens nothing. While locked, an app asking is told plainly that Kišib is locked. **Turn it off** (Runner › On or off) **erases everything**: keys, pairings, the log; turned on again, it starts from nothing. That is also the way out of a forgotten passphrase: off, on with a new one, the signing words given again, each app paired again.
- **Held here** (Identities): give an identity by its ID, a home and its signing key's words; Kišib checks they give the key its homes serve, and refuses a chain key's words. The chain key is never kept.
- **Pairing, started in the app, approved here.** In Dub: the identity panel (or the first screen), **+ Add › Use one from Kišib**, Ask Kišib to pair: Dub shows a code. In Kišib, **Runner › Apps** shows "Dub asks to pair" with the same code and its origin: choose which identities it may use, and, beside each, whether **Dub may open what is sealed to this identity** (off unless ticked; per identity, per app, draft 3), **Approve** (or Refuse). Dub then lists them, and uses one.
- **The panel inside the app's window.** Each act Dub would sign goes to Kišib, which reads it from its exact bytes and draws its panel in Dub's slot (the kit's `openSlot`): "Dub asks Kišib to sign a post", as whom, the act in plain words, on which line and position, **Sign it** or **Decline**. The frame is Kišib's page, sandboxed: Dub can neither read nor change it, and Kišib serves it only into a frame of the pairing's own origin. Signed, Dub sends the act as any act it signs.
- **Standing yeses**, per app and kind (posts and replies, private messages, display names, the contacts list): "Always yes for Dub's posts" in the panel, or Ask / Yes in **Apps**. **Money, agreements, relying on another's act, and keys always ask** (relying, decided again in draft 2: "Ok, agreed"), and so does anything Kišib cannot read.
- **Opening what is sealed** (draft 2, decided: "Yes"; per identity, per app, draft 3: "since an app can run multiple identities, per identity, per app"). An app approved with "may open what is sealed to this identity" for an identity (shown, and changed, as No / Yes beside each identity in its line under **Apps**) sends Kišib the sealed containers it finds addressed to the identity; Kišib opens them with the encryption key the signing words carry and returns the act inside and that act's own key, never the identity's keys (draft 3: "Understood and agreed"). No panel (an opening signs nothing); **each opening listed in Signed**, saying which app and what kind of act, and how many times. Revoking the app ends it. Dub then reads the replies to its private messages and keeps its contacts list sealed to the identity.
- **Acting for a collective: your own key, always through the panel** (draft 5; draft 3's member keys removed in round 8). There are no member keys any more: a member acts for a collective with their own key, the act naming the collective. So Kišib holds one key per identity, its own, and keeps no key for any collective. An app acting for a collective sends an ordinary request of your identity, the collective named in the act's objects; Kišib's panel shows it **always**, even where the app has a standing yes for that kind, and says "For the collective …, under its decision …, signed with Max's own key", with "Agreements decides whether this counts for the collective". Draft 3's ask "hold this identity's key for a collective" is refused, said plainly, and so is a request on draft 3's member line. A runner file from draft 3 or 4 that held member keys is opened as before; the keys are erased, and Kišib says so once.
- **A lane's record, without a panel** (draft 3, decided: "Yes, if establishing lanes it has to be setup this way"). A payment Dub makes under its own lane (granted in Damgar) is signed with the lane's key; for Damgar to keep its record, its key is delivered to the identity itself. Kišib signs that one kind of act unasked, once it reads at the identity's homes that the act is the identity's, signed under a lane, and opens with that key. It moves no money; each is listed in Signed ("a lane's record, to the identity itself"). Any other key delivery asks.
- **The seal in the macOS menu bar** (draft 3; asked: "can it be shown in the top bar on macOS, so one knows it is active?"). Hollow while the runner is off, a padlock while it is locked, full while it is on, with a number while something waits; its menu: **Open Kišib**, **Lock now**, **Quit Kišib**, **Turn off** (asked first). Lock now and Quit Kišib are on Kišib's page too (decided: "accessible from both"). A small Swift helper (`seal/main.swift`), built by `scripts/make-app.sh` where Apple's command-line tools are present (said plainly where they are not), started with Kišib; it talks to Kišib's program only, by a token Kišib writes in its folder at each start (`seal.json`). Never on Linux, never on the web.
- **Waiting**: requests not yet answered, in case a panel was closed; answered here, the app that waits has its answer. Ten minutes each; closing Kišib drops them, nothing signed.
- **Signed**: what the runner signed, and what it opened, newest first, each a card with the kit's two menus (Copy ID, Copy ID link, Open profile, the record; Copy act ID, Why it's valid, its standing read at the identity's homes and relays; for an opening, that nothing was signed). Kept, encrypted, until the runner is turned off.
- **Revoke** in Apps: the app signs and opens through Kišib no more.
- **The key check (H80)**: before signing for any app, the runner reads the identity from its homes; after a change of keys from paper it refuses, says so in Held here, and asks for the new signing key's words there.
- **Lines**: the runner signs on the app's own lines (public and private, F376), which the app keeps as for any identity; at a change of keys, **Change keys › Lines another app made › Add the cards of the lines Kišib's runner signed** writes the cards of the lines it signed (the same as the app shows in its Line and homes).
- **A draft 1 file** (its keys in the clear, from Kišib before draft 2): Kišib starts with the runner locked and asks a passphrase to be chosen, typed twice; then it is sealed under it.

**Which clock** (decided 23 October 2026): every app of the suite orders its feeds by the stamps of **the Dubsar clock**, identity `1c743854ba1884b470f02221395d18d1abe977631bf86f0f221e32e6d83cf453` (its genesis at home1 and home2; a public identity, not an address), written in `clients/barebone/src/clock.ts`; each card shows its time, "… · by the Dubsar clock", or the signer's own hint, labelled.

## Costs, stated

- **A stolen chain-key paper is spent at the next change of keys** (H79): each change commits a key of a new paper, so the old paper derives nothing that counts after it.
- **A new paper written for a change never sent is worthless** (Kišib closed between the words and the signing): the next sitting makes another (reading 3).
- **Closing Kišib after a change is sent, before its signing key's words are shown**, loses that signing key: the new paper, already written, makes the next change.
- **The words are typed into an online device.** The air-gapped way (an offline device, the bytes by QR code, F335 (c)) is the gold standard and comes later; the web client is for testing only.
- **A collective's rotating device holds the whole old key and creates the new one** (F337, the Module's F97): if it were compromised or dishonest, it could keep the new key and later act alone. Said on the must-read screen.
- **The starter types their signing key's words** to open the members' answers and sign the announcement and the sealed shares; those acts start a new line of theirs. Kišib leaves them, and a member's share, off the identity's homes (F373): no app continues their line, so they would only show up at the member's next change of keys as acts on no line.
- **Each member types their signing key's words** beside their paper share.
- **The runner keeps signing keys on this computer, encrypted under its passphrase, while on or locked** (decided, with the risk stated). **Still at risk:** someone who saw the passphrase typed, or guesses it (`runner.json` can be tried offline; scrypt slows each guess: a long passphrase is the defence); and, while Kišib is open and unlocked, whoever gets into the computer, or any program running as you, can sign through Kišib within what you allowed, read the program's memory, or answer the panel itself.
- **A forgotten passphrase cannot be found again**: the runner is turned off (everything erased) and the words given again.
- **The click can be covered or tricked** by a malicious app (decided, accepted). What was signed is listed in Runner › Signed.
- **An app allowed to open reads every private message sealed to the identity**, without a panel each time (decided); each opening is listed in Signed, and revoking the app ends it.
- **One key acts for the person and for their collectives** (draft 5, the redesign's cost, not the runner's): whoever can sign through Kišib can act for those collectives within that member's powers. Kišib's part is the panel, always shown for an act naming a collective. Telling such an act apart costs a reading of each identity the act names at the places Kišib knows; one it cannot read is treated as a collective all the same (it asks).
- **The seal's door** (draft 3): a program running as you can read Kišib's folder, and so lock, quit, or turn off the runner (erasing it) through it, as it can already stop the program; it cannot sign through it.
- **The runner must be running and unlocked** for its apps to sign and open: Kišib open, its passphrase given.
