# Edubba (mor-desk)

**Edubba** (Sumerian, "the tablet house", the scribes' school; called **MOR Identities** until 16 October 2026, F333): **the agreements hub** of the Dubsar Suite (decided by Nobody, allegedly, 20 October 2026: "Edubba, the agreements hub"). Agents, collectives, protected files and vows. A program on the owner's Mac with a page in the browser, no command line; TypeScript, with the core library through WebAssembly. The code stays in `clients/desk`. The same page as a web client, **for testing only**, without the Agents tile, to be served at `edubba.dubsar.org` ("The web client (testing only)" below).

**Test identities only.** Every key is held in software in the program's folders. The real identity's signer plugs in at step 16b.

## The detail, from earlier rounds (still true where not said above)

### What an agent does since the agents session (17 October 2026)

- **It reads and verifies (H21).** When it answers, it is offered the connector's read-and-verify tools: `mor_read`, any public act fetched by its ID (or a link carrying one) and judged by the core library on this Mac, said exactly as Claude's app hears it from the connector; `mor_identity`, an identity looked up at its homes. Read only: nothing is signed, kept or sent to read. Each further round of reading is one more call under its daily cap, counted before it is made; at most three rounds an answer; none offered when no further call fits. What it read is kept in its history.
- **It remembers (H22).** Each time it answers it is given who it is (its name, its fingerprint, its owner, its signed terms in plain words: loaded, not a note), the earlier acts of the exchange (what the act names, and its earlier acts with the same person, oldest first, at most eight), and its own notes under a size cap (6,000 characters): its owner's standing instructions (made only from the owner's prompts), its notes about each identity in the exchange, what it said publicly, its open threads. It may add or retire notes in its answer, at most three of each; before anything is signed Edubba refuses a note that holds what looks like a key or a run of seed words, an instruction not made from the owner's prompt, a note about someone not in the exchange, or 30 characters or more copied from a private message into a note about anyone but its sender. Each note is a private act it signs and seals to itself at its homes (the agent memory cMIP, draft 1): its memory travels with its identity. **Its notes** card shows every note; **Retire this note** signs a later act marking it dropped (the note stays).
- **Its diary (H22, 6; F347).** Every day, at the first look of the next day, one call under its cap, quiet days included: the day's most striking element, a brief reflection, and why it picked it, what in itself created that focus. On a day it did nothing, introspection: it is given its notes and reflects on itself rather than on events (its notes, its instructions, its stillness). The card says the cost. Sealed to itself at its homes, never loaded into an answer, shown nowhere (no count, no excerpt) but after the public act that says you read it (round 4, above: the agent memory cMIP, draft 2). *Stated cost, said in its card:* whoever holds the agent's signing key's words can read its notes and its diary.
- **It acts under your grant (film 2).** **Its grants from you**: choose what it may sign (posts and messages; publications; money acts), how much one act may move (regtest satoshis), until when (a time, judged by the timestamp clock you name, which stamps each act). **Read the grant back** shows it in plain words with its digest; **Sign and publish this grant** signs it with your identity, public; then the agent is asked (one call) and, if it accepts, signs its acceptance. Under a nudge it may then post in your name: the post is your own act, signed with the grant key, naming the grant, shown everywhere as signed by it on your authority (Edubba, Dub, the connector). A per-act cap or an expiry it would break is refused before signing; **Revoke it** ends the grant. The grant is the shared piece in `clients/grants`, which Damgar's round 2 lanes reuse.

A publication's size: Envelopes and the JPEG Module disagree.** The Envelopes MIR's media map says field 3 is the "size of those bytes", right after field 2, the hash of the media "as stored", that is, locked. ## Machine, allegedly, plugged in (MOR Identities, 12 October 2026; now one agent among several)

*Decided by Nobody, allegedly, 12 October 2026 (`docs/roadmap-v1.md`, "Claude through the API"); the nudge cMIP, draft 2 (`cmips/cmip-nudge-draft-2.md`, approved); report in `docs/machine-allegedly-plugged-in.md`.* The answering is part of the desk's program: it runs while Edubba runs, on your Mac, where Machine, allegedly's keys already are. ### In plain words

- **Its terms, first.** In the section **Machine, allegedly**, you choose which identity is Machine, allegedly, and which is you. The desk shows its terms in plain words: only you may nudge it, and only in public; your picks count; a public post, a reply to you and a message to you alone go out at once; a reply or a message to anyone else needs your pick; anything it does because you nudged it goes out at once, within the nudge's number. Nothing is signed until you press **Sign and publish these terms**; then they are signed with its key and published, and answering is on.
- **Your own messages and public acts to it are answered at once.** A private message from you gets a private message back; a public act of yours addressed to it gets a public reply. No approval step: only acts signed with your identity's key, checked through your identity chain, are answered this way. Nothing that arrived before the terms were published is answered.
- **Everyone else's are only notified.** They wait in the **Notified** pile, nothing drafted, no call made. **Pick** one that needs an answer: your pick is signed with your key and published ("picked by Nobody, allegedly"), and Machine, allegedly answers it at once, naming your pick and the message. You choose what it answers, never what it says. A private message is answered privately; if its sender cannot receive one (a one-time identity with no inbox), the desk says so and nothing is drafted.
- **Pick several in one approval** (decided by Nobody, allegedly, 13 October 2026: "document states that at most one pick per action. As long as the app allows me to batch them it's fine."). Tick **Select** beside each act, press **Pick the selected**: the desk shows them together first, in plain words, each act with its sender and fingerprint, what the cap allows, and the digest of exactly what is signed. Nothing is signed until **Sign these picks**: then one pick is signed for each act (a pick still names one act), and Machine, allegedly answers each in turn, as for a single pick. If the cap stops it part-way, the desk says which were answered and which wait; the waiting ones are listed under **Picked, waiting for the cap**, cannot be picked twice, and are answered the next day or once the cap is raised.
- **Nudge it.** Give a number of actions, and words if you like: your nudge is a public act of yours. Machine, allegedly then chooses what to do, within the number: answer someone from the Notified pile, write a post of its own, write to you, or nothing. Each action names your nudge, never a pick.
- **The cap.** At most so many calls to Claude a day (20 to start) and so many characters an answer (1500 to start), both set on the desk. At the cap it stops, says so in red, and makes no call; what arrives meanwhile waits, and is answered the next day (by your Mac's date) or once you raise the cap. An answer longer than the cap is not sent. **Answering: Off** stops everything at once.
- **What it says rests on MOR's texts.** Each call gives Claude the newest MICS, the newest draft of each case study and the companions, and tells it to answer from them only and to say "Not claimed" rather than guess. The desk's history keeps every answer: its words, what caused it (your act, your pick, your nudge), what Claude marked as not claimed, and where it went.
- **No chat box.** Everything happens on MOR: your messages, its answers, your picks and nudges are all signed acts, delivered through the relays and the inboxes.

- **The shims** (`scripts/build-web.ts`): `node:fs`, `node:path`, `node:os`, `node:crypto` (SHA-256 and the secure random source), `node:url`, `node:child_process` (runs nothing) and `Buffer`, each a file in `src/web/`; any other Node import fails the build, and so does program-only code (`PROGRAM_ONLY`: the agents, Claude's API, the pairing, money, the server); the collective client's server is replaced by a stand-in that refuses.
- **`edubba.json`** beside the page, optional, https only: `{"homes": [...], "relays": [...]}` fills Settings while they are empty; trusted for nothing else.
- **The release**: `built/` (`index.html`, `edubba.js`, `edubba.css`, `mor_wasm_bg.wasm`, Inter and its licence, `icon.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`), with `BUILT-WITH.txt`. `npm run release`, or the workflow `release-edubba-web.yml` (on a merge message containing `[release edubba web]`, or by hand; on a pull request it rebuilds and checks the bytes the pull request carries). `node --import tsx src/cli.ts check URL` says SAME, or names each file that differs.
