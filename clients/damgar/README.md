# Damgar (mor-damgar)

**Damgar** (𒁮𒃼, Sumerian dam-gàr, "the merchant"; F338): a money app that **holds no wallet**. It gathers the money acts other apps and people propose, reads each in plain words and offers it for signing; it checks payments from the rails' own evidence; and it keeps the account. On the Mac and Linux, a program and its page, as Dub; the same page as a web client, **for testing only** (to be served at `damgar.dubsar.org`), with a banner stating the risks on every screen. Dub's look (F332), tinted with gold `#e8b33a` by Kišib's rule (H36: separators 22%, outlines 30%, accents 60% over `#06080c`; red only for alarms; `src/page/view.ts`, `TINT`).

**Test identities and test networks only, until MOR is frozen. No real money.** The report of its first round: `docs/damgar-report.md`. Regtest on the Mac: `docs/damgar-regtest.md`.

## In plain words

Money in MOR is two records of each payment, each signed by one side: the payer's **claim** ("I paid") and the payee's **receipt** ("I was paid"). Both carry the proof the payment method itself gives (on Lightning, a secret only the payment reveals; on-chain, the transaction in its block), so anyone can check them without trusting either side. Money moves only in your own wallet: Damgar never holds a wallet, a node or a Bitcoin key.

**First use.** Bring an identity you have, by its ID and its signing key's words, as in Dub (Damgar never makes one). Then choose what you accept: each payment specification and Module, with its version and what it does (payments, the Lightning rail, the on-chain rail, Bitcoin's units, split, conversion, anchoring, the Bitcoin clock, the money messages). A money act under something you do not accept is shown **unknown** and is never signed. Your choice is sealed to your identity and kept at its homes, so every app holding the identity reads the same list (F340); you can change it in Settings.

**Damgar's line, for Kišib** (F373): every private act Damgar signs is left at your homes, locked, readable again by your other apps (sealed to you, or its key delivered to you); the identity window shows **Damgar's line** with its card for Kišib only once the homes hold its latest act (latest act, position, summary, how many of its acts Kišib cannot read, and Damgar's name); otherwise it says it is sending it again. Copy it whole into Kišib at a change of keys. Since F376 Damgar keeps its public acts (the published list, rails, receipts and claims made public) and its private acts on two lines, a card for each (and one for the mixed line held from before), joined by “; ”: anyone follows the public line after a change of keys. *Cost, stated (accepted 21 October 2026, F376):* whoever holds your signing words can read what Damgar sealed to others for you.

**When your key is changed elsewhere** (in Kišib), Damgar says so on opening and refuses before every act it would sign with your own key, as Dub does, with a box for the new signing key's words. *H80 (21 October 2026):* one path had skipped the check, a promise of a collective (your own key asks the other holders of its Money area); it checks first now. Acts signed under a lane's key wait too, since the check runs before Damgar chooses the key.

**To sign.** Payments prepared in Dub (a tip on a post; a request to be paid), in Edubba (a payment from an identity; the payment for anchoring a collective's act) and in Ummia (the payment for anchoring a release), each a draft sealed to your own identity; requests to be paid that others sent; invoices to make when someone pays you; receipts to sign once a payment to you is proven. Each is read in plain words, with its rail, then signed or declined.

**Pay.** The invoice to pay, or an on-chain address bound to that one payment. You pay in your own wallet and paste the proof back (the preimage, or the transaction's id); Damgar checks it and signs your claim.

**The account.** What came in, what went out, what is pending, what is owed: each with its claim and receipt, and whether each verifies. A payment paid and confirmed (settled, its claim and receipt each valid) is folded to one line; open it to see its record. Totals are per unit, never added across units.

**Lanes (round 2, F338, F351).** A lane lets an app sign some of your money acts by itself, within a cap per act and until a date: a grant of your identity to itself, its key held by that app, never by Damgar (a lane names a key, never an app, so an app not yet written can hold one). An app asks for one (Dub: its identity window), sealed to yourself; it waits in **To sign**, read in plain words (what it may sign, the cap, the expiry and its clock, that it is public, how to end it); **Grant this lane** signs the grant and your acceptance. **Lanes** lists every lane, its state (in force, revoked, past its expiry), every act its places hold signed under it, and **Revoke it**, read back first with its cost (F352): the revocation cites every act under the lane that your homes and relays hold, so each payee keeps their own counted; it is public and lists every act's id, a lane used long makes a long revocation, and an act Damgar does not find is void. Damgar can hold two lanes of its own: **receipts and invoices, by itself** (it checks a payment to you from the rail's evidence and signs the receipt; it answers an invoice request with an address, or with an invoice your node made through the receive-only link), and **referral shares, by itself** (it asks for a referral share you owe). With both lanes, a small tip is one tap for the payer, paid in their wallet, and nothing for the payee.

**The account shows how each act was signed**: by hand, by Damgar itself (an address, as in round 1), under a lane (named), or, for another's act, on whose authority ("Signed under a lane of …: backed").

**Settings.** Every section folds, and you open the ones you need (H45, Nobody, allegedly, from using Damgar); on first use, before anything is saved there, all start open, and a section you opened stays open while the page is. In order: **Where you are paid** (a Lightning node, a Bitcoin key: your payee pointer, with each rail's stated costs read back before it is signed), **The vault** (shown; set in Kišib), **Homes and relays**, **Every step by hand**, off by default, for those who want to follow the real process step by step: on, every signature waits for you, as in round 1. **The clock for lanes** (a timestamp clock's ID, for an expiry). **Making invoices by itself (receive-only)**: your node's REST address, a permission that makes invoices and nothing else (on lnd, a macaroon baked with `invoices:write invoices:read`), its certificate; Damgar refuses a permission that can read the balance; its cost said (a thief of it can make invoices in your node's name, never take anything). The web client never makes a link. Last, **What you accept**.

**Pay-per-view, the seller's side (round 2).** When Damgar signs the receipt of a payment following one of your offers (a sealed post sold in Dub), it tells your own Dub, sealed to yourself (a sale), and Dub delivers the key to the buyer. Where the buyer's claim names a repost they followed and your offer's words promise the reposter a share, Damgar prepares that payment (F351: a promise in words, not judged; the judged form, a one-party agreement with a split plan, is round 3's).

## Readings

- **A request to be paid is not a debt** (answer of Nobody, allegedly). In To sign it offers: accept it as a debt (sign an obligation, Money type 1), only if one means to owe it; pay it, in one's wallet; then record the payment (a claim with the proof). Most tips skip the first step.
- **The payer signs a claim, the payee a receipt** (Money rule 7). A payer's "receipt" does not exist.
- **Obligation states are unknown here** (Money rule 15, M5): Damgar keeps to the Money layer. Said wherever a debt is shown.
- **The on-chain request key** (option (A), on the project lead's lean): Damgar makes it and keeps it beside the signing key, and answers address requests by itself as they arrive through MOR; the Bitcoin key stays in the wallet. Its cost is said where it is made; replacing it is a new payee pointer naming a new key.
- **The vault** is set in Kišib (it changes what the identity's chain declares); Damgar shows it.
- **Collective payments and splits** are round 3.
- **A lane is a grant to oneself** (F351; Agreements draft 15, rule 73), its key made and held by the app; its words travel only in the sealed request and Damgar's record (a grant carries none). A lane asking for no cap is refused here.
- **What Damgar does by itself** is done only under a lane it holds, within its cap, and never while "Follow every step by hand" is on; on-chain, an address is still answered by itself as in round 1 (under the receipts lane where it reaches answers).
- **An answer to an invoice request names its amount** (the money messages cMIP draft 3, F352): the receipts lane's cap is judged on the answer itself by the grant limits cMIP, before Damgar signs it and by every reader after; above it, Damgar waits and says why.
- **A referral share is read from one sentence of the offer's words** ("Referral: N% of the price to the repost the buyer names."), the repost checked to name the publication and be signed by the referrer; the share rounded down.
- **Anchoring** is asked in Edubba for a collective's acts, and in Ummia for a release under one's own name (F339), and kept simple (decided by Nobody, allegedly): it shows that anchoring exists. A request from Edubba appears in To sign, is paid on regtest to the pooled service, and its ticket is shown; no tier beyond what the offer states, no refund or default handling here.
- **A money message it cannot read** is shown unknown in To sign, never guessed, nothing signed for it; it may be set aside (the messages cMIP, section 6).

## Costs, stated

- The signing key and the request key are held in software, in the program's folder or the browser's storage: a prototype, never for a real identity.
- The web client follows no chain: an on-chain payment there is shown paid, not checked; an anchor's ticket deadline is shown unchecked, said on its screen.
- The chain Damgar follows on a computer is a regtest node it trusts, said where it is used.
- The rails' own costs, shown before a rail is declared (`LIGHTNING_COSTS`, `ONCHAIN_COSTS`).

## Round 3: for a collective (F353)

*Decided by Nobody, allegedly, 18 and 19 October 2026 (F353; Agreements draft 16; the grant limits cMIP draft 2; the member keys cMIP draft 1). Money on the web Edubba is left out (F354, question 4): "A member holding a money area pays for the collective from Damgar with their own key".*

- **For a collective** (a tab): give a collective's ID (`mid1…` or hex). Damgar reads it as anyone does (its chain, the agreement in force, its Money area: holders, number of keys, cap) and finds your own key for it, sealed to you at your homes when you signed its founding or a member change. It is kept only if you hold a box in its Money area and your key is found; otherwise it says why.
- **A promise to pay, from the collective**: to whom, how much (regtest satoshis), what for; read back first, with what the area needs (within the cap, your key alone; above it, one more holder's key, as the constitution writes it); then **Sign it for the collective, with my own key**. It is a Money obligation, the collective the debtor, public, signed as the collective with your key (Agreements draft 16, rule 73). Where more keys are needed, the area's other holders are asked, each sent a negotiation message in their inbox, as Edubba sends a release to sign; they sign in Dub's Signing tab or Edubba's To sign, with their own keys.
- **Promises signed here** are listed with how each stands, read as anyone reads it: counting or not, how many keys of how many, above the cap or not. Damgar judges the cap itself (it implements the grant limits cMIP, draft 2); a reader that does not shows such an act as unknown.
- *Not built:* paying the promise from the collective's own money (the collective's income goes to its split service, then to its members).

## Round 3: paying where a split service answers (part C; the money messages cMIP, draft 4, F357)

- **Paying a collective, or a lone seller's one-party deal**: where the agreement the offer is made under (its field 0), or a collective payee's agreement in force, names a split service (terms field 14), the invoice request goes to the service, sealed to it; the payee stays the collective (or the seller) in the commitment. The service's answer is accepted from it, its invoice checked by the rail's rule against the address paid, and the claim goes to it. The receipt it signs with the payee's grant key is the payment's receipt.
- **Which pointer is paid**: a collective's payee pointer leads to the service's own rails. It is signed with the member keys of the collective's area reaching Money, so it is scoped, and Damgar reads it through Agreements (`agreementsChoose`; Money draft 10, rule 7, F358): it counts where that area's holders and threshold are met. Until then, paying the collective is refused before anything is signed ("It cannot be paid"). Where the payee's own pointer does not lead to the service (a lone seller, whose pointer stays theirs), Damgar pays the service's own pointer, the agreement's designated pointer (field 14, "its one grantee is the agreement's designated pointer", rule 15): *a reading, to confirm* (`docs/round-3-report.md`).
- **A payout from a split service** arrives as an ordinary invoice request: this Damgar answers it by itself under its receipts lane and signs the receipt once paid, as for any payment in.

## Transfer to the vault (H46; F355, decided by Nobody, allegedly, 19 October 2026)

In Settings, **The vault**: **Transfer to the vault**, one of its entries and an amount, **Read it back**: the vault entry (its rail, its node or Bitcoin key, the act that declared it with the chain key), the amount, the fee as far as Damgar can say it (your wallet's routing fee, or the miners' fee your wallet sets: Damgar holds no wallet and cannot see it), and, before anything is bound, that **this money can then be spent only with the chain key and its record** (the vault's own key, declared with your chain key in Kišib, and the record Damgar keeps sealed to you), and that it goes one way only: withdrawing needs the chain key and stays in Kišib. **Bind it** makes it a payment from you to you like any other (no new act): its commitment names the vault entry (`paid-to [1, declared-by, entry]`) and, as `fulfils`, your own payee pointer in force (*a reading, to confirm*: the payment cMIP's next draft lets it name the vault declaration, F355 (c)). On Lightning the invoice is made by your vault's node (on regtest, in the test wallet, on the vault's node) with the description hash Damgar shows, pasted in Pay and checked (its node the vault's, the amount, the hash); on-chain the address is made here with the vault's request key, where this Damgar holds it. You pay in your wallet and paste the proof; the rail Module checks it, and your claim, the record carrying the commitment and salt, is sealed to you at your homes. Declined: Damgar sending by itself (F355 (b)). Test: `vault.test.ts`.

## Round 3: a vow's claim in public (part B)

A payment an app makes under its lane for a vow (Dub's **Buy it**) is marked visible: once paid, its claim is signed again in public, so anyone looking at the relays sees the sale (Agreements rule 13; a reading, the build's). `confirmPurchase` signs one more public claim of the same payment acknowledging what was bought (the vow): the sale reads confirmed. Each is signed as the payment's claim was, under the same lane or by hand, and its proof is the one the payment carried (kept as `proof`). **A purchase confirmed from Edubba** (F357; the money messages cMIP, draft 4, 2.3): a draft of a new kind, "confirm a purchase", naming your own public claim and the vow; To sign reads it (the claim fetched and checked: yours, of that payee and amount) and signing signs the claim again, in public, acknowledging the vow. Every message is now written under the money messages cMIP draft 4; drafts 1 to 3 are still read.
