## The keyless collective (the redesigned core, 30 October 2026)

**In plain words.** A collective no longer has a key of its own to sign with, and its members have no special "member keys" either. When the collective does something (uploads a release, sets where its money goes, writes a record), one of its members does it with their own everyday key and names the collective in the act. Whether that act counts for the collective depends on a rule the founders wrote down for each of the five kinds of things a collective does, its **five layers**: Text (its posts), Envelopes (its uploads, a release among them), Money (where its income goes, its receipts, its debts), Agreements (its publications and grants) and Development (the extensions it adopts). For each layer the founding says how many members must act together; the review before founding shows it layer by layer, **Money first**, and says plainly when one member alone could act, for example point the collective's income somewhere. Text and Envelopes are two layers, never one. The only key the collective has is its chain key, cut into shares among the members, which rotates it when members change. Its income is shared by one stake, in the collective itself, carried in every member change. The service that splits its income is named in a publication, its **split agreement**, not in its terms.

**Precisely** (Agreements rules 56 to 60, Identity rule 1 and rotation check 4):

- **Founding and clones** through `collectiveTerms(g, members, parent?, mark?)` (no holder): the key grammar is the chain key's alone (field 12, key 1); field 19 names a rule for each of the five layers (`Governance.layers`, written out at founding: a layer not typed takes the number "any other change" needs, so that a later change of that number never moves a layer's rule unseen); field 34 the finer areas, by act type only (the Releases area, Envelopes type 0); field 33 the target `[1, null]`; field 7 the one stake, even shares until set, carried in each member change (`departedAfter`, `sharesAfter`). The page's founding form asks the five numbers (`layer-1` to `layer-5`), Money first. A departed holder named again as a member is dropped from the departed entry (F189 1; the entry names no party).
- **No signing key, no member keys.** The genesis is signed by a first chain key (`keylessGenesis`); a rotation carries no key and keeps no tip. Nothing in this client keeps, seals or reads a member key, or a signing key's holder: `signingHolder`, `nextHolder`, `recovering()` and the "recovery rotation" are gone, and so is the warning that a member holding the signing key blocks every line.
- **Who acts.** `Store.collective(id, actor?)` loads a collective with the member who acts for it (`c.by(member)`): a member held here whose voice remains, yours first; `Store.saveCollective` saves that member's file too. Each identity file is held by one object (`Store.identity` returns the same object while its file is unchanged), so a member acting for the collective and signing beside it never signs two acts at one place in their sequence. Records are drawn by any member (`drawer`): the one who stays in a member change, another member than the one leaving, the declaring member for a declaration of absence.
- **Releases.** A member uploads a collective's release with their own key, naming it (`uploader`, default the actor); that upload is their act for the Releases area, the first of the signatures it needs (the review says so). A member who uploaded it has nothing more to sign.
- **Everyday acts in a layer** no finer area reaches (a pointer, a debt, a notice, an acknowledgement, a post): made by a member, then the other members' signature acts the layer's rule needs (`cosignLayer`, `layerCount`).
- **Endings cite their line.** A fork or closing names, after its type's entries, the collective's latest decision and head as this device knows them (`citing()`); `agreementsHandOut(specs, collective, agreement, line)` and `agreementsLineUnheld(specs, collective, line)` read that line. Before a record or an ending, the acts made for the collective by its members that this device never made or saw are found from the members' own acts (those naming the collective), and warned about (a record) or refused (an ending).
- **The split service** is named by the collective's split agreement (Agreements rule 15): a publication selling nothing, naming the collective's ownership agreement by its line, the service and its plan, drafted by a member, signed by every member whose voice remains and by the service. Terms fields 8, 14, 28 and 29 are withdrawn; no grant, no grant key. A split names that publication and pays the owners' line by the ownership agreement's founding version.
- **What a rotation keeps:** nothing of the collective's own (Identity rotation check 4): the review says so; "Lines of acts" typed on the form are said not to be read.
- **Not yet:** a release of a collective's work to the public domain. A work is owned by its own ownership agreement on its claim (rules 7, 17), never by a stake in the collective's terms; this client does not yet make that claim and agreement, so the review refuses, saying why. A text with a picture for a collective carries two objects (the decision and the picture's bake as head), which Text rule 14 refuses: the shared `TestIdentity` cites every head.

## In plain words

A small program runs on your computer and holds your test identities and collectives. You use it from a page in your browser, on that computer only. Double-click **MOR Collective** and the page opens, already paired with the program; nothing is typed in a terminal.

From the page you can:

- make test identities: yourself, and the simulated members a test needs;
- publish a release of the code under your own name, as the first real release will be (step 17);
- found a collective, add and remove members, publish its releases, sign them as a member, and leave;
- step down from its release area, and change that area's own words;
- change who judges absence, a judicial change made under the clone rule, without a rotation;
- declare a member absent, under the collective's own rule: one of the other members signs, the others add their signatures, and the collective registers it at once;
- check any release by its id, as anyone would;
- see everything you signed.

**Nothing is signed until you have read what it means.** Every action goes in two steps. First the program makes the exact bytes to be signed, then reads them back through the core library and tells you, in plain words, what signing means: who is bound, who decides what, what each rule does, what changes compared with now, what is released and what changed since the last release. Words of an agreement are shown as plain text, character for character, with any invisible direction control shown as a code, so no clause can read in another order than it is written. Only when you press **Sign** does the program sign, and only those same bytes: if anything changed in between, it refuses and asks you to look again. What cannot be signed (rules Agreements refuse, a specification this client does not implement, files that differ from your checkout) is said plainly, and the Sign button stays off.

## Agreements draft 8

The client follows Agreements draft 8 (F103 to F109, with Flaw B1 and B2 to B13): as draft 7, and a mark's signers ascending by hash (B8), shown in the order the agreement lists its parties; a judicial-only change (B13). In plain words: a collective's agreement now says **who decides what**. The constitution (the members, the change rules, the keys, the areas, the constitution's words) changes only with every member whose voice remains, unless the founders chose a number: nobody loses their say without signing. Protected clauses (absence, keepers, arbitrators, time, succession) change for a member only with that member's signature. Releases are an **area**, held by the members, any k of whom make a release count; a holder may step down alone, and an area with nobody left is frozen until the members refit it. **Anyone can leave alone**, at any time, keeping what they own; it counts for the collective from its next record. Precisely: `readTerms`, `agreementsClonePlan`, `checkTerms` and the Verifier's Agreements calls all take the spec hashes (`AGREEMENTS_SPECS`); a clone's field 4 is its mark (F104); records (Agreements type 17) are the collective's everyday lines (rule 37c, F109).

## Agreements draft 10: money and endings (F121 to F123)

Each collective has a section **Money and endings**, every step reviewed in plain words before it is signed:

- **Stakes:** each member's (and departed holder's) share of all the collective's income: the one stake, in the collective itself (terms field 7, target `[1, null]`). A work is owned by its own ownership agreement, never by a stake in these terms. An ordinary change under the clone rule, recorded at once; every member signs it. A departed holder's stake is never lowered here without them (rule 46b). *Founding terms cannot carry these stakes (flaw S1): they come in the first clone.*
- **Leaving keeps a stake:** a member removed by a change of members who holds a stake in the collective is recorded, with it, in the departed members entry (field 22). A change of members that also changes who judges absence names both powers, every member whose voice remains signing for the judge (F122).
- **A split service:** a test identity held here, named by the collective's split agreement (a publication selling nothing), every member whose voice remains signing it and the service accepting it (Agreements rule 15). *Was: the collective's grant in terms field 14, withdrawn.*
- **Payee pointers** for the collective and for an identity (a test rail, no money), and **the pointer check** an Agreements client makes before paying: ordinary when every address is in the split service's own pointer, else shown as bypassing it, with the address that does not belong (F123).
- **A simulated payment and its split:** the service signs a receipt and a split delivered to every holder it pays, naming its fee and who received it (Q9); the core says whether the payouts sum exactly and whether any departed stake is paid less than a member's of the same size (equal treatment).
- **A release to the public domain:** refused for now, said why (see "Not yet" above).
- **The fork:** each member on a side; everyone signs with their own identity; once complete, the collective is closed in Agreements and the page refuses anything more from it (F121, B). Founding each side's new collective, naming the original in "forked from", is not here yet.

## Acting for a collective (Edubba, F333)

`Actions.permissions(collective)`: what the identities marked yours may do for it, text and pictures for now, from Agreements' own reading of its agreement in force (its relays and homes): whether you are a member whose voice remains (no signing key: rules 56, 58); for a text (Text, type 0) and for a picture (a bake, then the text showing it), which finer area reaches it, or else the layer's rule, and how many members' acts it needs, as Agreements count the voices that remain (rule 60, `agreementsNextVoices`), a frozen area counting for nothing (rule 71); that an act is done once public and on the chain (rules 57, 58). An area drawn by act type cannot tell a release from a picture (field 34, a stated cost): said. Each item's `state` says what can be done now (`byAgreement`, where set, what the agreement alone would allow).

`Actions.preparePost({ collective, text, jpeg })`: the post read back before it is signed (the text as plain text, the picture stripped, whose signature acts it needs, simulated consent said), with its digest; `confirm` has a member make it with their own key, naming the collective, then the signature acts its layer or area needs besides theirs, yours first, then reads back Agreements' consent for each act. The text carries one object, the collective's pointer to its own decision: Text rule 14 allows it since F334 (draft 9), and Agreements rule 58 asks it.

## Readings

Where the texts are silent, the client takes these readings. *To be confirmed by Nobody, allegedly.*

1. **A program on the machine, a page in the browser.** The keys live in files in the program's folder, as the genesis and repo clients keep them; the page holds only its pairing key. *Set aside:* a page alone, with keys in browser storage: clearing a site's data would lose them, and a release reads the files git tracks, which a browser cannot.
2. **Pairing without typing.** As step 11, except that the code is never typed: the launcher asks the running program for one, over a secret in `run.json` that only the owner's account can read, and opens the page at a link carrying it in the fragment, which the browser never sends. A code can also be made on the page for another browser. *Cost, stated:* anything running under the owner's account can read that secret, as it can read the keys themselves.
3. **What you sign is what you saw (Agreements rule 4a, Text rule 5a).** The reading is made from the exact bytes, as the core library decodes them, never from what was typed into a form; confirming needs the reading's digest; the plan lasts 15 minutes and serves once; it is refused if an identity or collective file it depends on changed after it was shown. Agreements' words are shown as plain text by default, every invisible control of Text rule 5 as an escape.
4. **Fail closed (Agreements rule 2, Envelopes rule 11).** Terms naming a task cMIP, an extension or a time reference this client does not implement cannot be signed here. *Since Agreements draft 10's F121 answers, a split service can (a test identity held here).* Terms using a field whose format is still open are not read at all.
5. **Every objection at once.** Agreements stop at their first objection; the client also checks the numbers against the member count, so every one is listed together: the client's own hints first, then Agreements' objection once, by its code, in Agreements' own words. The client never matches Agreements' wording to fold one into the other: the core's words may change, its codes do not.
6. **A clone's field 4 is its mark,** a statement of fact (Agreements draft 7, rule 45, F104): the review says “it comes in by *this power* signed by *these members*”, and that Agreements check these are exactly the powers its changes need, met by those named; a false mark sinks the clone. No longer a question: F104 settled it. Founding terms exist only once every party has signed them (Q11), and the review says so.
7. **Simulated consent, said each time.** In a test collective every member is held by this program; every review lists who signs on this device and says their consent is simulated.
8. **Leaving is not a member change** (Agreements draft 7, rule 37a). The Leave button prepares a resignation the member signs alone, which the collective registers at once by a record, its line: nothing else changes, no rule is rewritten, and nothing (not even F96) can block it. From that line the member's signature counts for nothing, except for an act of the collective made before it (C1). The review says what follows: the members who stay refit the collective (Change members, removing the member who left), rotating to keys that member never held; that member is not asked to resign again. *Consequence, stated:* with two members left, F96 still allows only "either member alone can rotate", or an escrowed share, which is not supported yet: the refit lowers the shares needed, and its review says so in words. A member removed by Change members who has not left yet signs a resignation first (here a simulated member, held here, which the review says), registered by a record before the clone.
9. **Words and rules.** With no words written, the standard words for the rules are used. If the rules change and the words were standard, they follow; if members wrote their own, they stay, with a warning that they may no longer say what the rules do.
10. **No release while a member change waits:** it would cite a decision the change is about to replace. Nor a record of leaving, stepping down or new words.
11. **A member signs a release** only if they are a party to the agreement in force at the release's binding, have not signed it yet, have not left or stepped down on a line before the release (judged on this device's copy of the collective's sequence), and every file matches their checkout (repo client reading 17). The checkout defaults to the MOR repository the program is part of.
12. **Names stay on the device.** Names of identities and collectives are kept in `book.json` and never published.
13. **Changes of members or rules are constitutional.** Their clone is marked with the constitutional change rule, signed by the members whose voice remains: by default all of them, held here; with a number k ("Members who must sign a change of members or rules"), any k of them, all of those who remain if fewer do (rule 44d). A rules change that would need another power only (a change of the absence rule alone is judicial) is refused here rather than marked falsely. *Since 8 October 2026:* every mark is composed from Agreements, never from this device's copy: the powers from the agreement in force as Agreements find it and the clone's bytes (`agreementsClonePlan`), and for each the voices Agreements count at the collective's next line (`agreementsNextVoices`, rule 44d), less those whose resignations the change registers first. Too few of them held here blocks the review, in plain words. Agreements count again from the relays before the clone is proposed and before the record or rotation is sent; if it would not count the mark, the change stops there, nothing more is signed and nothing is put in force (`docs/step-11b-false-mark-2026-10-08.md`). Where Agreements read a collective as broken, its box says so with Agreements' reason, and no change or release of it can be signed.
14. **Stepping down** from the release area is a resignation naming area 1, registered at once by a record (rule 37b, "Stepping down at once"). The other holders carry on; with nobody left the area is frozen, releases are refused here, and the review says the members must refit it. *Reading:* the client counts a holder who stepped down as holding the area again only after a constitutional change that redraws the area's entry and that they sign (rule 37b); the core also counts them again under any later version naming them that they signed (rule 44d). *Question for the texts* below.
15. **New words for the release area** are an ordinary change (rule 37c, Q8): a clone marked with the area's power, signed by enough of its holders whose voice remains, written on the collective's record at once, drawn by a member with their own key; no rotation.
16. **Before drawing a record,** the client fetches the acts its members made naming the collective from its relays and homes, and warns (without blocking) of any this device never made or saw: the record does not cite it, so it stays outside its line (Agreements rule 58; client conformance: devices share their heads before a line is drawn).
17. **Who judges absence is a judicial change** (Agreements draft 8, B13; rules 44a, 46a). A rules change in which only "who judges absence" differs, with no member joining or leaving and no words typed, is prepared as a clone marked with the clone rule, signed by enough members whose voice remains (any k of them, all who remain if fewer do), and written on the collective's record at once: no rotation. The review says that for a member who does not sign it, absence stays judged by the clause they signed. *Reading:* the constitution's words are constitutional, so this change leaves them as they are, even standard words naming the old number; the review warns, and a change of the rules (constitutional) rewrites them.
18. **Declaring absence** (Agreements draft 8, B12, B15; rules 49, 51, 53). Any k of the other members judge absence, k as the clause the absent member signed last says (rule 46a), all who remain if fewer do (rule 44d). One of them signs the declaration, naming the agreement in force, that clause's version (found from the member's own signature acts at the collective's relays, and checked again by the core library) and outcome 0, their voice removed; the others add signature acts naming it; the collective registers it at once by a record that also acknowledges those signatures (Envelopes `acks`), so that they count at its line. From that line the member's signature counts for nothing; they keep what they own; the members who remain then refit the collective, as after a resignation. *Since the keyless collective:* no member holds a key the collective needs, so the declaring member draws the record at once, whoever is declared absent; nothing waits for a recovery rotation (Agreements rules 56, 60).
19. **A broken collective's way back: a rollback** (Agreements draft 10, rule 37d, F185). Where Agreements read a collective as broken and a rollback can bring it back (`agreementsBrokenAct`), its box offers *Review a rollback*: a new rotation declares a new version of the agreement in force just before the broken act, naming the broken act, rebuilt from the rules this device kept for that agreement (`rules` in the collective's file, kept from this version on), without the members whose resignations it registers: every resignation this device holds by a party whose voice Agreements still counts there, naming that agreement or one it descends from. Its mark is that agreement's constitutional change rule, and the judicial tier's rule where it changes a judge (`agreementsRollbackPlan`), each with the voices Agreements count for the rollback (`agreementsRollbackVoices`); Agreements count again from the relays before the clone and before the rotation (`agreementsRollbackAgreement`). Where the earlier numbers do not fit the members left (F96, absence), the review says so and the members give new numbers (*Roll back with new numbers*). The review says that the broken act and every act since stay shown and count for nothing, for good.
20. **Leaving a broken collective** (rule 37a, F185): the resignation names the agreement in force just before the broken act; no record is drawn, since a record of the broken stretch counts for nothing; it takes effect at the rollback, which registers it.
21. **The last voice** (rule 37a, F185, client conformance): where the member leaving is the last voice that remains (Agreements' count at the next line, or, in a broken stretch, for the rollback), the review says so first, in plain words: the works will be frozen as they stand, and nobody will be able to change, release or move them. Leaving is never blocked. **The last constitutional voice** (RB5, decided 8 and 9 October 2026): where the constitutional change rule names some members only, the last of them whose voice remains is told first, "You are about to break the collective": the constitution freezes as it stands while the others keep their voices in their areas. Resignations the other members published from other devices are read from the relays too (`agreementsPublishedResignations`, F187 3), since Agreements count a resignation only once a line registers it.
22. **F187, Fable's review of 8 October** (`test/f187.test.ts`): the rollback's members are the voices Agreements count (`agreementsRollbackVoices`), never this device's copy, and a member a line registered as departed before the broken act is written to the departed entry, never dealt a share of the new chain key (1); every act the rollback registers is checked by Agreements from the relays before anything is signed and again before the rotation (`agreementsRollbackRegisters`), and "done" is said only when Agreements, reading the relays after, finds the rollback's clone in force (2); leaving names the agreement Agreements find in force, and where Agreements cannot be read the review says that the resignation names this device's copy and that no last-voice warning can be given (4); stepping down during a broken stretch is allowed, names the agreement in force just before the broken act, draws no record, and is registered by the rollback, whose clone leaves that member out of the area (8).
23. **RB1 to RB6** (decided 8 and 9 October 2026; `test/rb.test.ts`): the box shows, as open obligations, the payments the collective received during a broken stretch and owes back (`agreementsOwedBack`, RB2); it shows every declaration of absence naming a member, with the way to contest it, broken collective or not (`agreementsDeclarationsNaming`, RB3, client conformance; the contest act's format, Agreements type 14, is still open, so the client says so and cannot sign one); a declaration of absence made during the stretch names the agreement in force just before the broken act, draws no record, and is registered by the rollback with the other members' signature acts on it (RB3); a fork or closing of a broken collective is refused until it is rolled back (RB6).
24. **A collective's rotation keeps no tip** (Identity rotation check 4; was Identity rule 35, a rotation keeping every line of the collective's acts). A collective signs no act of its own, so it has no line for a rotation to keep or leave out: the acts made for it are its members', in their own sequences, and no rotation of the collective voids them. The review says so; lines or cards typed under **Lines of acts** are said not to be read.

## Using it (a human test, on the author's Mac)

*The build window cannot reach the deployed homes, and keeps no keys.*

1. Once, in a terminal, from this folder: `npm install`, then `sh scripts/make-app.sh`. (`npm run wasm` in `clients/genesis` first, if not built.)
