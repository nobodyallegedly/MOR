## In plain words

**Two versions of one deal (F188).**
- When a deal's split gets tangled (a split inside a split, three versions of one version, two rival settlements, a settlement that splits its own branch), the deal stays on the last version everyone agreed on. What the tangled versions changed is lost. A buyer who paid under a version every party signed keeps what they bought.
- A version that settles a split must be signed by the people of both branches, so nobody on the dropped side is dropped without agreeing. It names the latest version of the branch it drops, and cites it, so anyone fetching the deal finds it.
- If the parties cannot agree, the arbitrator named in the deal may settle the split, but only after one of the parties asks, by a signed request that the settlement names.
- A split service numbers every split it makes under a deal, across both branches. A holder whose numbers skip is warned: splits are being made somewhere it cannot see.
- A payment naming an older version gets a quiet notice, not the alarm. A payment naming a version the seller has never seen gets the alarm: that is the hidden branch the alarm is for.
- Before paying, a buyer's client checks that the offer is the latest version, and warns when it is not.

**A broken collective (F188).**
- After a rollback, what a grantee signs counts only once it cites the rollback.
- A broken collective keeps exactly what its old rules allowed. A payment is judged by the offer it names: bought under an offer from before the break, it is kept; under an offer made while broken, it is owed back.
- The last holder of constitutional power is told, in the words decided: "You are about to break the collective's constitutional layer. Once you leave, nobody will be able to change its rules again. The other members keep acting in their areas."
- A collective whose very first agreement fails is simply founded again.
- A member named in a declaration of absence can now sign a contest. It shows they are present and shows the dispute. It cancels nothing.

**Fable's review (F189).**
1. An old resignation can no longer remove a member who came back. Once they return, only a resignation signed after the return counts.
2. A draft, or a stranger's act, can no longer make a split deal unreadable.
3. A payment naming a version the seller does not hold raises the alarm.
4. **Not fixed: it stops at a question (QF1, below).**
5. A settlement must name the latest version of the branch it drops.
6. A settlement also cites the version it settles.
7. While a collective is broken, no fork or closing counts, whatever line it names. Money owed back from the broken stretch blocks a closing.
8. A declaration of absence counts only if it is public, or addressed to the member it names.

**The WebAssembly changed.** The display client must be released again.

## The question that stops item 4, and five more

These are also in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft". One at a time, as you prefer.

1. **QF1.** F188 says: if a newer version of the dropped branch turns up that the settlement did not name, the split is not settled. F189 (4) says: nothing that grows on the dropped branch unsettles the deal. A complete version on the dropped branch, past the version the settlement named, is both. MOR has no clock, so it cannot tell whether that version came before the settlement or after it. *As built:* Law refuses to name a version in force there. Where the later act names the settlement, it plainly came after: both decisions agree, and it changes nothing. Which rule decides the rest?
2. **QF2.** Terms field 13 names "arbitrators or verifiers" in one list. Which one is the arbitrator, and may any of several settle a split? *As built:* only where exactly one is named; the chain of judgment's takeover is not built.
3. **QF3.** How does a tangled deal settle cleanly? A settling version names one tip; a tangle has more. *As built:* it stays on its reference for good.
4. **QF4.** Is a deal's split with no number, or a repeated number, a fault that breaks the plan, or only shown? *As built:* only shown.
5. **QF5.** How is money owed back shown as refunded? Finance has no refund act. *As built:* it stays owed until the sale is signed anew, so a collective that refunded off the network still cannot close.
6. **QF6.** Is an old stepping down spent too, once the member is named a holder of the area again? *As built:* no, only resignations, as decided.

Readings taken, to confirm, are listed in the same section (eleven, among them: "signed after the return" checked by the agreement the resignation names; in a tangled fork, a payment judged under the version it names, everything else under the reference; the formats of types 14, 22 and 23 and split key 5).

## Limits, stated

- **No split service and no buyer's wallet are built** for deals. The service's numbering is the split format and `splitGaps`; the buyer's check is `offerCheck`, a function a wallet would call.
- **No grantee's client is built** (no client signs with a grant key), so BQ1's client rule is written, not built; the core enforces the rule itself.
- The arbitrator's settlement is built in the core and bindings; no client signs one yet. `requestSettlement` lets a party ask.
- The desk groups splits by service and deal from what it received; it does not open sealed splits it could not open before.
