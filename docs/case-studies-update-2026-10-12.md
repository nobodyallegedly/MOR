## In plain words

**What the studies now say that they did not.** When someone on MOR sells a thing that is not bytes (a bowl, an hour of repairs, a mix, a live session, a seat in the stand, access to a stream), the seller first signs a short act in words saying what is promised: a **vow**. The offer names that vow, and the buyer's payment names it too. From then on the core keeps one fact about the sale, and only one: it is **pending** until the buyer confirms, **contested** if the buyer says the promise was not kept, and nothing else moves it. Only someone who paid onto the vow, or signed the free offer, can confirm or contest; the seller vouching for their own delivery changes nothing, and a stranger's complaint changes nothing. Everything about what was promised (seats, quantities, stock, delivery, what the promise became) belongs to the cMIPs. Where a delivery proves itself from data alone (a key that opens the promised bytes, a hash in an anchoring batch), no vow is needed: that is a proven offer, under the access form.

**The other decisions.** A vow may bundle several promises (a season, a numbered series), and a contest names the one sale it contests, never the whole vow. Any agreement may be a party of another: an album's agreement has its songs' agreements as parties, a course's agreement may have its lessons' agreements as parties. Whoever pays an agreement pays the one pointer it designates and is discharged there, in every chain, so the point of failure is easy to find. A compilation made with its parts' consent is a layer that pays each part's pointer; made without it, it is a derivative work paying its sources by its own agreement, visibly or visibly not. In the live sports study, a vow names what is promised and never the event: the club's vow is for a seat, Dario's is for access to his stream; neither names the other, each may reference the match if the match ever exists on MOR, each sits under its own agreement. A clip names the recording, a work, as its source. How Dario shapes a subscription is his choice, not the core's.

**How the studies were changed.** Each new draft is the previous one with the decisions written in and nothing else changed: the sentences the review listed as now reading wrong are rewritten, and where a decision needed a place in the story (a preorder, a commission, a ticket as a sale of a vow) a short paragraph was added in the study's own voice. Each study stays an invitation, ending with what others can build. Every claim was checked against draft 11's wording; what the core does not support is not written, and is listed at the end under "Not claimed".

**Three things for Nobody, allegedly, to glance at,** none of them a question of rule: the learning study's "streams" of ratings became "strands", because "stream" is now a technical word (the author may prefer another word); the live sports study's ticket is confirmed when the fan's wallet acknowledges "the door scanner's act", the vow build's own example, which a ticketing cMIP would define; and in the commerce study the resale marking (F247) is written conditionally, "where an agreement of Inès's claims the series' vow", since a lone seller's offer is itself the agreement (F215) and claims nothing by itself.

## Not claimed

Things the review or the earlier drafts suggested, or a reader might expect, that the core does not support, and that no study says:

1. **That the core refuses, caps or signs off a ticket's resale.** It shows an offer naming the club's vow outside the club's agreement as such (client conformance, rule 14); it refuses nothing (17).
2. **That the club's agreement claims Dario's stream vow,** or that F247 marks unlicensed offers on his stream for the club. F259: two separate vows, each under its own agreement; the study does not show the club claiming his (17).
3. **That a vow is the match,** or that Dario's vow names the club's, or the club's Dario's (F259 revised) (17).
4. **That the core catches a resale under a fresh vow** (F258, a stated cost): the provenance cMIP's, written as a limit (16).
5. **That the seller's proof of delivery confirms a sale:** a courier's photo, a handover, a delivered key. Each is evidence in the sale (F261, F264); only the buyer's confirmation or contest moves it (12, 16, 17).
6. **That a contest voids anything, refunds anything, or fixes what is owed back** when a promise or part of a bundle fails: the terms', the offer's words' or a cMIP's (rule 13) (12, 14, 16, 17).
7. **That an accreditation module reads a student's contest** (Q14-2, "an overreach") (14).
8. **That the core counts stock, seats or numbers** under an offer (F237): the listing, ticketing and provenance cMIPs' (16, 17).
9. **That a claim on a vow's name reaches what it became:** a stake in the album's promise, a claim on a series' vow, a clip's lineage through a stream's name (rule 7 unchanged; Q17-4) (08, 16, 17).
10. **That a stake in a vow can be sold or that resale shares are paid automatically:** a reseller's split shows whether it honours the maker's terms, as before (16).
11. **That the core decides how a subscription, a season or a job's milestones are shaped** (Q17-2, F253): the seller's choice, each study showing one (12, 17).
12. **That the publisher's publication "made for the label" makes the label the receiver:** payment goes to the pointer the album's agreement designates (F248), whoever that is (08).
13. **That the split service is a judge, or that its backups are stages:** attempts per payment, the next payment starting again from the first (F249, F250, rule 17) (08, 12, 14, 16).
14. **That a stream never closed has a recording** (the live media cMIP's stated cost) (17).
15. **That a cMIP's say-so proves a delivery** (F261): a verifier checks the proof itself; none of the five studies rests a sale on a service's signed word.
