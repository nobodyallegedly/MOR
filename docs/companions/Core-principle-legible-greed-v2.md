# Core principle: Don't ban greed, make it legible

*Version 2, 27 September 2026. Decided by Nobody, allegedly; reworded after review round 2 (F64, F72) so that the promise is exactly what the core keeps.*

**Don't ban greed, make it legible.** MOR never judges whether a deal is fair. A label may take 80%, a platform may take a large cut, a publisher may ask for a stake, an outlet may even steal. What MOR guarantees is that terms, stakes, cuts, fees, ownership and lineage are signed, named and visible to those they concern, and that every record which decides money is signed by a party who answers for it, or matched by a record from the other side.

**The measured promise.** Nobody can take without being seen taking, *by those who took part.* Every payment can be recorded from both ends: the receiver signs a receipt, and the payer's own claim, carrying the rail's proof, is equal evidence. A receiver who stays silent or under-reports is exposed by the payer; a split service that names its own sybil as the referrer earns nothing, because role-share evidence must be signed by someone on the other side. What the core cannot see is a payment made outside it, or a payer who chooses silence. So the exact promise is: **hiding income requires the payer's silence or collusion.** Owners who want no dependence on any service's honesty choose payer-side splitting, where each owner is paid directly.

**What legibility does not do.** The core ties a sale to the seller's agreement, not to a work's stakes. A co-owner who sells alone under their own plan breaks no core rule; they break a signed agreement, in the open, and the claim's holders, keepers and reputation services can all see it. Legibility makes such a sale visible and contestable; it does not prevent it, because preventing it would require the core to judge which claim is *the* claim, and that is legitimacy, which the core never decides.

**Why the core does not judge.** Banning greed would require the core to decide what is fair, and whoever decides that rules everyone. Making greed legible requires only that every act be signed, every term be shown, and every record that decides money be checkable against something its beneficiary cannot produce alone.

**Paired with the right of exit.** The two principles need each other:

- legibility without exit is just watching someone take from you;
- exit without legibility is leaving blind;
- together, they let greed compete: everyone sees the terms and anyone can walk away from them, so a fairer offer can prove it is fairer.

**Review test.** For every rule, ask: can anyone use it to hide what they take? And its sharper form from round 2: is the record that decides this signed by the party who benefits from it being wrong? Together with the right of exit's test (can anyone use it to trap someone?), these are the questions every rule must pass.

*Expressed across the case studies: tough label terms that every band can read, platform and streamer cuts visible in settlements, attention-based deals allowed but named for what they are, a co-owner's lone sale shown as outside the agreement rather than blocked, and a propaganda outlet's stolen photo shown as an uncredited copy rather than blocked.*
