## 0. Introduction

**Agreements is not to be used bare.**

It is not one unified way of agreeing. It offers the basic sockets from which rule books are drafted: more a language, used to decide how to agree, than a set of rules for agreeing. The rules people agree by are written in the cMIPs built on it.

Among what it offers is the keeper: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where the agreement is kept.

An agreement has two classes of participant, not two situations of one: its known parties, and its unknown parties, who join through a spot. Each class has its own rules (§3.2). Agreements protects the unknown parties: each is bound by the version they accepted, and no update reaches them (§5.10).

Knots are a concept of all of MOR, not of Agreements alone. Their importance in agreements is taken up where this text treats relying on acts (§5.13).

---

## 1. Purpose

Agreements is a grammar: the acts in which agreeing is written (Terms and Statement); the grammar rules by which a verifier decides, from acts alone, **who is bound by which Terms, and what is shown, unknown or contradicted**; and the tasks that cMIPs fill so that people can agree on anything, by rules they choose. Its central idea is that an agreement can be a party to another agreement (§5.1): works, services, deals and collectives are built with it, and none is a separate thing in the grammar. Agreements does not decide what anyone must agree, and it does not decide whether anything happened in the world: it records who said so. It does not define how words are fixed, how signatures work, how acts travel, how a shared order is kept, or how value is held; it names those operations (State, Sign, Deliver, Order, Control) where it needs them.

**One constraint on everything below.** Every grammar rule is checked from copies of acts alone, plus the answers of the keepers the Terms name, where §5.8 applies. No grammar rule needs a particular server, platform or software author to remain for an agreement to be left: a keeper that falls silent is left by a new version that every signer it needs signs (5.5.1(b)). What an act fixes must still be there and unaltered when needed, and an outsider holding copies must be able to test it: Agreements relies on both and adds no grammar rule for them.

## 3. Definitions

Each word here means one thing throughout this text. Where a word is defined in another text and only used here, that is said.

### 3.1 The acts and their parts

- **Act**: a signed statement in the core's sense. Published or signed again, never updated. The fields the core gives every act (who signed, the signature, the act's own fingerprint) are not repeated here.
- **Fingerprint**: the identifier the core gives to fixed bytes. "Named by fingerprint" means referred to by that identifier and nothing else. An act's own fingerprint is its **act id**.
- **Grammar rule**: one of the validity rules of §5. It says how acts are read. It never says what people must agree. Always called a grammar rule; the word "rule" alone is never used for one.
- **Task**, or **socket**: a field of an act where a cMIP plugs in and answers one question. One cMIP per socket, with as many modules as it needs; one cMIP may fill several sockets. Every field of an act that names a cMIP is a task, and nothing else is. A task says its one question, what the cMIP in it accepts, what it produces, and whom it makes you trust. The eight tasks are K1 to K8 (§6). The same question asked in two fields is two tasks: the rule in `in_force` and the rule of a power are not one task. These grammar rules take a task's output and use it where they need it; they never compute it themselves. A socket holds at most one cMIP, with its parameters. A socket left empty has the default these grammar rules give it, where they give one, and otherwise asks nothing.
- **Module**: a part a cMIP uses to give its answer: a rule that computes, a witness, a judge, a teller and the like (§6). A cMIP may use several. A module is not a task: no field of an act names it; the cMIP in a socket does.
- **cMIP**: a document, named by fingerprint, that fills a socket: it answers that task's question, for some thing or situation. Anyone may write one. Many cMIPs may fit the same socket in different ways (one for land, another for shares). One cMIP may fill several sockets, of one act or of many; it is then asked each socket's question apart. Choosing a cMIP is how people choose their own rules.
- **Rule**: a cMIP, or a module of one, that computes: named by fingerprint, with its parameters; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone. It is the people's own rule. The word alone always means this, and never a grammar rule. It computes; it trusts nobody. A rule is not a task: it sits in a socket (`in_force`, a power, `condition`), or is a module of the cMIP that does (§6).
- **Terms**: an act that states what is agreed, signed by an author of it (§5.3). Always capitalised and singular ("a Terms act", "these Terms"). Terms are the record of an agreement; stating Terms is not agreeing to them.
- **Agreement**: the first Terms act together with every Terms act that replaces it, directly or through others, and every Statement made within them. Its identifier is the fingerprint of the first Terms act (the **root**). Its Terms acts are its record; what binds is the agreement, as §5.4 to §5.6 and §5.10 say.
- **Statement**: any other act made within an agreement. Its **kind** says what it is (task K7). Four kinds are defined here: **assent**, **counter signature**, **contest** and **resolve**, the last a resolving update in its signer's own line and nothing more (§4.3, §5.12).
- **Assent**: a Statement by which its signer agrees to one Terms act.
- **Counter signature**: a Statement by which an earlier assent to one Terms act is cancelled: its signer's own, or one made in the same party's name. It is available only for as long as §5.4 allows. It sets nothing aside: the assent it cancels stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled (§5.6).
- **Signature of an agreement**: an act made in an agreement's name, under a power its own Terms give (§5.1). An act signed so is signed by that agreement.
- **The words** (of Terms): the Text act, public or private, that carries what Terms say in words, and that the Terms name, in `body`, by its act id. The words are an act of the Text layer, made before the Terms that name them (§5.2). The grammar does not read them; the body's cMIP does (task K1).
- **The set of acts**: one value that names a set of acts: computed from their act ids, sorted, with repeats dropped, hashed with a label for a set of acts (§4.1). The same acts give the same value, whoever writes it. It says nothing of whether the set is complete, what it means, or when it was read. The empty set has the label and no members. The value names no act a verifier can read without the list it was computed from, so it is no knot by itself.
- **Report**, **call**: words of MOR's shared language, whose block is the MICS's (§2); used here, not defined. In short: a task may carry a **call** for a report, or not; the call says whether a report is wanted and from whom; only the signers of the act that holds the task can make it. A call names no keeper and no reader: the where is implied by the leg. A **report** is a signed act that answers a call: it carries the call it answers, the acts it relied on, and its signer. Anything more is the task's to say. A report goes to the other side of its own leg, where that side receives, and no further, in every layer (the MICS). A report is data: any other path for it is one people agreed to, with their signature, by naming a data cMIP in Terms they signed (§5.11; task K8).
- **Leg report**: a report that answers a call carried by Terms that hold the Control task (K6), about the legs carried out under those Terms (§5.11). What it carries beyond what every report carries is in K6's row (§6).
- **Payment**: an act of the layer that holds value (Money), signed by the payer, which names the version it read (§5.13). It carries out what the offer's Terms the buyer signed require of the buyer (§5.11). It holds the task of paying, and by default carries the payer's call for the receiving side's confirmation (§5.11). This text gives it no fields.
- **Settlement**: the signed evidence, from the layer that holds value (Control; the Money layer), that a payment was made. Agreements names it and does not define it. The Statement field `settlement` is where it plugs in (§5.9). That field names an act and no cMIP, so it is not a task.
- **Vow**: the same thing as an offer, without bytes attached. This text gives the word no more meaning than that. These grammar rules read a contest made against a vow exactly as one made against an offer, so no answer of a verifier turns on which of the two an agreement is.
- **Contest**: an act of the grammar: a Statement of kind `contest`, made against an offer or a vow (§5.11). It reaches the signers of what it is made against. A **buyer's contest** is a contest by which a buyer says that what they bought was not delivered after payment.
- **Data cMIP**: the cMIP that Terms name in the field `data` (task K8): a path, beyond one leg, for the reports of the people bound by those Terms, which they agreed to with their signature (§5.11).

### 3.2 Parties

**Two classes.** The participants of an agreement are of two classes, not two situations of one class, and each has its own rules. The **known parties** of a version write it, sign it, update it and give powers, and are bound by the versions they sign. An **unknown party** fills a spot, is bound by the version they accepted, gives no power, has no say in updates, and no update reaches them. Each word is defined below.

- **Party**: one named in Terms. Named in one of three ways:
  - as a **signer**: a person, by the identifier the Sign layer gives them (an identity, not one key: Identity);
  - as a **role**: a spot for someone not yet known, linked to a signer later (§5.10);
  - as an **agreement**: another agreement, by its root (§5.1).

  Signers, and signers who fill a role, are the parties who may be bound. An agreement named as a party need not sign to be named, and where it has not signed it is not bound. Where it signs, it signs by its own signature, as its own Terms require: one level (§5.1). That signature binds its parties as its own Terms say.
- **Author**: one of the parties whose words the Terms are, and who sign them. An author is named as a signer, never by role and never as an agreement, and is a known party. The signer of a Terms act is its first author, whether or not `authors` names them; `authors` names the others, who sign by assents of their own (§5.3).
- **Known party**: of a version, a signer it names: a party it names as a signer, or an agreement it names as a party, as the version names it, which signs as its own Terms require (§5.1); never one who takes part through a role. The known parties are read version by version. The author is one. A party that an update names, and the version it replaces does not, is a known party from that update on, and is not one of the known parties of the versions before it. The known parties of a version are those who have a say in its updates, and, by default, those who sign them (§5.5).
- **Unknown party**: said of the holder of a filling: a signer who takes part through a role, and so was not known when the Terms that name that role were made. A spot that a known party of the version naming it fills is filled by a known party's act: that filling is no unknown party's, and the known parties' rules apply to it. An unknown party has no say in the agreement's updates, and no update reaches their filling, which the version its proof names governs (§5.10).
- **Spot**: a role's place among the parties, before and after it is filled. It has a **sort**: **done**, **received**, **holding**, **fits** or **future** (§5.10). Whether it still takes new identities is said by its cMIP, from the version in force (§5.10).
- **Fill**, **filling**: a spot is filled when the cMIP its role names (task K5) says that a signer is linked to it by a proof: the acts that earn it. A **filling** is one such link, tied to the proof that earned it and to the version that proof names. A spot may be filled many times; whether it holds one filling or many is its cMIP's (§5.10).
- **Rights**: what a version offers whoever earns a filling under it, with every condition that version puts on it. The grammar does not say what they are, or read them (§5.10).
- **Receiver**: whoever Terms name, in the field `receiver`, as receiving on the agreement's behalf: an identity (one of the agreement's own parties) or another agreement, by its root (§5.1). The word means that field's value and nothing else; the side of a leg that receives is the receiving side.
- **Receiver for an act**: the receiver named by the version of an agreement which that act names, followed through any agreement it names until one of an agreement's own parties is reached (§5.1).
- **Work**: an agreement whose Terms say who owns something and in what shares. A plain word for a use of the grammar, not a separate thing in it: every grammar rule reads a work as it reads any agreement.
- **Shares**: what an agreement's Terms say each party's part is. The grammar gives them no form: they are read from the Terms' words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1), and divided by whichever cMIP needs them (a power's, K3; the one that passes on, K6). No socket is kept for a dividing rule alone. An agreement with shares defines as much or as little of how it governs itself as it chooses (powers, §5.6); the grammar has no separate notion of a collective.
- **Service**, **deal**: a service is a signer who does a job for an agreement (dividing what it is paid, holding a payment); a hand (task K6). Its deal is an agreement between the service and the agreement it serves, named as a party. A deal is signed on both sides: by the service, and by the agreement it serves, as that agreement's own Terms require (§5.1). Plain words for a use of the grammar, as "work" is: what makes an agreement a deal, and what is read from it, are the service's cMIP's (§6).
- **Bound**: a person is bound by an act when §5.6 says so.
- **Power**: an entry in Terms saying that a Statement of a given kind, or a resolving update, binds the parties when a given rule says yes. A power has a **sort**: the kind of Statement it is for, or `resolves`, for a resolving update (§4.2). A power is for acting under the Terms as they are. No power makes a Terms act replace Terms (§5.6). A power a version carries is given by its known parties and binds only them: an assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power (5.6.1(b), 5.10.7).
- **Constitution**, **executive**, **judicial**: three words for three things these grammar rules keep apart: how the Terms may change (the update condition), who may act under them (the powers), and what the Terms set for a dispute (the contest, a judge they name, a power to resolve a fork). They are said once, in §5.6, and nowhere else.
- **Buyer**: a signer of an offer's Terms, whose identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed them, and whom those Terms require to pay. A buyer signs by an assent to the version they buy under, as anyone fills a spot and is bound by their own assent (§5.10, §5.6). Elsewhere in this text, where only the paying matters, the same person is called the payer. Paying is a leg, with the buyer as its doer (§5.11).
- **Offer**: an agreement with a participant unknown at the time of signing: one whose Terms name a party by role (a spot, §5.10), so that who takes part was not known when the Terms were signed.

### 3.3 Versions, updates and forks

- **Version**: one Terms act of an agreement. The first is the root; each later one replaces the one before (§5.5). **The original**, said of an update, is the version that update would replace. The **last version** is the one in force (§5.5; while a fork of Terms stands, the one both branches replace, §5.12). One version of an agreement is in force at a time. A version that is no longer in force may still govern a filling earned under it (§5.10).
- **Chain**: the versions of one agreement, each replacing the one before: one agreement's own history. The word is used here for nothing else: not for one signer's acts (a line), not for what a keeper gives (its record, §3.4), not for acts that name one another (knots, §3.4), and not for anything that links one agreement to another. Two uses of the word are not this text's: **the envelope's chain**, and the chains §2 names, are the layer below's (Envelopes), named here, not defined.
- **Sequence**: agreements linked to one another, so that what one gives or owes reaches the next: a payment passing from a work to the works it stands on (§5.11). Never called a chain. The word is used for nothing else.
- **Succession**: not a term of this text. The rights of a baker (Envelopes' word) pass by transfers in the envelope's chain (Envelopes), and no agreement passes them.
- **Leg**: one obligation carried out, between two sides: whoever carries it out, and whoever receives it (§5.11). A payment to a work is a leg between the payer and the work's receiver. A payout divided among five is five legs, each between whoever pays out and one of the five. A leg is never an agreement: an agreement of a sequence is called that.
- **Doer**, **receiving side**: the two sides of a leg. The doer carries out the obligation. The receiving side receives it; where what is owed is owed to an agreement, the receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (§5.1). These grammar rules define the two sides and nothing more: which lines of a report each side receives is the task's cMIP's (§5.11).
- **Line**: acts of which each names the one before it: one signer's acts within an agreement, by `previous` (§5.7); an agreement's Terms, by `replaces` (§5.5), which is its chain; and any other acts the grammar reads that way (§5.12). A signer's acts across their whole life, by `prev`, are the layer below's (Envelopes, Identity); this text reads them and does not define them.
- **Update**: an act that names another act of a line as the one before it, and that carries the signatures that line asks of it: for an agreement's chain, those the update condition of the version it names accepts (the gate, §5.5); for one signer's line, that signer's (§5.7). An update of Terms replaces the version it names only as §5.5 says. A late update (below) is no update of the version it names. No act is ever changed: an update is a new act that follows an earlier one (see **Act**). A **proposed update** of Terms is a Terms act that names a version in `replaces`, taken as its bytes, whether or not it yet has the signatures it needs (§5.5).
- **Gate**: the first of the two checks an update of Terms passes: the update condition of the version it names, or its default where that version names none, answers **valid** (§5.5). No other assent is asked.
- **Pin**: the second check: an order is shown. For an update, in two parts (5.5.1(b)): it is shown after the acts that put the version it names in force; and every other update of that version that passes the gate is shown after it, and so is late. For a first version, the order of the signatures that put it in force. An update signed by every signer it needs, each shown after the other, is pinned by their knots (**shown after**), whether or not the Terms name a keeper. An update signed by fewer than all it needs is pinned by every keeper the Terms name, or, where they name none, by knots; where keepers pin, an act they do not yet show is not yet **placed** (§3.4, §5.4, §5.5).
- **Valid**, **not valid**: said of a proposed update, and of nothing else in this text: the output of the task `condition` (K4), or of its default where the socket is empty (§5.5). **Valid** is the gate's answer only: the update is allowed. It does not say the update is in force. Always given with that condition named beside it, or with the words "by default", and with the ground the cMIP gives. It is the whole answer about whether the update is allowed, who must sign it included: no power is read beside it (§5.5, §5.6). The core's "valid" has the same spelling and another meaning: there it is said of an act, where a rule the verifier can apply is seen to pass. This text never says "valid" of an act; of an act it says that it counts, or that it is shown. **Not valid** is a cMIP's answer about an update; it may rest on a time or on the acts held, and it is qualified as they are. **Invalid** is this text's answer for an act that itself shows it cannot count (3.6). The two are kept apart.
- **In force**: said of Terms only. An update is in force where it passes both checks: valid (the gate), and its order shown (the pin) (§5.5). A first version is in force where it has come into force as §5.4 says, and the order of the signatures that put it in force is shown (the pin, §5.4). An update can be valid and not in force: allowed, with its order not shown.
- **Fork**: two acts competing to change the same state, with no order shown between them: two updates of the same act of one line (two Terms acts each replacing the same version; two acts of one signer naming the same act as the one before, whatever else orders them, 5.7.2), Terms coming into force against a counter signature that cancels one of the assents they need, or any other such pair (§5.12). Three or more such acts are one fork, with that many tips. It is seen from the acts, not from what any person meant. Neither takes effect, and the earlier state stays, until the fork is resolved (§5.12). Where the fork is of a line, the act both follow is the **shared act**; each of the competing updates is a **tip**; a tip, with what follows it in the line, is a **branch**. "Fork" is also a verifier's answer.
- **Rival**: each of two or more acts that compete to change the same state and that nothing orders; the tips of a fork (§5.12).
- **Late update**, **late**: a Terms act whose `replaces` names a version that had already been replaced, and that is shown after the acts that put the version it should have named, the one that replaced it, through the gate (§5.5): lateness is read against the gate, not the pin. It replaces nothing and is no tip of a fork. Where nothing shows the order of the two, it is no late update: they are a fork. "Late" is also a verifier's answer.
- **Never ended**: an agreement never ends. It only gets new versions, each by an update that replaces the one before (§5.5), and goes on as its last version. This text has no defined term for ending.
- **Apply**, **no longer applies**: plain words, and not an answer of a verifier. A filling, and the rights it holds, apply from when the filling is earned. They last as long as the words of the version the filling was earned under say, counted on the clock that version names, never that of the Terms in force, as the cMIP that reads those words answers; and they change only as §5.10 says. No update ends them. These grammar rules do not read what the rights are: a verifier shows the cMIP's answer, and gives none of its own (§5.10).
- **Resolution**, **resolving update**: a fork's **resolution** is its end: a new act, made in an order that can be told, that the agreement's own update condition answers valid or that a power to resolve, given in advance, allows (§5.12). For a fork of a line, that act is a **resolving update**: an update of the shared act, signed as an update of that act must be, that names every tip and says which one continues. A fork is said to be resolved when its resolution is made; as a defined word, **resolve** is a Statement's kind only (§3.1).
- **Set aside**: a tip is set aside when a resolving update continues another tip. It never counted as the next act of its line: for Terms, it never counted as the next version (§5.12).
- **Built on a tip**: an act is built on a tip when it follows the tip in its line, or rests on the tip or on an act built on it: it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch. The words reach as far as the naming goes: an act that names an act built on a tip is itself built on that tip, however many namings lie between (§5.12). One thing is kept apart: an assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, and for nothing else (§5.12).
- **Condition**: short for **update condition**. It is the cMIP that a version names, by fingerprint, in the one field `condition`: the task K4. It answers whether the agreement can be updated or not: given the version in force and a proposed update, **valid** or **not valid** (§5.5). Which updates are valid, who must sign them included, and why, is that cMIP's to specify; where the field is empty, the default asks for the signatures of the known parties (§5.5). Nothing else decides whether an update is allowed. It is never asked about a filling: no update reaches one (§5.10). Nothing triggers. The plain word "conditions", said of what a version's words put on rights (§5.10), on a role (§5.10) or on what becomes of a work (§5.5), means those words, read where their version's cMIP puts them, and not this.

### 3.4 Order and time

- **Name** (or **cite**): an act names another act, or a version, when it carries its fingerprint in one of its fields. What an act names is what it is read against (§5.13).
- **Knot**: each such naming: the order acts give by naming one another. Knots cross acts and layers: any fingerprint of an act, carried in a field of any act the verifier holds, is a knot, whatever layer either act belongs to, the field `body` included. A verifier reads knots in fields, never in an act's content (§5.13).
- **Shown after**: act B is shown after act A when the verifier holds acts, each naming the one before by fingerprint, that lead from B back to A, or a record of a keeper the Terms name or an anchoring to the clock they name that places them so; any other keeper or anchoring is only a reader's evidence (5.13.3). It proves that B came after A, and nothing more: never that an act came before one that does not name it, and never that an act does not exist. Where an act those acts need is named and not held, the answer is unchecked (§5.13).
- **Keeper**: a relay that Terms name, in their keeper field, as the agreement's official relay: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where it is kept. A keeper relays the agreement's acts as any relay does, and vouches for their order only: which of them came after which. It never vouches for an act's validity, which every verifier checks for itself. Copies of the acts may be lodged at any relay; only the order given by the keepers the Terms name counts for the agreement. Terms may name one keeper or several; where they name several, every one is needed (§5.8). A keeper is named by an identifier and is not a cMIP, so the keeper field is not a task.
- **Keeper's record**: the list in which a keeper places an agreement's acts in order: for each act, its id and its position. Defined here for this text's own use.
- **Placed**: an act is placed when the keepers the Terms name show it in their records, at the position they give it; where the Terms name several keepers, when every one of them shows it (5.8.3). Said of acts only: **in force** is said of Terms only (§3.3).
- **Clock**: what an agreement's Terms name as saying what time it was for that agreement (§5.8). What a clock is, is not this text's.
- **Anchoring**: whatever a reader accepts as saying that nothing is later than an act, or what time it was. A keeper the Terms name is an anchoring, for the order of the agreement's acts; the clock they name is one, for what time it was. The keepers and the clock an agreement's Terms name are the ones every reader uses for that agreement's own answers (§5.8). An anchoring of a reader's own choosing gives an answer only as that reader's, named so; it never gives the Terms' own answer (§5.13). A condition in time needs an anchoring the Terms state (§5.8).

### 3.5 What relays do

These three verbs are MOR's, for every layer, one for each actor; this text uses them as defined here. Bytes that sit at a relay while nobody acts are **stored** there.

- **Lodge**: whoever puts an act at a relay (an uploader) lodges it there.
- **Relay**: a relay relays an act: it takes it in and passes it on, reading nothing and vouching for nothing. A keeper is a relay that, besides relaying, vouches for the order of one agreement's acts, because the Terms name it (3.4).
- **Fetch**: a client fetches an act from a relay.

### 3.6 The verifier and its answers

- **Client**: the software through which a person reads acts and signs them. A client is not a verifier, and what a client shows a person is not an act. One duty is written for clients, in one place (§5.12).
- **Verifier**: whoever applies these grammar rules to a set of acts. A verifier answers questions; it does not decide disputes.
- **Answers**: a verifier's answer is one of: **shown**, **not shown**, **unchecked**, **invalid** (below); and **valid** or **not valid** (of a proposed update), **in force** (of Terms), **fork** (of competing acts) and **late** (of a Terms act) (3.3). A verifier names the grammar rule with the answer, and states its reason.

  Answers about what is latest, what else exists, or what time it was, carry a qualifier: **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (§5.13), written **per keeper** where the anchoring is a keeper the Terms name and **per clock** where it is the clock they name; or **as shown** (meaning: among the acts this verifier happens to hold). An answer that rests on what someone relied on in the branch of a tip set aside also carries the qualifier **disputed** (below).

  Of reports, a verifier also shows what the MICS's report block asks it to show, in that block's words, which are not answers of this text.

  *Not an Agreements act.* An act that lacks a required field, or carries a field that §4 allows only beside another (as `continues` beside `resolves`) without it, or carries a field §4 does not give, or one §4 says it does not carry (a resolving update's `parties`, `authors` or `body`; `resolves` on a Statement of another kind than `resolve`), or holds in a field something other than that field's kind (anything but a fingerprint where §4 says fingerprint), or bytes that cannot be read as an act, never reach these grammar rules: §4 says which. This text gives them no answer.

  *A task's output.* Where a task answers yes or no (K2, K3, K6, K8; whether a spot still takes new identities, K5; and whether what a filling gave still applies, K1, 5.10.8), a verifier passes that output on as the task's, with the cMIP named and the ground or cause it gives, and not as an answer of this text; where it lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked**.
- **Shown**: the acts held establish it under these grammar rules.
- **Not shown**: the acts held do not establish it. Always given with its reason: among them, that an act not held, and named by none held, might establish it; that there is nothing to resolve; that a keeper the Terms name does not yet show it; that the keepers the Terms name disagree; that no order is shown; that a keeper's record contradicts the acts (§5.8).
- **Unchecked**: the verifier lacks something it needs to apply a grammar rule: a cMIP the Terms name, or an act named in a field that it does not hold. A missing act never becomes a yes, and never a no. A verifier that stops before it has applied a grammar rule answers **unchecked** for what it did not reach, and says how far it went.
- **Invalid**: an act cannot count as what it claims to be, and the act itself shows it: no act the verifier does not hold could make it count. The cases are those the grammar rules of §5 name invalid, in the order §4 gives, which is the order in which a verifier that names one case names the first it finds. An invalid act counts for nothing, for anything: no answer of these grammar rules rests on it, and no act counts through it. It stays readable as an act, and a verifier shows it, with the answer invalid. If an act the verifier does not hold could change the answer, the answer is never invalid. **Not valid** is not invalid (3.3).
- **Disputed**: the qualifier of an answer that rests on what someone relied on in the branch of a tip set aside (§5.12). It has that one meaning in this text. The core's "disputed" has the same spelling and another condition: there it is shown of an act that its signer's change of key would have made void, and that someone else relied on (Identity, rule 34). Both say that what others relied on stands and is shown so; a verifier that gives both says which one it means.

## 4. The data each act carries

Each field is listed with the grammar rules that read it. A field no grammar rule reads is not here. An act that lacks a field marked required, or carries a field these tables do not give, is **not an Agreements act** (§3.6): it never reaches the grammar rules, and this text gives it no answer. An act that has its fields and breaks a MUST of §5 which the act itself shows is **invalid** (§3.6). Every socket holds a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters, which only that cMIP reads. An entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is not read (§5.2).

### 4.1 The kinds of value

**Each field holds one kind of value**, given in its row: a fingerprint; a list of fingerprints; an identifier (of a signer, a keeper or a clock); a socket (a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters); the set of acts (below); or one of the words the row names. An act whose field holds something other than that field's kind is not an Agreements act, with three exceptions, which reach the grammar rules: a `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid** (§5.2); an entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is left unread (§5.2); and a role whose sort is not one of the five, or whose `what` is not a fingerprint, is read as lacking it, which makes the Terms **invalid** (§5.10). A `what` on a role of sort future is not read (§5.10). Only what stands in a socket's own place for a cMIP reaches §5.2: a socket that is not written as a socket at all (a cMIP and its parameters), and a power's sort that is neither one of its words nor a fingerprint, hold another kind than their field's, and are not Agreements acts. How each kind is written as bytes is the encoding's (encoding 3).

**The set of acts** (§3.1) is a value computed from a list of act ids: the ids sorted, repeats dropped, and hashed with a label for a set of acts. It is a value computed from a list, never a list written in a field: where a field holds one, the field says that what it holds is a set. The same acts give the same value, whoever computes it. It names a set of acts and nothing more: it says nothing of whether the set is complete, what it means, or when it was read. It has no order, and its members are acts only. The empty set has the label and no members. A set's value names no act a verifier can read without the list it was computed from, so it is no knot by itself (§5.13); the acts it was computed from are knots where a field of an act held names them. Where Terms, a Statement or a cMIP in any socket name a set of acts as one value, they name it by this value. It is the value form of any parameter of a cMIP that asks for acts, and of a body's evidence; whether a field of §4.2 or §4.3 takes it is open (§7). Its bytes and its label are the encoding's (encoding 3).

An assent's `content` is empty: its `terms` field is what it agrees to. A counter signature's `content` is empty too: its `terms` field names the Terms whose assent it cancels (§5.4). So is a resolve's: what it resolves is in `resolves` and `continues`. A contest's `terms` field names the version of the offer or vow it is made against (§5.11); what its `content` holds is not read by these grammar rules.

**An author's first act.** An author who wrote the Terms, and signed the Terms act, has that act as their first act within the agreement (§5.7): their assent to those Terms names it in `previous`, as their next act there does, whatever it is. A party that has made no act within the agreement yet names none.

A resolving update (§5.12) of a fork of Terms is a Terms act; one of a fork in one signer's line is an act of that signer: a Statement of kind `resolve`, or, where that signer's line forked in Terms acts, a Terms act written as a resolving update. `resolves` is what makes an act a resolving update.

**What a resolving update carries.** A Terms act written as a resolving update (with `resolves` and `continues`) carries `replaces`, `agreement`, `resolves`, `continues` and `previous`, and MAY carry `relies_on` (§5.13); it carries nothing else. One that also carries `parties`, `authors`, `body` or any other field is not an Agreements act: a resolving update carries no words of its own (§5.12). Whether it in fact resolves a fork is §5.12's, from other acts: one that resolves nothing is read as §5.12 says, and does not stop being an Agreements act after the fact. A Terms act that names `resolves` and no `continues` resolves nothing, and is read as an act like any other (§5.12): it carries what any Terms act carries. A Statement of kind `resolve` carries `agreement`, `terms`, `kind`, `previous`, `resolves` and `continues`, and MAY carry `for` and `under` (a resolving update made under a power to resolve, §5.12), `answers`, `relies_on` and `settlement`; it carries no `content`. It is a resolving update and nothing more: it is not also an assent, a counter signature or a contest, and no Statement of another kind carries `resolves` or `continues`.

A report (§3.1) is one act type, defined once, in the MICS's report block; every layer's tasks use that one type, and this text uses it and gives it no fields of its own. It is not a kind of Statement. These grammar rules read no report as standing in a signer's line: §5.7 reads Terms and Statements only, and no fork is seen among reports. They read a report as answering one call; whether one report may answer several is the block's (§2). What a leg report carries is in K6's row (§6). A payment (§5.11) is an act of the layer that holds value, and this text gives it no fields either.

### 4.4 One fault, one rule

Where one fault fits two rules, it is answered under one, and a verifier that finds several faults in one act names the first, in this order.

1. *Not an Agreements act* (§3.6), under the encoding's rule that gives the form at fault (encoding 3): bytes that cannot be read as an act; a field these tables do not give; a required field lacking; a field allowed only beside another (`agreement` beside `replaces`, `envelope_at` beside `envelope`, `continues` beside `resolves`, `for` beside `under`) without it; a field the act does not carry, being what it is (a resolving update's `parties`, `authors` or `body`; `resolves` or `continues` on a Statement of another kind than `resolve`); the same party named twice; a field that holds another kind than its own, save the three exceptions of §4.1. None of these reaches the grammar rules.
2. *Invalid*, under §5.2: the body's words, then the cMIP of the body's socket, of `in_force`, of `condition`, of `control`, of `data`, and of each power's rule, in the order of `powers`, not named by fingerprint; then the act `body` names, held and not a Text act.
3. *Invalid*, for each role in `parties`, in their order: its sort lacking or not one of the five, then its `what` lacking or not a fingerprint (but for sort future), then its spot's cMIP lacking (§5.10), or not named by fingerprint (§5.2).
4. *Invalid*, under §5.4: Terms that name a party by role and carry no `in_force`.
5. *Invalid*, under §5.3: Terms whose `authors` name a party named by role or as an agreement, or one who is not one of their parties, in the order of `authors`.
6. *Invalid*, under §5.7: an act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement.
7. *Invalid*, under Envelopes' rules for Terms that name an envelope (§2), where those rules give it.
8. *Invalid*, under §5.8: the case it names, of a condition in time with no anchor the Terms state.

A `body`, or a cMIP in any socket of Terms (a role's spot included), that is not named by fingerprint is invalid under §5.2, the grammar rule that says MUST, and under no other. A role's sort or `what` that is lacking, or is not one §5.10 allows, is invalid under §5.10. A field whose bytes are of the wrong kind is not an Agreements act, under the encoding's rule that gives that field's form, and under no grammar rule.

## 5. Validity rules (the grammar rules)

### 5.1 An agreement as a party

- **5.1.1** Terms MAY name a party as an agreement, by its root fingerprint.
- **5.1.2** **Named, and signing.** Naming an agreement as a party asks nothing of it: it need not sign to be named, it is not counted by the default of §5.4, and where it has not signed it is not bound by any act of these Terms, and neither are its parties. A party named as an agreement MAY sign: an assent to these Terms, where their `in_force` rule asks for it (§5.4). Its signing covers what those Terms carry, a call among them (§5.11). It signs as its own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts (a vote, or a signer its Terms give a power to sign for it). **An agreement's signature.** An act is signed by an agreement x when its `for` names x, by its root; its `under` names Terms of x that are in force (§5.5) and give a power for acts of that kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes (where they give two or more powers for that kind, any one of their rules). Whoever signs such an act (a signer x's power names, or whoever the rule of a vote accepts) signs on behalf of x's parties, under the power x gave. That act is x's signature. It binds every party bound by x's Terms, as x's Terms say (§5.6), and no one else; and it binds a party of x only where that party's own signature is on the version of x's Terms that gives the power: no update condition can give it for a party who did not sign that version (§5.6). It is an act within the agreement it is made in, and stands there in its signer's own line (§5.7). Where x's Terms give no such power, x signs by every one of its signers: one act by each of them, its `for` naming x and its `under` naming x's Terms in force. Each act has one signer, as the core has it. **Which acts are one signing.** Those acts are one signing of x where they are the same act but for their signer and their place in that signer's own line: they name the same `agreement`, `terms`, `kind`, `content`, `for`, `under`, `settlement`, `resolves` and `continues`; their `previous`, `answers` and `relies_on` may differ. x has signed once an act so matched is held from every one of its signers; for an assent, that is an assent by each of them, `for` x, to the same Terms. Each of those acts stands in its own signer's line (§5.7). Where an act's `for` names an agreement, its `under` MUST name Terms of that agreement: an act made for an agreement under any other Terms is not that agreement's signature. **One level.** Its signers are the parties its own Terms name as signers. A party it names as an agreement is not one of them, and neither are that agreement's people: these grammar rules MUST NOT be read as making an agreement's signing need the signature of anyone inside an agreement it names, unless its own Terms say so. A verifier reads what they require from that agreement's Terms in force (§5.5); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6).
- **5.1.3** Terms MAY name a `receiver`: one of their own parties named as a signer or as a role, by its position among the parties; or an agreement, by its root fingerprint, whether or not it is one of their parties. The two are told apart in the bytes, not by guessing (§4.2). A receiver that names, by position, a party named as an agreement makes the act not an Agreements act: an agreement is named as receiver by its root.
- **5.1.4** **Receiver for an act.** Whatever a payer, an act or a rule of one agreement gives or owes to an agreement, it gives or owes to that agreement's receiver for the act (§3.2, **Receiver for an act**), read from the version of that agreement that the act names (§5.13). Where that act names no version, a verifier MUST take the receiver from that agreement's Terms in force, per the keepers that agreement's Terms name, qualified **per keeper** with those keepers named, or, where they name none, **as shown** (§5.5, §5.8, §5.13). Which Terms of that agreement are in force is answered by §5.5: while a fork of its Terms stands, the receiver is the one named by the Terms both branches replace, and the verifier MUST show the fork with that answer and MUST NOT choose a branch (§5.5). Where the receiver so named is one of that agreement's own parties, the answer is that party. Where it is another agreement (5.1.3), the two are agreements of a sequence (§5.11), and a verifier reads that agreement's receiver in the same way, and so on, until one of an agreement's own parties is reached. That party is who receives: the receiving side of one leg (§5.11). These grammar rules follow a receiver no further. What that party then passes on is another leg, carried out under its own agreement's Terms and the cMIPs they name, and is read only as §5.11 says. If a version read names no receiver, or names as receiver a signer or a role that is not among its own parties, or the spot named is not filled, or is filled more than once (which of them receives is not said), or an agreement is met a second time on the way, no one receives, and the answer is **not shown**. Where the act that gives or owes names, as the version it read, Terms that are not shown as a version of that agreement, the answer is **not shown**; any version shown as a version of that agreement serves, in force or not, and whether a filling stood under it is §5.10's. Where it names Terms that name an envelope, the answer of Envelopes' rules for such Terms comes first (§2): where those Terms are invalid, no one receives by them, and the verifier shows the payment with them, and the answer invalid, that rule named; where that rule answers not shown or unchecked, so is the receiver. Where it names a tip of a fork that stands, the receiver is the one named by the Terms both branches replace, the fork is shown with it, and the act is shown as built on a tip (§5.5, §5.12); once that tip is set aside, §5.12 says how it is read. If the verifier lacks that agreement's acts, it is **unchecked** (§3.6). A verifier that stops following receivers from agreement to agreement before a party is reached answers **unchecked**, and says how far it followed (§3.6, §7).
- **5.1.5** A rule (§6), in any socket, MAY take as an input a receiver for an act, so answered, or what a leg report states (§5.11).

### 5.2 The words, and what the Terms use

- **5.2.1** The `body`, every piece in `relies_on`, and every cMIP named anywhere in a Terms act MUST be named by fingerprint.
- **5.2.2** **The words are a Text act.** The words of Terms MUST be a Text act (Text, rule 14), public or private, and the Terms MUST name it in `body` by its act id, beside the socket of the cMIP that reads it (task K1). The Text act comes first: Terms can name only an act that already exists, and naming it by its act id is all that this asks of them. Where the act `body` names is held and is not a Text act, the Terms are **invalid**: they lack what they need. A Text act invalid under Text's own grammar rules is no Text act here. Where that act is not held, or is held and cannot be opened, whether the Terms are invalid under this rule is **unchecked**, with a note of what is lacking; where several acts are checked together, the note names each. Where that act is disowned by its signer's key change (Identity, rule 34), the Terms stand, and they are **unchecked** as to their words. These grammar rules read that act id as they read any fingerprint (5.2.1); they do not read the words.
- **5.2.3** Anything a Terms act refers to in another way is not part of those Terms. A verifier MUST NOT read it as part of them. An entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is not read, and does not make the Terms invalid; a verifier says that it left it unread. A field §4 does not give is not left unread: the act is not an Agreements act (§4). A `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid**, under 5.2.1 (§3.6). **What is left unread**, in these grammar rules, is that entry of `relies_on` and four things more, each where it is said: an entry of `powers` of sort `replaces` (§5.6); a call in Terms that do not hold the task it is attached to (§5.11); a `what` on a role of sort future (§5.10); and the answer of `in_force` on coming into force, in a Terms act that carries `replaces` (5.4.2, §5.5). A verifier says of each that it left it unread.

### 5.3 Authors

- **5.3.1** `authors` MUST name one or more of the parties these Terms name, each named as a signer: an author is never a party named by role, and never one named as an agreement (§3.2). Terms whose `authors` name a party named by role or as an agreement, or one who is not one of their parties, are **invalid**. The signer of a Terms act is its first author, whether or not `authors` names them: `authors` names the other authors, and MAY name the signer too. No Terms are invalid for leaving their signer out of `authors`. The other authors sign by assents of their own, made after the Terms act (§4.3, **An author's first act**; §5.4).
- **5.3.2** The authors are the parties who bear the failure of the words to mean one thing. A cMIP that reads a judge's decision, in whichever socket it sits (§6), MUST accept `authors` as an input.

### 5.4 The first version, and what an assent binds

- **5.4.1** **Coming into force.** A Terms act without `replaces` is a first version. A first version comes into force when the assents its `in_force` rule requires are present (task K2). Where it carries no `in_force`, it comes into force when there is an assent to it in the name of every party it names as a signer. **The pin.** It is in force once the order of the signatures that put it in force is shown. Where it names keepers, they show it, at the positions they give those assents (§5.8). Where it names none, knots show it: each party in whose name one of those assents is counted has made an act in that same name within the agreement (a Terms act or a Statement of that agreement, and no other knot) that is shown after every one of those assents (§5.13). Those assents are the ones its `in_force` rule requires, as K2 names them, or the default's; not every assent counted. Any such act pins, a counter signature included: a counter signature so shown is late, and cancels nothing (5.4.5); one that nothing orders against those assents is a fork (5.4.5, §5.12). Until then it is not yet in force: a verifier answers **not shown** for it as in force, with that reason, no order being shown. Where an act named in a field of an act held is not held, and could be the later act of a party whose act the pin needs, the pin is **unchecked** (5.13.3). An update is pinned as 5.5.1(b) says. Where it names keepers, only they pin, and an act they do not yet show is not yet placed (§3.4). **Which keepers.** Before any Terms of an agreement are in force, the only keepers it has are those its first version names: whether that version is in force is judged per those keepers, and per the clock it names, and so is every assent that counts toward it (§5.8).
- **5.4.2** Terms that name a party by role MUST carry `in_force`, in every version: an update that names a party by role carries it too. In an update it is read for who fills a role (§5.10); its answer on coming into force is left unread there (5.2.3, 5.5.1).
- **5.4.3** **What an assent binds.** An assent binds its signer only while the Terms it names are in force, with one exception: an assent by which its signer earned an unknown party's filling (§3.2) binds that signer, and binds the known parties to them, as the version it names says, for as long as that version's words say, on the clock that version names, whether or not a later version is in force; no update reaches it (§5.10). About an assent, a verifier answers **shown** where it is counted in a party's name and those Terms are in force, or where it earned a filling under a version since replaced, with that version named (§5.10); otherwise **not shown**, with the reason (those Terms are not yet in force, no order being shown; they have been replaced; they are a tip of a fork, or a late update; the assent is cancelled, 5.4.5). An assent made within a branch set aside is shown as **disputed**, and binds no one (§5.12).
- **5.4.4** A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of 5.4.1. An `in_force` rule MAY ask for its signing; it has then signed when what its own Terms require is present (5.1.2).
- **5.4.5** **Counter signature.** No assent binds anyone, and nothing in Terms counts, until those Terms are in force (5.4.1, 5.4.3). A counter signature is a Statement of kind `counter_signature` whose `terms` names the Terms an assent names, and which cancels that assent. Made by the signer of the assent in their own name, it follows that assent in the signer's own line (§5.7): directly, or after other acts of that signer. Made for a party under a power of sort `counter_signature` (§5.6), or as an agreement's signature (5.1.2), it cancels the assent counted in that party's name that it names, directly or through acts that name it. It cancels that assent, and no one else's: an assent so cancelled is not counted by 5.4.1. It sets nothing aside: the assent stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled, and so does what it was made within. An assent made for a party by an agent, under a power (§5.6), is cancelled in the same way as one the party made itself, and in no other: by a counter signature in that party's name, which names that assent, directly or through acts that name it, while a counter signature is available (below); the party itself MAY make it, in its own name, as may the agent, as that assent's signer, or another under a power of sort `counter_signature` (§5.6). **Which assent.** A signer may assent, cancel, and assent again. An assent is read against the first counter signature in the same name that follows it; a later counter signature is read only against the assents in that name made after the one before it. Which is first is read by knots or keepers only, never by act id: two counter signatures in one name that nothing orders stand as one. **Until the completion.** A counter signature is available only until the assents that put the Terms in force (5.4.1, its first sentence) are shown before it, by the keepers the Terms name, at the positions they give, or, where they name none, by knots: one shown after them is late, cancels nothing, and pins as any act of its signer within the agreement does (5.4.1). One made for Terms that are in force changes nothing: Terms in force change only as §5.5 says. Where a party is an agreement, "signed", for that party, is whatever that agreement's own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts: one level (5.1.2). A verifier reads that from that agreement's Terms in force (5.5.3); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of 5.4.1 (5.4.4), so this has effect only where the Terms' own `in_force` rule asks for that party's signing. Such a party makes a counter signature by the same act as its assent: under a power of sort `counter_signature`, or by all its signers (5.1.2). **The order.** Whether the Terms were in force before the counter signature is an answer about what is latest: per keeper, or as shown (5.5.3). In both, the assent the counter signature cancels is counted as not yet cancelled. With keepers, the positions they give settle it: the counter signature changes nothing where its position is after the position at which they show the completion (the assents that put the Terms in force, 5.4.1, its first sentence), and cancels where it is before (§5.8). With none, only knots give an order (§5.13): where the counter signature is shown after acts enough for the Terms to come into force (5.4.1, its first sentence), it is late, and changes nothing; where, counting every act held but those shown after the counter signature, the Terms would not come into force, it came before, and the assent it cancels is not counted. Where the Terms would not come into force even with every act held counted, nothing had completed them, and the counter signature cancels. Otherwise the counter signature and the acts that would put the Terms in force are a fork (§5.12): two acts competing to change the same state, with no order shown. While it stands, neither takes effect: the Terms are not in force. A verifier answers **fork**, shows both, and MUST NOT choose between them; the fork is resolved as §5.12 says. **Its answer.** About a counter signature, **shown** means that it cancels the assent it is read against; **not shown** means that it is not shown as cancelling (it is late and changes nothing, or it follows no assent), with the reason; **fork** means that it and the Terms' coming into force are a fork. Where counter signatures lean on one another so that the reading does not settle, the MICS says what a verifier answers (§2). A later assent by the same signer to the same Terms is counted as any assent is.

### 5.5 Updates: the gate and the pin

- **5.5.1** **The gate and the pin.** An update replaces the version it names only where it passes two checks:
  - (a) **The gate.** The update condition of the version it names, or that condition's default where the version names none, answers **valid** for it (5.5.7). No other assent is asked.
  - (b) **The pin.** Its order after the last replacement of that version is shown: it is shown after the acts that put that version in force (for a first version, the assents of 5.4.1's first sentence; for one that is itself an update, the acts that put it through its gate); and every other update of that version that passes the gate is shown after it, and so is late. It is shown after them where it, or an act that put it through the gate, is. What shows the order depends on who signed. An update signed by every signer it needs, each shown after the other, is pinned by their knots (§5.13), whether or not that version names a keeper, qualified **as shown**. An update signed by fewer than all it needs is pinned by every keeper that version names (5.5.5(d), 5.8.3), or is not shown as pinned; where that version names none, by knots. Where keepers pin, an act they do not yet show is not yet placed (§3.4). The order between two updates of one version is read from the acts that put each through the gate, as 5.5.4 says: it does not wait on either update's own pin.

  **Valid, and in force.** Valid is the gate's answer, and says only that the update is allowed. In force needs both checks. An update can be valid and not in force: allowed, with its order not shown. A verifier then gives two answers: **valid** for it, and **not shown** for it as in force, with the reason: no order is shown, it is a tip of a fork, or the version it names is not in force. Two updates of one version that both pass the gate, neither shown after the other, are a fork (§5.12): both stop, and the version both name stays in force (5.5.4(e)). An update's own `in_force` is left unread (5.2.3): whether it replaces is answered by this rule alone.
- **5.5.2** **When Terms stop.** Terms stop being in force when an update that replaces them is in force (5.5.1). While a fork of them stands (5.5.4), no Terms that replace them are in force. Terms so replaced may still govern a filling earned under them (5.4.3, §5.10).
- **5.5.3** **Which Terms are in force.** Which Terms of an agreement are in force is an answer about what is latest. A verifier MUST take it per the keepers the Terms name, qualified **per keeper**, with those keepers named (§5.8); or, where they name none, from the knots, **as shown** (§5.13). For an update pinned by its signers' knots (5.5.1(b)), it is **as shown**. It MUST NOT give it as latest without saying what the answer stands on. A keeper or an anchoring of the reader's own choosing gives an answer only as that reader's, named so: it never gives the Terms' own answer, never puts Terms in force, and never makes an update late (§5.13). **Which keepers.** Before any Terms of an agreement are in force, the only keepers it has are those its first version names (5.4.1). Whether a later version has replaced the one before is judged per the keepers the one before names (5.5.5(d)). Where versions name different keepers, the answer that given Terms are in force carries the keepers those Terms name, or **as shown** where they name none; each replacement's own answer carries the keepers it was judged by.
- **5.5.4** **Two updates of one version, and a late update.** Where two Terms acts each name the same version in `replaces`, each passes that version's gate (5.5.1(a)), and neither is shown after the other, they are a fork (§5.12): two updates, each allowed by the version they name. **A late update.** Call **O** a version already replaced; **F** the update that replaced it (the Terms act that passed O's gate and pin, 5.5.1; or, where O's replacement was a fork, the resolving update that resolved it, §5.12); and **L** a Terms act whose `replaces` names O, and which passes O's gate. L is a late update (§3.3, **Late update**) where L is shown after the acts that put F, the version L should have named, through O's gate (5.5.1(a)), as (a) and (b) say: lateness is read against the acts that put F through O's gate, never against F's own pin: O stays replaced by F, and the line goes on from F as if L were not there. Where nothing shows it, L and F are a fork whose shared act is O, as for any two updates of one version, however late L may in fact be: every version after O is in the branch of F, and stops with it (§5.12).
  - (a) **Which knots count.** Every fingerprint in a field of every act the verifier holds is a knot, Terms or Statement, of this agreement or another, of any layer (§3.4, §5.13). Where O names no keeper, L is shown after O was replaced, by knots, where L is shown after acts enough for F to pass O's gate, or, for a resolving update, to resolve (§5.12); or where no choice of acts enough for L to pass O's gate avoids an act so shown. Each such choice is read whole, not act by act: it is enough that one act of every choice is so shown. A verifier gives that **as shown**, from the acts alone (§5.13). Where O names keepers, knots show L late only as a named keeper's contradiction ((c)).
  - (b) **Which keepers, and which clock.** Per keeper: the keepers O names (5.5.5(d)). L came after O was replaced, per those keepers, where the first position at which, counting only the acts they show at or before it, L passes O's gate comes after the first position at which, so counted, F passes O's gate (§5.8); a verifier gives that **per keeper**. Per clock: where the clock O names (§5.8) gives a time for each of those two events and the two times are ordered, **per clock** (§7). No other anchoring gives the Terms' own answer: a verifier MUST NOT read L as late by a time written in an act, by a keeper or a clock the Terms do not name, by what is likely, or in any other way. An anchoring of the reader's own choosing gives only that reader's answer, named so (5.5.3).
  - (c) **The answer.** About L, a verifier answers **late**, names this rule and what shows that L came late (the act of L, or the act it needs, shown after F's acts; or the positions the keepers give, or the times the clock gives), and qualifies the answer **as shown** where knots show it, or **per keeper** or **per clock** where only they do. Which Terms are in force is then answered as if L were not there. An assent to L binds no one (5.4.3), and nothing built on L counts: a late update is no tip and gives no previous. A missing act is never a yes and never a no: where the acts that could show L late need an act that is named in a field of an act held, and the verifier does not hold it, whether L is late is **unchecked** (§3.6, §5.13), and so is which Terms are in force from O on (5.5.5(e)). Where knots, the keepers or the clock show L late, nothing else (a later resolving update) makes L an update again. **Knots against a keeper.** Where O names keepers, knots show L late against them only as that keeper's contradiction: a keeper's record that contradicts a knot the verifier holds (it places L before an act that L, or an act L needs, is shown after) gives **not shown** for the answers that rest on that record, with the reason that the keeper contradicts the acts, and the record is kept as evidence (5.8.4).
  - (d) Whether an update made after another, and not shown to be late, may still be valid is the update condition's to say (5.5.7; §6).
  - (e) **While a fork stands.** An assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, so that the fork is seen, and for nothing else while the fork stands (§5.12). A resolving update is not a tip (§5.12). A verifier MUST answer **fork** for which Terms replace the version both tips name, show both tips, and MUST NOT choose between them, by position or in any other way. Both tips stop (§5.12): until the fork is resolved (§5.12), neither is in force, nothing built on either counts, and the Terms both replace, the last shared version, stay in force. A verifier MUST answer from them every answer that rests on the Terms in force: the powers (§5.6); the update condition (5.5.7); the roles, and who fills them (§5.10); the receiver for an act (5.1.4); whether they hold the Control task, and their calls (§5.11); their data cMIP (§5.11); the keepers and the clock (§5.8); and what any cMIP they name produces (tasks K1 to K8, §6). It MUST show the fork with each such answer, per keeper or as shown.
- **5.5.5** **Replacing.** A Terms act replaces the Terms its `replaces` names, the original (§3.3, **Version**), only as 5.5.1 says: by the gate, and the pin. The gate is the update condition's one answer, valid or not valid (5.5.7), and nothing else: who must sign an update is part of it. No power makes a Terms act replace (§5.6).
  - (a) **The default signers.** Where the original names no update condition, the signers its default asks for are the known parties of the original (§3.2), the author among them: the parties the original names as signers or as agreements; never a party named by role, and never a signer who fills a role. A party named as an agreement is a known party, and among the default signers, whether or not it has ever signed: how it signs is §5.1's (5.1.2), and an update it does not sign is not valid by default. A stranger's assent makes no one a party. A party the update names that the original does not is not among them: it is a known party from the update on (§3.2). They are the ones the default asks (5.5.7(e)). Two assents of one signer that name the same `previous` fork that signer's line (5.7.2), and neither counts for any update's gate until that line is resolved (§5.12): that is the line's fork, and the gate makes no test of its own of it. A second assent that names the first, directly or through acts that name it, is a knot: it may show the update it assents to late, by 5.5.4(a)'s test and by no other; where that test is not met, and nothing else orders the two updates, they are rivals, a fork of the versions (§5.12).
  - (b) **Valid.** Where the original names an update condition, the gate's answer is that cMIP's, who must sign included (5.5.7). Where it names none, the default answers: an update is valid when every known party of the original signs it (5.5.7(e)).
  - (c) Under the default, a Terms act that lacks one of those signatures does not pass the gate, and replaces nothing: the Terms it names stay in force, and it is not a tip of a fork. A verifier answers **not shown** for it as a replacement, per keeper or as shown: an assent it does not hold may exist (§3.6). A Terms act that a named update condition answers not valid replaces nothing either, whoever signed it, and is not a tip of a fork (5.5.7(c)); nor does a late update (5.5.4).
  - (d) **Which keepers.** Whether a Terms act has replaced the original is judged per the keepers, and the clock, that the original names (§5.8), whatever keepers the new version names: the Terms act, and each act its gate and its pin count, count once those keepers have placed them; but an update signed by every signer it needs, each shown after the other, is pinned by their knots, and neither it nor the acts its gate counts wait on those keepers (5.5.1(b)). Where the original names several keepers, every one is needed: an order counts once each of them shows it, and where they agree; until each has shown it, it is **not shown**, with that reason; where they disagree, it is **not shown**, with the reason that the keepers disagree, and the disagreement is kept as evidence (5.8.3).
  - (e) **One update that cannot be checked.** Where two Terms acts name the same original, and for one of them the verifier cannot tell whether it is an update (it lacks a cMIP or an act, §3.6), which Terms are in force is **unchecked** from there on, even where the other is answered valid: the first might be an update too, and the two a fork. The verifier names the last version it has established.
- **5.5.6** **No act ends an agreement but an update.** Terms stop being in force only as 5.5.2 says: when an update that replaces them is in force. No act ends Terms, or an agreement, without replacing them, and a verifier MUST NOT answer that Terms or an agreement have ended. Asked whether Terms that have been replaced are in force, a verifier answers **not shown**, names the Terms that replace them (5.5.2), and shows the agreement as going on in its last version. An agreement only gets new versions: a verifier reads every replacing version alike (5.5.5, §5.6), whatever its conditions say becomes of the work and its shares; what they say is theirs (§5.2, task K1). A version that dissolves an agreement is such an update: it replaces only as 5.5.1 says, by the update condition of the version it names, as that condition's cMIP dictates, and it is read from its fields, as every version is. A version that cannot be updated is one whose update condition answers not valid to every update (5.5.7(i)). Every version replaced stays an act of the agreement, readable as it was (§5.2).
- **5.5.7** **The update condition: valid or not valid.** Terms MAY name in `condition` (task K4) a cMIP, by fingerprint (5.2.1), with its parameters: their update condition (§3.3, **Condition**). It is one simple field, and these grammar rules read one thing of it: asked of a proposed update, **valid** or **not valid**, as that cMIP answers: the gate (5.5.1). Everything else is that cMIP's to specify: who must sign an update (two owners of three, one named party, everyone), which parts of an agreement an update may change (a share, a party, the rights a version offers, the offer itself, a power, the judge, the condition itself), when, and on what.
  - (a) **What it is given.** The version the proposed update would replace (the original: the version in force, 5.5.3, or, for an update of a version already replaced, the version its `replaces` names, 5.5.4: the condition is asked of it, and whether the update is late is 5.5.4's) and the proposed update: a Terms act whose `replaces` names that version. With them, as for any rule, the acts held, among them the assents to the proposed update that count: an assent in a tip that has stopped (5.7.2, 5.12.3) is not among them, and the verifier says which it left out and why; and the answers of the keepers and the clock the Terms name (§5.8). A condition that needs a clock or a keeper is given one only where the Terms name it: Terms that do not supply what the condition's cMIP declares it needs (a clock, a keeper) are **invalid** (5.8.6).
  - (b) **It runs from the bytes.** Whoever holds those bytes can apply the cMIP, to an update already signed or to one only proposed, and gets the same answer as anyone else (task K4). Its answer waits for no one to publish anything.
  - (c) **Not valid.** Where the cMIP answers not valid, the proposed update does not pass the gate, and replaces nothing, whoever signed it (5.5.5): the Terms it names stay in force, and it is not a tip of a fork. A verifier MUST answer **not valid** for it, and MUST name the condition beside that answer, with the ground the cMIP gives: an act, with who signed it, or a time, per the clock the Terms name (§5.8). A time has no other ground: no reader's anchoring answers for the Terms, and Terms that do not supply what the condition's cMIP declares it needs (a clock, a keeper) are invalid (5.5.7(a), 5.8.6). Where the cMIP counts signatures, its answer rests on the assents held: it is given per keeper or as shown, and it may differ once an assent the verifier did not hold is shown.
  - (d) **Valid.** Where the cMIP answers valid, the verifier answers **valid**, named and qualified in the same way. Valid is the gate's answer only: the update replaces the version it names once its order is also shown (5.5.1); until then it is valid, and not in force. Who must sign is part of the cMIP's answer. Nothing more is asked of the update's signatures, and no power is read for it (§5.6).
  - (e) **Which condition.** The condition asked is the one named by the original, the version the update would replace (5.5.5); while a fork of Terms stands, the one named by the Terms both branches replace (5.5.4). **The default update condition.** Where Terms name no update condition, an update is valid when every known party of those Terms signs it (5.5.5(a)): the author among them, and never a signer who fills a role. A verifier answers **valid**, with the words "by default", where an assent to the update in the name of each of them is held; where one is lacking it answers **not shown** for the update as a replacement (5.5.5(c)), and never **not valid**: the missing assent may exist. There is no power for replacement (§5.6): Terms that name no update condition are updated only by their known parties, whatever their `powers` say.
  - (f) **Nothing changes by itself.** These grammar rules have no condition that triggers. What a version offers changes only through an update. What a filling gave lasts as long as the words of the version it was earned under say, counted on the clock that version names, as the cMIP that reads those words answers: no update ends it, and none is needed (§5.10).
  - (g) **A resolving update.** A resolving update of Terms is an update of the shared Terms (§5.12), and the condition those Terms name is asked of it as of any update: its answer says who must sign it. With none named, the default asks for the known parties (5.5.5(a)). A resolving update carries no words of its own, so there is nothing else in it to answer about; each tip it names was itself answered valid, or is no tip (5.5.5). One signed under a power to resolve that the shared Terms give resolves as §5.12 says, and the condition is not asked of it.
  - (h) Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, whether the update passes the gate is **unchecked** (§3.6), and so is every answer that rests on it (5.5.5(e)).
  - (i) **Output, and nothing more.** These grammar rules name the update condition's cMIP, take the output of that task, valid or not valid, and use it where it is needed: here, in 5.5.1 and in 5.5.5. They have no notion of a cMIP overruling the default, and no value "locked". A named cMIP is not read as set against the default: the socket is filled, and its output is the answer. An update condition whose cMIP answers not valid to every update is read as any other: the version that names it cannot be updated, and that is all that "cannot be updated" means in this text. So is one that answers valid to an update fewer than all have signed, which then passes the gate (5.5.1). A verifier MUST NOT answer, of itself, that an agreement is locked, or that it can never be updated: it gives the condition's answer about each proposed update.

### 5.6 Who is bound

- **5.6.1** A person is bound by an act if, and only if:
  - (a) they signed it in their own name (an act made for another under a power is not made in its signer's own name: 5.6.3), or
  - (b) the act's `for` names them, or names an agreement by whose Terms they are bound (5.1.2); its `under` names Terms by which they are bound and which are in force (§5.5); their own signature is on the version of those Terms that gives the power, the version `under` names, as an assent counted in their name (5.6.5(f)), and they are a known party of that version: no update condition can give a power for a person who did not sign the version that gives it, and an assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power; for a filling's holder, an act made under a power counts as their signature only where they gave that power in an act of their own (5.10.7); those Terms give a power for acts of this kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes (where those Terms give two or more powers for that kind, the act binds where any one of their rules says yes), or
  - (c) the act is a Terms act that replaces Terms by which they are bound as a known party (§3.2), and it passes the gate and the pin of those Terms (5.5.1) (5.6.2). No update binds anyone through an unknown party's filling (§3.2): such a filling is bound by the version its proof names, and by no update (5.4.3, §5.10).
- **5.6.2** **A replacing Terms act.** A replacing Terms act (one with `replaces`) binds a party by that party's own assent to it (5.6.1(a)); or, with no assent of theirs, under 5.6.1(c): the party is bound, as a known party, by the Terms it replaces, and it passes their gate and their pin (5.5.1, 5.5.7). Under the default every known party has signed the update (5.5.7(e)). It binds no one through an unknown party's filling (§3.2): whoever holds one is bound by the version that filling's proof names, for as long as its words say, and by no update (5.4.3, §5.10). **No power for replacement.** No power makes a Terms act replace, and no power binds a party to one: `powers` has no sort `replaces`. An entry of `powers` of that sort gives no power; a verifier says that it left it unread (5.2.3). Which signatures are enough for an update is the update condition's, with the rest of the answer about an update, in one place (5.5.7). An assent to a replacing Terms act MAY be made for a party under a power of sort `assent`, as any assent may (5.6.1(b)): that is acting for someone, and it is counted as that party's signing.
- **5.6.3** An act with no `for` and no `under` is made in its signer's own name only. It MUST NOT later be counted as made for anyone else. An act with `for` and `under` is made in the name its `for` names, and binds only as 5.6.1(b) says: it binds the grantor (5.6.6), who gave the power, and never its signer, the agent, as an act of the agent's own. A power a version carries is given by that version's known parties, and binds only them (5.6.1(b)). So it is where the grantor is an agreement acting through powers its Terms give (5.1.2): the agreement is bound, as its Terms say, and the signer who acted for it is not bound by that act as an act of its own.
- **5.6.4** **An act under a power whose Terms were replaced.** Where whoever is bound through `for` is bound only through Terms that have been replaced, they are bound by the act if it was made before the Terms that replace those were in force, and not bound if it was made after. A verifier MUST answer from §5.8: per the keepers the Terms that gave the power name, by the act's position against the position at which the replacing Terms passed the gate and the pin: the first position at which, counting only the acts those keepers show at or before it, the replacing Terms pass that gate and that pin (5.5.1), qualified **per keeper**; where one of those keepers does not yet show it, or they disagree, **not shown**, with that reason (5.8.3). Where those Terms name no keeper, by knots (§5.13), **as shown**: the person is bound where the act is shown before the replacing Terms passed the gate and the pin, that is, where acts enough for them to pass it are shown after the act; not bound where the act is shown after such acts. Where nothing shows their order, the act is **not shown** as binding through those Terms, and nothing more: the act and the replacing Terms are no fork of the versions (§5.12). Whether Terms have been replaced is answered by §5.5; Terms are not ended otherwise (5.5.6).
- **5.6.5** **Constitution, executive and judicial.** Three things are kept apart, and this rule is the one place that says so.
  - (a) **The constitution is the update condition: how the Terms may change.** A version is replaced only by an update that passes its gate and its pin (5.5.1): the gate is its update condition's answer, valid, who must sign it included; with none named, an update every known party has signed (5.5.5(a), 5.5.7(e)).
  - (b) **The executive is the powers: who may act under the Terms, and in which kinds of act.** An act made for a person or for an agreement, a Statement of a kind (5.1.2, 5.6.1(b)). A power acts under the Terms as they are. No act made under a power is a version, and none changes what a version says.
  - (c) **The judicial is set in the Terms.** It is three things. The contest: these grammar rules give it, and whom one reaches is read from the version it is made against (§5.11). A judge, where Terms name one: in a socket of theirs, by the cMIP that socket names or by its parameters, fixed by fingerprint (§5.2, §6). And a power to resolve a fork: an entry of `powers` of sort `resolves` (§5.12). Each is read from the version in force (5.5.3); while a fork of Terms stands, from the last shared version (5.5.4(e)).
  - (d) **It changes only by an update.** Who the judge is, whether Terms give a power to resolve and what its rule accepts, and whom a contest reaches, change only when a new version says otherwise, made by an update that passes the gate and the pin ((a)). No power can change it. A verifier MUST NOT read an act made under a power as naming or replacing a judge; as giving, widening, narrowing or removing a power to resolve; or as changing whom a contest reaches. A cMIP that leans on a judge takes who the judge is from the Terms that name that cMIP, and from no act made under a power.
  - (e) **Using it is not changing it.** A judge's decision binds only through a power (5.6.1(b), §6). A resolving update signed under a power to resolve is that power used (§5.12). A contest made for someone under a power of sort `contest` is that person's contest. Each of these acts under what the Terms set, and changes none of it.
  - (f) **An agent within its limits.** An assent made for a party under a power of sort `assent` (5.6.1(b)) to an update, one that changes the judicial included, is that party's signing where the power's rule says yes; the update still has to pass the gate and the pin ((a); 5.5.1). Which updates a power to assent reaches is that power's rule's to say (K3), and a verifier reads its output and nothing more. That is not a power changing the judicial: the judicial changes by the update, and only by it ((d)).
- **5.6.6** **Nothing an agent made is set aside.** An act made for a person or an agreement under a power (5.6.1(b)), or as an agreement's signature (5.1.2), is an act like any other once made. Call the **grantor** the one in whose name it was made (the one who gave the power, or the agreement whose power it was). No act of the grantor, and no act of anyone else, sets aside, voids or undoes an act made under its grant. The grantor acts on what was made under its grant only by an update, under the grammar rules for updates (§5.5), and as anyone may act on any act:
  - (a) **On the grant.** By a version of the Terms that give the power, which ends or narrows it, made as any update of those Terms is: their update condition decides, as its cMIP dictates (5.5.7), or, where they name none, the default (5.5.5(a)); or as the power's own rule says, which answers when the power ends (task K3). A version that ends or narrows it reaches only acts made after that version is in force; an act made before it binds as 5.6.4 says.
  - (b) **On what was made.** By an update of the agreement the act was made in, which passes that agreement's gate and pin (5.5.1): its update condition's answer, or, where it names none, the default: every known party (5.5.5(a)). The grantor is among the known parties where it is one; an assent counted in its name, its agent's included, is its signing (5.6.5(f)).
  - (c) **Before the Terms are in force.** By a counter signature in the grantor's name (5.4.5), as for any assent, while it is available (5.4.5: until the assents that put the Terms in force are shown before it). It cancels the assent counted in that name, and sets nothing aside: the agent's assent stays an act, shown as cancelled. A counter signature that comes later, or one made once the Terms are in force, changes nothing (5.4.5), and only (a) and (b) remain.

  **Who is covered.** Whoever agreed to what the agent signed keeps what that version gave them, the same way as under any update: a version is replaced only by an update that passes its gate and its pin (5.5.1); a known party who signed it is bound by an update they did not sign only where 5.6.1(c) says so; and a filling is governed by the version its proof names, which no update reaches (5.4.3, §5.10). A verifier MUST NOT read any act as setting aside, voiding or undoing an act made under a power, because it was made by an agent, or because the grantor later acted against it. It reads the grantor's later acts as what they are: a version of the grant, an update of the agreement, or, while one is available, a counter signature. A fork is another thing: an act made under a power that is a tip of a fork stops as any tip does, and may be set aside by a resolving update (§5.12); that is the reading of a fork, not the grantor acting on its grant.

### 5.7 A signer's own line

- **5.7.1** Every act within an agreement MUST name in `previous` its signer's previous act within the same agreement, or none if it is the signer's first. An act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement (an act of another signer, or of another agreement; a report; a payment; any record that is no Terms act and no Statement) is **invalid** (§3.6). Where `previous` names nothing the verifier holds, the act counts, and the verifier shows that signer's line as **unchecked** before it. So it is where `previous` names an act the verifier holds and cannot open (a private act, held without its key): what that act is cannot be read, so the act counts, the signer's line is **unchecked** before it (§3.6), and the act is not invalid. An act held that is not an Agreements act (§3.6) is a record that is no Terms act and no Statement: an act whose `previous` names it is **invalid**. Whether two acts of a line are a fork, and whether a line is unchecked before an act, are answered from the acts held, **as shown**, whatever a keeper shows. Whether anything is later in a line is an answer about what is latest, and is given as §5.8 says.
- **5.7.2** Two acts by one signer within one agreement that name the same `previous` (or that both name none) are a fork, whatever else orders them: where `prev` or another knot shows one after the other (5.13.3), the line is still forked. A verifier holding both MUST answer **fork** for that signer, whatever a keeper shows: an act the keepers do not show is still a tip. It MUST NOT choose between the tips. Both tips stop (§5.12), and the fork is resolved only as §5.12 says. Where the two acts are assents, neither counts for any update's gate, nor for a first version's coming into force (5.4.1), until that signer's line is resolved, by a resolving update in it (5.5.5(a), 5.5.7(a), §5.12).

### 5.8 The keeper, and the clock

- **5.8.1** **The keeper.** Terms MAY name, in `keeper`, one keeper or several, each by the identifier the layer that orders acts gives it. A keeper so named is the agreement's official relay: the way the drafters of the agreement agree on where it is kept. A keeper relays the agreement's acts as any relay does (§3.5), and vouches for their order only: which of them came after which, at the positions it gives them in its keeper's records (§3.4). It never vouches for an act's validity, which every verifier checks for itself. These grammar rules take a keeper's answers about order and define nothing more of it.
- **5.8.2** **Copies.** Copies of an agreement's acts MAY be lodged at any relay. Only the order given by the keepers the Terms name counts for the agreement: a relay the Terms do not name gives no order for it, whatever it relays.
- **5.8.3** **Several keepers.** Where the Terms name several keepers, every keeper named is needed: an act counts for an answer about order only once each of them shows it, and that order counts only where they agree. Until each has shown it, a verifier answers **not shown**, with that reason, and names the keeper that does not yet show it. Where they disagree, there is none for what they disagree on: a verifier answers **not shown** for the answers that rest on it, with the reason that the keepers disagree, names the keepers, and shows the records of each as evidence of the disagreement; answers that rest only on what they agree on stand. Where the verifier holds two answers of one keeper that contradict each other, it has no answer from that keeper: **unchecked** (§3.6). An answer **not shown** for want of a keeper changes only when that keeper shows it, or when a version that every signer it needs signs replaces the keepers: a keeper that falls silent is left so (5.5.1(b)). Every keeper named is needed for an update signed by fewer than all it needs; an update signed by every signer it needs, each shown after the other, is pinned by their knots (5.5.1(b)).
- **5.8.4** **Order per keeper.** Where the Terms in force name a keeper, or several, an act within the agreement counts for any answer about order (which Terms are in force, 5.5.3; the pin, 5.4.1, 5.5.1; whether an update is late, 5.5.4; whether an act under a power came before a replacement, 5.6.4) only once it is placed (§3.4), at the position the keepers give it, but for the pin of an update signed by every signer it needs (5.5.1(b)); where they are several, only where they agree (5.8.3). A verifier MUST take those answers from the keepers, qualified **per keeper**, with the keepers named. Where a new version names other keepers, or none, whether it has replaced the version in force is judged per the keepers the version it would replace names (5.5.5(d)). Before any Terms of the agreement are in force, the keepers are those its first Terms act names (5.4.1). Knots do not pin, or order the agreement's acts, in the keepers' place, but where 5.5.1(b) says so; they expose a keeper: a keeper's record that contradicts a knot the verifier holds (B placed before A, while A names B) gives **not shown** for the answers that rest on that record, with the reason that the keeper contradicts the acts, and the record is kept as evidence (5.5.4(c)).
- **5.8.5** **Whose keepers and clock count.** The keepers and the clock the Terms in force name are the ones every reader uses for that agreement, whether a party to it or not, except for a filling (below): a verifier MUST NOT give an answer of 5.8.4 for that agreement per another anchoring as the Terms' answer. A keeper or an anchoring of the reader's own choosing, or one named only by a rule the parties never signed, gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, and never the Terms' own (§3.4, 5.5.3). Terms MAY also name a `clock`. Where the Terms in force name one, what time it was, for that agreement, is answered per that clock by every reader, qualified **per clock**: for every answer that turns on a time, the ground of an update condition (5.5.7(c)) among them. For a filling, the keepers and the clock are those its own version names (the version its proof names), never those of the Terms in force: how long what a filling gave lasts is counted on that version's clock (5.4.3, 5.10.2, 5.10.8). What a clock is, and how it is identified, is for the layer that gives it.
- **5.8.6** **No stated anchor, no condition in time.** A condition in time with no anchor the Terms state is refused, not answered. Where the bytes show it, a verifier refuses it: Terms that do not supply what a cMIP they name, in any socket, declares it needs (a clock, a keeper) are **invalid** (§4.4, case 8). A keeper is not a clock: Terms that name a keeper and no clock do not supply a clock. A cMIP declares what it needs in its own published text, in the form Development fixes (§7), and a verifier reads it from that act; where the verifier lacks the cMIP, this rule is **unchecked** for it. Where only the words show it (the words speak of a time, and no cMIP the Terms name declares a need for a clock or a keeper), these grammar rules do not read the words (§5.2) and have no grammar rule for it: a verifier gives no answer of these grammar rules about that time. In either case, an anchoring of the reader's own choosing gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, and never the Terms' answer (5.8.5).

### 5.9 The settlement

- **5.9.1** **Settlement.** A Statement MAY name a `settlement` it is tied to: the evidence, from the layer that holds value, that a payment was made. This field is where that layer's proof plugs into Agreements; what a settlement is, is that layer's to say. It names an act and no cMIP, and is not a task. A settlement named is reported on the word of whoever gave it (the MICS, §2). What is done with it (counting only what led to a settlement, dividing what one shows) is a rule's, in whichever socket it sits (§6).

### 5.10 Spots, and the unknown party (the buyer who joins later)

- **5.10.1** A role in `parties` MUST name:
  - its sort (§3.2, **Spot**), one of: done (whoever proves they have done X), received (whoever proves they have received Y), holding (whoever proves they are holding Z), fits (whoever proves their identity fits the conditions in W), or future;
  - its `what`: the X, Y, Z or W, by fingerprint (§5.2), except for sort future (5.10.4);
  - its spot socket (task K5): the cMIP that says who fills it, by fingerprint, with its parameters. The role (its sort and its `what`) is that task's input, and not a task of its own.
- **5.10.2** A spot is filled, by a signer, when its K5 cMIP, applied to the acts held and the keepers' answers, names that signer on the strength of a **proof**: the acts that earn the spot. A spot MAY be filled many times; each filling is tied to the proof that earned it. Whether a spot holds one filling or many is said by its K5 cMIP. A verifier MUST answer each filling with its proof, each act reported by who signed it (the MICS, §2), qualified per the keepers its own version names, or as shown where that version names none: a filling's keepers and clock are those of its own version, never those of the Terms in force (5.8.5). For a filling yet to be earned, the role and its cMIP are read from the Terms in force; while a fork of Terms stands, from the Terms both branches replace (5.5.4(e)). A filling already earned is read from the version its proof names, its role and that role's cMIP as that version states them, whatever a later version says of the role (5.10.6): the spot is the one named by the version and the role's position among its parties that the question gives. That cMIP is given that version and its acts, and nothing a later version made. A filling's proof stands as the keepers of its own version showed it.
- **5.10.3** Each signer who fills a spot is a party in it from then on. They are bound only by their own assent (§5.6). Filling a spot does not bind any other party; what binds the known parties to the holder of a filling is the version its proof names (5.10.6).
- **5.10.4** A role of sort **future** names no `what`: its K5 cMIP defines who the participant is as well as how they are confirmed. It is for participants none of the four sorts can describe in advance. No verifier can check that a future spot was used only for them; whether it was is the authors' word, carried by them (§5.3). A `what` on a role of sort future is left unread, and a verifier says so (5.2.3).
- **5.10.5** **New identities.** Whether a spot still takes new identities is said by its K5 cMIP, applied to the version in force (the role as that version states it), the acts held, and the answers of the keepers and the clock the Terms in force name (5.8.5). These grammar rules define no act and no field for closing a spot or for opening it again: an offer stops taking new identities, or takes them again, by a new version (§5.5), or by what the version in force itself states in advance (a date, a count), as that cMIP reads them. The update condition is not asked for this answer: it is asked only of a proposed update (5.5.7). A verifier MUST give that answer with its cause, as the cMIP gives it, per keeper, per clock or as shown (§5.8, §5.13). A condition in time in that answer is answered per the clock the Terms in force name, and for a filling already earned per the clock its own version names (5.8.5, 5.10.2): an anchoring of the reader's own choosing never gives the Terms' answer (5.8.5, 5.8.6). Whatever that answer is, a filling already earned stays a filling, shown with its proof (5.10.2), and keeps the rights of the version it was earned under (5.10.7). Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6).
- **5.10.6** **No update reaches a filling.** For an unknown party's filling (§3.2), no later update exists; each later version exists for the agreement. No update of the known parties reaches a filling in any way, and no power a version carries is used for its holder but one they gave in an act of their own (5.10.7): its holder has no say in the agreement's updates (§3.2, 5.5.5(a)), and no update binds them (5.6.1(c)). What exists for the holder is the version the filling's proof names (5.10.7): that version binds the holder, by the assent that earned the filling (5.4.3), and binds the known parties to the holder, for as long as its words say, on the clock that version names (5.10.8), whether or not a later version is in force (5.4.3). A verifier MUST NOT read any update, or any update condition's answer, as reaching a filling (5.5.7).
- **5.10.7** **Rights.** A filling has exactly the rights offered by the version it was earned under, with every condition that version puts on them. The version a filling was earned under is the version its proof names (§5.13), as the spot's cMIP gives it with the filling (K5's row, §6). What the rights and their conditions are is that version's (§5.2, task K1); these grammar rules do not read them. Nothing stops those rights but what that version's own words say (5.10.8). Nothing changes what a filling earned except an assent its holder signs in their own name (5.6.1(a)), whether or not it is counted for the later version, or one made for them under a power they gave in an act of their own (5.6.1(b), 5.6.5(f)). An assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power: a power the version carries is given by its known parties and binds only them. A verifier MUST name, with each filling, the version it was earned under, and each later version that changes what it earned, with the assent that rests on: the holder's own, or the one made for them under a power they gave, with that power named.
- **5.10.8** **How long, and no trigger.** What a filling gave lasts as long as the words of the version it was earned under say, measured on the clock that version names, from the anchoring of the purchase; where the version names no clock, no term in time can be read from it, and a verifier shows nothing about that term (5.8.6). The cMIP that reads those words (task K1), given that version and its acts and nothing a later version made, answers whether what the filling gave still applies; a verifier shows that answer as that cMIP's, with the ground it gives, qualified **per clock**, and gives none of its own; where it lacks that cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). No update ends what a filling gave, and none is needed (5.5.7(f)). The update condition is read in one place (5.5.7), and is never asked about a filling. No condition triggers: these grammar rules have no answer **triggered**, and a verifier MUST NOT give one. What a version offers changes only through an update (§5.5); what a filling earned changes only as 5.10.7 says. Whether a spot still takes new identities under the version in force is 5.10.5's. Nothing stops a later version: one made as §5.5 says MAY offer again, and MAY rest what it offers on values already in the chain, named by fingerprint (§5.2, §5.13), or on none. Who fills a spot under it is said by its own K5 cMIP (5.10.2), and a filling under it has the rights that version offers (5.10.7).

### 5.11 Legs of a sequence

- **5.11.1** **Sequence, and leg.** A sequence, a leg, and a leg's doer and receiving side are as §3.3 defines them (**Sequence**, **Leg**, **Doer**, **receiving side**). What one agreement gives or owes reaches another through a party named as an agreement (5.1.1) or a receiver that is an agreement (5.1.3); a payment to an agreement, or a payout to one, is a leg whose receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (5.1.4); whoever pays out a payout divided among several is a service, or a party who receives for the others. Each leg is carried out under the Terms that hold its task (5.11.2), or, for a payment, under the offer's Terms the buyer signed, which require it, the payment itself holding the task of paying and its call (5.11.7); the cMIPs that read it are the ones those Terms name (§5.2). Nothing in one agreement's choice of cMIPs binds another agreement, and nothing is chosen for a sequence as a whole. The agreements of a sequence MAY name the same cMIPs; none is required to.
- **5.11.2** **The call.** Terms hold the Control task (K6) where their field `control` names a cMIP (§5.2); or, with that socket empty, where they name as `receiver` one of their own parties (5.1.3) and give a part to any other party, so that this party holds for the others and passing on to them is its obligation under those Terms. Whether Terms give a part to a party is read from their words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1): a share for any party other than the receiver. Where the verifier cannot read the body, or lacks the cMIP in `control`, whether those Terms hold the task is **unchecked** (§3.6). Terms that hold the task MAY carry a call for it, in `calls`, as the report block of the shared language gives it (the MICS, §2): from whom a report is expected (the doer, the receiving side, or both). A call names no keeper and no reader (§3.1). For K6, the doer is whoever carried out the passing on (the controller: a service, or a party that receives for the others): the party those Terms name as `receiver` among their own parties. Where the receiver those Terms name is not one of their own parties (another agreement, or none), no doer is shown under those Terms: what reaches them is passed on under the Terms of the agreement it reaches (5.1.4). And the receiving side of each leg is whoever something was passed on to; where that is an agreement, it is that agreement's receiver for the act (5.1.4), and no other of its parties. The call is made by the signers of those Terms, and binds no one else. In a deal, those signers are the service and the agreement it serves, which signs by its own signature (5.1.2): the call is that agreement's parties' as much as the service's, and each party that signature binds has signed the deal through its agreement. Whoever receives is the receiving side of its leg whether or not it signed the Terms that carry the call. Where it did not, itself or through an agreement's signature, these grammar rules do not make the path to it a signed one: closing that gap is a cMIP's (§6). A call is attached to a task, in the Terms under which that task is carried out; none is attached to a receiving side. A call in Terms that do not hold the task it is attached to is left unread, and the verifier says so (5.2.3). These grammar rules make no Terms carry a call, and make no one answer one. A call is part of signed Terms and is read as they are (§5.2, 5.5.3), by anyone, before any payment is made.
- **5.11.3** **Leg report.** A leg report is a report that answers such a call. As every report does, it carries the call it answers and the acts it relied on, each by fingerprint, and its signer. Among the acts it relied on are the version of the Terms it passed on by (§5.13) and each settlement it speaks of (5.9.1). Beyond that, it carries what K6's row says (§6). Each signer reports its own half only. What K6's row asks of a leg report MUST be readable without any cMIP those Terms name. Where one doer carries out several legs under one call (a payout to several), which lines of its report each receiving side receives (all of them, or those of its own leg) is said by the cMIP that answers the task (K6). These grammar rules do not say it. How a leg report is read is the MICS's report block's (§2).
- **5.11.4** **Before paying.** Asked which agreements of a sequence carry a call for reports, a verifier answers from their Terms alone (5.11.2), one agreement at a time, per keeper or as shown (5.5.3), and **unchecked** where it lacks them. It answers in the same way, from the same Terms, from whom each call expects reports, and whether those Terms name a data cMIP, in `data` (task K8), by which their signers agreed to a path beyond the leg (5.11.5): **shown**, with its fingerprint, where they name one; where `data` is empty, it answers that no path beyond the leg is agreed in those Terms. For an offer, that answer is also the path a buyer agrees to for their own leg by signing its Terms (5.11.6). The agreements of a sequence, for this question, are found from the agreement asked about by following `receiver` from agreement to agreement, until one names one of its own parties, or none (5.1.4). Parties named as agreements are not walked: which of them a payment reaches is their own Terms' and cMIPs' to say.
- **5.11.5** **Reports are data: a path beyond one leg.** A report is data. A path by which a report, or what it states, reaches anyone who is not a side of its leg (a partner seeing further, a request from upstream) is a path the people whose data it carries agreed to, with their signature. It is built as a cMIP about data, focused on agreements, which Terms they signed name by fingerprint (§5.2). These grammar rules give no such path themselves: no call, no field and no answer of this text carries a report beyond its leg, and a verifier MUST NOT read any as doing so. What such a cMIP says is its own: who may read what, how a reader puts together the legs it may read, how far it follows, and what a loop answers. **The data task.** Terms name such a cMIP in the field `data`: the task K8 (§6). Its one question, asked for one reader and one report of a leg carried out under those Terms: may this reader read it, or what it states, beyond its leg? It answers yes or no. These grammar rules take that output and add nothing to it. The socket speaks for the people bound by the Terms that name it (5.6.1), and for no one else. Where it is empty the answer is no: one leg. Where a report carries the data of people bound by different Terms (the two sides of a leg), the data cMIP of each is asked, and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes. **Which Terms are asked.** About one report: the Terms that carry the call it answers; and, for each agreement a line of the report names as passed on to or owed to, that agreement's Terms in force (5.5.3). A receiving side named as a signer of the Terms that carry the call is asked through those Terms. Where Terms that are to be asked are not held, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). What a data cMIP is beyond that socket is not said here.
- **5.11.6** **Who has agreed.** A verifier MUST NOT read the people of a leg as having agreed to be read from beyond their leg unless Terms they signed say so (5.6.1): by naming such a data cMIP in `data` (K8), or by a rule in them under which others decide it. Which consent a data cMIP asks of the people it reads is that cMIP's to say. Which cMIP, and which rule, apply is what the signers chose when they made the agreement, and is read from the Terms they signed (§5.2). Where those Terms say that a majority decides, the majority decides (5.6.1(b)): a verifier reads a member who was outvoted as that rule says, and MUST NOT answer, beyond it, whether that member agreed. **What the buyer agreed to.** A buyer signs Terms: those of an offer, by an assent to the version they buy under, and paying is what those Terms require of them (5.11.7). So a buyer is bound by that version by their own assent (5.6.1(a), 5.10.3), its data socket included. For a report of the buyer's leg (a confirmation of a payment), the Terms asked are the version the payment names (§5.13), where an assent of the payer to that version is held: its data cMIP speaks for the buyer, and for the receiving side where the receiver for the payment is one of that version's own parties (5.1.4). Where the receiving side is reached through another agreement (5.1.4), that agreement's Terms in force are asked too, as for any report that carries the data of people bound by different Terms (5.11.5), and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes. Where the version's `data` is empty, the answer is no: one leg. Where no assent of the payer to the version its payment names is held, the payer is not shown as bound by those Terms, nothing they signed says that they agreed to be read from beyond their leg, and the answer is no for every reader who is not a side of that leg.
- **5.11.7** **The buyer's leg.** Paying an agreement is a leg (5.11.1). Its doer is the buyer: a signer of the offer's Terms, whose assent names the version they buy under, and whom those Terms require to pay (5.11.6). Paying is the consequence of the Terms the buyer signed, as for any signer of any Terms; the buyer differs only in that their identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed it (§3.2, **Offer**). The buyer also signs the payment (§5.13), which carries out what those Terms require. Its receiving side is the receiver, for that payment, of the agreement paid (5.1.4). A payment is the act that holds the task of paying: the Control task (K6), with the payer as its doer. The payer, its signer, may call for the receiving side's confirmation, as the signer of any act that holds a task may (the MICS's report block, §2). These grammar rules read every payment as carrying that call: the buyer's confirmation by default is that call, made by default, and no Terms need write it. The confirmation is a report that answers it. A report names the call it answers by naming the act that holds it; for this call, made by default, it names the act that stands for it: the settlement the payment names, the act both sides can see, and never the payment itself. A report whose call names that settlement is the confirmation of that payment. The payment is the doer's half of that leg: its signer is the buyer, and it names the settlement. The confirmation is the receiving side's report, its signer's word (the MICS, §2). How a payment and its confirmation are read together is the MICS's report block's (§2). After the confirmation comes access to what was bought. What that is, and who gives it, is said by the version the payment names and its cMIPs (5.10.7, §5.13; delivery, §6); these grammar rules do not read it. By default the buyer is shown nothing further. What the receiving side then does with the payment is further legs, and their reports go to the two sides of each (the MICS, §2). The buyer's own leg is read beyond it only as the data cMIP of the offer's Terms the buyer signed gives (5.11.6); the buyer reads a further leg only as a data cMIP the people of that leg agreed to gives (5.11.5). The call binds only its signer, the payer: these grammar rules make no one confirm (5.11.2).
- **5.11.8** **The contest: an act, made against an offer or a vow.** A contest is an act of these grammar rules: a Statement of kind `contest`. It is made against an offer or a vow (§3.1, **Vow**; §3.2, **Offer**): its `agreement` names the root of that offer or vow, and its `terms` names the version it is made against. Whom it reaches follows from what it is made against: the signers of that offer or vow (one level, 5.1.2), and no one else. A contest made against a vow is read exactly as one made against an offer. A verifier reads a contest made against any version alike: whether that version names a party by role is shown beside it, and changes no answer. The signers a contest reaches are the parties that version names as signers; a signer who fills a role is not counted among them. Its `terms` names Terms shown as a version, in force or not, or a tip set aside (then the contest is shown as **disputed**, §5.12); otherwise the contest is **not shown**. A contest is its signer's word, reported as such (the MICS, §2), and stands in its signer's own line (§5.7). **A buyer's contest** (§3.1, **Contest**) is made against the offer or vow the buyer bought under, names the version the payment read (§5.13), and names in `settlement` the settlement the payment names, the act both sides can see (5.9.1), and never the payment itself. These grammar rules read a buyer's contest for that case only. A verifier shows a contest as a buyer's where it holds a payment by the same signer that names the same settlement and the same version; where it holds no such payment, whether the contest is a buyer's is **unchecked** (§3.6). That it was not delivered is the buyer's word (the MICS, §2). A verifier MUST NOT read a contest as contesting anyone but the signers of what it is made against: not a service those signers picked, not a deal of theirs, not any other agreement of the sequence. It MUST NOT read a contest as giving its signer sight of any leg (5.11.5, 5.11.7). Where those signers believe the fault is their service's, they contest the service: a contest of their own, made within the deal they signed with it (5.1.2), which reaches the service and is read no further. Whether such a deal, signed by parties who were known, is an offer or a vow is not said. A baker may contest Terms for another envelope of the same work (5.11.9). Who else may make a contest, and when, is not said. What a contest does beyond standing on the record, who answers it and how, and how money comes back, are not these grammar rules' (§6).
- **5.11.9** **A baker may contest.** The holder of the baker's rights in an envelope, as the envelope's chain names them (Envelopes, §2), MAY make a contest (5.11.8) against Terms that name another envelope of the same work. It reaches the signers of those Terms, is its signer's word (the MICS, §2), and stands on the record. The holder of the baker's rights is read at the act the contest cites: a contest names an envelope, whose baker's rights its signer holds, and the act of that envelope's chain it relies on (5.13.2), and the holder is read at that act of the envelope the contest names, not from the contested Terms. A contest by a signer who is not the holder at that act is shown as a contest, and **not shown** as a baker's. Who holds the baker's rights, and what follows from them, are Envelopes' (§2). That its signer is the one who made the work is not shown by any act.

### 5.12 Forks

- **5.12.1** **A fork is a fork.** Where two acts compete to change the same state, with no order shown between them, these grammar rules know one thing: the fork (§3.3). Where a fork exists there may be a race between its sides, or it may be resolved among all involved; these grammar rules do not say which, and read both alike. Neither act takes effect, and the earlier state stays, until the fork is resolved by a new act, made in an order that can be told, as the agreement's own update condition asks or under a power to resolve given in advance (5.12.4, 5.12.5). A verifier answers **fork**, and MUST NOT choose between its sides. How forks are treated in different cases is not these grammar rules': it is the cMIPs' that the parties chose, and the parties' own.
- **5.12.2** **Lines, updates and forks.** A line, an update, a fork, and a fork's shared act, tips and branches, are as §3.3 defines them (**Line**, **Update**, **Fork**): for one signer's line, by `previous` (5.7.1); for an agreement's Terms, by `replaces` (§5.5), the gate deciding which signatures an update needs (5.5.1(a), 5.5.5, 5.5.7); for Terms, a late update is no update of the version it names (5.5.4). Only one act may follow each act of a line: where an act has one update, that update is the next act of the line. Two acts of one signer that both name no `previous` are a fork with no shared act. These grammar rules see a fork of a line only where each of the two acts was signed by all the parties that needed to sign it.
- **5.12.3** **Both tips stop.** While a fork stands, neither tip counts as the next act of its line, and nothing built on either tip counts: no act that follows a tip in the line, and no act that rests on a tip or on an act built on it (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), however many namings lie between. **One thing an assent to a tip still counts for.** A fork of Terms is two updates each signed by all who needed to sign, and the signing is the assents. So an assent to a tip, among the assents that count (5.5.7(a)), counts for whether that tip was signed, and for nothing else: it binds no one while the fork stands, since the Terms it names are not in force (5.4.3). Every other act that rests on a tip (through `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or as the version a payment read) counts for nothing while the fork stands; a payment that names a tip is read as 5.1.4 says. The shared act stays the last act of the line that counts: for Terms, the last shared version stays in force (5.5.4(e)). A verifier MUST show both tips, and what it holds that is built on them, with the fork; MUST answer **fork** where 5.5.4 and 5.7.2 say; and MUST NOT choose between the tips, by position or in any other way. A fork stands until 5.12.4 resolves it. Terms that were in force before a fork was made stay in force: Terms stop only by replacement (5.5.2). A fork of a signer's line made after an update is in force does not reach back: the update stays in force, and the fork is that signer's, resolved in that signer's line (5.7.2).
- **5.12.4** **Resolving update.** A fork of a line is resolved by an update of the shared act: an act of the same line whose `previous`, or for Terms whose `replaces`, names the shared act; which names every tip in `resolves`, by the list of the tips' ids (§4.2, §4.3); and which names one of them in `continues`. It resolves the fork only when it is signed as an update of the shared act must be: for one signer's line, by that signer (§5.7); for Terms, so that the update condition of the shared Terms answers valid for it, which says who must sign it; by default, the known parties (5.5.5(a), 5.5.7(g)); or under a power to resolve (5.12.5). A resolving update of Terms that resolves is an update of the shared Terms and takes an update's answers: **valid**, by their update condition or its default, and **in force** once pinned (§5.5); one signed under a power to resolve is answered as 5.12.5 says. A resolving update in one signer's line that resolves is answered **shown**, naming this rule. The tip it names in `continues` then counts as the next act of the line, what is built on that tip counts as these grammar rules read it, and the line goes on from that branch. Every other tip is set aside (§3.3, **Set aside**), and no act that follows it in the line counts. A resolving update is not a tip of the fork it resolves, and carries no words of its own: it carries only what §4.3 says it carries. An act that names `resolves` and no `continues` resolves nothing. An act that names `resolves` and does not in fact resolve the fork it sits in (it is not signed as this rule asks, or does not name every tip, or there is no fork: what it names in `resolves` is one update and no other) is an act like any other, and may itself be a tip: a fork is not hidden by adding the field. Where what it names in `resolves` are not the tips of a fork in its own line (for a Statement of kind `resolve`, its signer's line; for a Terms act, the agreement's chain), a verifier answers **not shown** for it as a resolving update, with the reason: nothing to resolve; where it fails otherwise, **not shown**, with the reason it fails (not signed as this rule asks, or not naming every tip). Where such an act names the shared act as the one before it, it is an update of that act beside the one it names, and the two are a fork. A resolving update MUST name every tip, and MAY name more acts than the tips. Where the two tips of a signer's line both name no `previous`, a resolving update that also names none resolves them. **After a fork is resolved.** An act that names the resolving update as the one before it (in `previous`, or for Terms in `replaces`) is read as following the last act of the continued branch. So the next act MAY name either. But two acts, one that names the resolving update and one that names the last act of the continued branch, are two updates of the same act, and a fork. For Terms the same holds: a version whose `replaces` names a resolving update replaces the last version of the continued branch, and the update condition asked of it is that version's (5.5.7(e)). These grammar rules give the form of a resolving update for a fork of a line only.
- **5.12.5** **A power to resolve.** Terms MAY give, in advance, a power (§5.6) to sign the resolving update of a fork: an entry in `powers` whose rule (task K3) says who may sign it, and which resolving update it accepts. A resolving update signed under such a power carries the signatures 5.12.4 asks for, and resolves the fork as 5.12.4 says. For a fork of Terms, the power is read from the Terms both tips replace, which stay in force (5.5.4(e)); it is an entry of `powers` of sort `resolves`, and of no other sort. A power to resolve is part of the judicial: it is set in the Terms, and it is given, changed or removed only by an update the update condition answers valid, never by a power (5.6.5). For a fork in one signer's line too, it is an entry of `powers` of sort `resolves`, in Terms by which that signer is bound: a power to make that act for the signer (5.6.1(b)): the resolving update then stands in the line of whoever signed it, its `previous` is that signer's own, its `for` names the signer whose line forked, and it resolves by naming the tips. That resolving update is a Statement of kind `resolve` (§4.3): a power of sort `resolves` is for Statements of that kind in a signer's line, and for no Statement of another kind. How the fork is resolved is the power's rule's: it may accept only the resolving update it computes from the acts and the keepers' answers, whoever signs it, or accept the one a named person signs (a judge, named in those Terms: 5.6.5; §6), or anything else; these grammar rules force no single way. A verifier MUST report such a resolving update with the power it was signed under and whoever it rests on (5.6.1; the MICS, §2). Its answer about it is **shown**, naming this rule, the power and its rule's answer, and the signer: the update condition is not asked of it (5.5.7(g)), so it is neither valid nor not valid.
- **5.12.6** **What others relied on stands.** An act signed by someone who signed none of the acts of a branch whose tip is set aside, and that rests on an act of that branch (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), stands: it has the effect these grammar rules gave it, or would have given it had that tip stood alone. A payment that names a version of that branch as the one it read (5.13.2) is such an act. A verifier MUST show that act, and every answer resting on it, as **disputed**. "The acts of a branch" are the acts of the line: for Terms, the Terms acts; whoever signed one of them is not protected here for what they built on it. What stands differs by act. A payment that names a version of that branch is read as if the tip had stood alone: its receiver is read from that version (5.1.4), and both are shown as disputed. **As if the tip had stood alone** means: the line is read with every other tip of that fork, every act built on them, and the resolving update, left out. A version further along that branch is then a version where it would have been one, and a payment that names it is read against it. **A filling earned within that branch** by a proof made by someone who signed none of its Terms acts stands in the same way: it is shown as **disputed**, with the version of that branch it was earned under named; no version of the continued line is named as a later version that changes its rights (5.10.7). An assent, or another Statement made within that branch, by someone who signed none of its Terms acts, stands as an act, shown as disputed, and binds no one: the Terms it names never counted as a version, and an assent binds only while the Terms it names are in force (5.4.3).
- **5.12.7** A fork that no resolving update has resolved stands: of Terms, as 5.5.4 says; in one signer's line, as 5.7.2 says.
- **5.12.8** **Rival resolving updates.** Two resolving updates of the same fork, each signed as 5.12.4 asks, that continue the same tip resolve it, and are no fork. A resolving update of a fork of Terms is an update of the shared Terms (5.5.7(g)): one that is shown, as 5.5.4 says, to have come after another had resolved the fork is a late update, and resolves nothing (5.5.4). Two that continue different tips are themselves a fork: two updates of the same shared act. Neither resolves the first fork while the other stands: every tip stays stopped (5.12.3), and the shared act stays the last act of the line that counts. That fork is resolved the same way: by a resolving update that names the two rival ones in `resolves` and one of them in `continues`, signed as 5.12.4 asks or under a power given in advance (5.12.5).
- **5.12.9** **A client's duty (client conformance).** A client that holds both tips of a fork MUST NOT show either tip, or anything built on either, as a resolved act: not as the next version, not as in force, not as that signer's latest act. It shows the fork as unresolved, until 5.12.4 resolves it. This is a duty of clients and not a grammar rule a verifier applies. No verifier can check it: what a client showed a person leaves no act. It is written here and nowhere else.

### 5.13 Knots: what an act names, and "shown after"

- **5.13.1** **What an act is read against.** An act is read against the versions and acts it names, in any field that holds a fingerprint (`body`, `terms`, `under`, `replaces`, `agreement`, `previous`, `answers`, `resolves`, `continues`, `relies_on`, `settlement`, `envelope`, `envelope_at`, the layer below's `prev` and `refs`, and any other), the body's field included. Each such naming is a knot (§3.4): any fingerprint in any field of any act the verifier holds, whatever layer either act belongs to; a verifier reads the fields of every layer of the core (§2). A verifier reads knots in fields, never in an act's content. A report is read in the same way, against the call and the acts it names (5.11.3). A version that replaces one an act names does not change what these grammar rules read that act as having done. Whether the act's signer is bound from then on is 5.4.3 and §5.6, unchanged.
- **5.13.2** **An act names what it relied on.** An act that relied on a version or an act, of its own agreement or of another, names it: a Statement in `relies_on` where no other field names it, a Terms act in `relies_on` (§5.2). A verifier MUST NOT read an act as having relied on a version or act it does not name. A payment to a work names the version of the work agreement it read; how a settlement carries that name is for the layer that gives the settlement (Money, §2).
- **5.13.3** **Shown after.** Act B is shown after act A when the verifier holds acts, each naming the one before by fingerprint, that lead from B back to A, or a keeper's record or an anchoring that places them so (5.13.5). Here an anchoring is one to the clock the Terms name, and a keeper's record is one of a keeper the Terms name. Any other anchoring or keeper is evidence a reader may show, labelled as that reader's: it never pins, and never makes an update late (5.5.3, 5.13.5). Knots between acts (a fingerprint of one act in a field of another) count wherever the verifier holds them. This is the one reading of "shown after" in this text, and every grammar rule that asks whether one act came after another reads it so: the pin (5.4.1, 5.5.1(b)), a counter signature (5.4.5), a late update (5.5.4), an act under a power whose Terms were replaced (5.6.4), and a signer's line (§5.7). It proves that B came after A, and nothing more: never that an act came before one that does not name it, and never that an act does not exist. Where the Terms name keepers, the order of the agreement's acts is theirs: only they pin (5.5.1(b)), and knots do not order the agreement's acts in their place; knots expose a keeper whose record contradicts them (5.8.4). **A missing act.** Where the acts that could show B after A need an act that is named in a field of an act held, and the verifier does not hold it, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6): never a yes, and never a no. No grammar rule goes around it.
- **5.13.4** **Order from naming.** An act that names another, directly or through acts that name it, came after it. A verifier MAY give that order as **shown**, from the acts alone, with no keeper and no qualifier. Between two acts of which neither so names the other, no order is shown from the acts alone. **Before or after Terms came into force, from naming.** Where the Terms name no keeper: an act came after Terms came into force where the acts it is shown after are enough for those Terms to pass the gate and the pin (5.5.1), or, for a first version, to come into force and be pinned (5.4.1). It came before where, counting every act held but those shown after it, those Terms do not pass them. Otherwise no order between them is shown. **Per keeper**, the position at which Terms came into force is the first position at which, counting only the acts the keepers show at or before it, they pass the gate and the pin (5.4.1, 5.5.1, 5.6.4).
- **5.13.5** **Anchoring.** Whether anything is later than an act, and what time it was, are never shown by naming. For an agreement whose Terms in force name keepers, every reader takes "nothing later" from those keepers; for one whose Terms in force name a clock, every reader takes the time from that clock (5.8.5). A verifier MUST NOT give, per a reader's own anchoring, an answer that the keepers or the clock the agreement names give. An anchoring of the reader's own choosing, whether or not the agreement names any, gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, given beside the Terms' answer and never in its place: it never gives the Terms' own answer, never puts Terms in force, and never makes an update late (5.5.3, 5.8.5). Where the reader accepts an anchoring from which the verifier holds no answer, or holds two that contradict each other, every answer that rests on it is **unchecked** (§3.6), and not **as shown**. A verifier MUST give each such answer **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (**per keeper** or **per clock** where it is one the Terms name), or **as shown**.

What else the cMIP says (how a reader puts together the legs it may read, how far it follows, what a loop answers, which consent it asks) is its own, and is not read by these grammar rules (5.11.5) | 5.11.4, 5.11.5, 5.11.6, 5.11.7; the MICS (§2). Empty: no path beyond the leg | none where the cMIP only computes; otherwise whoever its modules name |

A payment holds the task of paying, the Control task (K6), with the payer as its doer (5.11.7). Which field of a payment holds it, and what fills it, is the layer that holds value's to say (Money, §2).

### 6.1 Modules: what a cMIP in a socket may lean on

A cMIP in a socket may use modules to give its answer. A module is a way of answering, not a task: no field of an act names it; the cMIP in a socket does (§3.1, **Module**). Each says whom an answer makes you trust, and a verifier reports that with every answer (the MICS, §2).

| Module | What it answers | Whom it makes you trust |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule** | what follows from these acts: yes or no, a list of who, or amounts; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone | none: anyone can recompute |
| **Identity** | who is behind this signer: signed statements by those who vouch | a witness (Sign layer) |
| **Assurance** | what was done to make it likely that a party was able, free and understood: signed statements of what was done; never a pass | a witness |
| **Witness** | what happened in the world: each named witness's signed statement | the witness |
| **Tally** | what a vote decided, without showing who voted how: the result, with whatever proof the method allows | the teller |
| **Delivery** | whether an act reached a person: the recipient's signed acknowledgment, or the Deliver layer's evidence | the recipient, or the Deliver layer |
| **Judge** | what the named judge decided, given the Terms (with `authors`, 5.3.2) and the acts of the dispute: a signed decision, which binds only through a power (K3, §5.6). The judge is named in the Terms, by the cMIP in a socket or its parameters, and is changed only by an update, never by a power (5.6.5) | the judge |

Two more things answer questions and are not sockets of an act.

- **The keeper** (`keeper`) and **the clock** (`clock`) are named by Terms by an identifier, not as cMIPs. Their answers are another layer's (§5.8), and are given to every cMIP in a socket. Whether a clock is itself a module under a cMIP of another layer is that layer's to say.
- **Standing**: what one signer's acts show about them, to anyone who looks. A reader chooses the cMIP; no act names it, so it is no task of an act. It computes from signed acts and keepers' answers only, qualified per keeper or as shown; anyone can recompute it, and nobody keeps it.

## 7. Open technical parameters

The values these grammar rules depend on and this text does not fix.

- **The set of acts: its hash and its label** (§3.1, §4.1). Fixed by the encoding (encoding 3).
- **Which fields take the set form** (§4.1). Whether any field of §4.2 or §4.3 holds a set of acts as one value; in this text none does.
- **The empty set** (§3.1, §4.1). How its value is written, and where a field or a parameter may hold it.
- **How Terms name their clock** (§4.2, `clock`; 5.5.4(b), 5.8.5). What identifies a clock, and whether the `clock` field stands as written.
- **How a cMIP declares what it needs** (5.8.6, §6). The form in which a cMIP's own published text states that it needs a clock or a keeper: Development's.
- **How far a verifier follows** (5.1.4). How many agreements a receiver is followed through before a verifier stops and answers **unchecked**, saying how far it followed.
