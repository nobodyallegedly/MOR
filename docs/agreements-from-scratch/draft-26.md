# Agreements, draft 26

---

## 0. Introduction

**Agreements is not to be used bare.**

It is not one unified way of agreeing. It offers the basic sockets from which rule books are drafted: more a language, used to decide how to agree, than a set of rules for agreeing. The rules people agree by are written in the cMIPs built on it.

Among what it offers is the keeper: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where its acts are. Terms may name one keeper or several, and a socket beside them says at how many an act must be.

An agreement has no versions. It lives until its ending condition is met; any change is that end and a new agreement, whose first act may say that the old one is no longer valid and that it once existed.

An agreement has two classes of participant, not two situations of one: its known parties, and its unknown parties, who join through a spot. Each class has its own rules, and its own protection (§3.2). Agreements protects those who take part through a spot: each is bound by the Terms they accepted, and no later act reaches them (§5.10).

Knots are a concept of all of MOR, not of Agreements alone. Their importance in agreements is taken up where this text treats relying on acts (§5.13).

---

## 1. Purpose

Agreements is a grammar: the acts in which agreeing is written (Terms and Statement); the grammar rules by which a verifier decides, from acts alone, **who is bound by which Terms, and what is shown, unknown or contradicted**; and the tasks that cMIPs fill so that people can agree on anything, by rules they choose. What an agreement does is always a cMIP's answer; the grammar keeps what lies beneath every cMIP (§6). Its central idea is that an agreement can be a party to another agreement (§5.1): works, services, deals and collectives are built with it, and none is a separate thing in the grammar. Agreements does not decide what anyone must agree, and it does not decide whether anything happened in the world: it records who said so. It does not define how words are fixed, how signatures work, how acts travel, how a shared order is kept, or how value is held; it names those operations (State, Sign, Deliver, Order, Control) where it needs them.

**One constraint on everything below.** Every grammar rule is checked from copies of acts alone, plus what the keepers the Terms name hold, where §5.8 applies. No grammar rule needs a particular server, platform or software author to remain for an agreement to be left: its parties may end it by a new agreement whose closing pointer meets its ending condition, wherever the new one is kept (§5.5); those who take part through a spot do not go on with it, and stay bound by their Terms (§5.10). What an act fixes must still be there and unaltered when needed, and an outsider holding copies must be able to test it: Agreements relies on both and adds no grammar rule for them.

## 3. Definitions

Each word here means one thing throughout this text. Where a word is defined in another text and only used here, that is said. A definition says what a word means and where the grammar rules that use it are; the grammar rules are in §4 to §7.

### 3.1 The acts and their parts

- **Act**: a signed statement in the core's sense. Published or signed again, never changed: every act is a new act. The fields the core gives every act (who signed, the signature, the act's own fingerprint) are not repeated here.
- **Fingerprint**: the identifier the core gives to fixed bytes. "Named by fingerprint" means referred to by that identifier and nothing else. An act's own fingerprint is its **act id**.
- **Grammar rule**: one of the validity rules of §5. It says how acts are read. It never says what people must agree. Always called a grammar rule; the word "rule" alone is never used for one.
- **Task**, or **socket**: a field of an act where a cMIP plugs in and answers one question. One cMIP per socket, with as many modules as it needs; one cMIP may fill several sockets. Every field of an act that names a cMIP is a task, and nothing else is. A task says its one question, what the cMIP in it accepts, what it produces, and whom it makes you trust. The nine tasks are K1 to K9 (§6). The same question asked in two fields is two tasks: the rule in `in_force` and the rule of a power are not one task. These grammar rules take a task's output and use it where they need it; they never compute it themselves. A socket holds at most one cMIP, with its parameters. A socket left empty has the default these grammar rules give it, where they give one, and otherwise asks nothing.
- **Module**: a part a cMIP uses to give its answer: a rule that computes, a witness, a judge, a teller and the like (§6). A cMIP may use several. A module is not a task: no field of an act names it; the cMIP in a socket does.
- **cMIP**: a document, named by fingerprint, that fills a socket: it answers that task's question, for some thing or situation. Anyone may write one. Many cMIPs may fit the same socket in different ways (one for land, another for shares). One cMIP may fill several sockets, of one act or of many; it is then asked each socket's question apart. Choosing a cMIP is how people choose their own rules.
- **Rule**: a cMIP, or a module of one, that computes: named by fingerprint, with its parameters; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone. It is the people's own rule. The word alone always means this, and never a grammar rule. It computes; what it leans on is named by its modules (§6.1). A rule is not a task: it sits in a socket (`in_force`, a power, `condition`), or is a module of the cMIP that does (§6).
- **Terms**: an act that states what is agreed, signed by an author of it (§5.3). Always capitalised and singular ("a Terms act", "these Terms"). Terms are the record of an agreement. The act that states them is its signer's assent to them, and no one else's (§4.3, **An author's first act**).
- **Agreement**: one Terms act, and every Statement made within it: every Statement whose `terms` names it. Those are the agreement's acts, and no others are. Its identifier is the fingerprint of its Terms act (the **root**). An agreement has one Terms act and no versions: nothing replaces its Terms (§5.5).
- **Statement**: any other act made within an agreement. Its **kind** says what it is (task K7). Four kinds are defined here: **assent**, **counter signature**, **contest** and **resolve**, the last the act that resolves a fork and nothing more (§4.3, §5.12).
- **Assent**: a Statement by which its signer agrees to one Terms act. A Terms act is also counted as its signer's assent to it (§4.3, **An author's first act**).
- **Counter signature**: a Statement by which an earlier assent to one Terms act is cancelled: its signer's own, or one made in the same party's name. It is available only for as long as §5.4 allows. It sets nothing aside: the assent it cancels stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled (§5.6).
- **Signature of an agreement**: an act made in an agreement's name, under a power its own Terms give (§5.1). An act signed so is signed by that agreement.
- **The words** (of Terms): the Text act, public or private, that carries what Terms say in words, and that the Terms name, in `body`, by its act id. The words are an act of the Text layer, made before the Terms that name them (§5.2). The grammar does not read them; the body's cMIP does (task K1).
- **The set of acts**: one value that names a set of acts: computed from their act ids, sorted, with repeats dropped, hashed with a label for a set of acts (§4.1). The same acts give the same value, whoever writes it. It says nothing of whether the set is complete, what it means, or when it was read. The empty set has the label and no members. The value names no act a verifier can read without the list it was computed from, so it is no knot by itself.
- **Report**, **call**: words of MOR's shared language, whose block is the MICS's (§2); used here, not defined. In short: a task may carry a **call** for a report, or not; the call says whether a report is wanted and from whom; only the signers of the act that holds the task can make it. A call names no keeper and no reader: the where is implied by the leg. A **report** is a signed act that answers a call: it carries the call it answers, the acts it relied on, and its signer. Anything more is the task's to say. A report goes to the other side of its own leg, where that side receives, and no further, in every layer (the MICS). A report is data: any other path for it is one people agreed to, with their signature, by naming a data cMIP in Terms they signed (§5.11; task K8).
- **Leg report**: a report that answers a call carried by Terms that hold the Control task (K6), about the legs carried out under those Terms (§5.11). What it carries beyond what every report carries is in K6's row (§6).
- **Payment**: an act of the layer that holds value (Money), signed by the payer, which names the Terms it read (§5.13). It carries out what the offer's Terms the buyer signed require of the buyer (§5.11). It holds the task of paying, and by default carries the payer's call for the receiving side's confirmation (§5.11). This text gives it no fields.
- **Settlement**: the signed evidence, from the layer that holds value (Money), that a payment was made. Agreements names it and does not define it. The Statement field `settlement` is where it plugs in (§5.9). That field names an act and no cMIP, so it is not a task.
- **Vow**: the same thing as an offer, without bytes attached. This text gives the word no more meaning than that. These grammar rules read a contest made against a vow exactly as one made against an offer, so no answer of a verifier turns on which of the two an agreement is.
- **Contest**: an act of the grammar: a Statement of kind `contest`, made against an offer or a vow (§5.11). It reaches the signers of what it is made against. A **buyer's contest** is a contest by which a buyer says that what they bought was not delivered after payment.
- **Data cMIP**: the cMIP that Terms name in the field `data` (task K8): a path, beyond one leg, for the reports of the people bound by those Terms, which they agreed to with their signature (§5.11).

### 3.2 Parties

**Two classes.** The participants of an agreement are of two classes, not two situations of one class, and each has its own rules and its own protection. The known parties write the Terms (§5.3), sign them (§5.4), end the agreement as its ending condition says (§5.5) and give powers (5.6.1(b)), and are bound by the Terms they sign (5.6.1(a), (c)); they are protected by their own signatures, or by the scheme of signatures they agreed to (the ending condition, the powers, the keepers' socket): nothing binds them that they, or that scheme, did not sign (§5.5, §5.6). An unknown party fills a spot (§5.10), is bound by the Terms they accepted (5.4.3), gives no power (5.6.1(b)), has no say in the agreement's ending (5.5.2), and no later act reaches them (5.10.6); they are protected by the principle: bound by their Terms, and by nothing that follows them, and the agreement is not dissolved while an obligation toward them is open (5.5.3). Each word is defined below.

- **Party**: one named in Terms. Named in one of three ways:
  - as a **signer**: a person, by the identifier the Sign layer gives them (an identity, not one key: Identity);
  - as a **role**: a spot for someone not yet known, linked to a signer later (§5.10);
  - as an **agreement**: another agreement, by its root (§5.1).

  Signers, and signers who fill a role, are the parties who may be bound. An agreement named as a party need not sign to be named, and where it has not signed it is not bound. Where it signs, it signs by its own signature, as its own Terms require: one level (§5.1). That signature binds its parties as its own Terms say.
- **Author**: one of the parties whose words the Terms are, and who sign them. An author is named as a signer, never by role and never as an agreement, and is a known party. The signer of a Terms act is its first author, whether or not `authors` names them, and that act is their assent (§4.3); `authors` names the others, who sign later, each by an assent of their own (§5.3).
- **Known party**: a signer the Terms name: a party they name as a signer, or an agreement they name as a party, which signs as its own Terms require (§5.1); never one who takes part through a role. The author is one. A known party who also fills a spot stays a known party: filling a spot makes no one known, and makes no known party unknown. A buyer whom the Terms name as a signer is a known party: a partner, bound as the known parties are, by what they signed, its ending condition included. The known parties are those who, by default, end the agreement (§5.5).
- **Unknown party**: said of the holder of a filling: a signer who takes part through a role, and so was not known when the Terms that name that role were made. A spot that a known party of the same Terms fills is filled by a known party's act: that filling is no unknown party's, and the known parties' rules apply to it. An unknown party has no say in the agreement's ending, and nothing that follows the Terms their proof names reaches their filling (§5.10).
- **Spot**: a role's place among the parties, before and after it is filled. It has a **sort**: **done**, **received**, **holding**, **fits** or **future** (§5.10). Whether it still takes new identities is said by its cMIP, from the Terms (§5.10).
- **Fill**, **filling**: a spot is filled when the cMIP its role names (task K5) says that a signer is linked to it by a proof: the acts that earn it. A **filling** is one such link, tied to the proof that earned it and to the Terms that proof names. A spot may be filled many times; whether it holds one filling or many is its cMIP's (§5.10).
- **Rights**: what Terms offer whoever earns a filling under them, with every condition they put on it. The grammar does not say what they are, or read them (§5.10).
- **Receiver**: whoever Terms name, in the field `receiver`, as receiving on the agreement's behalf: an identity (one of the agreement's own parties) or another agreement, by its root (§5.1). The word means that field's value and nothing else; the side of a leg that receives is the receiving side.
- **Receiver for an act**: the receiver named by the Terms of the agreement which that act names, followed through any agreement it names until one of an agreement's own parties is reached (§5.1).
- **Work**: an agreement whose Terms say who owns something and in what shares. A plain word for a use of the grammar, not a separate thing in it: every grammar rule reads a work as it reads any agreement.
- **Shares**: what an agreement's Terms say each party's part is. The grammar gives them no form: they are read from the Terms' words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1), and divided by whichever cMIP needs them (a power's, K3; the one that passes on, K6). What a share is, how it moves and who must sign to change it are the cMIPs'. No socket is kept for a dividing rule alone. An agreement with shares defines as much or as little of how it governs itself as it chooses (powers, §5.6); the grammar has no separate notion of a collective.
- **Service**, **deal**: a service is a signer who does a job for an agreement (dividing what it is paid, holding a payment); a hand (task K6). Its deal is an agreement between the service and the agreement it serves, named as a party. A deal is signed on both sides: by the service, and by the agreement it serves, as that agreement's own Terms require (§5.1). Plain words for a use of the grammar, as "work" is: what makes an agreement a deal, and what is read from it, are the service's cMIP's (§6).
- **Bound**: a person is bound by an act when §5.6 says so.
- **Power**: an entry in Terms saying that a Statement of a given kind binds the parties when a given rule says yes. A power has a **sort**: the kind of Statement it is for, `resolves` among them, for a resolve (§4.2). A power is for acting under the Terms as they are. A power Terms carry is given by their known parties and binds only them: an assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power (5.6.1(b), 5.10.7).
- **Constitution**, **executive**, **judicial**: three words for three things these grammar rules keep apart: how the agreement ends (the ending condition), who may act under the Terms (the powers), and what the Terms set for a dispute (the contest, a judge they name, a power to resolve a fork). They are said once, in §5.6, and nowhere else.
- **Buyer**: a signer of an offer's Terms, whose identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed them, and whom those Terms require to pay. A buyer signs by an assent to the Terms they buy under, as anyone fills a spot and is bound by their own assent (§5.10, §5.6). Elsewhere in this text, where only the paying matters, the same person is called the payer. Paying is a leg, with the buyer as its doer (§5.11).
- **Offer**: an agreement with a participant unknown at the time of signing: one whose Terms name a party by role (a spot, §5.10), so that who takes part was not known when the Terms were signed.

### 3.3 Ending, closing and forks

- **Chain**: an agreement's own acts: its Terms act and the Statements made within it. Its two jobs are to guard the agreement's end (§5.5) and to give the knots that order acts (§5.13). Agreements linked by closing pointers show lineage, and are not one chain. The word is used here for nothing else: not for one signer's acts (a line), not for acts that name one another (knots, §3.4), and not for anything that links one agreement to another. Two uses of the word are not this text's: **the envelope's chain**, and the chains §2 names, are the layer below's (Envelopes), named here, not defined.
- **Sequence**: agreements linked to one another, so that what one gives or owes reaches the next: a payment passing from a work to the works it stands on (§5.11). Never called a chain. The word is used for nothing else.
- **Succession**: not a term of this text. The rights of a baker (Envelopes' word) pass by transfers in the envelope's chain (Envelopes), and no agreement passes them. No agreement succeeds another as a party: an agreement that follows another is a new party (§5.5).
- **Leg**: one obligation carried out, between two sides: whoever carries it out, and whoever receives it (§5.11). A payment to a work is a leg between the payer and the work's receiver. A payout divided among five is five legs, each between whoever pays out and one of the five. A leg is never an agreement: an agreement of a sequence is called that.
- **Doer**, **receiving side**: the two sides of a leg. The doer carries out the obligation. The receiving side receives it; where what is owed is owed to an agreement, the receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (§5.1). These grammar rules define the two sides and nothing more: which lines of a report each side receives is the task's cMIP's (§5.11).
- **Line**: acts of which each names the one before it: one signer's acts within an agreement, by `previous` (§5.7); and any other acts the grammar reads that way (§5.12). A signer's acts across their whole life, by `prev`, are the layer below's (Envelopes, Identity); this text reads them and does not define them.
- **Ending condition**, **condition** for short: the cMIP that Terms name, by fingerprint, in the one field `condition`: the task K4. It answers whether the agreement is ended, by the acts held: **valid** or **not valid** (§5.5). It may end it by signatures, or by any other condition the bytes can check, an anchoring to a clock among them. Which acts end the agreement, whose signatures they need, and why, are that cMIP's to specify; where the field is empty, the default asks for the signature of every known party (§5.5). The plain word "conditions", said of what Terms' words put on rights (§5.10) or on a role (§5.10), means those words, read where their cMIP puts them, and not this.
- **Ending**: the acts by which an agreement's ending condition is met: the signatures on a closing pointer that names it, or on a Statement made within it of a kind that ends it, as that condition reads them; or none, for a condition the bytes check without signatures, a clock among them (§5.5).
- **Ended**: an agreement is ended once its ending condition answers valid (§5.5). An ended agreement is not in force; what it still owes is delivered under it (§5.5).
- **Dissolved**: an agreement is dissolved when it is ended and no obligation toward an unknown party is open under it (§5.5).
- **Standing obligation**: what an agreement owes under acts made within it before it was ended, an unknown party's filling among them, until it is delivered (§5.5).
- **Closing pointer**: a naming, in a field of its own of a Terms act, of an earlier agreement, by its root. It says two things, and no more: that agreement is no longer valid, and it once existed. It closes that agreement only as §5.5 says. It is no continuation of it, and no reliance on it (§5.13).
- **Pin**: an order shown, for the signatures that put Terms in force: each party in whose name one of them is counted has made a later act within the agreement, shown after every one of them (§5.4). Knots pin (**shown after**, §3.4).
- **Valid**, **not valid**: said of an ending, and of nothing else in this text but what a closing pointer says of the agreement it names (**Closing pointer**): the output of the task `condition` (K4), or of its default where the socket is empty (§5.5). **Valid** is the ending condition's answer only: the agreement is ended by those acts. Always given with that condition named beside it, or with the words "by default", and with the ground the cMIP gives. It is the whole answer about whether the agreement is ended, who must sign included, but what §5.5 asks of an agreement that acts as a party: no power is read beside it (§5.5, §5.6). The core's "valid" has the same spelling and another meaning: there it is said of an act, where a rule the verifier can apply is seen to pass. This text never says "valid" of an act; of an act it says that it counts, or that it is shown. **Not valid** is a cMIP's answer about an ending; it may rest on a time or on the acts held, and it is qualified as they are. **Invalid** is this text's answer for an act that itself shows it cannot count (3.6). The two are kept apart.
- **In force**: said of an agreement, its Terms, only. Terms are in force where they have come into force as §5.4 says, the order of the signatures that put them in force is shown (the pin, §5.4), any clock they state is anchored (§5.8), and the agreement is not ended (§5.5).
- **Fork**: two acts competing to change the same state, with no order shown between them: two acts of one signer naming the same act as the one before, whatever else orders them (5.7.2); two acts that each end the same agreement (§5.5); Terms coming into force against a counter signature that cancels one of the assents they need (§5.4); or any other such pair (§5.12). Three or more such acts are one fork, with that many tips. It is seen from the acts, not from what any person meant. Neither takes effect, and the earlier state stays, until the fork is resolved (§5.12). Each of the competing acts is a **tip**; where the fork is of a line, the act both follow is the **shared act**, and a tip, with what follows it in the line, is a **branch**. "Fork" is also a verifier's answer.
- **Rival**: each of two or more acts that compete to change the same state and that nothing orders; the tips of a fork (§5.12).
- **Apply**, **no longer applies**: plain words, and not an answer of a verifier. A filling, and the rights it holds, apply from when the filling is earned. They last as long as the words of the Terms the filling was earned under say, counted on the clock those Terms name, as the cMIP that reads those words answers; and they change only as §5.10 says. The agreement's ending does not end them. These grammar rules do not read what the rights are: a verifier shows the cMIP's answer, and gives none of its own (§5.10).
- **Resolution**, **resolve**: a fork's **resolution** is its end: a new act, made in an order that can be told, as the agreement's own rules allow: for a fork of a line, its signer; for any fork, a power to resolve given in advance (§5.12). That act is a Statement of kind **resolve**, which names every tip and says which one continues. A fork is said to be resolved when its resolution is made; as a defined word, **resolve** is a Statement's kind only (§3.1).
- **Set aside**: a tip is set aside when a resolve continues another tip. It never counted: for a line, as the next act of its line; for two endings, as an ending (§5.12).
- **Built on a tip**: an act is built on a tip when it follows the tip in its line, or rests on the tip or on an act built on it: it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch. The words reach as far as the naming goes: an act that names an act built on a tip is itself built on that tip, however many namings lie between (§5.12).

### 3.4 Order and time

- **Name** (or **cite**): an act names another act when it carries its fingerprint in one of its fields. What an act names is what it is read against (§5.13).
- **Knot**: each such naming: the order acts give by naming one another. Knots cross acts and layers: any fingerprint of an act, carried in a field of any act the verifier holds, is a knot, whatever layer either act belongs to, the field `body` included. A verifier reads knots in fields, never in an act's content (§5.13). Knots are what order acts (§5.13).
- **Shown after**: act B is shown after act A when the verifier holds acts, each naming the one before by fingerprint, that lead from B back to A. It proves that B came after A, and nothing more: never that an act came before one that does not name it, and never that an act does not exist. Where an act those acts need is named and not held, the answer is unchecked (§5.13). An act's place against a time is shown by its own anchoring only (**Anchoring**).
- **Keeper**: a relay that Terms name, in their keeper field, as the stated where of the agreement's acts: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where its acts are, so that the where itself can be checked. The core asks of a keeper what it asks of any relay (§3.5); it gives no order and vouches for nothing. Terms may name one keeper or several; at how many of them an act must be is said by the cMIP the Terms name beside them, and with none named it must be at every one (§5.8). A keeper is named by an identifier and is not a cMIP, so the keepers named are not a task; the cMIP beside them is (K9). A keeper that keeps an ordered, signed record of an agreement's acts is a **keeper service**: a cMIP's, named in that socket, and not the core's (§5.8).
- **Clock**: what an agreement's Terms name as saying what time it was for that agreement (§5.8). What a clock is, is not this text's.
- **Anchoring**: an act's anchoring to a clock: what the clock's layer gives as placing that act in time. An act that names a clock is anchored to it, and Terms that state a clock wait for their anchoring (§5.8). The clock the Terms name is the one every reader uses for that agreement's answers in time (§5.8). An anchoring of a reader's own choosing gives an answer only as that reader's, named so; it never gives the Terms' own answer (§5.13). A condition in time needs a clock the Terms state (§5.8).

### 3.5 What relays do

These three verbs are MOR's, for every layer, one for each actor; this text uses them as defined here. Bytes that sit at a relay while nobody acts are **stored** there.

- **Lodge**: whoever puts an act at a relay (an uploader) lodges it there.
- **Relay**: a relay relays an act: it takes it in and passes it on, reading nothing and vouching for nothing. A keeper is a relay the Terms name as the stated where of the agreement's acts (3.4); it may do more, as a service, and the core depends on nothing more.
- **Fetch**: a client fetches an act from a relay.

### 3.6 The verifier and its answers

- **Client**: the software through which a person reads acts and signs them. A client is not a verifier, and what a client shows a person is not an act. One duty is written for clients, in one place (§5.12).
- **Verifier**: whoever applies these grammar rules to a set of acts. A verifier answers questions; it does not decide disputes.
- **Answers**: a verifier's answer is one of: **shown**, **not shown**, **unchecked**, **invalid** (below); and **valid** or **not valid** (of an ending), **in force** (of Terms) and **fork** (of competing acts) (3.3). A verifier names the grammar rule with the answer, and states its reason.

  Answers about what is latest, what else exists, whether anything came after, or what time it was, carry a qualifier: **as shown** (meaning: as far as the acts this verifier holds show), naming how many keepers the answer rests on, and which; or **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (§5.13), written **per clock** where it is the clock the Terms name. An answer that rests on what someone relied on in the branch of a tip set aside also carries the qualifier **disputed** (below).

  Of reports, a verifier also shows what the MICS's report block asks it to show, in that block's words, which are not answers of this text.

  *Not an Agreements act.* An act that lacks a required field, or carries a field that §4 allows only beside another (as `continues` beside `resolves`) without it, or carries a field §4 does not give, or one §4 says it does not carry (`resolves` on a Statement of another kind than `resolve`), or holds in a field something other than that field's kind (anything but a fingerprint where §4 says fingerprint), or bytes that cannot be read as an act, never reach these grammar rules: §4 says which. This text gives them no answer.

  *A task's output.* Where a task answers yes or no (K2, K3, K6, K8, K9; whether a spot still takes new identities, K5; and whether what a filling gave still applies, K1, 5.10.8), a verifier passes that output on as the task's, with the cMIP named and the ground or cause it gives, and not as an answer of this text; where it lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked**.
- **Shown**: the acts held establish it under these grammar rules.
- **Not shown**: the acts held do not establish it. Always given with its reason: among them, that an act not held, and named by none held, might establish it; that there is nothing to resolve; that an act is not at the keepers the Terms name, or not at as many as their socket asks; that no order is shown; that the Terms are not yet anchored (§5.8).
- **Unchecked**: the verifier lacks something it needs to apply a grammar rule: a cMIP the Terms name, an act named in a field that it does not hold, or the answer of a keeper the Terms name, which cannot be reached. A missing act never becomes a yes, and never a no. A verifier that stops before it has applied a grammar rule answers **unchecked** for what it did not reach, and says how far it went.
- **Invalid**: an act cannot count as what it claims to be, and the act itself shows it: no act the verifier does not hold could make it count. The cases are those the grammar rules of §5 name invalid, in the order §4 gives, which is the order in which a verifier that names one case names the first it finds. An invalid act counts for nothing, for anything: no answer of these grammar rules rests on it, and no act counts through it. It stays readable as an act, and a verifier shows it, with the answer invalid. If an act the verifier does not hold could change the answer, the answer is never invalid. **Not valid** is not invalid (3.3).
- **Disputed**: the qualifier of an answer that rests on what someone relied on in the branch of a tip set aside (§5.12). It has that one meaning in this text. The core's "disputed" has the same spelling and another condition: there it is shown of an act that its signer's change of key would have made void, and that someone else relied on (Identity, rule 34). Both say that what others relied on stands and is shown so; a verifier that gives both says which one it means.

## 4. The data each act carries

Each field is listed with the grammar rules that read it. A field no grammar rule reads is not here. An act that lacks a field marked required, or carries a field these tables do not give, is **not an Agreements act** (§3.6): it never reaches the grammar rules, and this text gives it no answer. An act that has its fields and breaks a MUST of §5 which the act itself shows is **invalid** (§3.6). Every socket holds a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters, which only that cMIP reads. An entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is not read (§5.2).

### 4.1 The kinds of value

**Each field holds one kind of value**, given in its row: a fingerprint; a list of fingerprints; an identifier (of a signer, a keeper or a clock); a socket (a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters); the set of acts (below); or one of the words the row names. An act whose field holds something other than that field's kind is not an Agreements act, with three exceptions, which reach the grammar rules: a `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid** (§5.2); an entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is left unread (§5.2); and a role whose sort is not one of the five, or whose `what` is not a fingerprint, is read as lacking it, which makes the Terms **invalid** (§5.10). A `what` on a role of sort future is not read (§5.10). Only what stands in a socket's own place for a cMIP reaches §5.2: a socket that is not written as a socket at all (a cMIP and its parameters), and a power's sort that is neither one of its words nor a fingerprint, hold another kind than their field's, and are not Agreements acts. How each kind is written as bytes is the encoding's (encoding 3).

**The set of acts** (§3.1) is a value computed from a list of act ids: the ids sorted, repeats dropped, and hashed with a label for a set of acts. It is a value computed from a list, never a list written in a field: where a field holds one, the field says that what it holds is a set. The same acts give the same value, whoever computes it. It names a set of acts and nothing more: it says nothing of whether the set is complete, what it means, or when it was read. It has no order, and its members are acts only. The empty set has the label and no members. A set's value names no act a verifier can read without the list it was computed from, so it is no knot by itself (§5.13); the acts it was computed from are knots where a field of an act held names them. Where Terms, a Statement or a cMIP in any socket name a set of acts as one value, they name it by this value. It is the value form of any parameter of a cMIP that asks for acts, and of a body's evidence; whether a field of §4.2 or §4.3 takes it is open (§7). Its bytes and its label are the encoding's (encoding 3).

An assent's `content` is empty: its `terms` field is what it agrees to. A counter signature's `content` is empty too: its `terms` field names the Terms whose assent it cancels (§5.4). So is a resolve's: what it resolves is in `resolves` and `continues`. A contest's `terms` field names the Terms of the offer or vow it is made against (§5.11); what its `content` holds is not read by these grammar rules.

**An author's first act.** The act that publishes Terms is its signer's assent to them: it is the assent counted in that signer's name, and the signer does not assent to them again. The other parties assent later, each in an act of their own. The Terms act is its signer's first act within the agreement (§5.7): that signer's next act there, whatever it is, names it in `previous`. A party that has made no act within the agreement yet names none.

**What a resolve carries.** A Statement of kind `resolve` carries `agreement`, `terms`, `kind`, `previous`, `resolves` and `continues`, and MAY carry `for` and `under` (a resolve made under a power to resolve, §5.12), `answers`, `relies_on` and `settlement`; it carries no `content`. It is a resolve and nothing more: it is not also an assent, a counter signature or a contest, and no Statement of another kind carries `resolves` or `continues`. Whether it in fact resolves a fork is §5.12's, from other acts: one that resolves nothing is read as §5.12 says, and does not stop being an Agreements act after the fact.

A report (§3.1) is one act type, defined once, in the MICS's report block; every layer's tasks use that one type, and this text uses it and gives it no fields of its own. It is not a kind of Statement. These grammar rules read no report as standing in a signer's line: §5.7 reads Terms and Statements only, and no fork is seen among reports. They read a report as answering one call; whether one report may answer several is the block's (§2). What a leg report carries is in K6's row (§6). A payment (§5.11) is an act of the layer that holds value, and this text gives it no fields either.

### 4.4 One fault, one grammar rule

Where one fault fits two grammar rules, it is answered under one, and a verifier that finds several faults in one act names the first, in this order.

1. *Not an Agreements act* (§3.6), under the encoding's rule that gives the form at fault (encoding 3): bytes that cannot be read as an act; a field these tables do not give; a required field lacking; a field allowed only beside another (`envelope_at` beside `envelope`, `continues` beside `resolves`, `for` beside `under`) without it; a field the act does not carry, being what it is (`resolves` or `continues` on a Statement of another kind than `resolve`); the same party named twice; a `keeper` present and holding no keeper (encoding 3, E18); a field that holds another kind than its own, save the three exceptions of §4.1. None of these reaches the grammar rules.
2. *Invalid*, under §5.2: the body's words, then the cMIP of the body's socket, of `in_force`, of `condition`, of `control`, of `data`, and of each power's rule, in the order of `powers`, not named by fingerprint; then the act `body` names, held and not a Text act. Where that act is not held, or cannot be opened, there is no case: the note of what is lacking names 5.2.2.
3. *Invalid*, for each role in `parties`, in their order: its sort lacking or not one of the five, then its `what` lacking or not a fingerprint (but for sort future), then its spot's cMIP lacking (§5.10), or not named by fingerprint (§5.2).
4. *Invalid*, under §5.4: Terms that name a party by role and carry no `in_force`.
5. *Invalid*, under §5.3: Terms whose `authors` name a party named by role or as an agreement, or one who is not one of their parties, in the order of `authors`; then Terms whose signer is not one of the parties they name as signers.
6. *Invalid*, under §5.7: an act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement.
7. *Invalid*, under Envelopes' rules for Terms that name an envelope (§2), where those rules give it.
8. *Invalid*, under §5.8: the case it names, of a condition in time that names no clock the Terms state.

A `body`, or a cMIP in any socket of Terms (a role's spot included), that is not named by fingerprint is invalid under §5.2, the grammar rule that says MUST, and under no other. A role's sort or `what` that is lacking, or is not one §5.10 allows, is invalid under §5.10. A field whose bytes are of the wrong kind is not an Agreements act, under the encoding's rule that gives that field's form, and under no grammar rule.

## 5. Validity rules (the grammar rules)

### 5.1 An agreement as a party

- **5.1.1** Terms MAY name a party as an agreement, by its root fingerprint.
- **5.1.2** **Named, and signing.** Naming an agreement as a party asks nothing of it: it need not sign to be named, it is not counted by the default of §5.4, and where it has not signed it is not bound by any act of these Terms, and neither are its parties. A party named as an agreement MAY sign: an assent to these Terms, where their `in_force` rule asks for it (§5.4), or an ending of them (§5.5). Its signing covers what those Terms carry, a call among them (§5.11). It signs as its own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts (a vote, or a signer its Terms give a power to sign for it). **An agreement's signature.** An act is signed by an agreement x when its `for` names x, by its root; its `under` names x's Terms, in force (§5.4, §5.5), which give a power for acts of that kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes (where they give two or more powers for that kind, any one of their rules). Whoever signs such an act (a signer x's power names, or whoever the rule of a vote accepts) signs on behalf of x's parties, under the power x gave. That act is x's signature. An act in x's name that a pin asks for (5.4.1) is one act made for x under a power its Terms give; where they give none, it is x's signing by every one of its signers (below), which serves the pin as one act. It binds every party bound by x's Terms, as x's Terms say (§5.6), and no one else; and it binds a party of x only where that party's own signature is on x's Terms, which give the power: no ending condition can give it for a party who did not sign them (§5.6). It is an act within the agreement it is made in, and stands there in its signer's own line (§5.7). Where x's Terms give no such power, x signs by every one of its signers: one act by each of them, its `for` naming x and its `under` naming x's Terms. Each act has one signer, as the core has it. **Which acts are one signing.** Those acts are one signing of x where they are the same act but for their signer and their place in that signer's own line: they name the same `agreement`, `terms`, `kind`, `content`, `for`, `under`, `settlement`, `resolves` and `continues`; their `previous`, `answers` and `relies_on` may differ. x has signed once an act so matched is held from every one of its signers; for an assent, that is an assent by each of them, `for` x, to the same Terms. Each of those acts stands in its own signer's line (§5.7). Where an act's `for` names an agreement, its `under` MUST name Terms of that agreement: an act made for an agreement under any other Terms is not that agreement's signature. **One level.** Its signers are the parties its own Terms name as signers. A party it names as an agreement is not one of them, and neither are that agreement's people: these grammar rules MUST NOT be read as making an agreement's signing need the signature of anyone inside an agreement it names, unless its own Terms say so. A verifier reads what they require from that agreement's Terms (§5.4); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6).
- **5.1.3** Terms MAY name a `receiver`: one of their own parties named as a signer or as a role, by its position among the parties; or an agreement, by its root fingerprint, whether or not it is one of their parties. The two are told apart in the bytes, not by guessing (§4.2). A receiver that names, by position, a party named as an agreement makes the act not an Agreements act: an agreement is named as receiver by its root.
- **5.1.4** **Receiver for an act.** Whatever a payer, an act or a rule of one agreement gives or owes to an agreement, it gives or owes to that agreement's receiver for the act (§3.2, **Receiver for an act**), read from that agreement's Terms, which the act names (§5.13). An agreement has one Terms act: an act that names the agreement by its root names its Terms. Where the receiver so named is one of that agreement's own parties, the answer is that party. Where it is another agreement (5.1.3), the two are agreements of a sequence (§5.11), and a verifier reads that agreement's receiver in the same way, and so on, until one of an agreement's own parties is reached. That party is who receives: the receiving side of one leg (§5.11). These grammar rules follow a receiver no further. What that party then passes on is another leg, carried out under its own agreement's Terms and the cMIPs they name, and is read only as §5.11 says. If the Terms read name no receiver, or name as receiver a signer or a role that is not among their own parties, or the spot named is not filled, or is filled more than once (which of them receives is not said), or an agreement is met a second time on the way, no one receives, and the answer is **not shown**. Where the act that gives or owes names, as the Terms it read, an act that is not those Terms, the answer is **not shown**. Where an agreement on the way is ended, its Terms still name its receiver, and an act that names it is read under its words (5.5.3); whether a filling stood under it is §5.10's. Where it names Terms that name an envelope, the answer of Envelopes' rules for such Terms comes first (§2): where those Terms are invalid, no one receives by them, and the verifier shows the payment with them, and the answer invalid, that rule named; where that rule answers not shown or unchecked, so is the receiver. If the verifier lacks that agreement's acts, it is **unchecked** (§3.6). A verifier that stops following receivers from agreement to agreement before a party is reached answers **unchecked**, and says how far it followed (§3.6, §7).
- **5.1.5** A rule (§6), in any socket, MAY take as an input a receiver for an act, so answered, or what a leg report states (§5.11).

### 5.2 The words, and what the Terms use

- **5.2.1** The `body`, every piece in `relies_on`, and every cMIP named anywhere in a Terms act MUST be named by fingerprint.
- **5.2.2** **The words are a Text act.** The words of Terms MUST be a Text act (Text, rule 14), public or private, and the Terms MUST name it in `body` by its act id, beside the socket of the cMIP that reads it (task K1). The Text act comes first: Terms can name only an act that already exists, and naming it by its act id is all that this asks of them. Where the act `body` names is held and is not a Text act, the Terms are **invalid**: they lack what they need. A Text act invalid under Text's own grammar rules is no Text act here. Where that act is not held, or is held and cannot be opened, whether the Terms are invalid under this rule is **unchecked**, with a note of what is lacking; where several acts are checked together, the note names each. Where that act is disowned by its signer's key change (Identity, rule 34), the Terms stand, and they are **unchecked** as to their words. These grammar rules read that act id as they read any fingerprint (5.2.1); they do not read the words. The keepers provide the agreement's acts; the words are fetched where Text acts are, by the act id the Terms name, so words that are not those the Terms name are caught.
- **5.2.3** Anything a Terms act refers to in another way is not part of those Terms. A verifier MUST NOT read it as part of them. An entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is not read, and does not make the Terms invalid; a verifier says that it left it unread. A field §4 does not give is not left unread: the act is not an Agreements act (§4). A `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid**, under 5.2.1 (§3.6). **What is left unread**, in these grammar rules, is that entry of `relies_on` and two things more, each where it is said: a call in Terms that do not hold the task it is attached to (§5.11); and a `what` on a role of sort future (§5.10). A verifier says of each that it left it unread.

### 5.3 Authors

- **5.3.1** `authors` MUST name one or more of the parties these Terms name, each named as a signer: an author is never a party named by role, and never one named as an agreement (§3.2). Terms whose `authors` name a party named by role or as an agreement, or one who is not one of their parties, are **invalid**; so are Terms whose signer is not one of the parties they name as signers. The signer of a Terms act is its first author, whether or not `authors` names them: `authors` names the other authors, and MAY name the signer too. No Terms are invalid for leaving their signer out of `authors`. The other authors sign by assents of their own, made after the Terms act (§4.3, **An author's first act**; §5.4).
- **5.3.2** The authors are the parties who bear the failure of the words to mean one thing. A cMIP that reads a judge's decision, in whichever socket it sits (§6), MUST accept `authors` as an input.

### 5.4 Coming into force, and what an assent binds

- **5.4.1** **Coming into force.** Terms come into force when the assents their `in_force` rule requires are present (task K2). Where they carry no `in_force`, they come into force when there is an assent to them in the name of every party they name as a signer, the Terms act itself being its signer's (§4.3). **The pin.** They are in force once the order of the signatures that put them in force is shown, by knots: each party in whose name one of those assents is counted has made an act in that same name within the agreement (a Statement of that agreement, and no other knot) that is shown after every one of those assents (§5.13). Those assents are the ones its `in_force` rule requires, as K2 names them, or the default's; not every assent counted. Any such act pins, a counter signature included: a counter signature so shown comes after the completion, and cancels nothing (5.4.5); one that nothing orders against those assents is a fork (5.4.5, §5.12). Until then they are not yet in force: a verifier answers **not shown** for them as in force, with that reason, no order being shown. Where an act named in a field of an act held is not held, and could be the later act of a party whose act the pin needs, the pin is **unchecked** (5.13.3). Where the Terms name keepers, each act the pin counts counts only where it is at the stated where (5.8.2). Where the Terms state a clock, they are in force only once anchored (5.8.5). An agreement is in force from then until it is ended (§5.5).
- **5.4.2** Terms that name a party by role MUST carry `in_force`.
- **5.4.3** **What an assent binds.** An assent binds its signer only while the Terms it names are in force, with one exception: an assent by which its signer earned an unknown party's filling (§3.2) binds that signer, and binds the known parties to them, as the Terms it names say, for as long as their words say, on the clock they name, whether or not the agreement has since ended (5.5.3); nothing that follows those Terms reaches it (§5.10). About an assent, a verifier answers **shown** where it is counted in a party's name and those Terms are in force, or where it earned a filling under Terms since ended, with those Terms named (§5.10); otherwise **not shown**, with the reason (those Terms are not yet in force, no order being shown, or not yet anchored; the agreement is ended, and the assent was made after its ending, 5.5.3; the assent is cancelled, 5.4.5). An assent made within a branch set aside is shown as **disputed**, and binds no one (§5.12).
- **5.4.4** A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of 5.4.1. An `in_force` rule MAY ask for its signing; what "signed" then is, for it and for its counter signature, is 5.4.5's (5.1.2).
- **5.4.5** **Counter signature.** No assent binds anyone, and nothing in Terms counts, until those Terms are in force (5.4.1, 5.4.3). A counter signature is a Statement of kind `counter_signature` whose `terms` names the Terms an assent names, and which cancels that assent. Made by the signer of the assent in their own name, it follows that assent in the signer's own line (§5.7): directly, or after other acts of that signer. Made for a party under a power of sort `counter_signature` (§5.6), or as an agreement's signature (5.1.2), it cancels the assent counted in that party's name that it names, directly or through acts that name it. It cancels that assent, and no one else's: an assent so cancelled is not counted by 5.4.1. It sets nothing aside: the assent stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled, and so does what it was made within. An assent made for a party by an agent, under a power (§5.6), is cancelled in the same way as one the party made itself, and in no other: by a counter signature in that party's name, which names that assent, directly or through acts that name it, while a counter signature is available (below); the party itself MAY make it, in its own name, as may the agent, as that assent's signer, or another under a power of sort `counter_signature` (§5.6; 5.6.6(c)). A power is given only by Terms in force (5.6.1(b)): a power the same Terms carry acts on nothing before they are in force. **Which assent.** A signer may assent, cancel, and assent again. An assent is read against the first counter signature in the same name that follows it; a later counter signature is read only against the assents in that name made after the one before it. Which is first is read by knots only, never by act id: two counter signatures in one name that nothing orders stand as one. **Until the completion.** A counter signature is available only until the assents that put the Terms in force (5.4.1, its first sentence) are shown before it, by knots: one shown after them comes after the completion, cancels nothing, and pins as any act of its signer within the agreement does (5.4.1). One made for Terms that are in force changes nothing: Terms in force change in nothing; the agreement only ends (§5.5). Where a party is an agreement, "signed", for that party, is whatever that agreement's own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts: one level (5.1.2). A verifier reads that from that agreement's Terms (5.1.2); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of 5.4.1 (5.4.4), so this has effect only where the Terms' own `in_force` rule asks for that party's signing. Such a party makes a counter signature by the same act as its assent: under a power of sort `counter_signature`, or by all its signers (5.1.2). **The order.** Whether the Terms were in force before the counter signature is an answer about what is latest, **as shown** (§3.6). The assent the counter signature cancels is counted as not yet cancelled. Only knots give an order (§5.13): where the counter signature is shown after acts enough for the Terms to come into force (5.4.1, its first sentence), it comes after the completion, and changes nothing; where, counting every act held but those shown after the counter signature, the Terms would not come into force, it came before, and the assent it cancels is not counted. Where the Terms would not come into force even with every act held counted, nothing had completed them, and the counter signature cancels. Otherwise the counter signature and the acts that would put the Terms in force are a fork (§5.12): two acts competing to change the same state, with no order shown. While it stands, neither takes effect: the Terms are not in force. A verifier answers **fork**, shows both, and MUST NOT choose between them; the fork is resolved as §5.12 says. **Its answer.** About a counter signature, **shown** means that it cancels the assent it is read against; **not shown** means that it is not shown as cancelling (it comes after the completion and changes nothing, or it follows no assent), with the reason; **fork** means that it and the Terms' coming into force are a fork. Where counter signatures each name the other (a knot, §5.13) so that the reading does not settle, the MICS says what a verifier answers (§2). A later assent by the same signer to the same Terms is counted as any assent is.

### 5.5 The ending condition, and the closing pointer

- **5.5.1** **No versions.** Terms are never replaced. An agreement has one Terms act, and lives until its ending condition is met (5.5.2). No act changes what its Terms say: what its parties want changed is its end and a new agreement, whose Terms MAY carry a closing pointer to it (5.5.4). A verifier MUST NOT read any act as changing the Terms of an agreement, or as making one agreement go on as another.
- **5.5.2** **The ending condition: valid or not valid.** Terms MAY name in `condition` (task K4) a cMIP, by fingerprint (5.2.1), with its parameters: their ending condition (§3.3, **Ending condition**). These grammar rules read one thing of it: asked whether the agreement is ended by the acts held, **valid** or **not valid**, as that cMIP answers. It may end the agreement by signatures, or by any other condition the bytes can check, an anchoring to a clock among them. Which acts end it, whose signatures they need, and when, are that cMIP's to specify.
  - (a) **What it is given.** The Terms that name it, and the acts held, among them the acts that would end the agreement and the signatures counted on them: the assents to Terms that carry a closing pointer naming it (5.5.4), and Statements made within it of a kind that ends it; and the answers of the clock the Terms name (§5.8). An act that is not at the stated where is not among them (5.8.2), nor an act in a tip that has stopped (5.12.3), and the verifier says which it left out and why. A condition that needs a clock or a keeper is given one only where the Terms name it: Terms that do not supply what the condition's cMIP declares it needs are **invalid** (5.8.6).
  - (b) **It runs from the bytes.** Whoever holds those bytes can apply the cMIP, and gets the same answer as anyone else (task K4). Its answer waits for no one to publish anything.
  - (c) **Not valid.** Where the cMIP answers not valid, the agreement is not ended by those acts, whoever signed them. A verifier MUST answer **not valid**, and MUST name the condition beside that answer, with the ground the cMIP gives: an act, with who signed it, or a time, per the clock the Terms name (§5.8). A time has no other ground: no reader's anchoring answers for the Terms. Where the cMIP counts signatures, its answer rests on the acts held: it is given **as shown**, and it may differ once an act the verifier did not hold is shown.
  - (d) **Valid.** Where the cMIP answers valid, the verifier answers **valid**, named and qualified in the same way, and the agreement is ended (5.5.3). Nothing more is asked of the ending's signatures, but what 5.5.6 asks of an agreement that acts as a party, and no power is read for it (§5.6).
  - (e) **The default ending condition.** Where Terms name no ending condition, the agreement is ended where every known party has signed an act that ends it (5.5.4; (a)): the author among them, and every party named as a signer or as an agreement; never a party named by role, and never one whose only place is a filling. A known party who also fills a spot stays among them (§3.2). A party named as an agreement is among them whether or not it has ever signed: how it signs is §5.1's (5.1.2). A verifier answers **valid**, with the words "by default", where an act in the name of each of them is held; where one is lacking it answers **not shown** for the ending, and never **not valid**: the missing act may exist. These grammar rules name no Statement kind that ends an agreement: a kind that does is a K7 cMIP's.
  - (f) **By a clock.** An ending by a clock needs no further act: once the clock the Terms name shows the time the ending condition states, the agreement is ended, per clock. It is the one ending that comes by itself.
  - (g) Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, whether the agreement is ended is **unchecked** (§3.6), and so is every answer that rests on it.
  - (h) **Output, and nothing more.** These grammar rules name the ending condition's cMIP, take the output of that task, valid or not valid, and use it where it is needed. They have no notion of a cMIP overruling the default, and no value "locked". An ending condition whose cMIP answers not valid to every act is read as any other: the agreement cannot be ended, and that is all that "cannot be ended" means in this text. A verifier MUST NOT answer, of itself, that an agreement is locked, or that it can never be ended: it gives the condition's answer about the acts held.
- **5.5.3** **Ended, and what still binds.** An agreement is ended once its ending condition answers valid (5.5.2). An ended agreement is not in force, and nothing made within it after its ending counts under it, but as this rule says. What it still owes under acts made before its ending, its standing obligations, is delivered under it, by its Terms, until each is: an unknown party's filling among them, which binds the known parties to its holder as 5.4.3 and §5.10 say. It MAY take a new commitment with the signatures of those the commitment is with, as any agreement does; these grammar rules write no bar on it, and how is its cMIPs'. **Not dissolved while an obligation toward an unknown party is open.** An ended agreement is dissolved only once no obligation toward an unknown party is open under it: while a filling's Terms still apply to it, by their words and on their clock (5.10.8), the agreement is not dissolved, whatever its ending condition says. Whether that obligation is open is the answer of the cMIP that reads those words (task K1, 5.10.8); without it, it is **unchecked**; where the acts held do not show every such obligation closed, a verifier answers **not shown** for the dissolution, with the reason, and never a yes. **Once ended, with a filling open.** Its spots take no new identities; no new assent to its Terms counts; and a payment that names it is read under its words, as the Terms it read (5.1.4, §5.13). **Answers of absence.** That an agreement is not ended, that no act ending it exists, that no obligation is open: each is given as far as the acts held show, **as shown**, naming how many keepers it rests on, and never as a proof that nothing came after (§3.6).
- **5.5.4** **The closing pointer.** Terms MAY carry, in `closing_pointer`, one or more closing pointers, each naming an earlier agreement by its root. Each says two things, and no more: the agreement it names is no longer valid, and it once existed. It is no continuation of that agreement, and no reliance on it: a naming in `relies_on` says that what it names still stands, and is relied on (5.13.2). A closing pointer closes the agreement it names only where its signatures meet that agreement's ending condition (5.5.2): its signatures are the assents to the Terms that carry it, counted in their signers' names, the Terms act being its signer's (5.4.1). Each pointer meets its own agreement's ending condition, and closes that agreement alone. It closes wherever the Terms that carry it are kept: the where the closed agreement states is not asked for the pointer, which is an act of the new agreement. Where a pointer closes nothing, the Terms that carry it are in force among their signers as §5.4 says, each signer bound by both agreements, and the agreement it names goes on. No naming of an agreement in any other field, and no act that names it without a closing pointer, ends it: an agreement made without one is a new agreement beside the old, which goes on.
- **5.5.5** **Two endings.** Two acts that each end the same agreement (each meets its ending condition with the other left out), neither shown after the other, are a fork (§5.12): neither takes effect, and the agreement stays in force, until the fork is settled by its own rules (5.12.4, 5.12.5). Where one is shown after acts enough for the other to meet the ending condition (§5.13), the other ends the agreement, and the later closes nothing: an act that names the agreement after its ending is read as 5.5.3 says, and Terms that carry a pointer that closes nothing are read as 5.5.4 says. Where several sets of acts would each be enough for the other to meet the ending condition, the later act is shown after the ending where it is shown after at least one act of every such set. Where the acts that could show the order need an act named in a field of an act held, and the verifier does not hold it, the order is **unchecked** (5.13.3). An act that is not at the stated where makes no fork (5.8.2).
- **5.5.6** **An agreement that acts as a party.** An agreement bound as a party in another agreement that is in force (5.1.2) is ended only where every party of that other agreement it is bound to signs its ending, unknown parties excluded: they stay covered by their Terms (5.5.3). Its own ending condition is asked as well (5.5.2). **No succession.** No agreement takes another's place as a party: an agreement that follows another, by a closing pointer or otherwise, is a new party. Nothing names it in the place of the old one in any agreement where the old one is a party; each other party of those agreements is asked by its own signature, and renews with the new one or exits.

### 5.6 Who is bound

- **5.6.1** A person is bound by an act if, and only if:
  - (a) they signed it in their own name (an act made for another under a power is not made in its signer's own name: 5.6.3), or
  - (b) the act's `for` names them, or names an agreement by whose Terms they are bound (5.1.2); its `under` names Terms by which they are bound and which were in force when the act was made, as 5.6.4 places it (§5.4, §5.5); their own signature is on those Terms, the Terms `under` names, as an assent counted in their name (5.6.5(f)), and they are a known party of them, or, for a filling's holder, they gave that power in an act of their own: a Statement in their own name, of a kind a K7 cMIP gives (these grammar rules name no kind for it), that names the one the power is given to and the power's sort, and that the power's rule reads through that cMIP (K3) (5.10.7); no ending condition can give a power for a person who did not sign the Terms that give it, and an assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power; those Terms give a power for acts of this kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes (where those Terms give two or more powers for that kind, the act binds where any one of their rules says yes), or
  - (c) the act ends an agreement by which they are bound as a known party (§3.2), meeting its ending condition (5.5.2): they are bound by that ending, as the scheme of signatures they agreed to says, and by nothing else in the act: Terms that carry a closing pointer bind only their own signers (5.5.4). No ending binds anyone through an unknown party's filling (§3.2): such a filling is bound by the Terms its proof names, and the agreement's ending does not end it (5.4.3, 5.5.3, §5.10).
- **5.6.2** **An agent's assent, and an ending.** An assent MAY be made for a party under a power of sort `assent`, as any assent may (5.6.1(b)): that is acting for someone, and it is counted as that party's signing. An assent so made to Terms that carry a closing pointer signs that pointer for the party, as its own signature, and counts toward the ending condition of the agreement it names (5.5.4), unless the power's rule says otherwise (K3). Which acts a power to assent reaches is that power's rule's to say, and a verifier reads its output and nothing more.
- **5.6.3** An act with no `for` and no `under` is made in its signer's own name only. It MUST NOT later be counted as made for anyone else. An act with `for` and `under` is made in the name its `for` names, and binds only as 5.6.1(b) says: it binds the grantor (5.6.6), who gave the power, and never its signer, the agent, as an act of the agent's own. A power Terms carry is given by their known parties, and binds only them (5.6.1(b)). So it is where the grantor is an agreement acting through powers its Terms give (5.1.2): the agreement is bound, as its Terms say, and the signer who acted for it is not bound by that act as an act of its own.
- **5.6.4** **An act under a power whose agreement has ended.** Where whoever is bound through `for` is bound only through Terms whose agreement has ended (5.5.3), they are bound by the act if it was made before that ending, and not bound if it was made after. A verifier MUST answer from knots (§5.13), **as shown**: the person is bound where the act is shown before the ending, that is, where acts enough for the ending condition to answer valid are shown after the act; not bound where the act is shown after such acts. For an ending by a clock, the act's place against the time is shown only by its own anchoring to that clock (5.8.5), **per clock**; an act not anchored is not shown as before or after it. Where nothing shows their order, the act is **not shown** as binding through those Terms, and nothing more: the act and the ending are no fork (§5.12). What the agreement still owes after its ending is delivered under it as 5.5.3 says.
- **5.6.5** **Constitution, executive and judicial.** Three things are kept apart, and this rule is the one place that says so.
  - (a) **The constitution is the ending condition: how the agreement ends.** An agreement ends only as its ending condition answers, who must sign included; with none named, when every known party has signed (5.5.2). It changes in nothing: a change is its end and a new agreement (5.5.1).
  - (b) **The executive is the powers: who may act under the Terms, and in which kinds of act.** An act made for a person or for an agreement, a Statement of a kind (5.1.2, 5.6.1(b)). A power acts under the Terms as they are. No act made under a power changes what Terms say.
  - (c) **The judicial is set in the Terms.** It is three things. The contest: these grammar rules give it, and whom one reaches is read from the Terms it is made against (§5.11). A judge, where Terms name one: in a socket of theirs, by the cMIP that socket names or by its parameters, fixed by fingerprint (§5.2, §6). And a power to resolve a fork: an entry of `powers` of sort `resolves` (§5.12). Each is read from the Terms.
  - (d) **It changes only by a new agreement.** Who the judge is, whether Terms give a power to resolve and what its rule accepts, and whom a contest reaches, change only by the agreement's end and a new agreement that says otherwise ((a)). No power can change it. A verifier MUST NOT read an act made under a power as naming or replacing a judge; as giving, widening, narrowing or removing a power to resolve; or as changing whom a contest reaches. A cMIP that leans on a judge takes who the judge is from the Terms that name that cMIP, and from no act made under a power.
  - (e) **Using it is not changing it.** A judge's decision binds only through a power (5.6.1(b), §6). A resolve signed under a power to resolve is that power used (§5.12). A contest made for someone under a power of sort `contest` is that person's contest. Each of these acts under what the Terms set, and changes none of it.
  - (f) **An agent within its limits.** An assent made for a party under a power of sort `assent` (5.6.1(b)), to Terms that carry a closing pointer included, is that party's signing where the power's rule says yes (5.6.2). That is not a power changing the judicial: the judicial changes only by the agreement's end ((d)).
- **5.6.6** **Nothing an agent made is set aside.** An act made for a person or an agreement under a power (5.6.1(b)), or as an agreement's signature (5.1.2), is an act like any other once made. Call the **grantor** the one in whose name it was made (the one who gave the power, or the agreement whose power it was). No act of the grantor, and no act of anyone else, sets aside, voids or undoes an act made under its grant. The grantor acts on what was made under its grant only as these grammar rules give, and as anyone may act on any act:
  - (a) **On the grant.** By the end of the agreement whose Terms give the power, as its ending condition decides (5.5.2), and a new agreement that gives it no more or gives it narrower; or as the power's own rule says, which answers when the power ends (task K3). An ending reaches only acts made after it; an act made before it binds as 5.6.4 says.
  - (b) **On what was made.** By the end of the agreement the act was made in, as its ending condition decides (5.5.2), the default being every known party. The grantor is among the known parties where it is one; an assent counted in its name, its agent's included, is its signing (5.6.2). What the agreement owes under that act is still delivered under it (5.5.3).
  - (c) **Before the Terms are in force.** By a counter signature in the grantor's name (5.4.5), as for any assent, while it is available (5.4.5: until the assents that put the Terms in force are shown before it). It cancels the assent counted in that name, and sets nothing aside: the agent's assent stays an act, shown as cancelled. A counter signature that comes after the completion, or one made once the Terms are in force, changes nothing (5.4.5), and only (a) and (b) remain.

  **Who is covered.** Whoever agreed to what the agent signed keeps what those Terms gave them: Terms are never replaced (5.5.1); a known party who signed them is bound by an ending they did not sign only where 5.6.1(c) says so; and a filling is governed by the Terms its proof names, which the agreement's ending does not end (5.5.3, §5.10). A verifier MUST NOT read any act as setting aside, voiding or undoing an act made under a power, because it was made by an agent, or because the grantor later acted against it. It reads the grantor's later acts as what they are: an ending, a new agreement, or, while one is available, a counter signature. A fork is another thing: an act made under a power that is a tip of a fork stops as any tip does, and may be set aside by a resolve (§5.12); that is the reading of a fork, not the grantor acting on its grant.

### 5.7 A signer's own line

- **5.7.1** Every act within an agreement MUST name in `previous` its signer's previous act within the same agreement, or none if it is the signer's first. A signer's line starts afresh in each agreement: the first act of a signer within a new agreement names none, whatever that signer made within an agreement it closes. An act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement (an act of another signer, or of another agreement; a report; a payment; any record that is no Terms act and no Statement) is **invalid** (§3.6). Where `previous` names nothing the verifier holds, the act counts, and the verifier shows that signer's line as **unchecked** before it. So it is where `previous` names an act the verifier holds and cannot open (a private act, held without its key): what that act is cannot be read, so the act counts, the signer's line is **unchecked** before it (§3.6), and the act is not invalid. An act held that is not an Agreements act (§3.6) is a record that is no Terms act and no Statement: an act whose `previous` names it is **invalid**. Whether two acts of a line are a fork, and whether a line is unchecked before an act, are answered from the acts held, **as shown**. Whether anything is later in a line is an answer of absence, and is given as 5.5.3 says.
- **5.7.2** Two acts by one signer within one agreement that name the same `previous` (or that both name none) are a fork, whatever else orders them: where `prev` or another knot shows one after the other (5.13.3), the line is still forked. A verifier holding both MUST answer **fork** for that signer. Where the Terms name keepers, an act that is not at the stated where is no act of the agreement and no tip: it makes no fork (5.8.2); where a keeper cannot be reached for it, whether it is a tip is **unchecked** (5.8.3). It MUST NOT choose between the tips. Both tips stop (§5.12), and the fork is resolved only as §5.12 says. Where the two acts are assents, neither counts for an ending (5.5.2), nor for Terms' coming into force (5.4.1), until that signer's line is resolved, by a resolve in it (§5.12).

### 5.8 The keeper, and the clock

- **5.8.1** **The keeper.** Terms MAY name, in `keeper`, one keeper or several, each by its identifier as a relay. A keeper so named is the stated where of the agreement's acts: the way the drafters of the agreement agree on where its acts are, so that the where itself can be checked, against what others hold and what the acts name. The core asks of a keeper what it asks of any relay: it accepts the lodge, relays the act and lets clients fetch it (§3.5). It may do more, as a service; the core depends on nothing more. A keeper gives no order and vouches for nothing: every verifier checks each act for itself, and knots order acts (§5.13).
- **5.8.2** **An act counts where it is at the where.** Where the Terms name keepers, an act counts for the agreement where it is at the where they state, at as many of them as 5.8.3 asks. An act that is not there is no act of the agreement: it counts for no answer, and makes no fork (5.5.5, 5.7.2). Copies of an agreement's acts MAY be lodged at any relay; a copy at a relay the Terms do not name is evidence a reader may show, and makes no act count. Where the Terms name no keeper, an act counts wherever the verifier holds it. A keeper's where is for the agreement's own acts: the Terms act and the Statements made within it (§3.1, **Agreement**). Terms that carry a closing pointer are an act of the agreement they make, and count where that agreement's own Terms state (5.5.4).
- **5.8.3** **Several keepers.** Where the Terms name several keepers, at how many of them an act must be is said by the cMIP the same Terms name beside them (task K9): a count or a set of the keepers named, never an order. Where that socket is empty, an act must be at every keeper named. Where an act is not at as many as that cMIP, or this default, asks, a verifier answers **not shown** for it as an act of the agreement, with that reason, and names the keepers that do not hold it. Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). **A keeper that cannot be reached.** Where a keeper whose answer an act needs cannot be reached, whether the act is at the where is **unchecked**, and every signed act the verifier holds that bears on it (the act itself; a signer's reliance on it, 5.13.2; copies; the acts of others that name it) is shown beside that answer as evidence, for people, or a resolver the Terms name, to weigh. **A keeper service.** A keeper that keeps an ordered, signed record of an agreement's acts is a service, not the core's: where the Terms name such a service as the cMIP in this socket, its answer is that cMIP's output, labelled as that service's, and these grammar rules read it as they read any task's output, and take no order from it otherwise.
- **5.8.4** **Order.** Order between an agreement's acts is what their knots show (§5.13). A keeper gives no order, and a relay's holding an act shows nothing of when it was made. The clock gives time only (5.8.5). Where knots show no order between two acts that compete to change the same state, they are a fork (§5.12).
- **5.8.5** **The clock, and anchoring.** Terms MAY name a `clock`. The clock the Terms name is the one every reader uses for that agreement's answers in time, whether a party to it or not, except for a filling (below), qualified **per clock**: for every answer that turns on a time, the ground of an ending condition (5.5.2(c)) among them. **An act that names a clock is anchored to it.** An act's place against a time is shown only by its own anchoring to the clock; an act not anchored is not shown as before or after a time. **Provisional until anchored.** Terms that state a clock are provisional until their anchoring to it is shown: until then, a verifier answers **not shown** for them as in force, with the reason "not yet anchored", and nothing counts under them; once anchored, they are read from their anchoring. For a filling, the clock is the one its own Terms name (the Terms its proof names): how long what a filling gave lasts is counted on that clock (5.4.3, 5.10.2, 5.10.8). A keeper or an anchoring of the reader's own choosing, or one named only by a rule the parties never signed, gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, and never the Terms' own (§3.4). What a clock is, and how it is identified, is for the layer that gives it.
- **5.8.6** **No stated clock, no condition in time.** A condition in time that names no clock the Terms state is refused, not answered: where the bytes show it, the Terms are **invalid** (below); Terms that state a clock and are not yet anchored are not refused, and wait (5.8.5). Where the bytes show it, a verifier refuses it: Terms that do not supply what a cMIP they name, in any socket, declares it needs (a clock, a keeper) are **invalid** (§4.4, case 8). A keeper is not a clock: Terms that name a keeper and no clock do not supply a clock. A cMIP declares what it needs in its own published text, in the form Development fixes (§7), and a verifier reads it from that act; where the verifier lacks the cMIP, this rule is **unchecked** for it. Where only the words show it (the words speak of a time, and no cMIP the Terms name declares a need for a clock), these grammar rules do not read the words (§5.2) and have no grammar rule for it: a verifier gives no answer of these grammar rules about that time. In either case, an anchoring of the reader's own choosing gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, and never the Terms' answer (5.8.5).

### 5.9 The settlement

- **5.9.1** **Settlement.** A Statement MAY name a `settlement` it is tied to: the evidence, from the layer that holds value, that a payment was made. This field is where that layer's proof plugs into Agreements; what a settlement is, is that layer's to say. It names an act and no cMIP, and is not a task. A settlement named is reported on the word of whoever gave it (the MICS, §2). What is done with it (counting only what led to a settlement, dividing what one shows) is a rule's, in whichever socket it sits (§6).

### 5.10 Spots, and the unknown party (the buyer who joins later)

- **5.10.1** A role in `parties` MUST name:
  - its sort (§3.2, **Spot**), one of: done (whoever proves they have done X), received (whoever proves they have received Y), holding (whoever proves they are holding Z), fits (whoever proves their identity fits the conditions in W), or future;
  - its `what`: the X, Y, Z or W, by fingerprint (§5.2), except for sort future (5.10.4);
  - its spot socket (task K5): the cMIP that says who fills it, by fingerprint, with its parameters. The role (its sort and its `what`) is that task's input, and not a task of its own.
- **5.10.2** A spot is filled, by a signer, when its K5 cMIP, applied to the acts held, names that signer on the strength of a **proof**: the acts that earn the spot. A spot MAY be filled many times; each filling is tied to the proof that earned it. Whether a spot holds one filling or many is said by its K5 cMIP. A verifier MUST answer each filling with its proof, each act reported by who signed it (the MICS, §2), **as shown**, naming the keepers its Terms name: a filling's keepers and clock are those of its own Terms (5.8.5). A filling is read from the Terms its proof names, its role and that role's cMIP as those Terms state them: the spot is the one named by those Terms and the role's position among their parties that the question gives. That cMIP is given those Terms and their acts, and nothing a later agreement made.
- **5.10.3** Each signer who fills a spot is a party in it from then on. They are bound only by their own assent (§5.6). Filling a spot does not bind any other party; what binds the known parties to the holder of a filling is the Terms its proof names (5.10.6).
- **5.10.4** A role of sort **future** names no `what`: its K5 cMIP defines who the participant is as well as how they are confirmed. It is for participants none of the four sorts can describe in advance. No verifier can check that a future spot was used only for them; whether it was is the authors' word, carried by them (§5.3). A `what` on a role of sort future is left unread, and a verifier says so (5.2.3).
- **5.10.5** **New identities.** Whether a spot still takes new identities is said by its K5 cMIP, applied to the Terms (the role as they state it), the acts held, and the answers of the clock the Terms name (5.8.5). These grammar rules define no act and no field for closing a spot or for opening it again: an offer stops taking new identities by what its Terms state in advance (a date, a count), as that cMIP reads them, or by its ending: once the agreement is ended, its spots take no new identities (5.5.3). The ending condition is not asked for this answer. A verifier MUST give that answer with its cause, as the cMIP gives it, per clock or as shown (§5.8, §5.13). A condition in time in that answer is answered per the clock the Terms name (5.8.5): an anchoring of the reader's own choosing never gives the Terms' answer (5.8.5, 5.8.6). Whatever that answer is, a filling already earned stays a filling, shown with its proof (5.10.2), and keeps the rights of the Terms it was earned under (5.10.7). Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6).
- **5.10.6** **Nothing that follows reaches a filling.** For an unknown party's filling (§3.2), what exists for the holder is the Terms the filling's proof names (5.10.7): those Terms bind the holder, by the assent that earned the filling (5.4.3), and bind the known parties to the holder, for as long as their words say, on the clock those Terms name (5.10.8), whether or not the agreement has since ended (5.5.3). No ending of the agreement, no new agreement, and no closing pointer reaches a filling in any way, and no power the Terms carry is used for its holder but one they gave in an act of their own (5.10.7): its holder has no say in the agreement's ending (§3.2, 5.5.2), and no ending binds them (5.6.1(c)). A verifier MUST NOT read any ending, or any ending condition's answer, as reaching an unknown party's filling (5.5.2). Where a keeper the agreement names cannot be reached, whether such a filling stands is **unchecked**, with every signed act that bears on it shown beside that answer as evidence (5.8.3), at as many keepers as 5.8.3 asks; it stays bound by its Terms for as long as their words say.
- **5.10.7** **Rights.** An unknown party's filling (§3.2) has exactly the rights offered by the Terms it was earned under, with every condition they put on them. A spot a known party fills is a known party's act (§3.2), and this rule does not stand for it. The Terms a filling was earned under are those its proof names (§5.13), as the spot's cMIP gives them with the filling (K5's row, §6). What the rights and their conditions are is those Terms' (§5.2, task K1); these grammar rules do not read them. Nothing stops those rights but what those Terms' own words say (5.10.8). Nothing changes what a filling earned except an assent its holder signs in their own name (5.6.1(a)), to Terms of a new agreement, or one made for them under a power they gave in an act of their own, a Statement of theirs that the power's rule reads (5.6.1(b), 5.6.5(f)). An assent that fills an unknown party's spot gives no power: a power the Terms carry is given by their known parties and binds only them. A verifier MUST name, with each filling, the Terms it was earned under, and each later act that changes what it earned, with the assent that rests on: the holder's own, or the one made for them under a power they gave, with that power named.
- **5.10.8** **How long.** What a filling gave lasts as long as the words of the Terms it was earned under say, measured on the clock those Terms name, from the anchoring of the purchase; where those Terms name no clock, no term in time can be read from them, and a verifier shows nothing about that term (5.8.6). The cMIP that reads those words (task K1), given those Terms and their acts and nothing a later agreement made, answers whether what the filling gave still applies, and so whether the obligation toward its holder is open (5.5.3); a verifier shows that answer as that cMIP's, with the ground it gives, qualified **per clock**, and gives none of its own; where it lacks that cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). The agreement's ending does not end what an unknown party's filling gave (§3.2). The ending condition is read in one place (5.5.2), and is never asked about a filling. Whether a spot still takes new identities is 5.10.5's. A new agreement MAY offer again, and MAY rest what it offers on values already in earlier acts, named by fingerprint (§5.2, §5.13), or on none. Who fills a spot under it is said by its own K5 cMIP (5.10.2), and a filling under it has the rights its Terms offer (5.10.7).


### 5.11 Legs of a sequence

- **5.11.1** **Sequence, and leg.** A sequence, a leg, and a leg's doer and receiving side are as §3.3 defines them (**Sequence**, **Leg**, **Doer**, **receiving side**). What one agreement gives or owes reaches another through a party named as an agreement (5.1.1) or a receiver that is an agreement (5.1.3); a payment to an agreement, or a payout to one, is a leg whose receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (5.1.4); whoever pays out a payout divided among several is a service, or a party who receives for the others. Each leg is carried out under the Terms that hold its task (5.11.2), or, for a payment, under the offer's Terms the buyer signed, which require it, the payment itself holding the task of paying and its call (5.11.7); the cMIPs that read it are the ones those Terms name (§5.2). Nothing in one agreement's choice of cMIPs binds another agreement, and nothing is chosen for a sequence as a whole. The agreements of a sequence MAY name the same cMIPs; none is required to.
- **5.11.2** **The call.** Terms hold the Control task (K6) where their field `control` names a cMIP (§5.2); or, with that socket empty, where they name as `receiver` one of their own parties (5.1.3) and give a part to any other party, so that this party holds for the others and passing on to them is its obligation under those Terms. Whether Terms give a part to a party is read from their words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1): a share for any party other than the receiver. Where the verifier cannot read the body, or lacks the cMIP in `control`, whether those Terms hold the task is **unchecked** (§3.6). Terms that hold the task MAY carry a call for it, in `calls`, as the report block of the shared language gives it (the MICS, §2): from whom a report is expected (the doer, the receiving side, or both). A call names no keeper and no reader (§3.1). For K6, the doer is whoever carried out the passing on (the controller: a service, or a party that receives for the others): the party those Terms name as `receiver` among their own parties. Where the receiver those Terms name is not one of their own parties (another agreement, or none), no doer is shown under those Terms: what reaches them is passed on under the Terms of the agreement it reaches (5.1.4). And the receiving side of each leg is whoever something was passed on to; where that is an agreement, it is that agreement's receiver for the act (5.1.4), and no other of its parties. The call is made by the signers of those Terms, and binds no one else. In a deal, those signers are the service and the agreement it serves, which signs by its own signature (5.1.2): the call is that agreement's parties' as much as the service's, and each party that signature binds has signed the deal through its agreement. Whoever receives is the receiving side of its leg whether or not it signed the Terms that carry the call. Where it did not, itself or through an agreement's signature, these grammar rules do not make the path to it a signed one: closing that gap is a cMIP's (§6). A call is attached to a task, in the Terms under which that task is carried out; none is attached to a receiving side. A call in Terms that do not hold the task it is attached to is left unread, and the verifier says so (5.2.3). These grammar rules make no Terms carry a call, and make no one answer one. A call is part of signed Terms and is read as they are (§5.2), by anyone, before any payment is made.
- **5.11.3** **Leg report.** A leg report is a report that answers such a call. As every report does, it carries the call it answers and the acts it relied on, each by fingerprint, and its signer. Among the acts it relied on are the Terms it passed on by (§5.13) and each settlement it speaks of (5.9.1). Beyond that, it carries what K6's row says (§6). Each signer reports its own half only. What K6's row asks of a leg report MUST be readable without any cMIP those Terms name. Where one doer carries out several legs under one call (a payout to several), which lines of its report each receiving side receives (all of them, or those of its own leg) is said by the cMIP that answers the task (K6). These grammar rules do not say it. How a leg report is read is the MICS's report block's (§2).
- **5.11.4** **Before paying.** Asked which agreements of a sequence carry a call for reports, a verifier answers from their Terms alone (5.11.2), one agreement at a time, as shown (§3.6), and **unchecked** where it lacks them. It answers in the same way, from the same Terms, from whom each call expects reports, and whether those Terms name a data cMIP, in `data` (task K8), by which their signers agreed to a path beyond the leg (5.11.5): **shown**, with its fingerprint, where they name one; where `data` is empty, it answers that no path beyond the leg is agreed in those Terms. For an offer, that answer is also the path a buyer agrees to for their own leg by signing its Terms (5.11.6). The agreements of a sequence, for this question, are found from the agreement asked about by following `receiver` from agreement to agreement, until one names one of its own parties, or none (5.1.4). Parties named as agreements are not walked: which of them a payment reaches is their own Terms' and cMIPs' to say.
- **5.11.5** **Reports are data: a path beyond one leg.** A report is data. A path by which a report, or what it states, reaches anyone who is not a side of its leg (a partner seeing further, a request from upstream) is a path the people whose data it carries agreed to, with their signature. It is built as a cMIP about data, focused on agreements, which Terms they signed name by fingerprint (§5.2). These grammar rules give no such path themselves: no call, no field and no answer of this text carries a report beyond its leg, and a verifier MUST NOT read any as doing so. What such a cMIP says is its own: who may read what, how a reader puts together the legs it may read, how far it follows, and what a loop answers. **The data task.** Terms name such a cMIP in the field `data`: the task K8 (§6). Its one question, asked for one reader and one report of a leg carried out under those Terms: may this reader read it, or what it states, beyond its leg? It answers yes or no. These grammar rules take that output and add nothing to it. The socket speaks for the people bound by the Terms that name it (5.6.1), and for no one else. Where it is empty the answer is no: one leg. Where a report carries the data of people bound by different Terms (the two sides of a leg), the data cMIP of each is asked, and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes. **Which Terms are asked.** About one report: the Terms that carry the call it answers; and, for each agreement a line of the report names as passed on to or owed to, that agreement's Terms (5.1.4). A receiving side named as a signer of the Terms that carry the call is asked through those Terms. Where Terms that are to be asked are not held, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6). What a data cMIP is beyond that socket is not said here.
- **5.11.6** **Who has agreed.** A verifier MUST NOT read the people of a leg as having agreed to be read from beyond their leg unless Terms they signed say so (5.6.1): by naming such a data cMIP in `data` (K8), or by a rule in them under which others decide it. Which consent a data cMIP asks of the people it reads is that cMIP's to say. Which cMIP, and which rule, apply is what the signers chose when they made the agreement, and is read from the Terms they signed (§5.2). Where those Terms say that a majority decides, the majority decides (5.6.1(b)): a verifier reads a member who was outvoted as that rule says, and MUST NOT answer, beyond it, whether that member agreed. **What the buyer agreed to.** A buyer signs Terms: those of an offer, by an assent to the Terms they buy under, and paying is what those Terms require of them (5.11.7). So a buyer is bound by those Terms by their own assent (5.6.1(a), 5.10.3), its data socket included. For a report of the buyer's leg (a confirmation of a payment), the Terms asked are those the payment names (§5.13), where an assent of the payer to them is held: its data cMIP speaks for the buyer, and for the receiving side where the receiver for the payment is one of those Terms' own parties (5.1.4). Where the receiving side is reached through another agreement (5.1.4), that agreement's Terms are asked too, as for any report that carries the data of people bound by different Terms (5.11.5), and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes. Where those Terms' `data` is empty, the answer is no: one leg. Where no assent of the payer to the Terms its payment names is held, the payer is not shown as bound by those Terms, nothing they signed says that they agreed to be read from beyond their leg, and the answer is no for every reader who is not a side of that leg.
- **5.11.7** **The buyer's leg.** Paying an agreement is a leg (5.11.1). Its doer is the buyer: a signer of the offer's Terms, whose assent names the Terms they buy under, and whom those Terms require to pay (5.11.6). Paying is the consequence of the Terms the buyer signed, as for any signer of any Terms; the buyer differs only in that their identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed it (§3.2, **Offer**). The buyer also signs the payment (§5.13), which carries out what those Terms require. Its receiving side is the receiver, for that payment, of the agreement paid (5.1.4). A payment is the act that holds the task of paying: the Control task (K6), with the payer as its doer. The payer, its signer, may call for the receiving side's confirmation, as the signer of any act that holds a task may (the MICS's report block, §2). These grammar rules read every payment as carrying that call: the buyer's confirmation by default is that call, made by default, and no Terms need write it. The confirmation is a report that answers it. A report names the call it answers by naming the act that holds it; for this call, made by default, it names the act that stands for it: the settlement the payment names, the act both sides can see, and never the payment itself. A report whose call names that settlement is the confirmation of that payment. The payment is the doer's half of that leg: its signer is the buyer, and it names the settlement. The confirmation is the receiving side's report, its signer's word (the MICS, §2). How a payment and its confirmation are read together is the MICS's report block's (§2). After the confirmation comes access to what was bought. What that is, and who gives it, is said by the Terms the payment names and their cMIPs (5.10.7, §5.13; delivery, §6); these grammar rules do not read it. By default the buyer is shown nothing further. What the receiving side then does with the payment is further legs, and their reports go to the two sides of each (the MICS, §2). The buyer's own leg is read beyond it only as the data cMIP of the offer's Terms the buyer signed gives (5.11.6); the buyer reads a further leg only as a data cMIP the people of that leg agreed to gives (5.11.5). The call binds only its signer, the payer: these grammar rules make no one confirm (5.11.2).
- **5.11.8** **The contest: an act, made against an offer or a vow.** A contest is an act of these grammar rules: a Statement of kind `contest`. It is made against an offer or a vow (§3.1, **Vow**; §3.2, **Offer**): its `agreement` names the root of that offer or vow, and its `terms` names its Terms. Whom it reaches follows from what it is made against: the signers of that offer or vow (one level, 5.1.2), and no one else. A contest made against a vow is read exactly as one made against an offer. A verifier reads a contest made against any Terms alike: whether they name a party by role is shown beside it, and changes no answer. The signers a contest reaches are the parties those Terms name as signers; a signer who fills a role is not counted among them. Its `terms` names the Terms of the agreement its `agreement` names, in force or ended; otherwise the contest is **not shown**. A contest is its signer's word, reported as such (the MICS, §2), and stands in its signer's own line (§5.7). **A buyer's contest** (§3.1, **Contest**) is made against the offer or vow the buyer bought under, names the Terms the payment read (§5.13), and names in `settlement` the settlement the payment names, the act both sides can see (5.9.1), and never the payment itself. These grammar rules read a buyer's contest for that case only. A verifier shows a contest as a buyer's where it holds a payment by the same signer that names the same settlement and the same Terms; where it holds no such payment, whether the contest is a buyer's is **unchecked** (§3.6). That it was not delivered is the buyer's word (the MICS, §2). A verifier MUST NOT read a contest as contesting anyone but the signers of what it is made against: not a service those signers picked, not a deal of theirs, not any other agreement of the sequence. It MUST NOT read a contest as giving its signer sight of any leg (5.11.5, 5.11.7). Where those signers believe the fault is their service's, they contest the service: a contest of their own, made within the deal they signed with it (5.1.2), which reaches the service and is read no further. Whether such a deal, signed by parties who were known, is an offer or a vow is not said. A baker may contest Terms for another envelope of the same work (5.11.9). Who else may make a contest, and when, is not said. What a contest does beyond standing on the record, who answers it and how, and how money comes back, are not these grammar rules' (§6).
- **5.11.9** **A baker may contest.** The holder of the baker's rights in an envelope, as the envelope's chain names them (Envelopes, §2), MAY make a contest (5.11.8) against Terms that name another envelope of the same work. It reaches the signers of those Terms, is its signer's word (the MICS, §2), and stands on the record. The holder of the baker's rights is read at the act the contest cites: a contest names the contested Terms in `terms`, and in `relies_on` the act of the chain of its signer's own envelope that it relies on (5.13.2), and the holder is read at that act, not from the contested Terms. A contest by a signer who is not the holder at that act is shown as a contest, and **not shown** as a baker's. Who holds the baker's rights, and what follows from them, are Envelopes' (§2). That its signer is the one who made the work is not shown by any act.


### 5.12 Forks

- **5.12.1** **A fork is a fork.** Where two acts compete to change the same state, with no order shown between them, these grammar rules know one thing: the fork (§3.3). Where a fork exists there may be a race between its sides, or it may be resolved among all involved; these grammar rules do not say which, and read both alike. Neither act takes effect, and the earlier state stays, until the fork is resolved by a new act, made in an order that can be told, as the agreement's own rules allow (5.12.4, 5.12.5). A verifier answers **fork**, and MUST NOT choose between its sides. How forks are treated in different cases is not these grammar rules': it is the cMIPs' that the parties chose, and the parties' own.
- **5.12.2** **Which forks.** A fork, its tips, and for a line its shared act and branches, are as §3.3 defines them (**Line**, **Fork**). These grammar rules see three: a fork of one signer's line, by `previous` (5.7.2); two acts that each end the same agreement (5.5.5); and Terms coming into force against a counter signature that cancels one of the assents they need (5.4.5). Only one act may follow each act of a line. Two acts of one signer that both name no `previous` are a fork with no shared act. An act that is not at the stated where is no tip (5.8.2).
- **5.12.3** **Both tips stop.** While a fork stands, neither tip counts, and nothing built on either tip counts: no act that follows a tip in the line, and no act that rests on a tip or on an act built on it (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), however many namings lie between. For two endings, neither ends the agreement, which stays in force; the Terms that carry each pointer are read as 5.5.4 says for a pointer that closes nothing, until the fork is resolved. Every act that rests on a tip (through `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`) counts for nothing while the fork stands. A verifier MUST show both tips, and what it holds that is built on them, with the fork; MUST answer **fork** where 5.4.5, 5.5.5 and 5.7.2 say; and MUST NOT choose between the tips, by position or in any other way. A fork stands until 5.12.4 resolves it. A fork of a signer's line made after an agreement's ending does not reach back: the ending stands, and the fork is that signer's, resolved in that signer's line (5.7.2). The fork is made after the ending where the tip that competes with that signer's counted act is shown after acts enough for the ending (§5.13); where nothing shows that, the agreement is **not shown** as ended, with the reason that a line whose act it counts forked, no order being shown.
- **5.12.4** **The resolve.** A fork is resolved by a Statement of kind `resolve` that names every tip in `resolves`, by the list of the tips' ids (§4.3), and one of them in `continues`. For a fork of one signer's line, it is an act of that line whose `previous` names the shared act, and it resolves the fork only when that signer signs it (§5.7), or it is made under a power to resolve (5.12.5). For two endings, or for a counter signature against Terms' coming into force, it is made within the agreement under a power to resolve its Terms give (5.12.5); with none, the fork stands (5.12.7). A resolve that resolves is answered **shown**, naming this rule. The tip it names in `continues` then counts: for a line, as the next act of the line, which goes on from that branch; for two endings, as the ending of the agreement; for a counter signature, as what the resolve continues. What is built on that tip counts as these grammar rules read it. Every other tip is set aside (§3.3, **Set aside**), and no act that follows it in the line counts; a pointer set aside closes nothing (5.5.4). A resolve is not a tip of the fork it resolves, and carries no words of its own: it carries only what §4.3 says it carries. An act that names `resolves` and does not in fact resolve the fork it sits in (it is not signed as this rule asks, or does not name every tip, or there is no fork: what it names in `resolves` is one act and no other) is an act like any other, and may itself be a tip: a fork is not hidden by adding the field. Where what it names in `resolves` are not the tips of a fork, a verifier answers **not shown** for it as a resolve, with the reason: nothing to resolve; where it fails otherwise, **not shown**, with the reason it fails (not signed as this rule asks, or not naming every tip). Where such an act names the shared act of a line as the one before it, it is a third act after that act, and the line's fork has one more tip. A resolve MUST name every tip, and MAY name more acts than the tips. Where the two tips of a signer's line both name no `previous`, a resolve that also names none resolves them. **After a line's fork is resolved.** An act that names the resolve as the one before it, in `previous`, is read as following the last act of the continued branch. So the next act MAY name either. But two acts, one that names the resolve and one that names the last act of the continued branch, both follow the same act, and are a fork.
- **5.12.5** **A power to resolve.** Terms MAY give, in advance, a power (§5.6) to sign the resolve of a fork: an entry in `powers` of sort `resolves`, whose rule (task K3) says who may sign it, and which resolve it accepts. A resolve signed under such a power resolves the fork as 5.12.4 says. A power to resolve is part of the judicial: it is set in the Terms, and changes only by the agreement's end (5.6.5). For a fork in one signer's line, it is in Terms by which that signer is bound: a power to make that act for the signer (5.6.1(b)): the resolve then stands in the line of whoever signed it, its `previous` is that signer's own, its `for` names the signer whose line forked, and it resolves by naming the tips. A power of sort `resolves` is for Statements of kind `resolve`, and for no Statement of another kind. How the fork is resolved is the power's rule's: it may accept only the resolve it computes from the acts, whoever signs it, or accept the one a named person signs (a judge, named in those Terms: 5.6.5; §6), or anything else; these grammar rules force no single way. A verifier MUST report such a resolve with the power it was signed under and whoever it rests on (5.6.1; the MICS, §2). Its answer about it is **shown**, naming this rule, the power and its rule's answer, and the signer: the ending condition is not asked of it, so it is neither valid nor not valid.
- **5.12.6** **What others relied on stands.** An act signed by someone who signed none of the acts of a branch whose tip is set aside, and that rests on an act of that branch (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), stands: it has the effect these grammar rules gave it, or would have given it had that tip stood alone. A payment that names Terms of that branch as those it read (5.13.2) is such an act. A verifier MUST show that act, and every answer resting on it, as **disputed**. "The acts of a branch" are the tip and what follows it in the line, or, for two endings, the tip and the Terms that carry it; whoever signed one of them is not protected here for what they built on it. What stands differs by act. A payment that names Terms of that branch is read as if the tip had stood alone: its receiver is read from those Terms (5.1.4), and both are shown as disputed. **As if the tip had stood alone** means: the fork is read with every other tip of it, every act built on them, and the resolve, left out. **A filling earned within that branch** by a proof made by someone who signed none of its acts stands in the same way: it is shown as **disputed**, with the Terms it was earned under named (5.10.7). An assent, or another Statement made within that branch, by someone who signed none of its acts, stands as an act, shown as disputed, and binds no one while the branch is set aside: an assent binds only while the Terms it names are in force (5.4.3).
- **5.12.7** A fork that no resolve has resolved stands: in one signer's line, as 5.7.2 says; of two endings, as 5.5.5 says; of a counter signature, as 5.4.5 says.
- **5.12.8** **Rival resolves.** Two resolves of the same fork, each signed as 5.12.4 asks, that continue the same tip resolve it, and are no fork. Two that continue different tips are themselves a fork. Neither resolves the first fork while the other stands: every tip stays stopped (5.12.3). That fork is resolved the same way: by a resolve that names the two rival ones in `resolves` and one of them in `continues`, signed as 5.12.4 asks or under a power given in advance (5.12.5).
- **5.12.9** **A client's duty (client conformance).** A client that holds both tips of a fork MUST NOT show either tip, or anything built on either, as a resolved act: not as the act that counts, not as an ending, not as that signer's latest act. It shows the fork as unresolved, until 5.12.4 resolves it. This is a duty of clients and not a grammar rule a verifier applies. No verifier can check it: what a client showed a person leaves no act. It is written here and nowhere else.

### 5.13 Knots: what an act names, and "shown after"

- **5.13.1** **What an act is read against.** An act is read against the acts it names, in any field that holds a fingerprint (`body`, `terms`, `under`, `agreement`, `closing_pointer`, `previous`, `answers`, `resolves`, `continues`, `relies_on`, `settlement`, `envelope`, `envelope_at`, the layer below's `prev` and `refs`, and any other), the body's field included. Each such naming is a knot (§3.4): any fingerprint in any field of any act the verifier holds, whatever layer either act belongs to; a verifier reads the fields of every layer of the core (§2). A verifier reads knots in fields, never in an act's content. A report is read in the same way, against the call and the acts it names (5.11.3). An agreement's ending does not change what these grammar rules read an act made within it before the ending as having done. Whether the act's signer is bound from then on is 5.4.3, 5.5.3 and §5.6, unchanged.
- **5.13.2** **An act names what it relied on.** An act that relied on an act, of its own agreement or of another, names it: a Statement in `relies_on` where no other field names it, a Terms act in `relies_on` (§5.2). A naming in `relies_on` says that what it names still stands, and is relied on; a naming in `closing_pointer` says the opposite (5.5.4). A verifier MUST NOT read an act as having relied on an act it does not name. A payment to a work names the Terms of the work agreement it read; how a settlement carries that name is for the layer that gives the settlement (Money, §2). Where the verifier cannot reach the where an act it relies on was at, that reliance is evidence beside the answer (5.8.3), and no more.
- **5.13.3** **Shown after.** Act B is shown after act A when the verifier holds acts, each naming the one before by fingerprint, that lead from B back to A. Knots between acts (a fingerprint of one act in a field of another) count wherever the verifier holds them, at the stated where for acts of an agreement that names keepers (5.8.2). An anchoring to a clock places an act in time, not after another act: an act's place against a time is shown only by its own anchoring to the clock the Terms name (5.8.5); any other anchoring, or a keeper's or a keeper service's order, is evidence a reader may show, labelled as that reader's, or a cMIP's output where it is one (5.8.3). This is the one reading of "shown after" in this text, and every grammar rule that asks whether one act came after another reads it so: the pin (5.4.1), a counter signature (5.4.5), two endings (5.5.5), an act under a power whose agreement has ended (5.6.4), a fork made after an ending (5.12.3), and a signer's line (§5.7). It proves that B came after A, and nothing more: never that an act came before one that does not name it, and never that an act does not exist. **A missing act.** Where the acts that could show B after A need an act that is named in a field of an act held, and the verifier does not hold it, the answer is **unchecked** (§3.6): never a yes, and never a no. No grammar rule goes around it.
- **5.13.4** **Order from naming.** An act that names another, directly or through acts that name it, came after it. A verifier MAY give that order as **shown**, from the acts alone, with no keeper and no qualifier. Between two acts of which neither so names the other, no order is shown from the acts alone. **Before or after Terms came into force, or the agreement ended, from naming.** An act came after Terms came into force where the acts it is shown after are enough for those Terms to come into force and be pinned (5.4.1); it came after the agreement ended where they are enough for its ending condition to answer valid (5.5.2). It came before where, counting every act held but those shown after it, they are not enough. Otherwise no order between them is shown.
- **5.13.5** **Anchoring.** Whether anything is later than an act, and what time it was, are never shown by naming. For an agreement whose Terms name a clock, every reader takes the time from that clock, by each act's own anchoring (5.8.5). A verifier MUST NOT give, per a reader's own anchoring, an answer that the clock the agreement names gives. An anchoring of the reader's own choosing, whether or not the agreement names a clock, gives an answer only as that reader's, named so, given beside the Terms' answer and never in its place: it never gives the Terms' own answer, never puts Terms in force, and never ends an agreement (5.8.5). Where the reader accepts an anchoring from which the verifier holds no answer, or holds two that contradict each other, every answer that rests on it is **unchecked** (§3.6), and not **as shown**. That nothing is later than an act is an answer of absence, given as 5.5.3 says. A verifier MUST give each answer in time **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (**per clock** where it is the one the Terms name), or **as shown**.

## 6. Tasks handed to cMIPs

What an agreement does is always a cMIP's answer, given by the cMIP in its socket; where the verifier lacks that cMIP, the answer is **unchecked**. Beneath every cMIP, these grammar rules keep what no cMIP may break: who signed (§5.1, §5.4, §5.6, §5.7), what names what (§5.13), a fork (§5.12), the stated where (§5.8), an anchoring (§5.8), and the unknown party's protection (§5.10, 5.5.3). The nine tasks (§3.1, **Task**) are listed here, each with its one question, what the cMIP in it accepts and produces, where these grammar rules use its output, and whom it makes you trust. A list field has one socket for each entry: each entry of `powers` is its own socket, and so is each role in `parties`. So an agreement may name many rules, and never two cMIPs in one socket. Every cMIP in a socket is also given its own parameters, the acts held, and the answers of the clock the Terms name (§5.8); the column "Accepts" lists what it is given beyond those. A cMIP declares what it needs (a clock, a keeper) in its own published text, and a verifier reads it from that act; Terms that do not supply it are invalid (5.8.6). The form of the declaration is open (§7).

### 6.1 Modules: what a cMIP in a socket may lean on

A cMIP in a socket may use modules to give its answer. A module is a way of answering, not a task: no field of an act names it; the cMIP in a socket does (§3.1, **Module**). Each says whom an answer makes you trust, and a verifier reports that with every answer (the MICS, §2).

| Module | What it answers | Whom it makes you trust |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule** | what follows from these acts: yes or no, a list of who, or amounts; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone | none: anyone can recompute |
| **Identity** | who is behind this signer: signed statements by those who vouch | a witness (Sign layer) |
| **Assurance** | what was done to make it likely that a party was able, free and understood: signed statements of what was done; never a pass | a witness |
| **Witness** | what happened in the world: each named witness's signed statement | the witness |
| **Tally** | what a vote decided, without showing who voted how: the result, with whatever proof the method allows | the teller |
| **Delivery** | whether an act reached a person: the recipient's signed acknowledgment, or the Deliver layer's evidence | the recipient, or the Deliver layer |
| **Judge** | what the named judge decided, given the Terms (with `authors`, 5.3.2) and the acts of the dispute: a signed decision, which binds only through a power (K3, §5.6). The judge is named in the Terms, by the cMIP in a socket or its parameters, and is changed only by the agreement's end, never by a power (5.6.5) | the judge |

Two more things answer questions and are not sockets of an act.

- **The keeper** (`keeper`) and **the clock** (`clock`) are named by Terms by an identifier, not as cMIPs (the cMIP beside the keepers is K9). What the keepers hold, and the clock's anchorings, are another layer's (§5.8), and are given to every cMIP in a socket. Whether a clock is itself a module under a cMIP of another layer is that layer's to say.
- **Standing**: what one signer's acts show about them, to anyone who looks. A reader chooses the cMIP; no act names it, so it is no task of an act. It computes from signed acts only, qualified as shown; anyone can recompute it, and nobody keeps it.

## 7. Open technical parameters

The values these grammar rules depend on and this text does not fix.

- **The set of acts: its hash and its label** (§3.1, §4.1). Fixed by the encoding (encoding 3).
- **Which fields take the set form** (§4.1). Whether any field of §4.2 or §4.3 holds a set of acts as one value; in this text none does.
- **The empty set** (§3.1, §4.1). How its value is written, and where a field or a parameter may hold it.
- **How Terms name their clock** (§4.2, `clock`; 5.8.5). What identifies a clock, and whether the `clock` field stands as written.
- **How an act is anchored to a clock** (5.8.5, 5.6.4). What the clock's layer gives as an act's anchoring, and how a verifier holds it.
- **How a cMIP declares what it needs** (5.8.6, §6). The form in which a cMIP's own published text states that it needs a clock or a keeper: Development's.
- **How far a verifier follows** (5.1.4). How many agreements a receiver is followed through before a verifier stops and answers **unchecked**, saying how far it followed.
- **The keepers' cMIP** (§4.2, `keeper`; 5.8.3; K9). Where its socket is written beside the keepers, and under which key: the encoding's (encoding 3).
- **The closing pointer's field** (§4.2, `closing_pointer`; 5.5.4). Its name and its key: the encoding's (encoding 3). This text calls it `closing_pointer`.
