## 1. Purpose

Agreements is a grammar: the acts in which agreeing is written (Terms and Statement); the grammar rules by which a verifier decides, from acts alone, **who is bound by which Terms, and what is shown, unknown or contradicted**; and the tasks that cMIPs fill so that people can agree on anything, by rules they choose. Its central idea is that an agreement can be a party to another agreement (G1): works, services, deals, delegations and collectives are built with it, and none is a separate thing in the grammar. Agreements does not decide what anyone must agree, and it does not decide whether anything happened in the world: it records who said so. It does not define how words are fixed, how signatures work, how acts travel, how a shared order is kept, or how value is held; it names those operations (State, Sign, Deliver, Order, Control) where it needs them. Every grammar rule here answers two questions: which lie does it expose that signed, ordered text cannot already expose, and whom does it protect who did not choose it.

**One constraint on everything below.** Every grammar rule is checked from copies of acts alone, plus the answers of a named place where G7 applies. No grammar rule needs a particular server, keeper, platform or software author to remain. What an act fixes must still be there and unaltered when needed (#9 Keep), and an outsider holding copies must be able to test it (#10 Check): Agreements relies on both and adds no grammar rule for them.

## 2. Definitions

Each word here means one thing throughout this text.

- **Act**: a signed statement in the core's sense. Published or signed again, never updated. The fields the core gives every act (who signed, the signature, the act's own fingerprint) are not repeated here.
- **Fingerprint**: the identifier the core gives to fixed bytes. "Named by fingerprint" means referred to by that identifier and nothing else.
- **Grammar rule**: one of the fourteen of section 4, written G1 to G14, with clauses written G4.8. It says how acts are read. It never says what people must agree. Always called a grammar rule; the word "rule" alone is never used for one.
- **Task**, or **socket**: a field of an act where a cMIP plugs in and answers one question. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "task has always been the socket in my mind. One cMIP per socket, multiple modules where needed, possibly multiple sockets in one cMIP." Every field of an act that names a cMIP is a task, and nothing else is. A task says its one question, what the cMIP in it accepts, what it produces, and whom it makes you trust. The eight tasks are K1 to K8 (section 5). The same question asked in two fields is two tasks: the rule in `in_force` and the rule of a power are not one task. These grammar rules take a task's output and use it where they need it; they never compute it themselves. A socket holds at most one cMIP, with its parameters. A socket left empty has the default these grammar rules give it, where they give one, and otherwise asks nothing.
- **Module**: a part a cMIP uses to give its answer: a rule that computes, a witness, a judge, a teller and the like (section 5.3). A cMIP may use several. A module is not a task: no field of an act names it; the cMIP in a socket does.
- **cMIP**: a document, named by fingerprint, that fills a socket: it answers that task's question, for some kind of thing or situation. Anyone may write one. Many cMIPs may fit the same socket in different ways (one for land, another for shares). One cMIP may fill several sockets, of one act or of many; it is then asked each socket's question apart. Choosing a cMIP is how people choose their own rules.
- **The research's 49 tasks** (#1 to #49): the questions an agreement may have to answer, from `agreement-tasks.md`. They are a checklist of what can go wrong, not this text's tasks. Where each one lands in this text is recorded in `companion-3.md`.
- **Terms**: an act that states what is agreed. Always capitalised and singular ("a Terms act", "these Terms"). Stating Terms is not agreeing to them.
- **Statement**: any other act made within an agreement. Its **kind** says what it is (task K7). Three kinds are defined here: **assent**, **counter signature** and **contest**.
- **Assent**: a Statement by which its signer agrees to one Terms act.
- **Counter signature**: a Statement by which an earlier assent to one Terms act is cancelled: its signer's own, or one made in the same party's name (G4.10). It is available only until every party has signed (G4.10). It sets nothing aside: the assent it cancels stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled (G5.7).
- **Agreement**: the first Terms act together with every Terms act that replaces it, directly or through others, and every Statement made within them. Its identifier is the fingerprint of the first Terms act (the **root**).
- **Signature of an agreement**: an act made in an agreement's name, under a power its own Terms give (G1.2). An act signed so is signed by that agreement.
- **Version**: one Terms act of an agreement. The first is the root; each later one replaces the one before (G4.8). **The original**, said of an update, is the version that update would replace: the word is that of Nobody, allegedly (G4.8). The **last version** is the one in force (G4.6; while a fork of Terms stands, the one both branches replace, G4.7).
- **Chain**: the versions of one agreement, each replacing the one before: one agreement's own history. The word is used here for nothing else: not for one signer's acts (a line), and not for anything that links one agreement to another.
- **Sequence**: agreements linked to one another, so that what one gives or owes reaches the next: a payment passing from a work to the works it stands on (G11.1). Never called a chain. The word is used for nothing else.
- **Succession**: the agreements for one baked work by which the baker's rights pass from holder to holder, starting with the baker, each naming the one before it (G14.2). It runs forward in time, and is not a sequence.
- **Leg**: one obligation carried out, between two sides: whoever carries it out, and whoever receives it (G11.1). A payment to a work is a leg between the payer and the work's receiver. A payout divided among five is five legs, each between whoever pays out and one of the five. A leg is never an agreement: an agreement of a sequence is called that.
- **Doer**, **receiving side**: the two sides of a leg. The doer carries out the obligation. The receiving side receives it; where what is owed is owed to an agreement, the receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (G1.4). These grammar rules define the two sides and nothing more: which lines of a report each side receives is the task's cMIP's (G11.3).
- **Report**, **call**: words of MOR's shared language, used here and not defined (section 8.1). In short: a task may carry a **call** for a report, or not; the call says whether a report is wanted and from whom; only the signers of the act that holds the task can make it. A call names no place and no reader: the where is implied by the leg. A **report** is a signed act that answers a call: it carries the call it answers, the acts it relied on, and its signer. Anything more is the task's to say. A report goes to the other side of its own leg, where that side receives, and no further, in every layer (G11.6). A report is data: any other path for it is one people agreed to, with their signature, by naming a data cMIP in Terms they signed (G11.6(a); task K8).
- **Leg report**: a report that answers a call carried by Terms that hold the Control task (K6), about the legs carried out under those Terms (G11.3). What it carries beyond what every report carries is in K6's row (section 5).
- **Buyer**: a signer of an offer's Terms, whose identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed them, and whom those Terms require to pay. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "A buyer signs terms of an agreement with unknown party, that requires the buyer to pay, as a consequence", and "it’s simply that his hash was unknown when the agreement was drafted and signed by its maker." A buyer signs by an assent to the version they buy under, as anyone fills a spot and is bound by their own assent (G10.3, G5.1(a)). Elsewhere in this text, where only the paying matters, the same person is called the payer. Paying is a leg, with the buyer as its doer (G11.7). Draft 20 read a buyer as signing a payment and no Terms; that reading is gone (G11.6(b)).
- **Offer**: in the words of Nobody, allegedly, "an agreement with unknown participant at the time of signing". Read here as: an agreement whose Terms name a party by role (a spot, G10), so that who takes part was not known when the Terms were signed. That reading is Claude's (section 8.4).
- **Vow**: in his words, "the same thing but without bytes attached": the same thing as an offer. This text gives the word no more meaning than that. These grammar rules read a contest made against a vow exactly as one made against an offer, so no answer of a verifier turns on which of the two an agreement is. What "bytes attached" means is open (section 8.4).
- **Contest**: an act of the grammar: a Statement of kind `contest`, made against an offer or a vow (G11.8). It reaches the signers of what it is made against. A **buyer's contest** is a contest by which a buyer says that what they bought was not delivered after payment.
- **Payment**: an act of the layer that holds value, signed by the payer, which names the version it read (G13.2). It carries out what the offer's Terms the buyer signed require of the buyer (G11.7). It holds the task of paying, and by default carries the payer's call for the receiving side's confirmation (G11.7). This text gives it no fields.
- **Never ended**: an agreement never ends. It only gets a new version, by an update its own update condition answers valid (G4.8, G4.9), and goes on as its last version. This text has no defined term for ending.
- **Party**: one named in Terms. Named in one of three ways:
  - as a **signer**: a person, by the identifier the Sign layer gives them (in the core's words, an identity and not one key: section 9);
  - as a **role**: a spot for someone not yet known, linked to a signer later (G10);
  - as an **agreement**: another agreement, by its root (G1).

  Signers, and signers who fill a role, are the parties who may be bound. An agreement named as a party need not sign to be named, and where it has not signed it is not bound. Where it signs, it signs by its own signature, as its own Terms require: one level (G1.2). That signature binds its parties as its own Terms say.
- **Work**: an agreement whose Terms say who owns something and in what shares. A plain word for a use of the grammar, not a separate thing in it: every grammar rule reads a work as it reads any agreement.
- **Shares**: what an agreement's Terms say each party's part is. The grammar gives them no form: they are read from the Terms' words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1), and divided by whichever cMIP needs them (a power's, K3; the one that passes on, K6). No socket is kept for a dividing rule alone (section 8.4). An agreement with shares defines as much or as little of how it governs itself as it chooses (powers, G5); the grammar has no separate notion of a collective.
- **Service**, **deal**: a service is a signer who does a job for an agreement (dividing what it is paid, holding a payment); a hand, in the research's sense (task K6). Its deal is an agreement between the service and the agreement it serves, named as a party. A deal is signed on both sides: by the service, and by the agreement it serves, as that agreement's own Terms require (G1.2). Plain words for a use of the grammar, as "work" is: what makes an agreement a deal, and what is read from it, are the service's cMIP's (section 5).
- **Spot**: a role's place among the parties, before and after it is filled. It has a **kind**: **done**, **received**, **holding**, **fits** or **future** (G10). Whether it still takes new identities is said by its cMIP, from the version in force (G10.5).
- **Fill**: a spot is filled when the cMIP its role names (task K5) says that a signer is linked to it by a proof: the acts that earn it. Each filling is tied to the proof that earned it. A spot may be filled many times; whether it holds one filling or many is its cMIP's (G10.2).
- **Rights**: what a version offers whoever earns a filling under it, with every condition that version puts on it. The grammar does not say what they are, or read them (G10.6).
- **Receiver**: whoever Terms name, in the field `receiver`, as receiving on the agreement's behalf: an identity (one of the agreement's own parties) or another agreement, by its root (G1.3). The word means that field's value and nothing else; the side of a leg that receives is the receiving side.
- **Receiver for an act**: the receiver named by the version of an agreement which that act names, followed through any agreement it names until one of an agreement's own parties is reached (G1.4). Earlier drafts called it the "receiver now".
- **Author**: a party whose words the Terms are.
- **Power**: an entry in Terms saying that a Statement of a given kind, or a resolving update, binds the parties when a given rule says yes. A power is for acting under the Terms as they are. No power makes a Terms act replace Terms: draft 18's power for replacement is gone (G5.2).
- **Constitution**, **executive**, **judicial**: the words of Nobody, allegedly, for three things these grammar rules keep apart: how the Terms may change (the update condition), who may act under them (the powers), and what the Terms set for a dispute (the contest, a judge they name, a power to resolve a fork). They are said once, in G5.6, and nowhere else.
- **Delegation**: Terms in which one party, the **delegator**, gives another, the **agent**, a power to make acts for it. The delegator may be an identity or an agreement (G5.5).
- **Rule**: a cMIP, or a module of one, that computes: named by fingerprint, with its parameters; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone. It is the people's own rule. The word alone always means this, and never a grammar rule. It computes; it trusts nobody. A rule is not a task: it sits in a socket (`in_force`, a power, `condition`), or is a module of the cMIP that does (section 5.3). Drafts 8 to 17 called it task K4.
- **Settlement**: the signed evidence, from the layer that holds value (Control; the Money layer), that a payment was made. Agreements names it and does not define it. The Statement field `settlement` is where it plugs in (G8.5). That field names an act and no cMIP, so it is not a task; earlier drafts called it a socket, a word now kept for tasks.
- **Line**: acts of which each names the one before it: one signer's acts within an agreement, by `previous` (G6); an agreement's Terms, by `replaces` (G4), which is its chain; and any other acts the grammar reads that way (G12.1).
- **Update**: an act that names another act of a line as the one before it, and that all the parties that needed to sign it have signed (G12.1). Where an act has one update, that update is the next act of the line. In plain cases: for an agreement's chain, a version that replaces the one before (G4.8); for one signer's line, that signer's next act (G6.1). A late update (below) is no update of the version it names. The word is that of Nobody, allegedly. It names a new act that follows an earlier one: no act is ever changed (see **Act**). A **proposed update** of Terms is a Terms act that names a version in `replaces`, taken as its bytes, whether or not it yet has the signatures it needs (G4.11).
- **Fork**: two updates of the same act of one line, each signed by all the parties that needed to sign it (G12.1). Three or more such updates are one fork, with that many tips. It is seen from the acts, not from what any person meant. The act both follow is the **shared act**; each of the two updates is a **tip**; a tip, with what follows it in the line, is a **branch**. While a fork stands, both tips stop (G12.2).
- **Late update**: a Terms act whose `replaces` names a version that had already been replaced, where the knots, or an anchoring, show that it came after that version was replaced (G4.7). It replaces nothing and is no tip of a fork. Where neither shows it, it is no late update: it and the act that replaced the version are a fork. The words are Claude's; the rule is that of Nobody, allegedly: "Only the knots or anchoring can".
- **Built on a tip**: an act is built on a tip when it follows the tip in its line, or rests on the tip or on an act built on it: it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch. The words reach as far as the naming goes: an act that names an act built on a tip is itself built on that tip, however many namings lie between (G12.2). One thing is kept apart: an assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, and for nothing else (G12.2).
- **Resolve**, **resolving update**: a fork is resolved by an update of the shared act, signed as an update of that act must be, that names every tip and says which one continues (G12.3). "Resolve" is said of forks only.
- **Set aside**: a tip is set aside when a resolving update continues another tip. It never counted as the next act of its line: for Terms, it never counted as the next version (G12.3).
- **Join**: no longer a term of this text. What earlier drafts called a join that says which branch continues is the resolving update. A join that merged the branches is not kept (G12.3; section 8.4).
- **Client**: the software through which a person reads acts and signs them. A client is not a verifier, and what a client shows a person is not an act. One duty is written for clients, in one place (G12.8).
- **Bound**: a person is bound by an act when G5 says so.
- **In force**: Terms are in force when G4 says so. The words are said of Terms only.
- **Condition**: short for **update condition**. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "Condition is short for update condition". It is the cMIP that a version names, by fingerprint, in the one field `condition`: the task K4. It answers whether the agreement can be updated or not: given the version in force and a proposed update, **valid** or **not valid** (G4.11). Which updates are valid, who must sign them included, and why, is that cMIP's to specify. Nothing else governs an update (G4.8). Nothing triggers, and nothing under an agreement changes by itself. The plain word "conditions", said of what a version's words put on rights (G10.6), on ending a delegation (G5.5), on a role (G10.1) or on what becomes of a work (G4.9), means those words, read where their version's cMIP puts them, and not this.
- **Valid**, **not valid**: said of a proposed update, and of nothing else in this text: the output of the task `condition` (K4), or of its default where the socket is empty (G4.11). The words are those of Nobody, allegedly. The core's "valid" has the same spelling and another meaning: there it is said of an act, where a rule the verifier can apply is seen to pass. This text never says "valid" of an act; of an act it says that it counts, or that it is shown. **Not valid** is a cMIP's answer about an update. **Invalid** is this text's answer for an act that itself shows it cannot count (below). The two are kept apart (P19).
- **Triggered**: no longer a term of this text. Draft 16 gave it as an answer, said of a condition. The condition now answers valid or not valid, of an update (G4.11; section 8.4).
- **Apply**, **no longer applies**: plain words, and not an answer of a verifier. A filling, and the rights it holds, apply from when the filling is earned. They stop applying, or change, only through an update (G10.6, G10.7). These grammar rules do not read what the rights are, and no verifier answers that they no longer apply.
- **Place**: an ordered place to look, read as the layer that orders acts gives it, which can say of an act whether it is there and at what position, and whether anything is later. It is named by an identifier and is not a cMIP, so the field `place` is not a task (section 5.3). What that layer must provide is listed in section 8.1.
- **Name** (or **cite**): an act names another act, or a version, when it carries its fingerprint in one of its fields. What an act names is what it is read against (G13). Each such naming is a **knot**: the word of Nobody, allegedly, for the order acts give by citing (G13.3).
- **Anchoring**: whatever a reader accepts as saying that nothing is later than an act, or what time it was. A place is an anchoring, and so is a clock. The place and the clock an agreement's Terms name are the ones every reader uses for that agreement (G7.4); a reader accepts an anchoring of its own choosing only where the agreement names none (G13.4).
- **Clock**: what an agreement's Terms name, in the field `clock`, as saying what time it was for that agreement. Named here, not defined (section 8.1).
- **Bake**, **envelope**, **baker**: a finished work is baked when its bytes are sealed in an envelope, which says who baked it: the baker. The core's words, named here and not defined (section 8.1). An envelope is named by its fingerprint (G14). Two envelopes **carry the same work** when the core says their bytes are the same work (G14.5); anyone may bake the same bytes, and each bake has its own baker.
- **Data cMIP**: the cMIP that Terms name in the field `data` (task K8): a path, beyond one leg, for the reports of the people bound by those Terms, which they agreed to with their signature (G11.6(a)).
- **Verifier**: whoever applies these grammar rules to a set of acts. A verifier answers questions; it does not decide disputes.
- **Answers**: a verifier's answer is one of:
  - **shown**: the acts held establish it under these grammar rules;
  - **not shown**: the acts held do not establish it; an act not held might;
  - **fork**: two acts are a fork (G12): two acts by one signer name the same previous act (G6.2), or two Terms acts each replace the same Terms (G4.7, G4.8);
  - **valid**, **not valid**: said of a proposed update: the update condition named by the version it would replace answers that the agreement can, or cannot, be updated so; where that version names none, the default answers (G4.11). Always given with that condition named beside it, or with the words "by default", and with the ground the cMIP gives. It is the whole answer about the update, who must sign it included: no power is read beside it (G4.8, G5.2). **Not valid** is not **invalid**: it is a cMIP's answer, it may rest on a time or on the acts held, and it is qualified as they are.
  - **invalid**: an act cannot count as what it claims to be, and the act itself shows it: no act the verifier does not hold could make it count. The cases are: Terms whose `body`, or a cMIP they name, is not named by fingerprint (G2.1); Terms whose `authors` name someone who is not one of their parties (G3.1); Terms that name a party by role and carry no `in_force` (G4.2); an act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement (G6.1); a role that lacks its kind, its `what` or its cMIP, or whose kind or `what` is there and is not one the grammar rule allows (G10.1); and Terms that name an envelope, where the verifier holds that envelope and the Terms name neither the baker it states among their parties nor a succession, and nothing they name is lacking (G14.3). A verifier names the grammar rule with the answer. An invalid act counts for nothing, for anything: no answer of these grammar rules rests on it, and no act counts through it. It stays readable as an act, and a verifier shows it, with the answer invalid. Draft 20 read the case of G14.3 as an answer about the work alone, the Terms being read by every other grammar rule; that reading is gone: those Terms are invalid as any act is (G14.3). The word is meant to mean here what it means in the other layers (P19).
  - *Not an Agreements act.* An act that lacks a required field, or carries a field that section 3 allows only beside another (as `continues` beside `resolves`) without it, or holds in a field something other than that field's kind (a fingerprint where section 3 says fingerprint), or bytes that cannot be read as an act, never reach these grammar rules: section 3 says which. A verifier shows them as **rejected**, which is no answer of this text.

  - *A task's output.* Where a task answers yes or no (K2, K3, K6, K8, and whether a spot still takes new identities, K5), a verifier passes that output on as the task's, with the cMIP named and the ground or cause it gives, and not as an answer of this text; where it lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (G9).

  **The line between invalid and not shown.** If an act the verifier does not hold could change the answer, the answer is **not shown**, never **invalid**. So, under the default, a replacement that lacks a signature is **not shown** as a replacement (G4.8(c)): the missing assent may exist. And Terms that point to an envelope are **invalid** where they name neither its baker among their parties nor a succession: no act anyone could later make puts the baker among the parties those Terms name, so they never count, for anything, their receiver included (G14.3). Where they name the baker, or a succession, or reach the baker only through a role, and the acts held do not show the baker, or the holder of her rights, to be their party as G14.2 asks, an act not held could show it; then the answer is **not shown**, and those Terms count for nothing until it is shown (G14.3). The envelope itself is named by fingerprint: a verifier that lacks it answers **unchecked** (G9).
  - **unchecked**: the verifier could not apply a rule it needed (G9).

  Answers about what is latest, what else exists, or what time it was, carry a qualifier: **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (G13.4), written **per place** where the anchoring is the place the Terms name and **per clock** where it is the clock they name; or **as shown** (meaning: among the acts this verifier happens to hold). An answer that rests on what someone relied on in the branch of a tip set aside also carries the qualifier **disputed** (G12.5). **Disputed** has that one meaning in this text. The core's "disputed" has the same spelling and another condition: there it is shown of an act that its signer's change of key would have made void, and that someone else relied on. Both say that what others relied on stands and is shown so; a verifier that gives both says which one it means.

  Of reports, a verifier also shows what the shared language's block asks it to show, in that block's words, which are not answers of this text: for an act that two reports name, **matched**, **mismatched** or **one side only**; for a call with no report, **unanswered**; for a task with no call, **no report called for** (G11.4).

## 3. The data each act carries

Each field is listed with the grammar rule that reads it. A field no grammar rule reads is not here. An act missing a field marked required is not an Agreements act: it is rejected, and never reaches the grammar rules (section 2). An act that has its fields and breaks a MUST of section 4 which the act itself shows is **invalid** (section 2). Every socket holds a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters, which only that cMIP reads. A field these tables do not give, and an entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint, are not read (G2.2).

**Each field holds one kind of value**, given in its row: a fingerprint; a list of fingerprints; an identifier (of a signer, a place or a clock); a socket (a cMIP by fingerprint, with its parameters); or one of the words the row names. An act whose field holds something other than that field's kind is **rejected**, with three exceptions, which reach the grammar rules: a `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid** (G2.2); an entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint is left unread (G2.2); and a role whose kind is not one of the five, or whose `what` is not a fingerprint, is read as lacking it, which makes the Terms **invalid** (G10.1). A `what` on a role of kind future is not read (G10.4). How each kind is written as bytes is the encoding's (section 10).

An assent's `content` is empty: its `terms` field is what it agrees to. A counter signature's `content` is empty too: its `terms` field names the Terms whose assent it cancels (G4.10). A contest's `terms` field names the version of the offer or vow it is made against (G11.8); what its `content` holds is not read by these grammar rules.

A resolving update (G12.3) of a fork of Terms is a Terms act; one of a fork in one signer's line is an act of that signer, Terms or Statement. `resolves` is what makes an act a resolving update. The fields `resolves` and `continues` are draft 15's `joins` and `continues`; an act that names `resolves` and no `continues` resolves nothing, and is read as an act like any other (G12.3).

A report (G11) is one act type, defined once, in the shared language; every layer's tasks use that one type, and this text uses it and gives it no fields of its own. It is not a kind of Statement. These grammar rules read no report as standing in a signer's line: G6 reads Terms and Statements only, and no fork is seen among reports. They read a report as answering one call; whether one report may answer several is the block's (section 8.1). What a leg report carries is in K6's row (section 5). How a report is written as an act is the block's to say (section 8.1). A payment (G11.7) is an act of the layer that holds value, and this text gives it no fields either.

## 4. Grammar rules

Each grammar rule is given precisely, with the lie it exposes and whom it protects in one line. Its plain words and its reasoning are in `reasons-21.md`, under the same number. MUST and MAY are used as in RFC 2119.

These grammar rules say how acts are read. None of them says what people must agree.

### G1. An agreement as a party

- G1.1 Terms MAY name a party as an agreement, by its root fingerprint.
- G1.2 **Named, and signing.** Naming an agreement as a party asks nothing of it: it need not sign to be named, it is not counted by the default of G4.1 (G4.4), and where it has not signed it is not bound by any act of these Terms, and neither are its parties. A party named as an agreement MAY sign: an assent to these Terms, where their `in_force` rule asks for it (G4.4). Its signing covers what those Terms carry, a call among them (G11.2). It signs as its own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts (a vote, a named delegate). **An agreement's signature.** An act is signed by an agreement x when its `for` names x, by its root; its `under` names Terms of x that are in force (G4.6) and give a power for acts of that kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes. Whoever signs such an act (a named delegate, or whoever the rule of a vote accepts) signs on behalf of x's parties, under the power x gave. That act is x's signature. It binds every party bound by x's Terms, as x's Terms say (G5.1(b)), and no one else. It is an act within the agreement it is made in, and stands there in its signer's own line (G6.1). Where x's Terms give no such power, x signs by every one of its signers: one act by each of them, its `for` naming x and its `under` naming x's Terms in force. Each act has one signer, as the core has it. **Which acts are one signing.** Those acts are one signing of x where they are the same act but for their signer and their place in that signer's own line: they name the same `agreement`, `terms`, `kind`, `content`, `for`, `under`, `settlement`, `resolves` and `continues`; their `previous`, `answers` and `relies_on` may differ. x has signed once an act so matched is held from every one of its signers; for an assent, that is an assent by each of them, `for` x, to the same Terms. Each of those acts stands in its own signer's line (G6.1). Where an act's `for` names an agreement, its `under` MUST name Terms of that agreement: an act made for an agreement under any other Terms is not that agreement's signature. **One level.** Its signers are the parties its own Terms name as signers. A party it names as an agreement is not one of them, and neither are that agreement's people: these grammar rules MUST NOT be read as making an agreement's signing need the signature of anyone inside an agreement it names, unless its own Terms say so. A verifier reads what they require from that agreement's Terms in force (G4.6); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (G9).
- G1.3 Terms MAY name a `receiver`: one of their own parties, named as a signer or as a role; or another agreement, by its root fingerprint.
- G1.4 **Receiver for an act.** Whatever a payer, an act or a rule of one agreement gives or owes to an agreement, it gives or owes to that agreement's **receiver for the act**: the `receiver` named by the version of that agreement which the act that gives or owes names (G13.1). Where that act names no version, a verifier MUST take the receiver from that agreement's Terms in force, per that agreement's own place, qualified **per place** with that place named, or, where it names none, per the anchoring the reader accepts, or **as shown** (G4.6, G7, G13.4). Which Terms of that agreement are in force is answered by G4.5 to G4.8: while a fork of its Terms stands, the receiver is the one named by the Terms both branches replace, and the verifier MUST show the fork with that answer and MUST NOT choose a branch (G4.7). Where the receiver so named is one of that agreement's own parties, the answer is that party. Where it is another agreement (G1.3), the two are agreements of a sequence (G11.1), and a verifier reads that agreement's receiver in the same way, and so on, until one of an agreement's own parties is reached. That party is who receives: the receiving side of one leg (G11.1). These grammar rules follow a receiver no further. What that party then passes on is another leg, carried out under its own agreement's Terms and the cMIPs they name, and is read only as G11 says. If a version read names no receiver, or names as receiver a signer or a role that is not among its own parties, or the spot named is not filled, or is filled more than once (which of them receives is not said), or an agreement is met a second time on the way, no one receives, and the answer is **not shown**. Where the act that gives or owes names, as the version it read, Terms that are not shown as a version of that agreement, the answer is **not shown**. Where it names Terms that name an envelope, the answer of G14.3 comes first: where those Terms are invalid, no one receives by them, and the verifier shows the payment with them, and the answer invalid, G14.3 named; where G14.3 answers not shown or unchecked, so is the receiver. Where it names a tip of a fork that stands, the receiver is the one named by the Terms both branches replace, the fork is shown with it, and the act is shown as built on a tip (G4.7, G12.2); once that tip is set aside, G12.5 says how it is read. If the verifier lacks that agreement's acts, it is **unchecked** (G9). A verifier that stops following receivers from agreement to agreement before a party is reached answers **unchecked**, and says how far it followed (G9.2; P8).
- G1.5 A rule (section 5.3), in any socket, MAY take as an input a receiver for an act, so answered, or what a leg report states (G11.3).

**Lie exposed.** "I receive for that work", said by someone who does not, by that work's own Terms; and "the work agreed", said of a signing its own Terms gave no one the power to make.

**Protects.** The parties of an agreement that is pointed at, who are not bound by what they did not sign; the parties of one that signs, who are bound only as their own Terms say; and the payer, who needs to know only the work.

### G2. Whole

- G2.1 The `body`, every piece in `relies_on`, and every cMIP named anywhere in a Terms act MUST be named by fingerprint.
- G2.2 Anything a Terms act refers to in another way is not part of those Terms. A verifier MUST NOT read it as part of them. An entry of `relies_on` that is not a fingerprint, and a field section 3 does not give, are not read, and do not make the Terms invalid; a verifier says that it left them unread. A `body`, or a cMIP in a socket, that is not named by fingerprint makes the Terms **invalid** (section 2).

**Lie exposed.** "The document we referred to said something else", and "there was a side piece".

**Protects.** Every reader who arrives later, and the party who signed without fetching what was referred to.

### G3. Author

- G3.1 `authors` MUST name one or more of the parties named as signers or roles.
- G3.2 The authors are the parties who bear the failure of the words to mean one thing (#4 Align). A cMIP that reads a judge's decision, in whichever socket it sits (section 5.3), MUST accept `authors` as an input.

**Lie exposed.** "We wrote this together", or "I meant something else by that word", said by the one who wrote it.

**Protects.** The party who accepted words they did not write.

### G4. In force

- G4.1 Terms are in force when the assents their `in_force` rule requires are present. If `in_force` is absent, they are in force when there is an assent to them in the name of every party named as a signer; Terms that name no party as a signer, and carry no `in_force`, are in force at once.
- G4.2 Terms that name a party by role MUST carry `in_force`.
- G4.3 An assent binds its signer only while the Terms it names are in force. About an assent, a verifier answers **shown** where it is counted in a party's name and those Terms are in force; otherwise **not shown**, with the reason (those Terms are not in force, have been replaced, or are a tip of a fork; the assent is cancelled, G4.10). An assent made within a branch set aside is shown as **disputed**, and binds no one (G12.5).
- G4.4 A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of G4.1. An `in_force` rule MAY ask for its signing; it has then signed when what its own Terms require is present (G1.2).
- G4.5 Terms stop being in force when Terms that replace them (G5.2, G4.8) are in force. While a fork of them stands (G4.7), no Terms that replace them are in force.
- G4.6 Which Terms of an agreement are in force is an answer about what is latest. A verifier MUST take it per the agreement's place, qualified **per place** (G7.2); or, where no place is named, per the anchoring the reader accepts, with it named, or else **as shown** (G7.3, G13.4). It MUST NOT give it as latest without saying what the answer stands on. **Which place.** Before any Terms of an agreement are in force, the only place it has is the one its first Terms act names: whether that act is in force is judged per that place, and the clock it names, and so is every assent that counts toward it (G7.2). Whether a later version has replaced the one before is judged per the place the one before names (G4.8(d)). Where versions name different places, the answer that given Terms are in force carries the place those Terms name, or **as shown** where they name none; each replacement's own answer carries the place it was judged by.
- G4.7 Where two Terms acts each replace the same Terms (G4.8), and each has the assents its own `in_force` requires (G4.1), they are a fork (G12): two updates, each signed by all the parties that needed to sign it; but a late update (below) is no update, and no tip. **A late update.** In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "Only the knots or anchoring can". Call **O** a version already replaced; **F** the update that replaced it (the Terms act its update condition answered valid, in force by its own `in_force`; or, where O's replacement was a fork, the resolving update that resolved it, G12.3); and **L** a Terms act whose `replaces` names O, which O's update condition answers valid and which has the assents its own `in_force` requires. L is a **late update** where the knots, or an anchoring, show that L came after O was replaced. A late update replaces nothing, and is no tip of a fork: O stays replaced by F, and the line goes on from F as if L were not there. Where neither the knots nor an anchoring show it, L and F are a fork whose shared act is O, as for any two updates of one version, however late L may in fact be: every version after O is in the branch of F, and stops with it (G12.2).
  - (a) **Which knots count.** The knots are the namings G13.1 reads: every field that holds a fingerprint, of every act the verifier holds, Terms or Statement, of this agreement or another, and a report's naming of its call and the acts it relied on (G11.3). An act came after O was replaced, by knots, where the acts it names, directly or through acts that name them, are enough for F to be valid and in force (G4.1, G4.11), or, for a resolving update, to resolve (G12.3); that is G13.3's reading. L came after O was replaced, by knots, where, counting every act held but those that came after O was replaced, L is not an update of O: O's update condition does not answer valid for it, or it is not in force by its own `in_force`. So L is late by knots where L itself, or an assent without which L is not an update of O, names, directly or through the acts it names, enough acts for F to be valid and in force. A verifier gives that **as shown**, from the acts alone, with no qualifier (G13.3).
  - (b) **Which anchoring counts.** Per place: the place O names (G4.8(d)). L came after O was replaced, per that place, where the first position at which, counting only the acts the place shows at or before it, L is valid and in force comes after the first position at which, so counted, F is valid and in force (G5.4, G13.3); a verifier gives that **per place**. Per clock: where the clock the agreement names (G7.4) gives a time for each of those two events and the two times are ordered, **per clock** (P16). Where O names no place and the agreement no clock, per the anchoring the reader accepts, with it named (G13.4). No other anchoring counts: a verifier MUST NOT read L as late by a time written in an act (G8.2), by a place or a clock the agreement does not name, by what is likely, or in any other way.
  - (c) **The answer.** About L, a verifier answers that it replaces nothing and is no tip, names G4.7 and what shows that L came late (the act of L, or the assent to it, whose namings reach F's acts; or the anchoring and the positions or times it gives), and qualifies the answer **as shown** where knots show it, or **per place**, **per clock** or **per anchoring** where only an anchoring does. Which Terms are in force is then answered as if L were not there. An assent to L binds no one (G4.3), and nothing built on L counts (G12.2). Where L, or an assent it needs, names an act the verifier does not hold, and that act might be what shows L came late, whether L is late is **unchecked** (G9), and so is which Terms are in force from O on (G4.8(e)). Where knots or an anchoring show it, nothing else (a place that shows L before F, a later resolving update) makes L an update again.
  - (d) Whether an update made after another, and not shown to be late, may still be valid is the update condition's to say (G4.11; section 5.2).
  - (e) **While a fork stands.** An assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, so that the fork is seen, and for nothing else while the fork stands (G12.2). A resolving update is not one of them (G12.3). A verifier MUST answer **fork** for which Terms replace them, show both tips, and MUST NOT choose between them, by position or in any other way. Both tips stop (G12.2): until the fork is resolved (G12.3), neither is in force, nothing built on either counts, and the Terms both replace, the last shared version, stay in force. A verifier MUST answer from them every answer that rests on the Terms in force: the powers (G5); the update condition (G4.11); the roles, and who fills them (G10.2); the receiver for an act (G1.4); whether they hold the Control task, and their calls (G11.2); their data cMIP (G11.6(a)); the place and the clock (G7); and what any cMIP they name produces (tasks K1 to K8). It MUST show the fork with each such answer, per place or as shown.
- G4.8 **Replacing.** A Terms act replaces the Terms its `replaces` names (the **original**) only if the update condition of the original answers **valid** for it (G4.11). An update is governed by that one answer, and by nothing else. Who must sign an update is part of it. No power makes a Terms act replace (G5.2). Whether the replacing Terms are themselves in force is G4.1's, as draft 18 has it (G4.5; section 8.4).
  - (a) **Everyone who signed the original** is: each party in whose name an assent to the original counts (a party named as a signer; a signer who fills a role; a party named as an agreement, where it signed, G1.2); and each party bound by the original with no assent of their own, because the update condition of the version before it answered valid for it (G5.1(c)), where the original itself still names them among its parties: a party that a version took out is not asked by a later update. A stranger's assent makes no one a party. **A role's cMIP not held.** Where the verifier lacks the cMIP of a role of the original (K5), who fills that role is not known: whether everyone who signed the original has signed an update is then **unchecked** only where the verifier holds an assent to the original by someone the original does not name as a signer, who might fill that role (G9). Where it holds no such assent, no one who fills a role is shown to have signed the original, and none is asked. They are the ones the default asks (G4.11(e)). Where no one signed the original (its `in_force` rule asked for no one's assent), the default asks for no one's signature, and a Terms act that replaces it needs only to be in force by its own `in_force`; that is listed as open (section 8.4).
  - (b) **Valid.** Where the original names an update condition, the answer is that cMIP's, who must sign included (G4.11). Where it names none, the default answers: an update is valid when everyone who signed the original signs it (G4.11(e)).
  - (c) Under the default, a Terms act that lacks one of those signatures replaces nothing: the Terms it names stay in force, and it is not a tip of a fork. A verifier answers **not shown** for it as a replacement, per place or as shown: an assent it does not hold may exist (section 2, "The line between invalid and not shown"). A Terms act that a named update condition answers not valid replaces nothing either, whoever signed it, and is not a tip of a fork (G4.11(c)); nor does a late update (G4.7).
  - (d) **Which place.** Whether a Terms act has replaced the original is judged per the place, and the clock, that the original names (G7.2), whatever place the new version names: the Terms act and each assent it needs count once that place shows them.
  - (e) **One update that cannot be checked.** Where two Terms acts name the same original, and for one of them the verifier cannot tell whether it is an update (it lacks a cMIP or an act, G9), which Terms are in force is **unchecked** from there on, even where the other is answered valid: the first might be an update too, and the two a fork. The verifier names the last version it has established.
  - (f) For a delegation whose Terms name no update condition, what the default asks of a version that ends or narrows it is said by G5.5.
  - (g) **What is left of the signatures.** Draft 18 asked two things of a replacing Terms act: the signatures the original required (everyone's, or what a power for replacement required), and the update condition's answer. One thing is left: the update condition's answer, or the default's. "Everyone who signed the original" is kept, as the default (a).
- G4.9 Terms stop being in force only as G4.5 says: when a version replacing them is in force. No act ends Terms, or an agreement, without replacing them, and a verifier MUST NOT answer that Terms or an agreement have ended. Asked whether Terms that have been replaced are in force, a verifier answers **not shown**, names the Terms that replace them (G4.5), and shows the agreement as going on in its last version. An agreement only gets a new version: a verifier reads every replacing version alike (G4.8, G5.2), whatever its conditions say becomes of the work and its shares; what they say is theirs (G2, task K1). Every version replaced stays an act of the agreement, readable as it was (G2).
- G4.10 **Counter signature.** No assent binds anyone, and nothing in Terms counts, until those Terms are in force (G4.1, G4.3). A counter signature is a Statement of kind `counter_signature` whose `terms` names the Terms an assent names, and which cancels that assent. Made by the signer of the assent in their own name, it follows that assent in the signer's own line (G6.1): directly, or after other acts of that signer. Made for a party under a power of kind `counter_signature` (G5.1(b)), or as an agreement's signature (G1.2), it cancels the assent counted in that party's name that it names, directly or through acts that name it. It cancels that assent, and no one else's: an assent so cancelled is not counted by G4.1. It sets nothing aside: the assent stays an act, readable, shown as cancelled, and so does what it was made within. An assent made for a party by an agent, under a power (G5.1(b)), is cancelled in the same way as one the party made itself, and in no other: by a counter signature in that party's name, which names that assent, directly or through acts that name it, while the Terms are not in force; the party itself MAY make it, in its own name, as may the agent, as that assent's signer, or another under a power of kind `counter_signature` (G5.7). **Which assent.** A signer may assent, cancel, and assent again. An assent is read against the first counter signature in the same name that follows it; a later counter signature is read only against the assents in that name made after the one before it. A counter signature is available only until every party has signed, which these grammar rules read as: until those Terms are in force (G4.1). One made for Terms that are in force changes nothing: Terms in force change only as G4.5 and G4.9 say. Where a party is an agreement, "signed", for that party, is whatever that agreement's own Terms require: every one of its signers, or what its own rule accepts: one level (G1.2). A verifier reads that from that agreement's Terms in force (G4.6); where it lacks them, the answer is **unchecked** (G9). This is the reading of G1.2. A party named as an agreement is not counted by the default of G4.1 (G4.4), so the reading has effect here only where the Terms' own `in_force` rule asks for that party's signing. Such a party makes a counter signature by the same act as its assent: under a power of kind `counter_signature`, or by all its signers (G1.2). Whether the Terms were in force before the counter signature is an answer about what is latest: per place, or as shown (G4.6). In both, the assent the counter signature cancels is counted as not yet cancelled. With a place, the positions it gives settle it: the counter signature changes nothing where its position is after the position at which the Terms came into force (G13.3), and cancels where it is before. With none, only citing gives an order, as G13.3 reads it for Terms coming into force: where the assents the counter signature names, directly or through acts that name them, are enough for the Terms to be in force, it came after, and changes nothing; where, counting every assent held but those that name the counter signature, directly or through acts that name it, the Terms are not in force, it came before, and the assent it cancels is not counted. Under the default of G4.1 this is: a counter signature that names every assent the Terms need came after them; a completing assent that names the counter signature came after it. Where the Terms would not be in force even with every assent held counted, nothing had completed them, and the counter signature cancels. Otherwise whether those Terms are in force is **not shown**, and the verifier says why. **Its answer.** About a counter signature, **shown** means that it cancels the assent it is read against; **not shown** means that it is not shown as cancelling (it changes nothing, it follows no assent, or the order is not shown), with the reason. Where counter signatures lean on one another so that the reading does not settle, G9.2 says what a verifier answers. A later assent by the same signer to the same Terms is counted as any assent is.
- G4.11 **The update condition: valid or not valid.** Terms MAY name in `condition` (task K4) a cMIP, by fingerprint (G2), with its parameters: their **update condition**, called the condition for short. It is one simple field, and these grammar rules read one thing of it: whether the agreement can be updated or not. Asked of a proposed update, that is **valid** or **not valid**, as that cMIP answers. Everything else is that cMIP's to specify: who must sign an update (two owners of three, one named party, everyone), which parts of an agreement an update may change (a share, a party, the rights a version offers, the offer itself, a power, the judge, the condition itself), when, and on what. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "The update condition would be dictated by the 2 out of 3 signature cMIP, no?"
  - (a) **What it is given.** The version the proposed update would replace (the original: the version in force, G4.6, or, for an update of a version already replaced, the version its `replaces` names, G4.7: the condition is asked of it, and whether it is late is G4.7's) and the proposed update: a Terms act whose `replaces` names that version. With them, as for any rule, the acts held, among them the assents to the proposed update, and the answers of the place and the clock the agreement names, or of the anchoring named where it names none (K4's row).
  - (b) **It runs from the bytes.** Whoever holds those bytes can apply the cMIP, to an update already signed or to one only proposed, and gets the same answer as anyone else (task K4). Its answer waits for no one to publish anything.
  - (c) **Not valid.** Where the cMIP answers not valid, the proposed update replaces nothing, whoever signed it (G4.8): the Terms it names stay in force, and it is not a tip of a fork. A verifier MUST answer **not valid** for it, and MUST name the condition beside that answer, with the ground the cMIP gives: an act, reported by who signed it (G8), or a time, per the clock the agreement names (G7.4), or, where it names none, per the anchoring named or as shown (G13.4). Where the cMIP counts signatures, its answer rests on the assents held: it is given per place or as shown, and it may differ once an assent the verifier did not hold is shown.
  - (d) **Valid.** Where the cMIP answers valid, the verifier answers **valid**, named and qualified in the same way, and the update replaces the version in force (G4.8). Who must sign is part of the cMIP's answer. Nothing more is asked of the update's signatures, and no power is read for it (G5.2).
  - (e) **Which condition.** The condition asked is the one named by the original, the version the update would replace (G4.8); while a fork of Terms stands, the one named by the Terms both branches replace (G4.7). **The default update condition.** Where Terms name no update condition, an update is valid when everyone who signed the original signs it. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "Everyone who signed the original is the default condition yes". Who they are is said in G4.8(a). A verifier answers **valid**, with the words "by default", where an assent to the update in the name of each of them is held; where one is lacking it answers **not shown** for the update as a replacement (G4.8(c)), and never **not valid**: the missing assent may exist. There is no power for replacement (G5.2): Terms that name no update condition are updated only by everyone who signed the original, whatever their `powers` say. For a delegation, G5.5 says what the default asks.
  - (f) **Nothing changes by itself.** These grammar rules have no condition that triggers. What a version offers, and the rights of those who earned a filling under it, change only through an update (G10.6, G10.7).
  - (g) **A resolving update.** A resolving update of Terms is an update of the shared Terms (G12.3), and the condition those Terms name is asked of it as of any update: its answer says who must sign it. With none named, the default asks everyone who signed the shared version. A resolving update carries no words of its own, so there is nothing else in it to answer about; each tip it names was itself answered valid, or is no tip (G4.8). One signed under a power to resolve that the shared Terms give resolves as G12.4 says, and the condition is not asked of it. Draft 18 did not ask the condition of a resolving update, and took its signatures from G4.8; that those signatures are now the condition's answer is Claude's reading (section 8.4).
  - (h) Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, whether the update replaces is **unchecked** (G9), and so is every answer that rests on it (G4.8(e)).
  - (i) **Output, and nothing more.** These grammar rules name the update condition's cMIP, take the output of that task, valid or not valid, and use it where it is needed: here, in G4.8 and in G10.6. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "The core wants the result of the task as computed by the cMIP and plug it into whatever it needs to do". They have no notion of a cMIP overruling the default, and no value "locked". A named cMIP is not read as set against the default: the socket is filled, and its output is the answer. An update condition whose cMIP answers not valid to every update is read as any other; so is one that answers valid to an update fewer than all have signed, which then replaces (G4.8). A verifier MUST NOT answer, of itself, that an agreement is locked, or that it can never be updated.

**Lie exposed.** "You signed, so you are bound", said by a party who never signed; "these are the Terms now", said of Terms replaced, of one branch of two, of a replacement that lacks a signature, or of one that the agreement's own update condition answers not valid; "we were first", said of an update the acts or the place show was made after the version it names was replaced; and "this agreement is over".

**Protects.** The first to sign; the party left out of a replacement; everyone in the versions made since, whom a late update cannot take back to an old one where the acts or the place show it late; everyone in the last version, which keeps binding until an update its own update condition answers valid replaces it; and whoever relies on a part of an agreement that its update condition lets no update touch.

### G5. Own signature

- G5.1 A person is bound by an act if, and only if:
  - (a) they signed it in their own name, or
  - (b) the act's `for` names them, or names an agreement by whose Terms they are bound (G1.2); its `under` names Terms by which they are bound and which are in force; those Terms give a power for acts of this kind; and that power's rule, applied to the acts held, says yes, or
  - (c) the act is a Terms act that replaces Terms by which they are bound, and the update condition of those Terms answers valid for it (G5.2).
- G5.2 **A replacing Terms act.** A replacing Terms act (one with `replaces`) binds a party by that party's own assent to it (G5.1(a)); or, with no assent of theirs, under G5.1(c): the party is bound by the Terms it replaces, and the update condition of those Terms answers valid for it (G4.8, G4.11). That party signed, or was bound by, Terms that say how they may be updated, and it is bound by an update made so. Under the default every such party has signed the update themselves (G4.11(e)). **No power for replacement.** No power makes a Terms act replace, and no power binds a party to one: `powers` has no kind `replaces`. An entry of `powers` of that kind gives no power; a verifier says that it left it unread (G2.2). What is left here of draft 18's power for replacement is nothing: what its rule answered, which signatures are enough, is the update condition's, with the rest of the answer about an update, in one place (G4.11). An assent to a replacing Terms act MAY be made for a party under a power of kind `assent`, as any assent may (G5.1(b)): that is acting for someone, and it is counted as that party's signing, as draft 18 has it (section 8.4).
- G5.3 An act with no `for` and no `under` is made in its signer's own name only. It MUST NOT later be counted as made for anyone else.
- G5.4 Where whoever is bound through `for` is bound only through Terms that have been replaced, they are bound by the act if it was made before the Terms that replace those were in force, and not bound if it was made after. A verifier MUST answer from G7: per place if the Terms that gave the power name one, by the act's position against the position at which the replacing Terms came into force: the first position at which, counting only the acts that place shows at or before it, the replacing Terms are valid (G4.11) and in force (G4.1); otherwise as shown, by citing alone, as G13.3 reads it for Terms coming into force; and **not shown** where citing gives no order. (Whether Terms have been replaced is answered by G4.5 to G4.8; Terms are not ended otherwise, G4.9.)
- G5.5 **Delegation.** Terms in which one party, the **delegator**, gives another, the **agent**, a power to make acts for it are a delegation. No field says so: that Terms are a delegation, who the delegator and the agent are, and what conditions the Terms put on ending it, are read from their words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1). Where a verifier cannot read the body of Terms that carry `powers` (it lacks the words or their cMIP), it cannot tell whether this clause applies, and whether a version that lacks signatures replaces them is **unchecked** (G9), not **not shown**. The delegator may be named as a signer or as an agreement (G1.1): these grammar rules read the two as the same delegation. By default the delegator can end it at any time: where those Terms name no update condition, a version of them that removes or narrows the power is valid, and replaces them (G4.8, G4.11(e)), when it carries the delegator's part alone, without the agent's. Where they name an update condition, its cMIP answers, as for any update (G4.11; section 8.4). A version removes or narrows the power when its `powers` are those of the version before less one entry or more; whether an entry whose rule was changed is narrower is not read by these grammar rules (section 8.4). The delegator's part alone is then all that the default of G4.8 and G4.1 ask of that version: it replaces, and is in force, without the agent's assent, however it lists its parties and whatever its own `in_force` says; the same holds for the agent's part alone under a condition that leaves ending to the agent. For a delegator named as a signer, that is its own assent. For a delegator named as an agreement, it is that agreement's signature (G1.2). The Terms of a delegation MAY state conditions on that ability (a notice, a term, a cause); such a version then replaces them only as those conditions say. A verifier reads them from those Terms, in the form their cMIP gives (G2, task K1); where it cannot, the answer is **unchecked** (G9). These grammar rules put no limit on what such a condition may say, and a verifier reads every one alike. Under a condition that leaves ending to the agent alone, a version carrying the delegator's part alone replaces nothing (G4.8). A verifier MUST NOT answer that such a delegation can never be ended: whether the agent will end it is **not shown**, and the verifier shows the condition as the Terms the delegator signed state it. These grammar rules give an agent no protection of their own: pay, notice, severance and the like are cMIPs the parties choose. Once a delegation has been so ended, G5.4 says how an act made under it is read. **In which order a verifier reads it.** "Terms that carry `powers`" are Terms with at least one entry in `powers`, of any kind. For a version that would replace them: (i) where those Terms name an update condition, its answer is the whole answer (G4.8), and the conditions their words state are not read for it: the cMIP in `condition` says whether they count; (ii) where they name none, and everyone who signed them has signed the version, it is valid by the default (G4.11(e)), and the body is not needed; (iii) otherwise the body is read: where it cannot be, the answer is **unchecked**; where it shows no delegation, the default answers (G4.8(c)); where it shows a delegation and the version's `powers` are the old ones less one entry or more, the delegator's part alone makes the version valid and in force, unless the conditions the words state ask more (or, under a condition that leaves ending to the agent, the agent's part alone does); a stated condition that the verifier cannot apply in the form the body cMIP gives it is **unchecked** (G9).
- G5.6 **Constitution, executive and judicial.** In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "Update equals constitution, and the judicial, power means the executional". Three things are kept apart, and this clause is the one place that says so.
  - (a) **The constitution is the update condition: how the Terms may change.** A version is replaced only by an update that its update condition answers valid, who must sign it included; with none named, by an update everyone who signed it has signed (G4.8, G4.11).
  - (b) **The executive is the powers: who may act under the Terms, and in which kinds of act.** An act made for a person or for an agreement, a Statement of a kind (G1.2, G5.1(b)). A power acts under the Terms as they are. No act made under a power is a version, and none changes what a version says.
  - (c) **The judicial is set in the Terms.** It is three things. The contest: these grammar rules give it, and whom one reaches is read from the version it is made against (G11.8). A judge, where Terms name one: in a socket of theirs, by the cMIP that socket names or by its parameters, fixed by fingerprint (G2; section 5.3). And a power to resolve a fork: an entry of `powers` of kind `resolves` (G12.4). Each is read from the version in force (G4.6); while a fork of Terms stands, from the last shared version (G4.7).
  - (d) **It changes only by an update.** Who the judge is, whether Terms give a power to resolve and what its rule accepts, and whom a contest reaches, change only when a new version says otherwise, made by an update the update condition answers valid ((a)). No power can change it. A verifier MUST NOT read an act made under a power as naming or replacing a judge; as giving, widening, narrowing or removing a power to resolve; or as changing whom a contest reaches. A cMIP that leans on a judge takes who the judge is from the Terms that name that cMIP, and from no act made under a power.
  - (e) **Using it is not changing it.** A judge's decision binds only through a power (G5.1(b); section 5.3), as draft 18 has it. A resolving update signed under a power to resolve is that power used (G12.4). A contest made for someone under a power of kind `contest` is that person's contest. Each of these acts under what the Terms set, and changes none of it.
  - (f) **An agent within its limits.** An assent made for a party under a power of kind `assent` (G5.1(b)) to an update, one that changes the judicial included, is that party's signing where the power's rule says yes; the update still has to be answered valid by the update condition ((a); G4.8). In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "The agent is operating within the limits (layer. Sockets, cMIPs and modules) that were handed to it. Your risk, your dial". Which updates a power to assent reaches is that power's rule's to say (K3), and a verifier reads its output and nothing more. That is not a power changing the judicial: the judicial changes by the update, and only by it ((d)).

- G5.7 **Nothing an agent made is set aside.** In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "It’s never set aside, it’s always update. Whoever agreed to what was signed by the agent is still covered, the same way as any updates of terms." An act made for a person or an agreement under a power (G5.1(b)), or as an agreement's signature (G1.2), is an act like any other once made. Call the **grantor** the one in whose name it was made (the delegator of a delegation, or the agreement whose power it was). No act of the grantor, and no act of anyone else, sets aside, voids or undoes an act made under its grant. The grantor acts on what was made under its grant only by an update, under the update rules these grammar rules already have, and as anyone may act on any act:
  - (a) **On the grant.** By a version of the Terms that give the power, which ends or narrows it (G5.5): it reaches only acts made after that version is in force; an act made before it binds as G5.4 says.
  - (b) **On what was made.** By an update of the agreement the act was made in, which that agreement's update condition answers valid, or, where it names none, the default: everyone who signed the original (G4.8, G4.11). The grantor is among those who signed the original where an assent counts in its name, its agent's included (G4.8(a)); and so is everyone who agreed to what the agent signed.
  - (c) **Before the Terms are in force.** By a counter signature in the grantor's name (G4.10), as for any assent, while nothing yet counts for anyone. It cancels the assent counted in that name, and sets nothing aside: the agent's assent stays an act, shown as cancelled. Once the Terms are in force, a counter signature changes nothing (G4.10), and only (a) and (b) remain.

  **Who is covered.** Whoever agreed to what the agent signed keeps what that version gave them, the same way as under any update: a version is replaced only by an update its own update condition answers valid (G4.8); one who signed it is bound by an update they did not sign only where that condition answers valid for it (G5.1(c)); and a filling's rights change only as G10.6 says. A verifier MUST NOT read any act as setting aside, voiding or undoing an act made under a power, because it was made by an agent, or because the grantor later acted against it. It reads the grantor's later acts as what they are: a version of the grant, an update of the agreement, or, before Terms are in force, a counter signature. A fork is another thing: an act made under a power that is a tip of a fork stops as any tip does, and may be set aside by a resolving update (G12); that is the reading of a fork, not the grantor acting on its grant.

**Lie exposed.** "She bound you", "the group decided", "I was still authorised", said without a power the person bound had signed for; and "we have the power, so the rules are changed", or "so there is a new judge", said by those who may act under the Terms and may not update them; and "my agent's signature does not count any more", said by a grantor to someone who agreed to what the agent signed.

**Protects.** Whoever is bound through someone else (the delegator, the outvoted member, the absent party), and whoever relies on an agent, who keeps what the agent's act gave them, as under any update.

### G6. Own line

- G6.1 Every act within an agreement MUST name in `previous` its signer's previous act within the same agreement, or none if it is the signer's first. An act whose `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not an act of the same signer within the same agreement (an act of another signer, or of another agreement; a report; a payment; any record that is no Terms act and no Statement) is **invalid** (section 2). Where `previous` names nothing the verifier holds, the act counts, and the verifier shows that signer's line as **unchecked** before it. Whether two acts of a line are a fork, and whether a line is unchecked before an act, are answered from the acts held, **as shown**, whatever a place shows. Whether anything is later in a line is an answer about what is latest, and is given as G7 says.
- G6.2 Two acts by one signer within one agreement that name the same `previous` (or that both name none) are a fork. A verifier holding both MUST answer **fork** for that signer, whatever a place shows: an act the place does not show is still a tip, so that no signer can hide a fork by keeping one tip out of the place. It MUST NOT choose between the tips. Both tips stop (G12.2), and the fork is resolved only as G12 says.

**Lie exposed.** One history shown to one person and another to another.

**Protects.** The stranger who is shown only one branch.

### G7. Place to look

- G7.1 Terms MAY name a `place`. A place is read as the layer that orders acts gives it: these grammar rules take its answers and define nothing of it (section 8.1 lists what that layer must provide).
- G7.2 Where the Terms in force name a place, an act within the agreement counts for any answer about what is latest (including which Terms are in force, G4.6), what is whole, who holds what, whether a period has passed, or whether something was made public, only once the place shows it, and at the position the place gives it. A verifier MUST take those answers from the place, qualified **per place**. Where a new version names another place, or none, whether it has replaced the version in force is judged per the place that version names (G4.8(d)). Before any Terms of the agreement are in force, the place is the one its first Terms act names (G4.6). Where the verifier holds two answers of one place that contradict each other, it has no answer from that place: **unchecked** (G9).
- G7.3 Where no place is named, a verifier MUST qualify every such answer **as shown**, or **per anchoring** where the reader accepts one, with it named (G13.4). It MUST NOT give such an answer as latest or whole without saying what it stands on.
- G7.4 The place the Terms in force name is the one every reader uses for that agreement, whether a party to it or not: a verifier MUST NOT give an answer of G7.2 for that agreement per another anchoring. Terms MAY also name a `clock`. Where the Terms in force name one, what time it was, for that agreement, is answered per that clock by every reader, qualified **per clock** (G8.2, G13.4). What a clock is, and how it is identified, is for the layer that gives it (section 8.1).

**Lie exposed.** "There is nothing later", "there is no side piece", "nobody else holds it".

**Protects.** Everyone who arrives later and must rely on "nothing else exists".

### G8. Say who said it

- G8.1 For any answer about the world (a deed done, a thing's quality or quantity, an event, a person's presence or absence), about time, or about value under Control, a verifier MUST report who stated it: the one who performed, the one who received, a named witness, the controller, or the place.
- G8.2 A time written inside an act is reported as its signer's claim. No field of section 3 holds a time; one written in a `content` or in the words of a body is read only by the cMIP of that socket, and is its signer's claim there too. Only a place gives a position. What time it was is answered per the clock the agreement names (G7.4); where it names none, only per an anchoring the reader accepts, with it named (G13.4).
- G8.3 A verifier MUST NOT report value as moved on the strength of an Agreements act alone. An act can instruct Control; whether Control obeyed is the controller's or the receiver's signed word.
- G8.4 Who fills a spot (G10) and what a leg received and passed on (G11) are answers about the world. A verifier MUST report them with whoever confirmed them.
- G8.5 **Settlement.** A Statement MAY name a `settlement` it is tied to: the evidence, from the layer that holds value, that a payment was made. This field is where that layer's proof plugs into Agreements; what a settlement is, is that layer's to say. It names an act and no cMIP, and is not a task. A settlement named is reported on the word of whoever gave it (G8.3). What is done with it (counting only what led to a settlement, dividing what one shows) is a rule's, in whichever socket it sits (section 5.3).

**Lie exposed.** A claim passed off as a fact: "it was delivered", "you were paid", on the word of the one who gains by it.

**Protects.** The receiving side, who did not choose to be declared paid or served, and every reader of a verifier's answer.

### G9. Unchecked is not passed

- G9.1 Where a verifier cannot apply a rule it needs, because it lacks a cMIP named by fingerprint, lacks an act named by fingerprint, lacks the acts of an agreement named as a party, or cannot get an answer from a named place, it MUST answer **unchecked** for every answer that depends on it.
- G9.2 **Where a verifier stops.** A verifier that stops following (powers through powers, G5; receivers from agreement to agreement, G1.4; the versions of a long chain) answers **unchecked** for what lies beyond where it stopped, and says how far it followed. It MUST NOT answer **not shown** or **invalid** because it stopped. A verifier MAY also stop following counter signatures that lean on one another (G4.10): where a signer assents and cancels many times and nothing names anything, the reading of one counter signature asks about the others, and past a number of such readings it answers **unchecked**, and says that it stopped. How far a verifier must follow is not fixed (P2, P8). Where forks, and powers to resolve them, lean on one another so that a verifier's reading of a set of acts does not settle, every answer that rests on a fork in that set is **unchecked**, and the verifier says so.

**Lie exposed.** "My software says it checks out", from software that could not check.

**Protects.** Anyone relying on someone else's verification.

### G10. Spots

- G10.1 A role in `parties` MUST name:
  - its **kind**, one of: **done** (whoever proves they have done X), **received** (whoever proves they have received Y), **holding** (whoever proves they are holding Z), **fits** (whoever proves their identity fits the conditions in W), or **future**;
  - its `what`: the X, Y, Z or W, by fingerprint (G2), except for kind future (G10.4);
  - its spot socket (task K5): the cMIP that says who fills it, by fingerprint, with its parameters. The role (its kind and its `what`) is that task's input, and not a task of its own.
- G10.2 A spot is filled, by a signer, when its K5 cMIP, applied to the acts held and the place's answers, names that signer on the strength of a **proof**: the acts that earn the spot. A spot MAY be filled many times; each filling is tied to the proof that earned it. Whether a spot holds one filling or many is said by its K5 cMIP. A verifier MUST answer each filling with its proof, each act reported by who signed it (G8.4), qualified per place or as shown (G7). The role and its cMIP are read from the Terms in force; while a fork of Terms stands, from the Terms both branches replace (G4.7).
- G10.3 Each signer who fills a spot is a party in it from then on. They are bound only by their own assent (G5). Filling a spot does not bind any other party.
- G10.4 A role of kind **future** names no `what`: its K5 cMIP defines who the participant is as well as how they are confirmed. It is for participants none of the four kinds can describe in advance. No verifier can check that a future spot was used only for them; whether it was is the authors' word, carried by them (G3).
- G10.5 **New identities.** Whether a spot still takes new identities is said by its K5 cMIP, applied to the version in force (the role as that version states it), the acts held, and the answers of the place and the clock the agreement names (G7). These grammar rules define no act and no field for closing a spot or for opening it again: an offer stops taking new identities, or takes them again, by a new version (G4.8, G4.11), or by what the version in force itself states in advance (a date, a count), as that cMIP reads them. The update condition is not asked for this answer: it is asked only of a proposed update (G4.11). A verifier MUST give that answer with its cause, as the cMIP gives it, per place, per clock, per the anchoring named or as shown (G7, G13.4). Whatever that answer is, a filling already earned stays a filling, shown with its proof (G10.2), and keeps the rights of the version it was earned under (G10.6). Where the verifier lacks the cMIP, the answer is **unchecked** (G9).
- G10.6 **Rights.** A filling has exactly the rights offered by the version it was earned under, with every condition that version puts on them. The version a filling was earned under is the version its proof names (G13.1), as the spot's cMIP gives it with the filling (K5's row). What the rights and their conditions are is that version's (G2, task K1); these grammar rules do not read them. Nothing stops those rights by itself (G4.11(f)). No later version changes the rights of a filling already earned unless:
  - (a) it carries an assent to it signed by that filling's holder in their own name (G5.1(a)); or
  - (b) the version the filling was earned under names an update condition, and that condition's cMIP, given that version and the later one, answers valid (G4.11).

  Under (b) the later version reaches the filling's rights as far as its own words say, and no further; what they say is that version's (G2, task K1), and these grammar rules do not read it. A verifier MUST NOT read a later version as changing a filling's rights under an update condition which that filling's own version does not name. Where the version a filling was earned under names no update condition, only (a) changes its rights. A verifier MUST name, with each filling, the version it was earned under, and each later version that changes its rights, with what that rests on: the holder's own assent, or the condition named and its answer.
- G10.7 **What is left of the condition.** The field `condition` names the update condition, and it is read in one place (G4.11). Nothing of it is read here but its answer about an update. No condition triggers: these grammar rules have no answer **triggered**, and none that a filling's rights **no longer apply**, and a verifier MUST NOT give either. What a version offers changes, and the rights of a filling earned under it stop applying or change, only through an update which the update condition answers valid (G4.8, G4.11); for a filling, only as G10.6 says. A filling whose rights an update has so changed stays a filling, shown with its proof (G10.2): nothing is undone. What the update says of those rights, whether the holder is still a party in the spot (G10.3), and whatever else follows, are as that update and the version the filling was earned under state; a verifier reads them from there (G2) and MUST NOT answer them of itself. Whether a spot still takes new identities under the version in force is G10.5's. Nothing stops a later version: one made as G4.8 says MAY offer again, and MAY rest what it offers on values already in the chain, named by fingerprint (G2, G13.2), or on none. Who fills a spot under it is said by its own K5 cMIP (G10.2), and a filling under it has the rights that version offers (G10.6).

**Lie exposed.** "I am the one who did it", confirmed by someone chosen afterwards; and "the terms have changed, so what you earned is gone".

**Protects.** Whoever earns a spot and was not there to sign, who keeps exactly what was offered when they earned it.

### G11. Legs of a sequence

- G11.1 **Sequence, and leg.** Where what one agreement gives or owes reaches another agreement (a party named as an agreement, G1.1; a receiver that is an agreement, G1.3), and so on from one to the next, those agreements are a **sequence**. A **leg** is one obligation carried out, between its two sides: the **doer**, who carries it out, and the **receiving side**, who receives it. A payment to an agreement is a leg between the payer and that agreement's receiver for the payment (G1.4). A payout divided among several is one leg for each of them: between whoever pays out (a service, or a party who receives for the others) and each one paid; where one paid is an agreement, that agreement's receiver for the act (G1.4). These grammar rules define the two sides of a leg, and nothing more of it. Each leg is carried out under the Terms that hold its task (G11.2), or, for a payment, under the offer's Terms the buyer signed, which require it, the payment itself holding the task of paying and its call (G11.7); the cMIPs that read it are the ones those Terms name (G2). Nothing in one agreement's choice of cMIPs binds another agreement, and nothing is chosen for a sequence as a whole. The agreements of a sequence MAY name the same cMIPs; none is required to.
- G11.2 **The call.** Terms hold the Control task (K6) where their field `control` names a cMIP (G2); or, with that socket empty, where they name as `receiver` one of their own parties (G1.3) and give a part to any other party, so that this party holds for the others and passing on to them is its obligation under those Terms. Whether Terms give a part to a party is read from their words, as the body's cMIP gives them (task K1): a share for any party other than the receiver. Where the verifier cannot read the body, or lacks the cMIP in `control`, whether those Terms hold the task is **unchecked** (G9). Terms that hold the task MAY carry a call for it, in `calls`, as the report block of the shared language gives it (section 8.1): from whom a report is expected (the doer, the receiving side, or both). A call names no place and no reader (G11.6). For K6, the doer is whoever carried out the passing on (the controller: a service, or a party that receives for the others): the party those Terms name as `receiver` among their own parties. Where the receiver those Terms name is not one of their own parties (another agreement, or none), no doer is shown under those Terms: what reaches them is passed on under the Terms of the agreement it reaches (G1.4), and a report that states a doer's lines under these Terms is listed, as its signer's word (G8), and ties nothing. And the receiving side of each leg is whoever something was passed on to; where that is an agreement, it is that agreement's receiver for the act (G1.4), and no other of its parties. The call is made by the signers of those Terms, and binds no one else. In a deal, those signers are the service and the agreement it serves, which signs by its own signature (G1.2): the call is that agreement's parties' as much as the service's, and each party that signature binds has signed the deal through its agreement. Whoever receives is the receiving side of its leg whether or not it signed the Terms that carry the call. Where it did not, itself or through an agreement's signature, these grammar rules do not make the path to it a signed one: closing that gap is a cMIP's (section 5.2). A call is attached to a task, in the Terms under which that task is carried out; none is attached to a receiving side. A call in Terms that do not hold the task it is attached to is left unread, and the verifier says so (G2.2). These grammar rules make no Terms carry a call, and make no one answer one. A call is part of signed Terms and is read as they are (G2, G4.6), by anyone, before any payment is made.
- G11.3 **Leg report.** A leg report is a report that answers such a call. As every report does, it carries the call it answers and the acts it relied on, each by fingerprint, and its signer. Among the acts it relied on are the version of the Terms it passed on by (G13.2) and each settlement it speaks of (G8.5). Beyond that, it carries what K6's row says (section 5). It is its signer's word (G8.4), and each signer reports its own half only. What K6's row asks of a leg report MUST be readable without any cMIP those Terms name: a verifier that lacks them still reads the leg reports, and reports what they state as stated by their signers. Whether what was passed on is what those Terms and cMIPs require is answered by those cMIPs, and is **unchecked** where the verifier lacks them (G9); that does not make the leg report itself unchecked. Where one doer carries out several legs under one call (a payout to several), which lines of its report each receiving side receives (all of them, or those of its own leg) is said by the cMIP that answers the task (K6). These grammar rules do not say it.
- G11.4 **Reading across legs.** A verifier asked about a result that depends on a sequence MUST read it leg by leg, from the reports it holds (G11.6), and give each leg's part with who signed it (G8.4).
  - (a) **Where to look.** The verifier reads, from the Terms that hold a Control task, whether they carry a call, and from whom it expects reports. No call says where: a leg's reports are with the two sides of that leg (G11.6), and a verifier reads those it holds. Where a leg report states something as passed on to an agreement, the receiving side is that agreement's receiver for the act (G1.4). Where that party is a service under a deal, or holds for others, it is the doer of the legs that follow, under the Terms that name it as `receiver` and hold its task (G11.2, G11.5). The reports of those legs are the ones that answer the call of those Terms, between their own sides; they are not looked for within the agreement the first report names.
  - (b) **The tie.** Two halves are tied by the act both name. Neither report names the other. Where the layer that holds value records one payment as two acts (a claim by the one who paid, a receipt by the one who received), the two halves are tied by that layer's answer to "the same payment", as it gives it: a report that names the claim and a report that names the receipt are then tied, where that layer answers that the two are of the same payment. This text calls what a report names for a payment its settlement (G8.5), and reads "the same settlement" so. At each leg the two halves are the doer's report and the receiving side's confirmation, which answer the same call (G11.5). As the block asks, the verifier shows, for each such act, **matched**, **mismatched** (both shown, neither chosen), or **one side only**. Where the verifier does not hold the settlement two reports name, or cannot get that layer's answer, the tie is **unchecked** (G9), and what each side states is still shown. A report ties only where its signer is a side of the leg: a doer's report where its signer is the doer the Terms name (G11.2), a confirmation where its signer is the receiving side as G1.4 reads it. A report by anyone else is listed, as its signer's word (G8), and ties nothing. **Who received.** Who something was passed on to is known only from reports. A doer's line that states something passed on to X makes X, or, where X is an agreement, X's receiver for the act (G1.4), the receiving side of that line, and only that signer's confirmation ties to it. A confirmation by a party those Terms name as a signer, of which no doer's report held speaks, is shown as **one side only**. A confirmation by anyone else is listed, and ties nothing. **When halves are compared.** Matched, mismatched and one side only are answered only where the call expects reports from both sides, or where the act that holds the call is itself one side's signed word of the act both name: a payment, signed by the buyer and naming its settlement, is the doer's half of the buyer's leg (G11.7). Where a call expects one side only, each report held is shown as stated, and no tie is answered.
  - (c) **Amounts.** Two leg reports that name the same settlement match where they state the same amount for it (K6's row). Where they do not, the verifier MUST show both, with the amount each states, and MUST NOT choose between them. What the layer that holds value counts toward a debt, and what a reader of reports is shown, are two questions. This text answers the second: it shows both halves and chooses neither. The first is Money's: where that layer counts one of two amounts as received, that counting is Money's, and a verifier that gives it says that it is Money's answer and not this text's.
  - (d) **Silence.** As the block asks, the verifier shows a call with no report as **unanswered**, and Terms that hold the Control task and carry no call as **no report called for**. Neither is read as anything else: not as kept, not as failed. From such a leg, nothing ties what lies beyond it to what reached it: as part of this result, the legs beyond are **not shown**, though their own reports are read as any are. **Unanswered** is said only by a verifier that reads as a side of that leg, or along a path the people of that leg signed for (G11.6(a)). **Who is a side, for silence.** With no report, no line says who received. A reader is then a side of a leg carried out under Terms that hold the Control task where it is the doer those Terms name (G11.2), or a party they name as a signer, or, for a party they name as an agreement, that agreement's receiver for the act (G1.4). It reads along a signed path where the data cMIP of those Terms answers yes for that reader, asked about the call that no report answers (K8's row). On whose behalf a verifier reads is not in the acts: whoever asks tells it. A verifier told nothing reads as no side of any leg. The reports a verifier holds are read whoever is reading: it does not ask how they reached it. A verifier that does not, because that leg's reports travel no further than the leg (G11.6), MUST NOT show the call as unanswered: for it, that leg's reports are **not shown**. Where the verifier lacks an act a report names, or the Terms that hold a leg's task, that part is **unchecked** (G9).
  - (e) **Before paying.** Asked which agreements of a sequence carry a call for reports, a verifier answers from their Terms alone (G11.2), one agreement at a time, per place or as shown (G4.6), and **unchecked** where it lacks them. It answers in the same way, from the same Terms, from whom each call expects reports, and whether those Terms name a data cMIP, in `data` (task K8), by which their signers agreed to a path beyond the leg (G11.6(a)): **shown**, with its fingerprint, where they name one; where `data` is empty, it answers that no path beyond the leg is agreed in those Terms. For an offer, that answer is also the path a buyer agrees to for their own leg by signing its Terms (G11.6(b)). The agreements of a sequence, for this question, are found from the agreement asked about by following `receiver` from agreement to agreement, until one names one of its own parties, or none (G1.4). Parties named as agreements are not walked: which of them a payment reaches is their own Terms' and cMIPs' to say.
- G11.5 **The same exchange at every leg.** At every leg the exchange of reports is the same. The doer's leg report states that it carried out the passing on. The receiving side's leg report confirms what it received. How the receiving side's report comes to be made is its own business and its way of paying's: on some ways of paying it can be automatic (P20). A receiving side that holds for others (G11.2), or that is a service under a deal, then has the next obligation: it passes on under the Terms that hold that task, and reports there as the doer, under their call where they carry one. Where the doer is itself one to whom those Terms give a part, its report states that part as its own, and a verifier MUST NOT read it as kept (K6's row). These grammar rules attach no call to a receiving side, and read no report as reporting on another report: each report speaks for its own signer, of its own leg.
- G11.6 **One leg.** A report answers the call of its own leg (G11.2, G11.7) and is addressed to the other side of that leg, where that side receives, and to no one else. That is the report block's, for every layer of MOR (section 8.1); these grammar rules add nothing to it. A call MUST NOT be read as naming a place, or a reader. A call MUST NOT be read as expecting a report on a task held by other Terms than the ones that carry it. No one who did not sign the act that holds a leg's task can call for that leg's reports (G11.2), however far upstream of the payment they stand. No report is read as carrying another leg's reports onward. **These grammar rules never follow a receiver beyond one leg.** G1.4 finds who receives, which is the receiving side of one leg, and stops there. What that side then passes on, to whom, through how many further legs, and whether it comes round to an agreement already passed (a loop), are not read by these grammar rules: they are for whoever follows further, along a path of (a), and are that data cMIP's to answer (P8).
  - (a) **Reports are data: a path beyond one leg.** A report is data. A path by which a report, or what it states, reaches anyone who is not a side of its leg (a partner seeing further, a request from upstream) is a path the people whose data it carries agreed to, with their signature. It is built as a cMIP about data, focused on agreements, which Terms they signed name by fingerprint (G2). These grammar rules give no such path themselves: no call, no field and no answer of this text carries a report beyond its leg, and a verifier MUST NOT read any as doing so. What such a cMIP says is its own: who may read what, how a reader puts together the legs it may read, how far it follows, and what a loop answers (P8). **The data task.** In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "data is needed by so many processes , so yes I think a task is needed there." Terms name such a cMIP in the field `data`: the task K8 (section 5). Its one question, asked for one reader and one report of a leg carried out under those Terms: may this reader read it, or what it states, beyond its leg? It answers yes or no. These grammar rules take that output and add nothing to it. The socket speaks for the people bound by the Terms that name it (G5.1), and for no one else. Where it is empty the answer is no: one leg. Where a report carries the data of people bound by different Terms (the two sides of a leg), the data cMIP of each is asked, and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes; that last reading is Claude's (section 8.4). **Which Terms are asked.** About one report: the Terms that carry the call it answers; and, for each agreement a line of the report names as passed on to or owed to, that agreement's Terms in force (G4.6). A receiving side named as a signer of the Terms that carry the call is asked through those Terms. Where Terms that are to be asked are not held, the answer is **unchecked** (G9). What a data cMIP is beyond that socket is not said here (section 8.4).
  - (b) **Who has agreed.** A verifier MUST NOT read the people of a leg as having agreed to be read from beyond their leg unless Terms they signed say so (G5.1): by naming such a data cMIP in `data` (K8), or by a rule in them under which others decide it. Which consent a data cMIP asks of the people it reads is that cMIP's to say. Which cMIP, and which rule, apply is what the signers chose when they made the agreement, and is read from the Terms they signed (G2). Where those Terms say that a majority decides, the majority decides (G5.1(b)): a verifier reads a member who was outvoted as that rule says, and MUST NOT answer, beyond it, whether that member agreed. **The buyer's leg.** A buyer signs Terms: those of an offer, by an assent to the version they buy under, and paying is what those Terms require of them (G11.7). In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "A buyer signs terms of an agreement with unknown party, that requires the buyer to pay, as a consequence", and "it’s simply that his hash was unknown when the agreement was drafted and signed by its maker." So a buyer is bound by that version by their own assent (G5.1(a), G10.3), its data socket included. For a report of the buyer's leg (a confirmation of a payment), the Terms asked are the version the payment names (G13.2), where an assent of the payer to that version is held: its data cMIP speaks for the buyer, and for the receiving side where the receiver for the payment is one of that version's own parties (G1.4). Where the receiving side is reached through another agreement (G1.4), that agreement's Terms in force are asked too, as for any report that carries the data of people bound by different Terms ((a)), and the report is read beyond its leg only where every one asked answers yes. Where the version's `data` is empty, the answer is no: one leg. Where no assent of the payer to the version its payment names is held, the payer is not shown as bound by those Terms, nothing they signed says that they agreed to be read from beyond their leg, and the answer is **no** for every reader who is not a side of that leg (section 8.3). Draft 20 read a buyer as signing a payment and no Terms, and so answered no for every buyer; that reading is gone.
  - (c) One leg is the block's, in every layer. What is said here, and in Agreements only, is how people agree to a further path: in an agreement they signed ((a), (b)). No other layer of MOR is to carry a report further than its leg, or to give such a path (section 8.1).
- G11.7 **The buyer's leg.** Paying an agreement is a leg (G11.1). Its doer is the buyer: a signer of the offer's Terms, whose assent names the version they buy under, and whom those Terms require to pay (G11.6(b)). Paying is the consequence of the Terms the buyer signed, as for any signer of any Terms; the buyer differs only in that their identity was not known when the offer's maker drafted and signed it (section 2, "Offer"). The buyer also signs the payment (G13.2), which carries out what those Terms require. Its receiving side is the receiver, for that payment, of the agreement paid (G1.4). A payment is the act that holds the task of paying: the Control task (K6), with the payer as its doer. The payer, its signer, may call for the receiving side's confirmation, as the signer of any act that holds a task may (the block's R1). These grammar rules read every payment as carrying that call: the buyer's confirmation by default is that call, made by default, and no Terms need write it. The confirmation is a report that answers it. A report names the call it answers by naming the act that holds it: a report whose call names a payment, or the settlement a payment names, is the confirmation of that payment, tied to it as G11.4(b) says. The payment is the doer's half of that leg: its signer is the buyer, and it names the settlement (G11.4(b)). A report that names nothing as its call is no report, as the block's R2 has it, and is rejected. What the block would need to say for this to be read as written is proposed in `report-block-3.md` (section 8.1). It is the receiving side's report as G11.5 has it: its signer's word (G8.4), and on some ways of paying it can be automatic (P20). As for any two halves, a verifier shows a payment so confirmed as **matched**, one whose confirmation states another amount as **mismatched**, and one the receiving side has not confirmed as **one side only**; it MUST NOT show a payment as received on the buyer's word alone (G8.3). After the confirmation comes access to what was bought. What that is, and who gives it, is said by the version the payment names and its cMIPs (G10.6, G13.2; delivery, section 5.3); these grammar rules do not read it. By default the buyer is shown nothing further. What the receiving side then does with the payment is further legs (G11.5), and their reports go to the two sides of each (G11.6). The buyer's own leg is read beyond it only as the data cMIP of the offer's Terms the buyer signed gives (G11.6(b)); the buyer reads a further leg only as a data cMIP the people of that leg agreed to gives (G11.6(a)). The call binds only its signer, the payer: these grammar rules make no one confirm (G11.2).
- G11.8 **The contest: an act, made against an offer or a vow.** A contest is an act of these grammar rules: a Statement of kind `contest`. It is made against an offer or a vow (section 2): its `agreement` names the root of that offer or vow, and its `terms` names the version it is made against. Whom it reaches follows from what it is made against: the signers of that offer or vow (one level, G1.2), and no one else. A contest made against a vow is read exactly as one made against an offer. A verifier reads a contest made against any version alike: whether that version names a party by role is shown beside it, and changes no answer. The signers a contest reaches are the parties that version names as signers; a signer who fills a role is not counted among them (P17). Its `terms` names Terms shown as a version, or a tip set aside (then the contest is shown as **disputed**, G12.5); otherwise the contest is **not shown**. A contest is its signer's word, reported as such (G8.1), and stands in its signer's own line (G6.1). **A buyer's contest** is a contest by which a buyer says that what they bought was not delivered after payment. It is made against the offer or vow the buyer bought under, names the version the payment read (G13.2), and names the payment in `settlement` (G8.5). These grammar rules read a buyer's contest only there: where what was bought was not delivered after payment. A verifier shows a contest as a buyer's where it holds a payment by the same signer that names the same settlement and the same version; where it holds no such payment, whether the contest is a buyer's is **unchecked** (G9). That it was not delivered is the buyer's word (G8.1). A verifier MUST NOT read a contest as contesting anyone but the signers of what it is made against: not a service those signers picked, not a deal of theirs, not any other agreement of the sequence. It MUST NOT read a contest as giving its signer sight of any leg (G11.6, G11.7). Where those signers believe the fault is their service's, they contest the service: a contest of their own, made within the deal they signed with it (G1.2), which reaches the service and is read no further. Whether such a deal, signed by parties who were known, is an offer or a vow is not said (section 8.4). A baker may contest Terms for another bake of the same work (G14.5). Who else may make a contest, and when, is not said (section 8.4). What a contest does beyond standing on the record (standing, section 5.3), who answers it and how, and how money comes back, are not these grammar rules' (sections 5.1, 7).

**Lie exposed.** "It all went through", said by one side of a leg alone; "you never asked", said by those whose own signers did; "your payment never arrived", said to a buyer who holds its confirmation; and "you agreed to be watched", said to people whose signed Terms name no such thing.

**Protects.** Both sides of every leg, who each hold the other's signed word; the buyer, who holds the confirmation of their own payment; and the people of every leg, whose reports move nowhere they did not sign for.

### G12. Forks

- G12.1 A **line** is a set of acts of which each names the one before it, so that only one act may follow each: one signer's acts within an agreement, by `previous` (G6.1); an agreement's Terms, by `replaces` (G4.8); and any other acts these grammar rules read as following one another. An **update** of an act is an act that names it as the one before, and that all the parties that needed to sign it have signed: for one signer's line, that signer (G6.1); for Terms, those whose signing the update condition asks, or the default (G4.8, G4.11), and those whose assent G4.1 asks for (for a version that ends or narrows a delegation, as G5.5 says). For Terms, a late update (G4.7) is no update of the version it names. Where an act has one update, that update is the next act of the line. A **fork** is two updates of the same act of one line (G4.7, G6.2); three or more are one fork with that many tips. Two acts of one signer that both name no `previous` are a fork with no shared act. These grammar rules see it from the acts alone: two acts were signed by all the parties that needed to sign them. They say nothing of what anyone meant. The act both follow is the **shared act**; each of the two updates is a **tip**; a tip, with every act that follows it in the line, is a **branch**.
- G12.2 **Both tips stop.** While a fork stands, neither tip counts as the next act of its line, and nothing built on either tip counts: no act that follows a tip in the line, and no act that rests on a tip or on an act built on it (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), however many namings lie between. **One thing an assent to a tip still counts for.** A fork of Terms is two updates each signed by all who needed to sign, and the signing is the assents. So an assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, and for nothing else: it binds no one while the fork stands, since the Terms it names are not in force (G4.3). Every other act that rests on a tip (through `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or as the version a payment read) counts for nothing while the fork stands; a payment that names a tip is read as G1.4 says. The shared act stays the last act of the line that counts: for Terms, the last shared version stays in force (G4.7). A verifier MUST show both tips, and what it holds that is built on them, with the fork; MUST answer **fork** where G4.7 and G6.2 say; and MUST NOT choose between the tips, by position or in any other way. A fork stands until G12.3 resolves it. Terms that were in force before a fork was made stay in force: Terms stop only by replacement (G4.9).
- G12.3 **Resolving update.** A fork is resolved by an update of the shared act: an act of the same line whose `previous`, or for Terms whose `replaces`, names the shared act; which names every tip in `resolves`; and which names one of them in `continues`. It resolves the fork only when it is signed as an update of the shared act must be: for one signer's line, by that signer (G6); for Terms, so that the update condition of the shared Terms answers valid for it, which says who must sign it; by default, everyone who signed the shared version (G4.8, G4.11(g)); or under a power to resolve (G12.4). The tip it names in `continues` then counts as the next act of the line, what is built on that tip counts as these grammar rules read it, and the line goes on from that branch. Every other tip is **set aside**: it never counted as the next act of the line (for Terms, it never counted as the next version), and no act that follows it in the line counts. A resolving update is not a tip of the fork it resolves, and carries no words of its own (section 3). An act that names `resolves` and no `continues` resolves nothing. An act that names `resolves` and does not in fact resolve the fork it sits in (it is not signed as G12.3 asks, or does not name every tip, or there is no fork: what it names in `resolves` is one update and no other) is an act like any other, and may itself be a tip: a fork is not hidden by adding the field. Where such an act names the shared act as the one before it, it is an update of that act beside the one it names, and the two are a fork. A resolving update MUST name every tip, and MAY name more acts than the tips. Where the two tips of a signer's line both name no `previous`, a resolving update that also names none resolves them. **After a fork is resolved.** An act that names the resolving update as the one before it (in `previous`, or for Terms in `replaces`) is read as following the last act of the continued branch. So the next act MAY name either. But two acts, one that names the resolving update and one that names the last act of the continued branch, are two updates of the same act, and a fork. For Terms the same holds: a version whose `replaces` names a resolving update replaces the last version of the continued branch, and the update condition asked of it is that version's (G4.11(e)).
- G12.4 **A power to resolve.** Terms MAY give, in advance, a power (G5) to sign the resolving update of a fork: an entry in `powers` whose rule (task K3) says who may sign it, and which resolving update it accepts. A resolving update signed under such a power carries the signatures G12.3 asks for, and resolves the fork as G12.3 says. For a fork of Terms, the power is read from the Terms both tips replace, which stay in force (G4.7); it is an entry of `powers` of kind `resolves`, and of no other kind. Draft 18 read it as a power for replacement that a rule might limit to resolving updates; that power is gone (G5.2). A power to resolve is part of the judicial: it is set in the Terms, and it is given, changed or removed only by an update the update condition answers valid, never by a power (G5.6). For a fork in one signer's line too, it is an entry of `powers` of kind `resolves`, in Terms by which that signer is bound: a power to make that act for the signer (G5.1(b)): the resolving update then stands in the line of whoever signed it, its `previous` is that signer's own, its `for` names the signer whose line forked, and it resolves by naming the tips. How the fork is resolved is the power's rule's: it may accept only the resolving update it computes from the acts and the place, whoever signs it, or accept the one a named person signs (a judge, named in those Terms: G5.6; section 5.3), or anything else; these grammar rules force no single way. A verifier MUST report such a resolving update with the power it was signed under and whoever it rests on (G5.1, G8). Its answer about it is **shown**, naming G12.4, the power and its rule's answer, and the signer: the update condition is not asked of it (G4.11(g)), so it is neither valid nor not valid.
- G12.5 **What others relied on stands.** An act signed by someone who signed none of the acts of a branch whose tip is set aside, and that rests on an act of that branch (it names it, in `terms`, `under`, `answers` or `relies_on`, or was made within or under Terms of that branch), stands: it has the effect these grammar rules gave it, or would have given it had that tip stood alone. A payment that names a version of that branch as the one it read (G13.2) is such an act. A verifier MUST show that act, and every answer resting on it, as **disputed**. "The acts of a branch" are the acts of the line: for Terms, the Terms acts; whoever signed one of them is not protected here for what they built on it. What stands differs by act. A payment that names a version of that branch is read as if the tip had stood alone: its receiver is read from that version (G1.4), and both are shown as disputed. **As if the tip had stood alone** means: the line is read with every other tip of that fork, every act built on them, and the resolving update, left out. A version further along that branch is then a version where it would have been one, and a payment that names it is read against it. **A filling earned within that branch** by a proof made by someone who signed none of its Terms acts stands in the same way: it is shown as **disputed**, with the version of that branch it was earned under named; no version of the continued line is named as a later version that changes its rights (G10.6). An assent, or another Statement made within that branch, by someone who signed none of its Terms acts, stands as an act, shown as disputed, and binds no one: the Terms it names never counted as a version, and an assent binds only while the Terms it names are in force (G4.3).
- G12.6 A fork that no resolving update has resolved stands: of Terms, as G4.7 says; in one signer's line, as G6.2 says.
- G12.7 **Rival resolving updates.** Two resolving updates of the same fork, each signed as G12.3 asks, that continue the same tip resolve it, and are no fork. A resolving update of a fork of Terms is an update of the shared Terms (G4.11(g)): one that the knots or an anchoring show came after another had resolved the fork is a late update, and resolves nothing (G4.7). Two that continue different tips are themselves a fork: two updates of the same shared act. Neither resolves the first fork while the other stands: every tip stays stopped (G12.2), and the shared act stays the last act of the line that counts. That fork is resolved the same way: by a resolving update that names the two rival ones in `resolves` and one of them in `continues`, signed as G12.3 asks or under a power given in advance (G12.4).
- G12.8 **A client's duty (client conformance).** A client that holds both tips of a fork MUST NOT show either tip, or anything built on either, as a resolved act: not as the next version, not as in force, not as that signer's latest act. It shows the fork as unresolved, until G12.3 resolves it. This is a duty of clients and not a grammar rule a verifier applies. No verifier can check it: what a client showed a person leaves no act. It is written here and nowhere else.

**Lie exposed.** "This one is the real one", said of one tip to whoever is shown only that tip, or shown it as settled.

**Protects.** Whoever relied on a tip without causing the fork; everyone in the last shared version, which goes on while the fork stands; and the reader, who is shown every tip, and none of them as resolved.

### G13. Now is what an act cites

- G13.1 An act is read against the versions and acts it names, in any field that holds a fingerprint (`terms`, `under`, `replaces`, `previous`, `answers`, `resolves`, `relies_on`, `settlement`). A report is read in the same way, against the call and the acts it names (G11.3). A version that replaces one an act names does not change what these grammar rules read that act as having done. Whether the act's signer is bound from then on is G4.3 and G5, unchanged.
- G13.2 An act that relied on a version or an act, of its own agreement or of another, names it: a Statement in `relies_on` where no other field names it, a Terms act in `relies_on` (G2). A verifier MUST NOT read an act as having relied on a version or act it does not name. A payment to a work names the version of the work agreement it read; how a settlement carries that name is for the layer that gives the settlement (section 8.1).
- G13.3 **Order from citing.** An act that names another, directly or through acts that name it, came after it: each naming is a knot. A verifier MAY give that order as **shown**, from the acts alone, with no place and no qualifier. Between two acts of which neither so names the other, no order is shown from the acts alone. **Before or after Terms came into force, from citing.** An act came after Terms came into force where the acts it names, directly or through acts that name them, are enough for those Terms to be in force (G4.1) and, for a replacing version, valid (G4.11). It came before where, counting every act held but those that name it, directly or through acts that name them, those Terms are not in force, or not valid. Otherwise no order between them is shown. **Per place**, the position at which Terms came into force is the first position at which, counting only the acts the place shows at or before it, they are valid and in force (G4.10, G5.4).
- G13.4 **Anchoring.** Whether anything is later than an act, and what time it was, are never shown by citing. For an agreement whose Terms in force name a place, every reader takes "nothing later" from that place; for one whose Terms in force name a clock, every reader takes the time from that clock (G7.4). A verifier MUST NOT give, per a reader's own anchoring, an answer that the place or the clock the agreement names gives. Only where the agreement names none does each reader accept the anchoring it chooses. Where the reader accepts an anchoring from which the verifier holds no answer, or holds two that contradict each other, every answer that rests on it is **unchecked** (G9), and not **as shown**. A verifier MUST give each such answer **per anchoring**, with the anchoring named (**per place** or **per clock** where it is the one the Terms name), or **as shown**.

**Lie exposed.** "You paid the wrong one", said to a payer after the agreement changed behind them; and "nothing came after", said on an anchoring the agreement does not name.

**Protects.** Whoever acts on a version they read and named: the payer above all.

### G14. The baker

- G14.1 Terms MAY name an `envelope`: the fingerprint of the envelope of a baked work those Terms are for (G2). What an envelope is, and who it says baked it, are read as the core gives them (section 8.1). The baker is reported as the envelope states it (G8).
- G14.2 Terms that name an envelope count, for that work and for anything else, only if:
  - (a) the baker is one of their parties, bound by them (G5.1); or
  - (b) one of their parties, bound by them, holds the baker's rights through a **succession**: agreements for the same envelope, of which the first meets (a); each passes the baker's rights to a party of the next, as its own Terms and the cMIPs in their sockets say: read from its words by its body cMIP (K1), as the part for machines that names to whom those Terms pass the baker's rights (section 5); and the last is these Terms. Each agreement of the succession names the one before it (G13.2): Terms name a succession where their `relies_on` names Terms of another agreement that name the same envelope.

  "Bound by them" means that the Terms are, or have been, a version, with an assent that counts in that party's name (G5.1). For this question alone, whether they are a version and in whose name an assent counts are read from the acts as for any Terms, before and apart from G14.3's answer, which waits on it. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "An agreement that points to an envelope already needs the baker (or the holder of the bakers rights) to be valid". Draft 20 asked (a) or (b) only of whether the Terms count as an agreement for the work; draft 21 asks it of whether they count at all (G14.3).
- G14.3 **Without the baker, nothing.** A verifier asked anything that rests on Terms that name an envelope (a receiver, which Terms are in force, a filling, a call, a power, a leg report, or whether they count as an agreement for that work) MUST first read the envelope, the baker it states, and, under (b), every agreement of the succession. Of a thief's attempt, Nobody, allegedly: "We cannot stop the attempt." So such Terms stay acts, readable as any are, and what follows is what they count for.
  - (a) **Invalid.** Where the verifier holds the envelope, and the Terms name neither the baker among their parties nor a succession, and nothing they name is lacking, they are **invalid** (section 2): they never count, for anything, their receiver included. No one receives by them (G1.4); they replace nothing, and are no tip of a fork (G4.7, G4.8); no assent to them binds anyone (G4.3); no spot of theirs is filled under them (G10.2); no call, power, update condition or data cMIP of theirs is read (G5, G11); and no act made within or under them, and no payment that names them, counts through them. A verifier shows them, and every act it holds that names them, with the answer **invalid** and G14.3 named. Draft 20's narrower reading, an answer about the work alone, with the Terms read by every other grammar rule, is gone.
  - (b) **Not shown.** Where they name the baker or a succession, and the acts held do not establish (a) or (b) of G14.2, the answer is **not shown**, and every answer that rests on them is **not shown** with it, G14.3 named: they count for nothing until it is shown. A baker who fills a role of those Terms is one of their parties from then on (G10.3): where the role's cMIP names the baker as filling it and an assent counts in the baker's name, (a) is met; where the acts held do not show that, and the Terms reach the baker only through a role, the answer is **not shown**. Where the words and the body cMIP (K1) of an earlier agreement of the succession are held and say nothing of passing the baker's rights, the answer is **not shown**.
  - (c) **Unchecked.** Where the verifier lacks the envelope (the baker is then not known), lacks an agreement of the succession, lacks something `relies_on` names (it might be the succession), or lacks the words or the body cMIP (K1) of an earlier agreement of the succession, the answer is **unchecked** (G9), and so is every answer that rests on those Terms.
  - (d) **Shown.** Where the acts held establish (a) or (b) of G14.2, the Terms count, for the work and for everything else, and are read by every other grammar rule as any Terms are.

  The answer is **invalid** only where the Terms name neither, and nothing they name is lacking. These grammar rules define no act that passes the baker's rights: an earlier agreement's own words say it, as its body cMIP reads them (K1; P18). A verifier MUST give the answer of this clause with every answer that rests on those Terms.
- G14.4 A work edited and baked again has another envelope and another baker. These grammar rules read it as any baked work, and do not read it as the first.
- G14.5 **The same work, baked again.** Anyone may bake the same bytes, unedited; that is another envelope, with its own baker, and Terms that name it and its baker meet G14.2(a). These grammar rules do not forbid it. They make it legible.
  - (a) **Shown beside a bake.** Where a verifier holds two or more envelopes that carry the same work, as the core says of their bytes (section 8.1), it MUST show, beside each of them and beside every answer of G14.3 about Terms for one of them, that the same work is baked by others too, with each other envelope it holds and the baker each states (G8). The answer is given **as shown**: an envelope the verifier does not hold may exist.
  - (b) **Never picks.** A verifier MUST NOT choose between bakes that carry the same work: not the first, by position, by time or in any other way, and it MUST NOT answer which baker made the work. The reasoning is given with the answer: the bytes say who uploaded and when, not who made it. First is not author (a leak, a fast copier), and which is first depends on the anchoring a reader accepts (G13.4).
  - (c) **A client may rank.** A client MAY rank such bakes for the person it serves: by knots, the order acts give by citing (G13.3), even where no anchoring is named; or by an anchoring (G13.4). A ranking is the client's, shown as the client's, and is not an answer of a verifier.
  - (d) **Judging is a cMIP's, or an arbiter's.** Whether one bake has a better claim to the work than another needs judgment. A cMIP that people name may give it, or a judge they name in their Terms (G5.6; section 5.3); a verifier reports such an answer with whoever it rests on (G8), and never as its own.
  - (e) **The original baker may contest.** A baker MAY make a contest (G11.8) against Terms that name another envelope of the same work. It reaches the signers of those Terms, is its signer's word (G8.1), and stands on the record. That its signer is the one who made the work is not shown by any act.

**Lie exposed.** "This work is mine to sell", and "pay me for it", said by someone who copied its bytes and names another's envelope; and "nobody else baked this", said of bytes that others baked too.

**Protects.** The baker, and whoever holds from her, whose work no stranger's Terms can rule; the buyer, who can check before paying, and is shown every bake of the same work the verifier holds; and whoever made the work, who can contest a copy and is not outranked by software that picked.

## 5. Tasks handed to cMIPs

In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "task has always been the socket in my mind. One cMIP per socket, multiple modules where needed, possibly multiple sockets in one cMIP." A task is a socket: a field of an act where a cMIP plugs in and answers one question (section 2). Every field of an act that names a cMIP is a task, and the table below lists them all. Each asks its one question, and one cMIP answers it. These grammar rules take the output and use it where the last column but one says; they never compute it.

- **One cMIP per socket.** A socket holds at most one cMIP, by fingerprint, with its parameters. A list field has one socket for each entry: each entry of `powers` is its own socket, and so is each role in `parties`. So an agreement may name many rules, and never two cMIPs in one socket.
- **Modules where needed.** The cMIP in a socket may use modules to give its answer: a rule that computes, a witness, a judge, a teller (section 5.3). A module is named by that cMIP, not by a field of the act.
- **Several sockets in one cMIP.** One cMIP may fill several sockets, of one act or of several. It is asked each socket's question apart, with that socket's inputs, and gives that socket's output.
- **The same question in another field is another task.** The rule in `in_force` and the rule of a power both compute from acts; they are two tasks, since each field asks its own question.
- **A role is an input.** A role (its kind and its `what`) is what the spot task is given. It is not a task of its own.
- **An empty socket.** Where a socket is empty, the default these grammar rules give applies, where they give one (K2, K4, K6, K8); otherwise nothing is asked.

Anyone may write a cMIP, and a cMIP may fit a socket for one kind of thing only (one for land, another for shares). Every cMIP in a socket is also given its own parameters, the acts held, and the answers of the place and the clock the agreement names, or of the anchoring named where it names none; the column "Accepts" lists what it is given beyond those.

### 5.2 What can be said in this language, and so is a cMIP's

The test, in the words of Nobody, allegedly: "What the previous version of agreement focused on solving issues… this one supports and specifies the language for creating solutions". And, of small problems the builds find: "The answer is often: you did not use the right cMIP then." Each thing below was found by a review or a test. Each can be said in the language as it stands, so the grammar adds nothing for it. None of this is a grammar rule; the right-hand column is one way to say it, not the only one. The list follows section 9 of Fable's review (`docs/reviews/fable-agreements-draft-14.md`), with three rows from the exchanges "The two sides of a leg" and "The wrong cMIP".

| What was found | How it can be said |
|---|---|
| Any two of three owners are to be enough to update the Terms | an update condition whose cMIP counts two signatures of three (G4.11, K4); no power is needed, and none would do it (G5.2) |
| An owner's share is rewritten by an update that fewer than all signed | an update condition that says, with who must sign, which parts an update may change; it is given the version in force and the proposed update (G4.11, K4) |
| A party wants to leave, and the Terms name no way | an update condition that answers valid for an update that party alone signed, only where it takes its own signer out (G4.11, K4) |
| A delegation only the agent may end | what the delegation's Terms say (G5.5); a body cMIP (K1) that shows it to whoever reads |
| A group that co-owns a work is not counted when the work's Terms change | Terms whose `in_force` rule, or whose update condition, counts it (G4.4, G4.11) |
| An outvoted member is seen from beyond their leg | the consent the data cMIP asks for (G11.6(b); K8) |
| A contest reaches one agreement, and the money has already been passed on | escrow, as a service's cMIP (K6) |
| Nothing is shown to anyone before they sign | a body cMIP (K1), and the client |
| When and how a pool is divided | a service's cMIP (K6), with a rule as its module; what a leg report names from a pool is still open (P6) |
| A doer's report shows each receiving side what every other was paid, or shows what is owed under a minimum to nobody | the task's cMIP (K6) says which lines each side of a leg receives (G11.3) |
| A member paid by a service also tells the agreement she is under that she was paid | that agreement's own Terms: a task and a call of their own, or a data cMIP (K8) |
| Someone receives under a call in Terms they did not sign, themselves or through an agreement's signature | a cMIP closes it; in the words of Nobody, allegedly: "I’d use a data cMIP to bridge the gaps." (G11.2) |
| An agreement that no later version may change (a lock) | an update condition whose cMIP answers not valid to every update (G4.11(i), K4); nothing new is needed |
| Those who may act for a group change who judges its disputes, or who resolves its forks | not possible under a power (G5.6); an update that the update condition answers valid (G4.11, K4) |
| The same work, baked by a copier | shown beside each bake, never picked (G14.5); judging it is a cMIP's or an arbiter's, and ranking a client's |
| A rival update signed late, against a version long since replaced, where neither the knots nor an anchoring show that it came late, takes the agreement back to it (G4.7) | where the knots or the place or clock the agreement names show it late, the grammar already reads it as replacing nothing (G4.7); beyond that, an update condition whose cMIP answers not valid for an update whose original is no longer the last version, on what that cMIP reads (G4.11, K4) |
| An agent signs, for a party, an update that changes the judge | the power's rule says how far the agent reaches (K3); the update still needs the update condition's answer (G5.6(f)) |
| The one an agent acted for regrets what the agent signed | an update of the agreement, as its update condition asks, or, before it is in force, a counter signature; and a version of the grant, for what comes after (G5.7); nothing is set aside |

### 5.3 Modules: what a cMIP in a socket may lean on

Drafts 8 to 17 listed thirteen "tasks" that were kinds of computation. Three of them were sockets all along (Body, Spot, Kind). One stood for three sockets (Rule: `in_force`, a power, `condition`). One was a socket with no field (Control). Those are the first seven tasks above; the eighth, Data, is new in draft 19. The others, and Rule itself as a kind of computation, are kinds of answer that a cMIP in a socket may use, as modules. They are not tasks: no field of an act names them. They are kept here because each says whom an answer makes you trust, and a verifier reports that with every answer (G8). The right-hand column gives the number each had until draft 17.

| Module | What it answers | Whom it makes you trust | Was |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rule** | what follows from these acts: yes or no, a list of who, or amounts; the same output for the same inputs, for anyone | none: anyone can recompute | K4 |
| **Identity** | who is behind this signer: signed statements by those who vouch | a witness (Sign layer) | K2 |
| **Assurance** | what was done to make it likely that a party was able, free and understood: signed statements of what was done; never a pass | a witness | K3 |
| **Witness** | what happened in the world: each named witness's signed statement | the witness | K5 |
| **Tally** | what a vote decided, without showing who voted how: the result, with whatever proof the method allows | the teller | K7 |
| **Delivery** | whether an act reached a person: the recipient's signed acknowledgment, or the Deliver layer's evidence | the recipient, or the Deliver layer | K9 |
| **Judge** | what the named judge decided, given the Terms (with `authors`, G3.2) and the acts of the dispute: a signed decision, which binds only through a power (K3, G5). The judge is named in the Terms, by the cMIP in a socket or its parameters, and is changed only by an update, never by a power (G5.6) | the judge | K10 |

Two more things answer questions and are not sockets of an act.

- **The place** (`place`) and **the clock** (`clock`) are named by Terms by an identifier, not as cMIPs. Their answers are another layer's (G7; section 8.1), and are given to every cMIP in a socket. The place was K6 until draft 17. Whether a clock is itself a module under a cMIP of another layer is that layer's to say (section 9).
- **Standing** (K13 until draft 17): what one signer's acts show about them, to anyone who looks. A reader chooses the cMIP; no act names it, so it is no task of an act. It computes from signed acts and place answers only, qualified per place or as shown; anyone can recompute it, and nobody keeps it.

## 6. Open parameters

These are values the grammar rules depend on and this draft does not fix. The numbers are kept from earlier drafts so that the records still point at them; P3, P10, P13 and P14 were answered there and are not listed (`history.md`).

- **P1. Default for `in_force`.** G4.1 sets it to "every party named as a signer". Whether a different default is wanted is open.
- **P2. Depth of powers through powers.** G5 lets Terms named in `under` themselves be bound through other Terms (an agent of an agent; a group within a group). Whether a verifier must follow any depth, or a limit applies, is open. *Said in draft 18:* a verifier that stops answers **unchecked** beyond where it stopped, and says how far it followed (G9.2). The number is not fixed; both C sessions' verifiers follow eight levels.
- **P4. One place or several.** G7 names one place. Whether Terms may name several places with a rule over their answers (plural witnesses of order) is open.
- **P5. What a verifier holds.** G6 and G9 depend on which acts the verifier holds. Whether a verifier answers from a set it is given, or must also ask the place for more, is open. Standing (section 5.3) depends on it most.
- **P6. Pools.** In plain words: some ways of paying cannot pay each share at the moment it is earned. Money must first gather in one pot, and be divided later. Nobody, allegedly: "Sadly some rails will need pools… this headache won’t be easy." This draft does not solve it. What is open, all of it:
  - who holds a pool, and on whose word it is said to hold what it holds (G8.3: only the holder's or controller's word);
  - when a pool is divided, and by which rule;
  - how a claim on a pool is counted when each false claim takes from the real claimants (`late-roles.md`: "each fake claim takes from real claimants, and the payer has little reason to resist");
  - what "tied to a settlement" (G8.5) means for a count paid out of a pool, where one settlement covers many counts;
  - what a leg report (G11.3) names as received where what it passes on comes out of a pool;
  - what happens to a pool's share that no one has yet claimed (the owed but unfindable).

  **What the click test showed** (`docs/click-sketch-report.md`, branch `claude/click-sketch`, its 3e; a test built on draft 10): one report for a batch of payments works, since it names them all as received. But "how much of *my* click reached the band" is then arithmetic the reader does pro rata, which nobody states; the sketch marks it "not stated". Left open. Whether one report may answer several calls at once is open in the block as well (section 8.1). *From draft 20:* these grammar rules read a report as answering one call (section 3.2); what would settle pools is listed in section 8.2, item 16.
- **P7. What happens to a filling.** A spot may be filled many times, each filling tied to its proof (G10.2). Still open: whether a filled spot can pass to another, and whether a later claimant can displace an earlier one.
- **P8. A receiver that is an agreement.** *Answered in draft 15, as far as the grammar goes:* these grammar rules never follow a receiver beyond one leg (G11.6). G1.4 reads a receiver, through any agreement it names, only until one of an agreement's own parties is reached: that finds the receiving side of one leg. What that party then passes on, through how many further legs, and whether it comes round again, is for whoever follows further: a data cMIP (G11.6(a)). **What the click tests showed:** when a song names as a party the film that stands on it, money goes round until fees and rounding eat it (`docs/click-sketch-report.md`, its 3f); and since nobody walks past one leg, such a loop matters only to a reader who puts several legs together (`docs/click-sketch-12-report.md`, its 3f). *Still open, inside one leg:* G1.4 sets no limit on how many agreements a receiver may point through before a party is reached, and answers **not shown** where an agreement is met a second time, since then no one receives. Both are Claude's least reading, for the MOR lead to confirm (section 8.4). *Said in draft 18:* a verifier that stops following receivers answers **unchecked** (G1.4, G9.2); a receiver that is a spot filled more than once, or that is no party, is **not shown** (G1.4).
- **P9. What a fork removes.** *Answered in draft 16, from the exchange "A fork, from MOR's side":* both tips stop. Nothing built on either counts until the fork is resolved, and the shared act stays the last act of its line that counts; for Terms, the last shared version stays in force (G4.7, G12.2). Two rival resolving updates are themselves a fork (G12.7). Draft 15's proposal (both branches bind their signer) is not taken. *Still open, for one signer's own line:* the exchange speaks of updates and of versions. Read for one signer's own line, as G12.2 reads it, an assent that is a tip, or follows one, is not counted while the fork stands; so a signer can hold back their own assent by forking, until they resolve the fork themselves. Before the Terms are in force, that gives a signer nothing the counter signature does not already give (G4.10). Once Terms are in force they stop only by replacement (G4.9), so a fork takes no Terms out of force; whether the signer of such an assent is bound meanwhile is not said (section 8.4). *Said in draft 18, for a fork of Terms:* an assent to a tip counts for whether that tip was signed, and for nothing else (G12.2); that is the reading of Nobody, allegedly, as the brief gives it.
- **P11. Dates.** A place gives positions, not dates. Terms may name a `clock`, and every reader uses it for that agreement (G7.4, G13.4). Still open: what a clock is and who gives it (P16).
- **P12. One act for many.** `for` names one person, or one agreement. An act made for an agreement binds that agreement's parties by one act (G1.2). Whether one decision or judgment binding many who are not the parties of one agreement is one act or one per person is open.
- **P15. Rights outside fillings.** G10.6 is about fillings. *Answered in draft 15, from the exchange "What the grammar is for":* the grammar gives a party named as a signer, or an agreement named as a party, no default of its own against a later version made under a power. What an update made under such a power may change was, in drafts 15 and 16, its rule's to say (allowed or not). *From draft 17* it is the update condition's: given the version in force and the proposed update, valid or not valid (G4.11; section 5.2). Where Terms name no update condition, draft 18 let a power reach the whole agreement, but for the rights of fillings already earned (G10.6). *From draft 19* no power makes an update (G5.2). An update that fewer than all signed is valid only where a named update condition answers so, and it then reaches what that condition lets it reach; with none named, the default asks everyone who signed the original (G4.11(e)).
- **P16. The clock.** Not said: what a clock is and which layer gives it; whether the place may also be the clock; and, where two versions of one agreement name different clocks, which one the update condition (G4.11) reads. This text says "the Terms in force", as G7.2 does for the place.
- **P17. An agreement that acts, or signs.** *Settled in draft 12:* an agreement named as a party signs, where it signs, as its own Terms require; a deal between a work and a service is signed by both, and a call in it is made by both (G1.2, G4.4, G11.2). *Settled in draft 13,* by the MOR lead as technical wording: signing is one level (G1.2). *Settled in draft 15,* from the exchange "The two sides of a leg": `for` may name an agreement; an act made in an agreement's name, under a power its own Terms give, is that agreement's signature, and binds its parties as its Terms say (G1.2, G5.1(b)). *Still not said:*
  - *written in draft 18 (G1.2), as the least reading, taken from the C session's Q11:* an agreement whose Terms give no power for it signs by one act of each of its signers, its `for` naming the agreement and its `under` its Terms in force; and an act made for an agreement under Terms that are not its own is not its signature. Both are for the MOR lead and for Nobody, allegedly, to confirm;
  - whether the default of G4.1 should count a party named as an agreement. It does not, as before: a work that only points at the works it stands on needs none of them to sign ("no collective involved"). So Terms that need an agreement's signing must say so in their own `in_force` rule, and nothing else in the grammar tells a party that must sign from one that is only pointed at. That is one deal people may make and not the only one: a group that wants a say signs Terms whose rule counts it (section 5.2);
  - whether someone who fills a role of an agreement is one of its signers when it signs as a party; G1.2 counts only the parties its Terms name as signers. And how Terms "say otherwise";
  - whether such a party can itself make a counter signature (G4.10). *Written in draft 20,* from the second C session's A14 and A15: which acts of an agreement's several signers are one signing (G1.2), and that such a party makes a counter signature by the same act as its assent (G4.10). Both are Claude's reading (draft 20's section 8.2, kept in `history.md`, section N.4). *Written in draft 21,* from the exchange "Setting aside what an agent made": nothing an agent made is set aside; before Terms are in force, an assent made for a party by an agent is cancelled as the party's own is, by a counter signature in that party's name, which the party itself may make (G4.10, G5.7). That the party itself may make it is Claude's reading of what follows (section 8.2).
- **P18. The baker's succession.** G14 says the succession starts with the baker and that each agreement passes the baker's rights as its own Terms say. Not said: what in an agreement counts as passing them (a version, a Statement of some kind, a filling of kind received); whether the agreements of that succession call for reports (G11); what is read where one envelope has several bakers; and how Terms that sell a baked work's bytes without naming its envelope are read (G14 reads only Terms that name one). *Said in draft 18:* how Terms name a succession, what "bound by them" means, and when the answer is unchecked (G14.2, G14.3); and that the same work baked again by someone else is shown beside each bake, and never picked (G14.5). *Said in draft 20,* from the second C session's A28 and A29: that the rights pass as an earlier agreement's own words say, read by its body cMIP (K1), with no act of the grammar for it; and that a baker who fills a role meets G14.2(a) where the acts held show it (G14.3). Still not said: what those words are (a part for machines of K1, whose shape is the encoding's and the body cMIP's). *Said in draft 21,* from the exchange "A thief's Terms": (a) or (b) of G14.2 is asked of whether Terms that name an envelope count at all, and not only for the work; Terms that name neither the baker nor a succession are invalid (G14.2, G14.3).
- **P19. Where "invalid" stops and "not shown" starts.** "Invalid" is meant as one word across the layers. This text draws one line (section 2): **invalid**, where the act itself shows that it cannot count, whatever other acts exist; **not shown**, wherever an act the verifier does not hold could change the answer. Whether that is the line the other layers draw is not known to this text (section 8.1). A replacement that lacks a signature is, by that line, not shown (G4.8); his word for such a case was "invalid" (section 8.4). From draft 17 there is also **not valid**, the update condition's answer about an update (G4.11). It is kept apart from **invalid**: it is a cMIP's answer, and may rest on a time or on the acts held. Whether the two should be one word is not said. *Said in draft 18:* **valid** and **disputed** each have one meaning in this text, and section 2 says how each differs from the core's word of the same spelling; and the cases of **invalid** are listed there. *Said in draft 21:* Terms that point to an envelope and name neither its baker among their parties nor a succession are one of those cases, an invalid act like the others, and no longer an answer about the work alone (G14.3).
- **P20. What a leg report carries.** K6's row says what a leg report carries beyond what every report carries: what was received, what was passed on and to whom, the doer's own part, what was kept, and, apart from those, what is still owed, each with its amount (G11.3). Not said: how an amount is written so that every reader reads it alike (an amount on a way of paying is the Money layer's to give; a right is something else); whether money shown as still owed is later shown as paid, and by what (what would settle it: section 8.2, item 15); and whether one report may cover many payments (P6). **A person who receives for others**, what the click test showed (its 3g): a work whose receiver is one of its owners passes no report, and none is asked. That owner then holds the others' money, unseen: in the sketch a library's first owner received for both owners, and the second was shown in no run. *Answered in draft 12* (G11.2, G11.5): every leg is the same exchange. Whoever paid that owner reports that they did; the owner confirms by a report of their own; then the owner has the next obligation, within the work's own agreement, and reports there as the doer, each other owner confirming. No call is attached to a receiving side. *Answered in draft 13* (K6's row; G11.5): such an owner's own share has a line of its own, the doer's own part, and is not read as a fee kept (the second click test's 2c). *Still not said:* by which act a doer's own part is named, since nothing moves when a holder keeps their share; how a receiving side's report is "automatic" on some ways of paying (who signs it, and whether a settlement can itself stand as the confirmation: the Money layer's, section 8.1); and whether a work whose owner receives for the others holds the Control task with its socket empty, as G11.2 reads it (section 8.4).
- **P21. One condition.** A version names one cMIP in `condition`: its update condition (G4.11). Not said: whether a version may name several (one for each part of an agreement), or whether the one cMIP's parameters must carry all of it. *Answered in draft 18, from the exchange "The default update condition":* an update that everyone signed still needs the output of a named condition; the core takes that output and has no notion of overruling or locking (G4.11(i)); with no condition named, the default is everyone who signed the original (G4.11(e)). Not said, and written in draft 18 by the least reading: whether the condition is asked of a resolving update (as draft 18 wrote it, it was not); and which condition is asked about a filling where several versions lie between (as written, the one named by the version the filling was earned under; G10.6). *Changed in draft 19:* the condition is asked of a resolving update, since who must sign an update is now part of its answer (G4.11(g); section 8.4).

## 7. Stated costs

Cases that do not fit the grammar. None is closed by a grammar rule.

- **Minds.** Whether a party was able (#3) or free (#5) cannot be shown by any act. Assurance (section 5.3) can make it more likely and record what was done; nothing assigns its failure. Only Align is assigned (G3).
- **Conceal (#39).** Terms can be kept private and their fingerprint lodged in a place; nothing can show the words were not told to someone, and telling cannot be undone.
- **Enforce beyond what was posted (#45).** Words can order a release; something must obey. Against value not under Control, force lies outside the protocol. A proven liar (G6) loses only what they posted and their standing with those who see the proof (standing, section 5.3). Against a stranger who vanishes, the proof is worth nothing.
- **Deeds lean on witnesses.** The more an agreement is about services and acts in the world instead of value under Control, the more it rests on witnesses (section 5.3), whose statements prove only that the witness said them.
- **Spots lean on their confirmers.** Each kind of spot has its own recorded fraud (`late-roles.md`): doing is faked "by simulating the act or stealing credit"; receiving "by forging the chain"; holding "by forging the object or issuing more than exists"; being "by false or multiplied identities". G10 fixes the test in advance and G8.4 reports who confirmed; neither makes the confirmer honest.
- **Future spots are the authors' word.** Whether a future spot was used only for participants no one could anticipate cannot be checked (G10.4). Authors who use one for an ordinary participant, to escape the four kinds' clearer tests, are not stopped by the grammar.
- **Counted attention leans on whoever counts, and the grammar names no one.** Being seen is counted by whoever a spot's cMIP names (section 5.1). The grammar itself no longer requires an agreed witness: an agreement whose cMIP names none, or names the payer, has counts that rest on whoever states them, and inflated attention (`late-roles.md`: "Roles counted by attention suffered fabrication of the event itself") is then exposed only as far as that cMIP goes. A rule that counts only what led to a settlement leaves out someone truly seen whom no payment yet stands behind; a rule that rewards pure attention rests every count on the one who counts.
- **"Now" is what was read, and no more.** Under G13, a payer who names the version they read has paid as that version says, even if the agreement had already changed without their being able to know. That protects the payer; it also means the owners under a later version can see money go by an earlier one. Whether the version named was still the last is answered only per an anchoring, or as shown (G4.6, G13.4), and the climb up a sequence gets less certain at each agreement that names no place.
- **A work that points nowhere cannot be paid.** Under G1, what is owed to an agreement whose version names no receiver has nowhere to go, and is not shown.
- **A deal is as complete as its words.** Who bears a service's fee, and what becomes of a payment that arrives after the work has named another receiver, are whatever the deal states, read by its cMIP. A deal that says nothing on them leaves them unsaid; the grammar supplies no answer.
- **A service is a hand.** Nothing in the grammar makes a service divide as the shares say, or pay at all. The grammar reads only its signed word of what it received, passed on, kept and still owes (G11), and only where its deal called for that word. Two reports that state different amounts for the same settlement are shown side by side, neither chosen, and anyone may count it (standing, section 5.3); getting the money is another matter ("Enforce beyond what was posted"). An owner who receives for the others is a hand in the same way (G11.2): nothing makes them pass it on.
- **A sequence is readable only as far as its agreements call for reports, and are answered.** G11 makes no Terms carry a call for reports, and makes nobody answer a call. From a leg whose call is unanswered, or which has none, nothing ties what lies beyond it to the click: the people beyond are not shown, though they never chose that leg (in the click test, one silent service hid a producer and a whole band). And a call binds only the signers of the Terms that carry it: a work cannot make the works it stands on, or their services, report. What a buyer has is this: before paying, which agreements carry a call for reports, and from whom, read from their signed Terms; afterwards, the receiving side's confirmation of their own payment, and then access to what they bought (G11.7). Which further calls were answered is seen by the two sides of each leg. And a report is its signer's word: it makes the whole readable, not true.
- **One leg, and no further.** By the grammar each report goes to the other side of its own leg, and nowhere else (G11.6). The buyer is shown that their payment was received, and by default no more (G11.7): not whether the money reached every member. The buyer's own leg is read beyond it only as the data cMIP of the offer the buyer signed says (G11.6(b)): a buyer who signs an offer whose Terms name one has agreed to it, as any signer has. And nothing makes the receiving side confirm: a payment nobody confirmed is shown as one side only. A buyer, or a partner, who wants to know whether a split among people at the far end was carried out reads it only where the people of every leg on the way agreed, with their signature, to a data cMIP that gives it. No call gives it. Terms name such a cMIP in `data` (K8), and this text does not say what it is beyond that socket (section 8.4): until one is written and signed for, nobody reads further than their own leg. Where one leg did not agree, the far end is not shown to that reader, whatever was reported there; and the one who received is the only one who says it was passed on. That is the price of not making surveillance cheap. The other side of it: where the people of a leg did sign for such a cMIP, they are read as far as they signed; and where they signed a rule by which the majority decides, a member who was outvoted is read as the majority decided. The grammar reads their signature, not whether they were free to refuse it ("Minds").
- **A contest reaches one agreement.** A buyer who paid and was not delivered to contests the signers of the offer or vow they bought under, and nobody else (G11.8). If the fault lies with a service those signers picked, the buyer depends on them to contest the service in turn; nothing makes them, and the buyer is not shown whether they did. A contest is a word on the record, the buyer's; it moves no money.
- **Paid, and the author gone.** An open offer whose only author goes absent, with nothing that delivers by itself: whoever paid and got nothing can contest. The contest reaches the signers of the agreement they bought under (G11.8), and here that is the author, who is gone. It stays on the record for anyone to read (standing, section 5.3). The record cannot give the money back, since nobody is left to sign. A buyer who wants that pays through an escrow service: a hand, under its deal, for a fee. Without one, the loss is the buyer's. Nothing in the grammar prevents an author from vanishing with what was paid; that is dealt with, if at all, in how services are built. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "MOR only offers ways to ring the alarm", and "MOR just tracks receipts".
- **Order without an anchoring is partial.** Citing shows only "after" (G13.3). Two acts that never cite each other have no order from the acts alone, and nothing shows "nothing later". Where an agreement names no place and no clock, readers who accept different anchorings can get different answers; each answer says which anchoring it stands on. So a late update is seen as late only as far as the acts cite one another, or an anchoring orders them (G4.7): the bytes alone cannot tell late from simultaneous, and a late update whose signers cited nothing that shows the replacement is a fork. Where it names them, every reader uses them (G7.4), and the answers are as good as that place and that clock.
- **Valid is as sure as the update condition's cMIP.** An update condition that reads someone's statement rests on whoever made the statement (G8); one that reads a time rests on the clock the agreement names (G7.4), or, where it names none, on the anchoring the reader accepts (G13.4): then two readers with different anchorings can differ on whether an update is valid, and so on which Terms are in force. A reader who lacks that cMIP cannot tell whether any update replaced: from there on, which Terms are in force is unchecked for them (G9). Nothing stops by itself: rights meant to stop on a date stop, as these grammar rules read them, only when someone makes the update, signed as the Terms require (G4.8), and those who must sign it may refuse. What a version's own words say of such a date is not read by the grammar (G10.6). And a condition is as wide or as narrow as people wrote it: one that answers valid for everything protects no part of the agreement, and one that answers valid for nothing leaves an agreement that even all its parties together cannot update (G4.11): a lock, which is people's own choice of cMIP, and which the grammar reads as any other output. Whoever earns a filling keeps what they earned only as far as the update condition of the version they earned under lets no update take it (G10.6).
- **Reputation is as wide as the acts one holds.** Standing (section 5.3) answers only from what the one asking holds, per place or as shown. Two people holding different acts compute different standing for the same signer, and honest forks (below) count against honest people.
- **Without a place, no scarcity and no freshness.** The same thing can be given twice in two branches, the latest act can be withheld, silence cannot be counted. G7.3 makes this visible; it does not cure it.
- **Ownership is as wide as its place.** Two parties who accept different places cannot settle who holds a thing between them. The same thing sold in two separate agreements is seen by neither agreement's rules. A stranger's sale of a baked work under its own envelope is seen: Terms for an envelope count, for anything, only with the baker, or a succession from the baker, and Terms that name neither are invalid (G14). The attempt itself cannot be stopped: it stays on the record as signed acts, which count for nothing. A stranger who bakes the same bytes again is the baker of that bake, and is shown, not stopped (G14.5). The baker, or a holder, who sells the same work twice in two successions is still seen by neither.
- **The baker is as the envelope says.** G14 rests on the envelope and on whoever it names as baker, as the core gives them. It does not stop anyone from editing a work and baking it again under their own name: that is another envelope, and whether it owes anything to the first is not read by this grammar. It reads only Terms that name an envelope (P18).
- **A copy is made legible, not forbidden.** Anyone may bake the same bytes again, unedited, and sell them under Terms of their own: those Terms count (G14.5). The bytes say who uploaded and when, not who made it, so the grammar never picks between bakes of the same work. A buyer is shown the other bakes only as far as the verifier holds them. Which is the original is a ranking a client may make, a judgment a cMIP or an arbiter may give, and a contest its maker may raise; none of them is a verifier's answer, and a contest moves no money.
- **Closed world hurts the one who did not lodge.** Under G7.2, a party's own act that never reached the place does not count, even if it is genuine.
- **Honest forks.** A signer who loses track of their last act (a lost device, a restored backup) and signs again produces a fork that looks exactly like a lie. Honest drift and lying look the same from outside. Both tips stop, with everything that signer built on either, until the signer resolves the fork (G12.3), if they still hold their key; what others relied on in the tip set aside stands, shown as disputed (G12.5).
- **A fork of Terms holds the agreement where it was.** Under G4.7, while two updates of the same Terms both stand, the Terms before them stay in force: neither tip's change takes effect, including a new receiver (G1.4), and nothing built on either counts (G12.2). If the agreement gave no power to resolve (G12.4), and those whose signatures a resolving update needs will not sign one, it stays there; the grammar shows it and picks no tip. And where the update condition lets fewer than all update the Terms, any group it accepts can hold the agreement where it was: by signing a rival update, and then, against a resolving update, a rival one (G12.7). A fork is then a veto. It can reach back, too: a rival to a version long since replaced, signed by those the update condition of that version accepts, is a fork where neither the knots nor an anchoring show that it came after that version was replaced, and every version made since then stops with the first tip (G4.7). Where they do show it, it replaces nothing. Those versions were signed, and are not undone; they stop until the fork is resolved, and what others relied on in them stands (G12.5). The way out can be said in the language: a power to resolve whose rule accepts only the one resolving update it computes from the acts and the place (G12.4). That is a cMIP's.
- **Resolving does not undo.** What others relied on in a tip set aside stands, shown as disputed (G12.5). A resolving update settles which tip goes on; it cannot take back what a stranger did relying on the other.
- **Withdrawing before in force.** Between a first assent and the last, the first signer holds an open option against themselves. They can close it: an assent may be cancelled, by a counter signature, until the Terms are in force (G4.10). It is still a race, between the counter signature and the last assent, that only a place, or an `in_force` rule with a deadline read from a place or a clock, settles.
- **An offer that stops taking new people.** The same race, between whatever stops the offer (a new version coming into force, a date, the hundredth buyer) and a stranger's filling the spot. Who came in time is per the place and the clock the agreement names, and without them only as shown (G10.5).
- **Secret votes need a teller.** A tally (section 5.3) requires trusting whoever counts.
- **Identity ends in acquaintance.** Who vouches for the voucher (identity, section 5.3) ends in someone knowing someone; the grammar cannot express that. Spots of kind fits rest on it.
- **The record holds no value.** Every act about value is a claim or an instruction. The gap between "the record says 100" and "there are 60" must be checked continuously (a witness, section 5.3; K6), not once.
- **Those who cannot sign.** A contributor who has no key (the long dead, the unknowing) can be named in words but is no party, and nothing can arrive for them.
- **No default way out.** By G4.11, an agreement that names no update condition changes only by the assent of everyone who signed it. And since no agreement ends (G4.9), none can be left by ending it: the last version keeps binding until those its Terms require sign a new one. If a party is gone and no successor was named in advance (#37, #38), it is stuck. (One exception: a delegation ends by its delegator alone, by default, G5.5; not where its signed conditions say otherwise.) The way out is designed in advance or not at all: for a party who wants to leave, an update condition that answers valid for an update that party alone signed, only where it takes its own signer out (section 5.2); this draft chooses that over any default that would let someone be bound without their signature.
- **A judge is as hard to change as the Terms.** A judge named in the Terms, and a power to resolve, change only by an update (G5.6). Where the update condition asks everyone, and the judge is gone, or one party will not sign, the judge stays named and nothing a power does can put another in their place. People who want a judge that can be replaced say how in their update condition, in advance.
- **Bound by an update one did not sign.** Where Terms name an update condition that fewer than all can meet, a party is bound by an update it never signed (G5.1(c)). It signed for that when it signed Terms that name the condition; the grammar shows the condition's fingerprint, and reads nothing of whether that party understood it ("Minds").
- **Ordering and lines show patterns.** G6 and G7 make the shape of an agreement (who acted, how often, in what order) visible to whoever sees the acts or the place, even when the words are concealed. Standing (section 5.3) reads exactly those patterns.
- **What an agent signed stays signed.** A grantor who regrets an agent's act cannot set it aside (G5.7): once the Terms are in force, it changes what was made only by an update, which those who agreed to it may have to sign, as the update condition asks. Whoever hands an agent a power carries what the agent made with it.
- **An agent has what was signed, and no more.** By default a delegator ends a delegation at any time, and the grammar gives the agent no notice and no pay (G5.5). An agent who wants either has it written, as signed conditions or as a cMIP beside the delegation. In the words of Nobody, allegedly: "we’re not Law." The same holds for the delegator: the grammar limits no signed condition, so a delegator who signs that only the agent may end the delegation has given up ending it alone, for as long as the agent chooses, and if the agent is gone, for good. The grammar shows the condition; it stops no one from signing it.

## 10. What the encoding must fix
