## 1. The spine: "What is the minimum two people have to write"

**In plain words.** Before draft 26 there was a question, put to people who had never heard of MOR: what does an agreement need? Their answer was a list of small tasks and a handful of operations that answer them. Read beside MOR, the operations turned out to be MOR's other layers, and what the operations could not do turned out to be the sockets where people plug in their own rules. Agreements is what is left once those are taken away: a short grammar for reading who signed what, in what order.

He asked for the spine on 17 November ("A companion for Agreements"):

> "Agreements has become quite abstract. It will need a good companion that explains why it is structured as such and how we got that that shape."
>
> "The how we got there needs to build around the research we ran on agreeing. « What is the minimum two people have to write »"
>
> "It's also a good place to state that what the review found was MOR other layers and the sockets."

The lead's line: "the companion's story of how Agreements got its shape is built around the research on how people agree, with his question as its spine: what is the minimum two people have to write"; and "the companion states that what the review of how people agree found turned out to be MOR's other layers and the sockets."

### 1.1 The research

On 10 November he asked windows that knew nothing of MOR to work out, from nothing, what agreeing requires. The questions used no MOR word, and not the word "grammar". Their texts are kept as received in `docs/strangers-2026-11/` on main (`README.md`).

**The minimum, answered.** `agreement-tasks.md` starts from four points: a record is "a defense against the other party's future self, and one's own"; "Each party must hold the other's commitment, not their own"; a mark deters a lie only if an outsider could be shown it, "So every written agreement is written for a third person, even one who never turns up"; and so: "the least two people need to write down is exactly the least a third person needs to see." That is the answer to his question, and it is the whole shape of draft 26 in one line: the grammar rules are what a third person, the verifier, can see from signed acts alone (draft 26 §1, "One constraint on everything below"; §3.6, Verifier).

**Forty-nine tasks.** The same file reasons through ten situations, asking each time "what new lie or failure becomes possible that the existing tasks do not defend against?", and arrives at 49 tasks in seven groups (making it, who holds what, doing it, acting through others, staying true over time, going wrong, ending). Draft 25 asked the same question of each of its own rules, in its "two questions" sentence (section 5 below).

**Five operations.** `agreement-grammar.md` tests the 49 against five operations a record can perform: **State** ("fix a set of words so they cannot shift"), **Sign**, **Deliver**, **Order** ("place them in a shared sequence, so "before", "after" and "latest" have an answer") and **Control** ("have power over a thing, so it moves only when a condition is met"); with a silent sixth, **Apply** ("applying a stated rule to known inputs", which "needs no trust: anyone can redo the calculation"). 33 of the 49 come out clean. **Keep** and **Check** come out as qualities, not tasks: a result must last, and an outsider must be able to test it. Draft 26 names the five operations in its §1 ("it names those operations (State, Sign, Deliver, Order, Control) where it needs them") and carries Keep and Check in its last sentence there ("What an act fixes must still be there and unaltered when needed, and an outsider holding copies must be able to test it"); draft 25 cited them by the research's numbers (#9 Keep, #10 Check), which draft 26 leaves out as history (the trace, §1).

**What no record reaches.** The residue is three things: **truth** ("Whenever the agreement touches the world outside the record, someone must say what happened, and their signature proves only that they said it"), **minds** (what someone understood, wanted or knows), and **hands** ("Words can order a release; something must obey"). For minds the file says: "do not try to prove; assign in advance who bears the failure", and its task list adds that in open offers "the whole burden of Align falls on the one who writes the words". Draft 26's 5.3.2 is that sentence in the grammar: "The authors are the parties who bear the failure of the words to mean one thing" (draft 25 tagged it "#4 Align").

**Four outside roles.** What the parties cannot do alone sorts into "A register: one ordered place to look", "A witness of the world", "A hand: has power over the value", and "A judge: decides when the others are disputed". Absences ("nothing later exists", "this is all of it") "can be solved, but only by a single place to look".

**No single shared sequence.** The second test supposes each party keeps only its own chained sequence. Twelve tasks fall, in two clusters, "only once" and "by when"; what survives is "two people signing words to each other". Four findings there are draft 26's ground for order: a shared sequence is needed "for exactly two things: scarcity (this thing, to one person only) and freshness (nothing later exists; the deadline has passed)"; "A fork convicts itself. Two signed entries pointing at the same predecessor prove the signer lied, with no judge"; a witness need only say "I saw this entry at this point in my own sequence"; and the closed world becomes local: "A thing's ownership is exactly as global as its witness set." And one sentence there is draft 26's principle before it had a name: "An amendment needs both signatures, so neither party can be surprised by one. Only a stranger arriving later is exposed." (Section 3.)

**The record cannot hold value.** The third test supposes the record holds only statements about value held elsewhere, which is MOR's situation for money. "The record falls back from control to proof"; the holder becomes an agent, acting for another; and "The receiver's signed receipt becomes the only clean proof that value moved." Draft 26's buyer's confirmation (5.11.7) and leg reports (5.11.3) are that receipt.

**Roles filled later.** `late-roles.md` (12 November) found about eighteen roles that agreements have always named before knowing who would fill them ("whoever brings a buyer", "whoever holds this ticket", "my heirs"), and that they "link to a person in only four ways: by doing something, by receiving from the previous holder, by holding an object, or by being someone who fits a description." Draft 26's four named kinds of spot are those four: **done**, **received**, **holding**, **fits**, with an open fifth, **future** (5.10.1). The person who fills such a spot is the unknown party of section 3. (The scribe's reading: the file is the research named in the assignment; no ledger line ties the four kinds to it.)

## 3. The principle: protect the unknown party

**In plain words.** Some people join an agreement after it was made: a buyer who takes up an offer, anyone who fills an open spot. They were not there when the Terms were written and have no say in how they change. Draft 26 protects them in one way: whatever they joined, they keep, as it was when they joined, for as long as its own words say. No later change by the people who made the agreement reaches them. That is the principle. It is stated here, not in draft 26; draft 26's rules apply it.

### 3.2 The principle, stated

Protect the unknown party. A person who takes part in an agreement through a role (a spot filled after the Terms were made) is its **unknown party** (draft 26 §3.2). She has no say in the agreement's updates. What exists for her is the version her proof names: it binds her, by the assent with which she earned her filling, and it binds the known parties to her, for as long as its words say, counted on the clock that version names. No update of the known parties reaches her, in any way. Nothing changes what she earned except an assent she signs, herself or through a power she gave. That is no update reaching her: a later version changes what she earned only by her own signature on it, which is why 5.10.7 still asks a verifier to name "each later version that changes what it earned, with the assent that rests on" (the scribe's reading of 5.10.6 beside 5.10.7).

The old way and the new. Draft 25 protected her by a veto: its default asked every filler to sign every update (G4.8(a)), and its G10.6(b) still let her own version's update condition change her rights. Draft 26 protects her by reach: she signs no update, and no update reaches her. "It’s the protect the unknown party expressed the old way." The cost he names: "a problem that really only impacts subscriptions models I think" (M10's stated costs, section 7).

**Who she is.** Draft 26 says: "Any filling of a role is an unknown party's, whenever it was made" (§3.2, Unknown party). That clause is the settled note's M15 ("A filling is any filling of a role, whenever made"), the lead's reading. His words are "she is unknown at the time of creation of the agreement". Read the other way (the brief, item 19), a person whose identity was known when the Terms were made, and who still came in through a role, would not be unknown. Draft 26 is written on M15; the other reading is not chosen.

**What the principle is, and is not** (the settled note's F16, the lead's reading): "G10.6 (a) is not an exception to "built on top, as a test": it is a guarantee no module may break (the admission test), not a rule about how people behave." "Built on top, as a test" (18 November) sends every rule "that settles how people should behave in a deal" to the cMIPs. The principle is kept in the grammar because no cMIP in any socket can be allowed to break it: whatever update condition the Terms name, it is "never asked about a filling" (5.5.7). Whether the principle binds a verifier beyond the rules that apply it is unsaid (the brief, item 16): the lead's line of 16:05 says "applied by Agreements' rules", and a verifier applies only grammar rules (§3.1, Grammar rule).

### 3.3 Where draft 26 applies it

**§5.10, the filling** (the principle itself):
- **5.10.6** "**No update reaches a filling.** For a filling, no later update exists." Its holder has no say in updates; no update binds them; what exists for the holder is the version the filling's proof names; "A verifier MUST NOT read any update, or any update condition's answer, as reaching a filling"
- **5.10.7** **Rights.** "A filling has exactly the rights offered by the version it was earned under"; "Nothing changes what a filling earned except an assent its holder signs in their own name (5.6.1(a)), or one made for them under a power they gave". The power clause is the lead's reading on G10.6(a) (section 6).
- **5.10.8** **How long, and no trigger.** What a filling gave "lasts as long as the words of the version it was earned under say, measured on the clock that version names, from the anchoring of the purchase"; the cMIP that reads those words answers; "No update ends what a filling gave, and none is needed" "the clock that version names", not the clock of the Terms in force, is the lead's reading of 13:20 (section 6).
- Beside them: 5.10.2 reads a filling already earned "from the version its proof names, its role and that role's cMIP as that version states them, whatever a later version says of the role" (the lead's reading of 13:20); 5.10.3 "what binds the known parties to the holder of a filling is the version its proof names"; 5.10.5 "a filling already earned stays a filling, shown with its proof".

**§5.6, who is bound:**
- **5.6.1(c)** binds a person by a Terms act they did not sign only "as a known party", and adds: "No update binds anyone through a filling: a filling is bound by the version its proof names, and by no update"
- **5.6.2** "It binds no one through a filling: whoever holds a filling is bound by the version that filling's proof names, for as long as its words say, and by no update"
- **5.6.1(b)** and 5.1.2: a power to act for someone binds them only where their own signature is on the version that gives it: "no update condition can give a power for a person who did not sign the version that gives it" ("Nothing about me without my signature", 18 November). The MOR lead named this rule among those that carry the principle when he asked where it is written (16:05: "the core carrying it by the rules that apply it (G10.6, "nothing about me without my signature")").
- **5.6.6**, *Who is covered*: "a filling is governed by the version its proof names, which no update reaches"

**§5.5, updates:**
- **5.5.5(a)** and **5.5.7(e)**: the default signers of an update are the known parties, "never a party named by role, and never a signer who fills a role". She is not asked, because nothing she holds is at stake.
- **5.5.7**: the update condition "is never asked about a filling: no update reaches one (§5.10)"; 5.5.7(f): "What a filling gave lasts as long as the words of the version it was earned under say … no update ends it, and none is needed."
- **5.5.3**, with **5.8.5** and **5.13.5**: a keeper or an anchoring the Terms do not name, a reader's own or one named only by a rule the parties never signed, "gives an answer only as that reader's, named so: it never gives the Terms' own answer, never puts Terms in force, and never makes an update late". This is the exchange in which the principle was first named ("Whose place counts", 15:51). Who is protected there is anyone who relies on the Terms without having chosen the anchoring (the scribe's reading: the exchange names no person).

**What it protects her from** (the scribe's summary of the rules above): an update of the known parties changing what she bought, her spot or her place (5.10.6); the update condition of the version she signed reaching her (5.5.7, 5.10.6; draft 25's G10.6(b), gone); a power she never gave (5.6.1(b)); a clock, a keeper or an anchoring she never accepted ending or ordering what she holds (5.10.8, 5.5.3, 5.13.5). What it does not give her: a say in updates, or any part of a version made after hers unless she signs it. She "can chose not to renew under new terms" (his words, 17:59).

**Left out where it reaches** (each listed in the brief, section 7, and in the trace): which act "the anchoring of the purchase" anchors (item 12); how long a version binds a filling whose words state no term (item 11); what a verifier shows of an agreement the known parties dissolved among themselves while her version still binds (item 13); whether words such as "renewed on the terms then current" are a later version reaching her (item 14; the settled note's M28, the lead's reading: "a buyer who signs them buys that knowingly; the grammar guarantees only that no update she did not sign reaches her"); the line's list "her rights, her spot, her place in the agreement" and "close the offer, or dissolve it among themselves" beyond his words (item 29).

## 4. Why the text is shaped so

**In plain words.** Draft 26 is short on purpose and says less than earlier drafts did. It warns that it is not to be used alone; it gives sockets, not answers; it names a keeper and says almost nothing more about it; it defines three verbs for what relays do. Each of those choices comes from something he said.

### 4.1 Only rules in the hashed text

"Only rules in the hashed text" (17 November): the MOR lead proposed taking everything that is not a rule out of the hashed text, "since a typo fixed in a history paragraph makes a new specification". He:

> "Yes, the text should be just that. Everything else goes into companion documents for the few who might be interested."

The lead's line: "a hashed text holds its rules and nothing else. The history, the open lists and the reasons go into companion documents beside it." So draft 25's quotations of his words, its *lie exposed / protects* lines, its history notes, its open lists and its stated costs are here, not in draft 26. Of old drafts he said, on 26 November: "Those old drafts should be buried deep, only for people who wish to do some archeology…" (section 11, M10's).

One thing in draft 26 is not a rule: its introduction (§0). "Agreements gets an introduction" (27 November) says "draft 26 opens with an introduction"; "a hashed text holds its rules and nothing else" stands against it. Whether the introduction is in the hashed text is the brief's item 15; draft 26 puts it there, as the assignment said (the outline, section 10, row 2).

### 4.2 The introduction's five points

All five rest on "Agreements gets an introduction" (27 November, 07:18–07:26), read whole. The MOR lead asked whether the warning should say plainly that Terms naming no keeper depend on every needed signer's knots. He:

> "I think agreement need an introduction that states a few things yes. I don’t think it should be stated as such."
>
> "Not to be used bare. Agreements offer the basic sockets to draft rule books, not one unified way of agreeing, more like a language used to decide how to agree."
>
> "Keepers (old “place”) gives the drafters of agreements a way to agree on where the agreement shall be stored. (This can be simply mentioned when introducing the concept of keeper)"
>
> "Knots is a MOR wide concept, but in agreement Rely is a good place to discuss their importance."

The lead's line: "draft 26 opens with an introduction that says: Agreements is not to be used bare; it offers the basic sockets to draft rule books, not one unified way of agreeing, more a language used to decide how to agree. The keeper is introduced as the drafters' way to agree where the agreement is kept. Knots are MOR-wide; in Agreements their importance is discussed where Rely is. The MOR lead's one-sentence warning about knots is not written as such. (The MOR lead's reading of his third line: "stored" means kept …)"

1. **Not to be used bare.** Draft 26 §0 opens: "**Agreements is not to be used bare.**", set in bold as the "big warning". His words, first in "No default way out" (17 November): "Yes, it.s still true under the right cMIPs. Agreements is not to be used bare. It's to build on top of."; then in "A late version 3 is rejected" (26 November, 15:37): "Probably right. This version of agreements is not to be used bare. Big warning needs to be placed on it." (the lead's line there: "Where and how the warning is written is for the rewrite"). The settled note's Q22 (the lead's): "The warning is the introduction's, not the verifier's output." Why (the scribe's reading): bare, the grammar has no default exit, no default buyer, no default for many sockets ("Named signers and buyer by assent stay cMIPs", 26 November); people agree through the cMIPs they name.
2. **Sockets for rule books; more a language.** Draft 26 §0: "It is not one unified way of agreeing. It offers the basic sockets from which rule books are drafted: more a language, used to decide how to agree, than a set of rules for agreeing. The rules people agree by are written in the cMIPs built on it." His words above, with 4.3 below. "than a set of rules for agreeing" is M2's wording (the trace, §0).
3. **The keeper, as the drafters' way to agree where it is kept.** Draft 26 says it twice, briefly: in §0 ("Among what it offers is the keeper: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where the agreement is kept") and where the keeper is defined (§3.4, Keeper; 5.8.1), since his bracket asks for it "when introducing the concept of keeper" and the assignment put it in §0. "Kept" for his "stored" is the lead's reading. Then he (07:27): "Yes, stored like on any other relay, no?", which led to the three verbs (4.5).
4. **Knots are MOR-wide; their importance where Rely is.** Draft 26 §0: "Knots are a concept of all of MOR, not of Agreements alone. ### §5.5 Updates: the gate and the pin

*Draft 25's lines* (G4, for §5.4 and §5.5), rewritten here, the scribe's, where the rules changed. *Lie exposed* (draft 25, holding): ""You signed, so you are bound", said by a party who never signed; "these are the Terms now", said of Terms replaced, of one branch of two, of a replacement that lacks a signature, or of one that the agreement's own update condition answers not valid; "we were first", said of an update the acts or the place show was made after the version it names was replaced; and "this agreement is over"." *Rewritten*: "these are the Terms now" is exposed also where an update is valid and its order not shown (5.5.1); "we were first" is shown by the knots, or by the keepers or the clock the version names, and by no anchoring the Terms do not name (5.5.4(b)); and "this agreement is over" is exposed as an update like any other (5.5.6). *Protects* (draft 25): "The first to sign; the party left out of a replacement; everyone in the versions made since, whom a late update cannot take back to an old one where the acts or the place show it late; everyone in the last version, which keeps binding until an update its own update condition answers valid replaces it; and whoever relies on a part of an agreement that its update condition lets no update touch." *Rewritten*: "until an update passes its gate and its pin replaces it"; "where the acts, the keepers or the clock show it late"; and, added, the unknown party, whom no update reaches (5.10.6), and the known parties, none of whom is bypassed by a default that counts fillers (5.5.5(a)).

**5.5.1** (N1, new). *What it says*: an update replaces the version it names only where it passes two checks: **the gate**, that version's update condition (or its default) answers valid; and **the pin**, its order is shown, by the keepers that version names or, where it names none, by knots. *The exchange*: "The gate and the pin; a fork, not a race" (26 November, 17:39–17:46), refining "Valid and in force, two words, two jobs" (18:00) and "Valid means in force: one gate for an update" (16:19). At 16:19 he had said "I agree with CP. to me it means the same thing… valid, in force, what’s the difference for the core? The core should only see one version as valid", and the lead's line made one gate, two valid updates being "a race". At 17:39 the MOR lead asked whether the pin stays, being "order and not consent". He: "I think I got it but want to be sure. Reword it pls"; "So the pin and the gates are…"; "In MOR terms, in specs pls"; then "How is this then enforced? Client compliance? When I think about agreements, I always think about malicious clients…"; "The agree,ent own way of resolving forks… how is that decided?"; and "Ok, that”s what I was trying to remember we forced the deadlock and push them to resolve it". *The lead's line*: "an update passes two checks: the gate … and the pin … Two updates that both pass the gate and that nothing orders are a fork … The deadlock is forced on purpose, to push them to resolve it. "Race", the MOR lead's word on 26 November, is withdrawn for updates of Terms." The wording of G4.8 and G4.7 in the entry is the MOR lead's (item 31, section 6).
