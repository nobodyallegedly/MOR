## In plain words

1. This list covers part 3 of 3: the documents (case studies, paper, companions). Almost all of them were written against the old Agreements rules (draft 24, in `spec/`), and 539 places in 23 of the 24 files lean on those rules. The companions' README has none.
2. Each place is counted once, under one of three kinds. Most places only use an old word (stake, split, grant, keeper, standing offer, time reference). Those are a change of word, 240 of them.
3. Of the rest, 195 places describe an old rule, and 104 lean on something draft 25 does not have at all: collectives with their own keys, claims, publications, stakes, endings, keepers.
4. In 20 documents the story itself changes, not only the words. Mostly it changes in five ways: who must sign a change, how a buyer signs on, who confirms a sale, whether anything ever ends, and who sees how money was divided.
5. Six places cannot be settled from draft 25 as written; they are listed at the end as U1 to U6, each with its reason.

## The story changes

One paragraph for each document whose story changes: what the document says happens, and what draft 25 says happens instead, both quoted, with the D-numbers of the table of places. Not here: the journalism case study (11), the case studies' README and the doors list, whose words change but whose story holds; and the companions' README, which has no place at all. Where draft 25 leaves the answer to a cMIP the parties choose, that is said plainly: the document then states as a rule of MOR something that draft 25 leaves to each agreement. U1 to U6 are the unclear places, listed at the end.

*A few words, as draft 25 uses them* (the working list above gives the old ones, such as clone, keeper and grant). **Terms**: the act that states what is agreed. **Update**: a new version of the Terms; an agreement never ends, it only gets new versions. **Update condition**: the cMIP an agreement names to say which updates are valid and who must sign them; with none named, everyone who signed the version before must sign. **cMIP**: a document, named by its hash, that the parties plug into a field of their agreement to answer one question (who fills a spot, how money is passed on, and so on). **Offer**: an agreement with a place for someone not yet known, such as a buyer. **Vow**: in draft 24, a promise sold before the thing exists, as an act of its own; in draft 25, only "the same thing" as an offer. **Deal**: in draft 25, the agreement between a service (such as one that divides money) and the agreement it serves. **Receiver**: whom an agreement names to receive what is paid to it. **Spot**: a place in an agreement for someone not yet known; its cMIP says who fills it and on whose word. **Envelope, baker**: a finished work is sealed in an envelope that names who baked it; the baker's rights can pass to another holder. **Judicial**: what the Terms set for a dispute (who judges, the contest). Rule numbers such as G4.11 are draft 25's grammar rules; K1 to K8 its eight fields for cMIPs.

**04, Augmented Democracy.** *Who signs for the association, and how a delegation ends.* The document: the association is "a full identity with its own home and keys", where "two of five board members sign everyday acts, and four of five rotate its chain key"; and a delegator "can revoke it with an everyday act until the round closes" (D-001, D-003). Draft 25: an agreement has no keys of its own; it signs through a power its Terms give, or else "x signs by every one of its signers" (G1.2), x being the agreement that signs. A delegation ends by a new version of its Terms: "By default the delegator can end it at any time: where those Terms name no update condition, a version of them that removes or narrows the power is valid, and replaces them" (G5.5).

**08, From Bedroom to Label.** *Who signs when a member leaves; how fans sign on; who confirms; what ends; who sees a sale outside the deal.* When the band's drummer leaves, the document says: "the remaining members clone the founding agreement without him" (D-030). Draft 25: "Where Terms name no update condition, an update is valid when everyone who signed the original signs it" (G4.11(e)), and the drummer signed the original, so without an update condition the remaining members cannot update the band's Terms without him. The document: a fan may contest "since only those who signed onto the vow, by paying, may", and each preorder is "pending until the fan's own wallet acknowledges the release" (D-042). Draft 25: the buyer is "a signer of the offer's Terms, whose assent names the version they buy under" (G11.7); the confirmation is the receiving side's report that the payment arrived, and "After the confirmation comes access to what was bought ... these grammar rules do not read it" (G11.7). The same holds where the band buys a mix from an engineer, sold as a vow: the document has the band confirm the sale once the mix arrives (D-029); in draft 25 the engineer's side confirms that the payment arrived, and the band, as the buyer, has only a buyer's contest, that "what they bought was not delivered after payment" (G11.8). The document: a member's preorder outside the deal is shown "to every fan as made outside the claiming agreement" (D-044). Draft 25 has nothing by which a deal claims a vow: it defines a vow only as "the same thing as an offer", and adds "This text gives the word no more meaning than that" (section 2, Vow). The document: a manager's deal signed "at the same time or after is void, unless the label adopts it" (D-045). Draft 25: such an act binds if made before the version that takes the manager's power away, not after, and is "not shown where citing gives no order" (G5.4); "No act of the grantor, and no act of anyone else, sets aside, voids or undoes an act made under its grant" (G5.7). The document: "the old deal closes. Nobody's stake shrinks without their own signature" (D-053, D-054). Draft 25: "No act ends Terms, or an agreement, without replacing them" (G4.9), and an update condition may answer valid "to an update fewer than all have signed, which then replaces" (G4.11(i)). And the split service is "named in the agreement through a grant" (D-023), where draft 25's deal "is signed on both sides: by the service, and by the agreement it serves" (section 2, Service, deal).

**09, From Cat Video to Coproduction.** *Who signs a share sale; how a deal ends; who sees the streamer's division.* The document: the team sells a stake to a supporter; "The sale is signed by the team and the buyer ... nobody else's stake changes" (D-074). Draft 25 has no stake transfer: who holds which share changes only by an update, and by default "everyone who signed the original signs it" (G4.11(e)). The document: "the team ends its agreement" with a platform (D-078). Draft 25: "a verifier MUST NOT answer that Terms or an agreement have ended" (G4.9). The document: "Nothing in a deal changes without the signature of every party whose voice remains" (D-088). Draft 25 leaves it to the deal: "Which updates are valid, who must sign them included, and why, is that cMIP's to specify" (section 2, Condition). The document: "Whichever model the streamer uses, the coproducers see it" (D-093). Draft 25: which lines of a report each receiving side receives "is said by the cMIP that answers the task (K6). These grammar rules do not say it" (G11.3).

**10, Migration to MOR2.** *Who signs the crossing, and whether anything ends.* The document: the drummer never moves to MOR2, and his stakes stay as they were: "the MOR2 clones carry them unchanged" (D-112). Draft 25: by default an update needs everyone who signed the original (G4.11(e)), which includes him unless the agreement names an update condition that says otherwise. The document: "Members can leave a collective and keep their stakes" (D-106). Draft 25 has no such rule; a party who wants to leave needs "an update condition that answers valid for an update that party alone signed" (draft 25, section 5.2). The document: a deal continues "until its parties agree to clone it, or until it ends", and agreements remain "until they agree, the agreement ends, or its abandonment clause applies" (D-115, D-120). Draft 25: "an agreement never ends. It only gets a new version" (section 2, Never ended). The band's rotation "needs the signatures its founding agreement requires" under its key grammar (D-109); draft 25 has no collective keys. Whether a MOR2 act can replace a MOR1 version is unclear (U2).

**12, One Reputation, Many Trades.** *How a client signs on, who confirms, who may contest, who judges.* The document: "the sale is pending until the client confirms it; if the client contests it, it is contested; nothing else moves it, and only the client, who signed onto the vow by paying, can" (D-144). Draft 25: the client signs by an assent to the offer's Terms (G11.7); there is no state of a sale; the confirmation is the receiving side's, and "it MUST NOT show a payment as received on the buyer's word alone" (G11.7). The document: "the core reserves it to those who signed onto the vow" (D-149); draft 25: "Who else may make a contest, and when, is not said" (G11.8). The document: "The agreement names its judges as a chain ... only the active judge settles, and a stage that passes unanswered hands the question to the next" (D-159). Draft 25 has no chain of judgment: a judge is a module named in the Terms, and "A judge's decision binds only through a power" (G5.6(e)). The document: the job's agreement "writes the stake in the worker's vow, held by the worker" (D-143); draft 25's vow carries no stake.

**13, From Pocket Game to Open Market.** *Who must sign a change to a licence.* The document: the engine licence "changes only by cloning, and a clone needs the signature of every party ... it cannot change the licence the studio already signed", and "nobody can change terms already signed" (D-185, D-195). Draft 25: that holds only where the licence names no update condition (G4.11(e)); where it names one, "Who must sign is part of the cMIP's answer" (G4.11(d)), and a party "signed, or was bound by, Terms that say how they may be updated, and it is bound by an update made so" (G5.2). So whether the engine maker can change it is the licence's own choice, readable in its Terms, not a guarantee of the core. Resale is unclear (U3).

**14, Shown, Not Listed.** *Whether a course needs the lessons' consent; who confirms an enrolment.* The document: "With the lessons' agreements as parties of the course's agreement, each consenting by its own rule, the course is a layer above them ... Without their consent, the course is a derivative work of its own" (D-203). Draft 25: "Naming an agreement as a party asks nothing of it: it need not sign to be named" (G1.2), and a payment the course makes to a lesson's agreement still reaches that lesson's receiver (G1.4); so the line the document draws between a layer and a derivative is not one draft 25 draws. The document: an enrolment is "pending until the student confirms" and "Only an enrolled student can contest" (D-200); draft 25: no state of a sale, a buyer's contest (G11.8), and who else may contest "is not said" (U1).

**15, Paid for Results, Seen for What It Is.** *Whose word earns a referrer's share.* The document: "the only referral that counts is the one the buyer signed ... the same rule: evidence for a share comes from the other side of the deal" (D-210), and "A share needs the buyer's signature" (D-217). Draft 25 keeps no such rule in the grammar: a spot is filled "when its K5 cMIP ... names that signer on the strength of a proof" (G10.2), and whom the spot's cMIP trusts is "whoever the cMIP names as confirming: for done, a witness or the counterparty" (K5's row). The buyer's signature is then one choice a spot's cMIP may make, not the core's guarantee.

**16, No Firm Required.** *Who confirms an order; what marks a resale.* The document: "When the bowl arrives, the buyer's client acknowledges the courier's last handover, and the sale is confirmed" (D-223), and "The last handover is what the buyer's client acknowledges to confirm the sale" (D-231). Draft 25: the confirmation is the receiving side's report that the payment arrived (G11.7), and delivery is not read: "By default the buyer is shown nothing further" (G11.7). The document: "a storefront that offers a maker's claimed vow outside the maker's agreement is shown as outside it by every conforming client" (D-230). Draft 25 has no claim on a vow, and nothing marks such an offer.

**17, From the Stands to the Main League.** *Who confirms a ticket; what marks a resale; whose word pays a relay.* The document: "Each fan's ticket is a sale, pending until the fan confirms ... the fan's wallet confirms on the way out" (D-255), and a resale "outside the club's agreement ... a conforming client shows every buyer as outside the club's agreement" (D-257; also D-262). Draft 25: no state of a sale, the receiving side confirms the payment (G11.7), and nothing marks a resale. The document: "Who is paid for a service follows the core's rule: ... one chosen at payment (a relay, a raid's referrer) is acknowledged by the viewer's wallet, and none is paid on its own record alone" (D-267). Draft 25 leaves whose word fills a spot to the spot's cMIP (G10.2; K5's row).

**18, Adult Content, Consent on the Record.** *How distribution stops, and who stops it.* The document: "the performer withdraws a publication made for her herself, and contests only those that are not", and "A platform's publication not made for the performer is the platform's to withdraw. One made for her, she withdraws herself" (D-275, D-284). Draft 25 has no publication and nothing "made for" someone: an offer stops taking new identities "by a new version (G4.8, G4.11), or by what the version in force itself states in advance" (G10.5), so who can stop it is whoever its update condition lets sign that version; and a contest is made against an offer or a vow (G11.8), its reach for a performer who did not sign it unclear (U1).

**19, Paying the Ones Everything Stands On.** *Who signs when a maintainer leaves; how the judge changes.* The document: "the others clone the agreement without her and rotate the keys" (D-295). Draft 25: by default "everyone who signed the original" signs an update (G4.11(e)), she included. The document: "who judges a member's absence, or which keeper records the shares, cannot change without the signature of every member whose voice remains" (D-296). Draft 25: the judge changes "only when a new version says otherwise, made by an update the update condition answers valid" (G5.6(d)), which may be fewer than all; and there is no absence clause or keeper.

**20, From Agents to Open Intelligence.** *Who signs for Lena, and how the delegation ends.* The document: "The agent holds a key of Lena's, limited to the grant ... what it signs with it is Lena's own act. Lena can revoke the grant at any moment with an everyday act" (D-311), and "The agent signs as Lena ... so the commissions and their ratings are on Lena's record" (D-317). Draft 25: there is no key; the agent signs its own act, naming Lena in `for` and the delegation in `under`, which binds her where the power's rule says yes (G5.1(b)), and "Every act within an agreement MUST name in `previous` its signer's previous act" (G6.1): the act stands in the agent's line. The delegation ends by a version of its Terms, by default the delegator's alone (G5.5), and what the agent already made is never undone: "It’s never set aside, it’s always update" (G5.7, quoting Nobody, allegedly).

**21, From One Sentence to a Research Programme.** *Who must sign a change; who decides who judges.* The document: "A deal changes only with every party's signature: the foundation cannot quietly change the second payment's terms" (D-355), and in the consortium "who judges a dispute changes only with every member's signature" (D-367). Draft 25 makes both the agreement's own choice: by default everyone who signed the original (G4.11(e)), otherwise "Who must sign is part of the cMIP's answer" (G4.11(d)); the judge changes by "an update the update condition answers valid" (G5.6(d)). The document's areas and lanes (parts of a collective's work held by some members), "held by her and the senior hydrologist together, both needed" (D-357), become powers whose rule says yes (G5.1(b)); its open question 4 (D-377), how a department's signed stamp of approval counts, has an answer in draft 25: a Statement of a kind a cMIP defines (K7), made under a power for that kind (G1.2).

**The paper, draft 3.** *Almost all of section 5.2 to 5.4, and the claims built on them.* The paper: "**Only your own signature binds you.** No rule in any terms can make a party owe what they did not sign" (D-393). Draft 25: a person is also bound where "the act is a Terms act that replaces Terms by which they are bound, and the update condition of those Terms answers valid for it" (G5.1(c)). The paper: "A clone never reduces a stake without its holder's signature" and "a deal changes only with every party" (D-394, D-413); draft 25 has no stake, and leaves who must sign to the update condition (G4.11). The paper: "A member can always leave alone" and "A collective can end in four ways" (D-403, D-404); draft 25: "an agreement never ends" (section 2) and leaving is an update its condition accepts (draft 25, section 5.2). **The unwritten layer, version 3.** *How buyers sign on, who sees the division, whether a deal ends, who owns.* The document: "every cut sits in a signed split that reaches everyone it pays", and "The filmmaker sees every split that pays her, whole" (D-424, D-432; also D-451). **MOR in one page, version 11.** *How a group ends, and how a work is given to everyone.* The document: "A group that can no longer agree can split: each side first starts its own new group ... and the old group closes", and "A work can also be given to everyone, now or on a date, if everyone who owns it signs" (D-533, D-534).
