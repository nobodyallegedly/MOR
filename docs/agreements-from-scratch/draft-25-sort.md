## In plain words

1. Of the 124 rules, 79 are grammar as they stand, 3 are built on top outright, 18 belong to another layer, 12 are part one thing and part another, and 12 I cannot tell.
2. Few rules settle outright how people should behave in a deal (what the ledger calls forcing the protocol "into a corner"), because draft 25 already hands most conduct to sockets. The three outright are the delegator's default way out (G5.5, and G4.8(f), which only points to it) and judging between two bakes of one work (G14.5(d)). Four more do it in part: who bears unclear words (G3.2), and who must pass on and report (G11.2, G11.3, G11.5).
3. The larger movement is to other layers, and the ledger already records both moves as agreed: the baker's rule (almost all of G14) to Envelopes, and the reading of reports (G11.4 with its sub-clauses (a) to (d), and G11.6 itself, though not its sub-clauses on consent to data) to the MICS.
4. If those rules left and moved, what would remain is the language itself: who is bound and by what, when Terms are in force and how they are replaced, fingerprints, roles and spots, forks, citing, honest answers, and the place as a pointer. It is 54 353 bytes of the rules' 99 787 (54%), and up to 75 450 (76%) if every split and undecided rule stayed whole.
5. The click: the remainder carries the buyer's click (her assent to the Terms and her payment) and finds who receives the payment. It stops there. Passing the money on to the two contributors, and showing that it was done, would be for a cMIP in the `control` socket, the report block in the MICS, and Money.
6. Of the 12 I cannot tell, five would change what a verifier answers about the same acts: the counter signature (G4.10), the signer's line (G6), rights already earned (G10.6), the buyer's leg (G11.7), and what outsiders relied on after a fork (G12.5).

## How to read the table

- **The four placements** are the MOR lead's wording, from the ledger's entry "What the core is for". *Grammar*: part of the shared language every act is written in, or a guarantee the bytes can check that no module may be able to break. *Built on top*: it settles how people should behave in a deal (who must pay, pass on, report, stay answerable, may leave) rather than what can be written and checked; it would belong to a cMIP in a socket. *Another layer*: Identity, Text, Envelopes, Money, Development or the MICS. *Cannot tell*: two placements are defensible, and both are given.
- **The first sentence** of each rule is copied from draft 25 exactly, with its own bold and quote marks. Where a rule opens with a bold title, the title and the sentence after it are both given.
- **"Part ..., part ..."** means the rule is split, and the "why" cell says which part is which.
- **Settled by a decision?** "yes" means an entry of the ledger settles where the rule lives. "touched" means an entry bears on what the rule says, not on where it lives. "no" means neither. Words in quote marks are copied from the ledger or from draft 25; where a quoted line is the MOR lead's "Arrived at (to check)" reading and not the words of Nobody, allegedly, the cell says so.
- **Read or reasoned** is about the placement only. *Read*: draft 25 or a ledger entry says it in words, quoted in the row. *Reasoned*: my inference from the definitions above.
- **Sockets** are named by their field in draft 25's section 5: `body` (K1), `in_force` (K2), `powers` (K3), `condition` (K4), spot (K5), `control` (K6), Statement kind (K7), `data` (K8).
- **Two limits of the four placements**, as I used them. First, "grammar" here means "is language", wherever its page ends up: rules such as G8 and G9 (say who said it; unchecked is not passed) hold for every layer and could sit in the MICS with no reader's answer changing. Nothing I read says so, so they are marked grammar. Second, the ledger's entry "The place is a pointer" takes most of G7 out of the grammar and gives it to "the relay's or a cMIP's" (the MOR lead's words). That is none of the six layers the sort names. I wrote "the layer that orders acts", draft 25's own name for it.
