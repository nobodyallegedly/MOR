## In plain words

1. Two parts of draft 25 leave Agreements, with several borders still open: most of the baker's rule (G14) goes to the Envelopes layer, and the rules for reading reports side by side (G11.4 and its kin) go to the shared language, the MICS.
2. The place (G7) shrinks to a field and one sentence (what else goes with the clock is open): where the Terms name a place, everyone reads what is latest from it, and from nothing else.
3. An update replaces the old Terms as soon as the old Terms' update condition says yes; every rule that also asked the new version to be "in force" by its own rule changes.
4. A new default enters: anyone who signed can walk out of Terms that are already in force, unless the Terms say otherwise. Draft 25 has the opposite stated cost, "No default way out", and gives no way to leave.
5. Where it is unclear whether someone's assent counts, the answers that depend on it become "unchecked" instead of being decided without that person.
6. Decisions 5, 6 and 7 add no rule to Agreements: no move to a later text is written into MOR 1; a signer's life-long line of acts belongs to the layer below, which is asked to keep it unbroken across a key change (whether draft 25's G6 is that line can be read two ways); and everything freezes once, together. Decision 1 puts three baker questions back on the open list.

## Decision 1. Three lines cut back

**The exchange.** The scribe found three "Arrived at" lines saying more than their exchanges: "bound by them" and "not by agreements" (The envelope states its owner), and "an act that never counted" (The holder at the time). The MOR lead leaned to cut all three back to his words, and to put anything draft 25 built on them back on the open list. He: "Agreed."

**The MOR lead's line (to check).** "the three lines now say only what the exchanges said. Open again, for the review: whether a holder of the baker's rights must be bound by the Terms, beyond being a party (draft 25 G14.2 has "bound by them", from the earlier exchange "The baker's rights after a sale"); whether agreements play any part in stating ownership; and whether a thief's Terms are an act that never counted."

This decision changes no rule. It takes away the ledger support for three readings that draft 25 relies on, and puts each back on the open list. What draft 25 built on each:

### 1a. "Bound by them": a party, or a party who is also bound?

Touched in draft 25:
- **G14.2**: "Terms that name an envelope count, for that work and for anything else, only if the holder at the act they cite (G14.1) is one of their parties, bound by them (G5.1), and they are not late (below)."
- **G14.2**, the paragraph "**"Bound by them"**, as draft 22 reads it, means that the Terms are, or have been, a version, and that the party is bound by them in any way G5.1 allows …".
- **G14.3(b)**: "Where they name the holder at the act they cite, or reach that holder through a role, and the acts held do not establish that the holder is bound by them, as G14.2 reads "bound by them", the answer is **not shown** …".
- **Section 5.2**, row "The baker sells her rights": "the holder is then bound by it, as G14.2 reads "bound by them" (G5.1(c))".

What happens: these sentences **stay as written for now; their basis is open again**. Since decision 8 moves G14 out of Agreements anyway, the question goes with whatever check stays (see decision 8).

What it opens: must the holder be a party *and* bound, or only a party? His own words point both ways, and the decision does not choose:
- Party only: "the owner of the bake rights have to be a party of the agreement that rules the envelope" ("A thief's Terms"); "An agreement that points to an envelope already needs the baker (or the holder of the bakers rights) to be valid" (the same exchange).
- Bound as well: in "The baker's rights after a sale", the MOR lead leaned "that the baker must be a party and bound, in any of the usual ways", and he answered "Agreed". That exchange's own "Arrived at" line still says "bound by them". Decision 1 cut "bound by them" only from the later line ("The envelope states its owner"), but its "Arrived at" line still reopens the question.

### 1b. "Not by agreements": can an agreement play any part in stating ownership?

Touched in draft 25:
- **G14.1**: "So the holder of the baker's rights is read from the envelope's own chain, and from nothing in any agreement".
- **G14.2**: "Nothing in an agreement passes the baker's rights: these grammar rules read no succession of agreements, and no reading of a body (K1) passes them."
- **K1's row**: "From draft 23 it gives nothing for the baker's rights: those are read from the envelope's chain (G14.2)".
- **Section 2, "Succession"**: "no longer a term of this text … From draft 23 those rights pass by transfers in the envelope's chain, and no agreement passes them (G14.2)."
- **Section 5.2**, row "The baker sells her rights": "Draft 22's rights cMIP in the body's socket (K1) passes nothing now".

What happens: **stays as written for now; open again**.

What it opens: his words are "the chain in question is the one (or should be) of the envelope, not the one of the baker" and "flips ownership to the thing being owned stating it". They say where ownership is stated (the envelope). They do not say whether an agreement may also take part, for example an agreement whose signers transfer the rights. The MOR lead's reading in that exchange said that "the succession through agreements and the rights cMIP passing rights in a body go", and he answered "Yes. I think that is the cleanest approach …". Decision 1 reopens it anyway. Both readings stand: (i) no agreement takes part, as draft 25 has it; (ii) the envelope states the owner, and how a transfer comes about (through an agreement or not) is left open.

### 1c. "An act that never counted": a thief's Terms

Touched in draft 25:
- **G14.3(a)**: "They never count, for anything, their receiver included." And: "A thief's Terms cite a point of the chain where he held nothing: they are invalid for good, and no later transfer, to him or to anyone, makes them count".
- **Section 2, "invalid"**: "An invalid act counts for nothing, for anything: no answer of these grammar rules rests on it, and no act counts through it."
- The head of draft 25 (outside sections 2 to 5): "so "invalid" stays an act that never counted (G14.2, G14.3)".

What happens: **stays as written for now; open again**.

What it opens: what his words cover, and what they leave open. When asked whether the whole agreement then counts for nothing, receiver included, he answered: "Invalid yes, sorry." When the MOR lead said a thief's Terms "stay invalid for good", he answered "Agreed" ("The holder at the time"). What was cut is "an act that never counted", which says the Terms never counted, from the start. The open question is whether "invalid", as he used it, means "never counted, from the start", or only "counts for nothing".

## Decision 2. The two gates

**The exchange.** The MOR lead set out the two checks an update passes today (the old Terms' update condition, and the new version's own `in_force`), the "two of three" example where the second gate makes the first hollow ("any two owners of three" may update, yet the new version, by default, is in force only once all three assent), and three choices: (a) the update condition alone decides, (b) both gates, (c) the new version's `in_force` alone; the MOR lead leaned (a). He: "Case against c? And for it?" … He: "A, agreed"

**The MOR lead's line (to check).** "(a). Where the update condition answers valid, the update replaces the version it names. The new version's own `in_force` governs only its own later life, not whether it replaces the old one."

His words are "A, agreed". The second sentence of the line ("governs only its own later life") is the MOR lead's. It says more than his words, and it can be read two ways (below).

Touched in draft 25, and what happens to each:
- **G4.1**: "Terms are in force when the assents their `in_force` rule requires are present." **Changes**: for a version that replaces another, this rule no longer decides whether the replacement happens.
- **G4.5**: "Terms stop being in force when Terms that replace them (G5.2, G4.8) are in force." **Changes**: the old Terms are replaced when the update condition says valid, not when the new Terms are in force by their own rule.
- **G4.7**: a fork is two Terms acts that "each replace the same Terms (G4.8), and each has the assents its own `in_force` requires (G4.1)"; and a late update L (L: the late Terms act; O: the version it names, already replaced; F: the update that replaced O) is one "which O's update condition answers valid and which has the assents its own `in_force` requires". **Changes**: the "its own `in_force`" half drops from both tests.
- **G4.7(a)**: "enough for F to be valid and in force (G4.1, G4.11)"; "or it is not in force by its own `in_force`". **Changes**, the same way.
- **G4.7(b)**: "the first position at which, counting only the acts the place shows at or before it, L is valid and in force". **Changes**, the same way.
- **G4.8**: "Whether the replacing Terms are themselves in force is G4.1's, as draft 18 has it (G4.5; section 8.4)." **Changes**: this was the second gate.
- **G4.8(a)**: "a Terms act that replaces it needs only to be in force by its own `in_force`". **Changes**, the same way.
- **G4.8(g)** and **G4.11(d)**: "One thing is left: the update condition's answer, or the default's"; "the update replaces the version in force (G4.8)". **Stay**: decision 2 confirms them.
- **G5.4**: "the replacing Terms are valid (G4.11) and in force (G4.1)". **Changes**, the same way.
- **G5.5**: "it replaces, and is in force, without the agent's assent, however it lists its parties and whatever its own `in_force` says". **Changes**: this exception goes; the rest of the clause stays. Fable's review, section 1(a), reads this as "an exception by hand for one case" of the second gate. Under (a) the exception has nothing left to do in this clause. Whether the delegator's default stays is another matter: Fable's review, section 6, item 4, raises it, and decision 2 does not answer it.
- **G12.1**: an update of Terms is signed by "those whose signing the update condition asks, or the default (G4.8, G4.11), and those whose assent G4.1 asks for". **Changes**: the last part drops.
- **G13.3**: "enough for those Terms to be in force (G4.1) and, for a replacing version, valid (G4.11)"; "the first position at which … they are valid and in force". **Changes**, for a replacing version.
- **K2's row** ("Are the assents that put these Terms in force present?", used at G4.1). **Changes**: for a version that replaces another, its answer no longer decides the replacement.
- **Section 8.3, item 2** ("The new version's own `in_force` … Still open"). **Answered**, for whether the update replaces. What the line's "own later life" means is still open.
- **Section 2, "Update"** ("all the parties that needed to sign it have signed") and **"Late update"**: **change** with G12.1 and G4.7.

What it opens:
- **"Its own later life."** These are the MOR lead's words, not his. Two readings: (i) a version that replaces is in force from that moment, and its `in_force` field is read only where something else asks it (whose assents later count, for example); (ii) the old version is replaced, but the new one is in force only once its own `in_force` is met, so for a while no Terms would be in force. Reading (ii) clashes with G4.9 ("Terms stop being in force only as G4.5 says"). The decision chooses neither.
- **G4.2** ("Terms that name a party by role MUST carry `in_force`"). Does this still bind a version that replaces another, if its `in_force` is not read for the replacement?
- **G4.10** (the counter signature is available "until those Terms are in force"). When is a version that replaces another "in force", for a signer cancelling their assent to it?
- **G14.2** ("an assent without which they are not in force or not a version"). Under (a), does "not in force" still apply to a version?

## Decision 3. A way out by default

**The exchange.** The MOR lead set out that a signer can cancel their own assent before Terms are in force but not after, so a person who does not negotiate lands on the default and cannot leave, and a receiver cannot resign; and three choices: (a) no default exit, (b) a core block always there, "from now on, not me", (c) an exit by default that Terms can switch off, said plainly before anyone signs; the MOR lead leaned (c), with leaving never erasing what is owed, the record showing who left, and a contest still possible. He: "Agreed. Only an agreement can keep you into something, and agreements have their own way out. But default should always offer a way out"

**The MOR lead's line (to check).** "(c). By default, a signer can leave Terms in force. Only Terms that say so can keep someone in, and those Terms have their own way out."

The line says *less* than the lean he agreed to. It leaves out three things the lean included: "leaving never erasing what is owed, the record showing who left, and a contest still possible".

Touched in draft 25, and what happens to each:
- **Section 7, "No default way out"**: "The way out is designed in advance or not at all … this draft chooses that over any default that would let someone be bound without their signature." **Goes**: this stated cost is the opposite of the decision. (Section 7 is outside the table, which covers sections 2 to 5.)
- **Section 5.2**, row "A party wants to leave, and the Terms name no way": "an update condition that answers valid for an update that party alone signed, only where it takes its own signer out (G4.11, K4)". **Changes**: by default the Terms no longer have to name a way. Building one in the update condition is still possible; whether it is still needed is open.
- **G4.3**: "An assent binds its signer only while the Terms it names are in force." **Changes**: a signer who has left is no longer bound, even though the Terms are still in force. That much follows from the decision; how leaving is done (the rows below) is open.
- **G5.1** ("A person is bound by an act if, and only if …"). **Open**: either G5.1 itself changes, or the way out is written elsewhere and G5.1 stays. Two readings; neither chosen.
- **G4.10**: "One made for Terms that are in force changes nothing: Terms in force change only as G4.5 and G4.9 say." **Stays for now, with an open question**: is the default way out a counter signature that keeps working after the Terms are in force, or some other act? The ledger does not say.
- **G4.9**: "No act ends Terms, or an agreement, without replacing them". **Stays**: someone leaving does not end the agreement. Whether leaving is itself a new version (an update) or an act of another kind is open.
- **G4.11(e)**, the default update condition ("an update is valid when everyone who signed the original signs it"). **Two readings**: the default way out could be written into this default (so it changes), or stand beside it as its own default (so it stays). Neither chosen.
- **G5.5** ("By default the delegator can end it at any time"). **Stays**. Draft 25 already lists it as the one exception in "No default way out". Decision 3 opens one question: is the agent a signer who, by default, can leave too?
- **G1.3** and **G1.4** (the receiver). **Stay**. The lean named the case "a receiver cannot resign". Can a receiver who leaves stop being the receiver, and who then receives? Open.
- **Section 2, "Never ended"**: "an agreement never ends." **Stays**: someone leaving is not the agreement ending.
- **P9** (section 6): "a signer can hold back their own assent by forking … whether the signer of such an assent is bound meanwhile is not said". **Open**: Fable's review, section 3, warns that without an exit "the unsaid one will be tried". The decision gives an exit; it does not say what P9 now answers.

**What leaving must not erase**, as far as draft 25 and the ledger tell:
- *What is owed.* The lean says "leaving never erasing what is owed", and he agreed to the lean. Draft 25 records what is owed in a doer's leg report ("**what is still owed**, each amount with to whom", K6's row). Nothing in draft 25 says what happens to a debt when someone leaves. Open.
- *What others relied on.* Draft 25's rules on what stays include: G12.5 ("What others relied on stands", for a set-aside tip); G5.7 ("Whoever agreed to what the agent signed keeps what that version gave them"); G10.6 (a filling keeps "exactly the rights offered by the version it was earned under"); G13.1 ("A version that replaces one an act names does not change what these grammar rules read that act as having done"); G14.2 ("Terms made with the holder of the moment keep counting after a later transfer"). Fable's review, section 3, asks whether leaving would work "with what others relied on standing as G12.5 lets it stand elsewhere". Neither the ledger nor his words say which of these carries over to leaving. Open.
- *The record of who left.* The lean says "the record showing who left". Draft 25 never deletes an act ("Published or signed again, never updated", section 2, "Act"; G4.9: "Every version replaced stays an act of the agreement, readable as it was"). So leaving would leave a signed act on the record, but draft 25 has no act for leaving. Open.
- *A contest.* The lean says "a contest still possible". G11.8 lets a contest be made "against an offer or a vow", and it "reaches the signers of that offer or vow". Can someone who has left still be reached by a contest, or make one? Open.

**How Terms "say so", to keep someone in.** His words: "Only an agreement can keep you into something, and agreements have their own way out." The line: "Only Terms that say so can keep someone in, and those Terms have their own way out." Draft 25 gives Terms two places where they could say so: the update condition (`condition`, K4) and the words (`body`, K1). The ledger names neither. Open:
- In which field Terms say so: in the update condition, in a field of its own, or in the words.
- What "said plainly before anyone signs" (the lean's (c)) asks of the bytes, or of a client.
- "Agreements have their own way out" can be read two ways: (i) Terms that keep someone in must themselves give a way out (the line reads it so: "those Terms have their own way out"); (ii) every agreement already has a way out, its updates, so nothing more is asked. Neither chosen.
- "Default should always offer a way out" can be read two ways: (i) as (c): the default offers it, and Terms can switch it off; (ii) "always" as a block that is always there, closer to (b). He said "Agreed" to the lean, which was (c), and that points to (i); reading (ii) is listed only because "always" is his own word, added after "Agreed". Neither chosen.

## Decision 4. Unclear means unchecked

**The exchange.** The MOR lead set out Carla's case: her assent's withdrawal has no visible order (A6), or her assent is private and held unread (A9), and Ana and Ben sign an update taking her share. (a) count only what is sure, so the update binds Carla without her signature, as verifier 2 does today; (b) unclear means unchecked, so the update is not shown as replacing the old Terms until Carla's position is clear. The MOR lead leaned (b) … He: "Agreed"

**The MOR lead's line (to check).** "(b). Where it is unclear whether someone's assent counts, every answer that depends on it is unchecked; an update is not shown as replacing the old Terms until that person's position is clear."

Touched in draft 25, and what happens to each:
- **G4.8(a)**, "Everyone who signed the original": "each party in whose name an assent to the original counts …". **Changes**: where it is unclear whether a party's assent to the original counts, whether everyone who signed the original has signed an update is unchecked. It is no longer decided by leaving that party out.
- **G4.10**: "Otherwise whether those Terms are in force is **not shown**, and the verifier says why." This is case A6: a withdrawal with no visible order. **Changes**: that answer becomes **unchecked**.
- **G6.1**: "So it is where `previous` names an act the verifier holds and cannot open (a private act, held without its key): what that act is cannot be read, so the act counts, the signer's line is **unchecked** before it (G9), and the act is not invalid." This is case A9. **Changes**: when the act that cannot be opened might be the assent in question, the answers that depend on it are unchecked.
- **G9.1** (the list of what makes an answer unchecked). **Changes**: the rule needs a home. Two readings: (i) a new cause in G9.1; (ii) written where each case arises (G4.8(a), G4.10, G6.1). Neither chosen.
- **G4.8(e)** ("One update that cannot be checked") and **G9.2**. **Stay**: both already answer "unchecked" when a verifier cannot tell.

What it opens:
- **How far "unclear" reaches.** Two readings: (i) only where an assent is held, but whether it counts is unclear (A6, A9); (ii) also where an assent might exist and is not held at all. Under (ii), G4.8(a)'s "Where it holds no such assent, no one who fills a role is shown to have signed the original, and none is asked" would be reached too, and so would G4.11(e)'s **not shown** for a lacking assent. The decision speaks of Carla's two cases and of "unclear". Neither reading is chosen.
- **What "until that person's position is clear" needs.** Which act makes it clear: a place's position, a later act of Carla's, a key. Draft 25 does not say.
- **Who bears the cost.** The MOR lead's lean put "the cost on those taking her share". If that is to be a stated cost in section 7, it is not written there yet.

He: "Agreed"

- **"Beyond the default way out"**: this ties decision 5 to decision 3. Is leaving the way out of a MOR 1 agreement that is never moved? The lead's words suggest so; his words do not say.

## Decision 6. Two threads, two owners

**The exchange.** The MOR lead set out the two threads every act sits on today, the signer's line (every act a person signs, across their life) and the agreement's thread (each version naming the one it replaces), and leaned: keep both … the signer's line belongs to the layer below, where acts and keys live, and Agreements only reads it; the agreement's thread belongs to Agreements; and Agreements asks one thing of the layer below, that a key change continues the signer's line and never starts a fresh one. He: "It does"

**The MOR lead's line (to check).** "both threads stay. The signer's line is the lower layer's (Identity and the act format); the agreement's thread is Agreements'. A key change continues the signer's line; it never starts a fresh one."

Touched in draft 25, and what happens to each:
- **G4.8** and section 3.1's `replaces` (each version names the one it replaces; section 2, "Chain"). **Stay**: this is the agreement's thread, and it is Agreements'.
- **G6.1** and **G6.2**, the line by `previous`: "Every act within an agreement MUST name in `previous` its signer's previous act within the same agreement". **Two readings**, neither chosen:
  - (i) **Stays**. The lead's "signer's line" is "every act a person signs, across their life", which is the core's line (Fable's review, section 5, item 3: "E1: the act is the next of **all** its signer's everyday acts"). G6's line runs within one agreement, so it is not the life-long line the decision gives to the layer below; it would stay in Agreements, beside the agreement's thread.
  - (ii) **Moves** to the layer below. G6's line is one signer's acts (draft 25 calls it "Own line" and "its signer's own line"), so it would be the signer's line. Agreements would then only read it, and G6.2's fork answer would rest on what the layer below gives.
- Every rule that speaks of "its signer's own line" follows G6's fate, under the same two readings: **G1.2** ("stands there in its signer's own line (G6.1)"); **G4.10** ("it follows that assent in the signer's own line (G6.1)"); **G12.1** to **G12.4** and **G12.6** for a fork "in one signer's line"; the Statement kind `resolve` (section 3.2: "a resolving update in the signer's own line"); **G12.8** ("not as that signer's latest act"). In the table these rows say **stays**: each clause stays in Agreements under either reading, and only its part about a signer's line follows G6's fate; the note on each points back here.
