# The encoding, first edition: report

14 November 2026. Written by Claude, for the MOR lead, and for the Dubsar Suite lead's briefing of the players. A record of work, not a decision.

**The wish** (Nobody, allegedly, `docs/decisions.md`, branch `claude/lead-records-2`): "I've decided for a total rewrite of agreements from scratch." "No translation, fresh from the research we had all agreed was excellent direction." On what must hold: "can I with a single click reward all the people that contributed to the service or good via MOR". And: "Building blocks over solutions."

**What this step answers** (the ledger's "The encoding first", his words): "Maybe that should be properly defined first? We have the wording but not the actual code?", and "I think it’s best to do this properly first."

## In plain words

The new Agreements (draft 24) had words and no bytes. Now it has bytes. A test identity can write a Terms act, a Statement and a report, sign each with the signing path every client already uses, and anyone can run a checker over the bytes and be told, in plain words, whether the act is well formed and properly signed, and if not, which rule it breaks.

Nothing of the old Agreements was read or reused. The new acts sit beside the old ones in the core library; the old module and its answers did not change.

What this step does not do: say whether Terms are in force, whether two acts are a fork, who receives, or anything else the grammar decides from many acts together. That is the next, larger step, the grammar verifier. The checker only says what one act's bytes (or a few acts' `previous`) show by themselves.

## What was written

- **The spec text**, `docs/agreements-from-scratch/encoding-1.md`: the bytes of Terms (type 0) and Statement (type 1) under draft 24's own spec hash, and of the report (type 0) under the report block's own proposal's spec hash, as a proposal. Each payload in CDDL, in the MICS's style: integer keys, required and optional fields, list order, every kind of value (fingerprint, identifier, socket, party, role, power, call), every canonical form. Draft 24's section 10 is answered item by item, by name (its section 10). Every choice the bytes forced and draft 24 did not make is marked **[lead]** and listed (its section 11).
- **The implementation**, `core/src/agreements_new/` (Rust), beside `core/src/agreements/` and not touching it: encode and decode strictly both ways (deterministic CBOR, closed maps, sorted sets, canonical text); the checks the bytes alone allow (the act format's own, the signature through the existing everyday act, every payload rule, and every case of invalid draft 24 says an act shows: G2.1, G3.1, G4.2, G10.1, and G6.1 among the acts held); and builders that give each payload ready to seal and sign.
- **The WebAssembly binding** (`wasm/src/agreements_new_js.rs`): `agreementsNewSpecs`, `agreementsNewTermsPayload`, `agreementsNewStatementPayload`, `agreementsNewReportPayload`, `agreementsNewCheck`, `agreementsNewCheckHeld`. A test identity writes an act with one of the builders and the existing `makeEveryday`. This was run end to end in Node: Terms, a private assent, a report and a contest written and signed by a test identity, each checked; the contest, whose `previous` named the report, was answered invalid by G6.1, as it should be. Nothing in `clients/`, `cmips/` or `modules/` was touched.
- **The checker**, `tools/agreements-check` (a new workspace crate): `cargo run -p agreements-check -- --hex FILE`. It reads acts' bytes and says PASS, REJECTED, INVALID, UNKNOWN, CANNOT OPEN or NOT THIS ENCODING, the rule, and the reason in plain words; `--json` for machines; several files are read as acts held together.
- **The vectors**, `core/vectors/agreements-new/` (`valid.json`, `invalid.json`, `README.md`), generated by `gen_vectors` from the code (`core/examples/support/agreements_new.rs`), never by hand: 34 valid cases (every kind of act and every field combination section 3 allows, some with notes) and IN34 invalid cases (one for every check, each with the expected answer and the rule it tests). Each case states its expected answer in the generator, and is written only once the library gives it.
- **The independent check**: `core/vectors/agreements_new_check.py`, run by `core/vectors/check.py`, judges every case again from encoding-1 (its own reading of every payload rule; only the act format's CBOR, lock, text and signature code is shared with `check.py`, which was written from the MIRs), and recomputes both spec hashes from their files. It agrees on every case.
- **Tests and CI**: unit tests in each new file; `core/tests/agreements_new_vectors.rs` (every answer is the library's, every rule has a case, every passing act decodes and encodes back to the same bytes); the wasm builders tested natively. CI's existing steps run all of it: the workspace tests, the vectors regenerated byte for byte, the Python check, the WebAssembly build. Before the push, `cargo test --workspace --locked` passed in full, `python3 core/vectors/check.py` agreed on every vector, and `mor-wasm` built for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`.

## The choices marked as the MOR lead's

Each is the simplest that fits the act format in force; each is the MOR lead's to take or change (encoding-1, section 11).

1. **Draft 24's spec hash** is computed as the core computes a MIR's (E2). *The brief said "the SHA-256 of the file's bytes, as the core already computes a MIR hash". The two differ: the core's is a tagged SHA-256 over the file's text in a small CBOR map, not the plain SHA-256 of the bytes. The core's was taken, since acts name specifications that way; both are given above.*
2. **The report** is written under the spec hash of its own block's proposal, not draft 24's (E2): draft 24 says the report is not its act type.
3. **No `objects` and no `acks`** on the new acts (E3): their lines (`replaces`, `previous`) and namings are in the payload, where draft 24 reads them.
4. **Closed maps** (E4): a field the encoding does not give is rejected, as in every format in force.
5. **The body's words** are named by their work hash (E5).
6. **A place and a clock** are named by a 32-byte identifier (E6).
7. **A socket** is `[cmip, params]`, its parameters always written (`null` for none); an empty socket is the field left out (E7).
8. **Draft 24's words** (role kinds, Statement kinds, power kinds, a call's "from whom") are small numbers (E10, E14, E15, E16).
9. **A role** is named by its position in `parties`, and carries no label (E10, E12, E13).
10. **A power of kind `replaces`** can be written (4), and is left unread (E14).
11. **A K7 kind** is a socket, with parameters (E15).
12. **One call per task** in a Terms act (E16).
13. **Sets are sorted**, each entry once, and an empty list is never written (E18).
14. **Terms with `resolves` and no `continues`** need parties, authors and body (E19).
15. **A contest's content, and a K7 kind's**, is any value (E20).
16. **A report** names one call, by `[act]` or `[act, task]`, and names each act it relied on once (E21).
17. **"Still owed"** is named by `[report, line]`, and a passed-on line may name it in `closes` (E22).

## What draft 24 could not settle for the bytes: questions, each with a lean

These are places where draft 24 is silent on a rule, not on its bytes. Nothing was invented: the bytes allow each case, or refuse it where noted, and the question is open.

1. **An unknown field: rejected, or read past?** G2.2 says a field section 3 does not give is "not read, and do not make the Terms invalid; a verifier says that it left them unread". Section 10, item 2, leaves the bytes to allow it or to say they do not. These bytes say they do not (E4), as every format in force does: such a field is rejected before the grammar. *Lean:* keep it closed; a later format is a new specification, so strictness costs nothing. G2.2's sentence about such a field then has no bytes to apply to, and could say so.
2. **A resolving update that carries words.** Draft 24 says a resolving update "carries no words of its own" (G12.3), and section 3.1 says `parties`, `authors` and `body` are required "except in a resolving update". It does not say what a resolving update that does carry a body or parties is. The bytes allow it. *Lean:* such fields are left unread in a resolving update; or, if Nobody, allegedly, prefers the stricter reading, rejected.
3. **`continues` that names no entry of `resolves`.** Section 3 gives `continues` as "one of the fingerprints in `resolves`", and says nothing of one that is not. These bytes reject it, reading it as a field holding another kind than its own (E19, E20). *Lean:* rejected, as written; the alternative is to read it as resolving nothing, as for `resolves` without `continues`.
4. **Two powers of the same kind.** The bytes allow two entries of `powers` of one kind with different rules (they are two sockets). G5.1(b) reads "those Terms give a power for acts of this kind; and that power's rule ... says yes", in the singular. *Lean:* an act of that kind binds where any one of those rules says yes; the other reading, one power per kind, would be a rule, and is not written.
5. **A receiver that names a party named as an agreement.** The receiver names a party by its position (E12). Draft 24 gives the receiver as a signer or a role among the parties, or another agreement's root. A position that points at an agreement's entry is read here as naming no signer or role (G1.4: no one receives, not shown). *Lean:* as written; to name an agreement as receiver, its root is written. The other reading is to take that agreement as the receiver.
6. **A K7 kind's parameters.** A power's K7 kind is a spec hash (section 3.1: "a K7 fingerprint"); a Statement's K7 kind is a socket, with parameters (E15). Is a Statement of kind (cMIP, params A) of the same kind, for a power, as one of (cMIP, params B)? *Lean:* yes: the kind is the cMIP; its parameters are what that cMIP reads.
7. **`relies_on` naming what another field names.** Section 3.2 says a Statement's `relies_on` holds what it "names in no other field". The bytes allow an entry that another field also names. *Lean:* left as it is, read once; not rejected.
8. **`previous` naming a held act that cannot be opened.** G6.1 makes an act invalid where its `previous` names, among the acts held, anything that is not the same signer's act within the same agreement. Where the act named is held but private and its key is not, nothing can be said of it. The checker answers that the line before is unchecked (G6.1, G9), a note, not invalid. *Lean:* as written; draft 24 could say so in G6.1.
9. **`previous` naming a held act that is rejected.** A rejected act "never reaches these grammar rules". The checker reads it, for G6.1, as a record that is no Terms act and no Statement, so the act naming it is invalid. *Lean:* as written.
10. **The place and the clock.** A 32-byte identifier (E6) may be too little for what the ordering layer will give (a chain and a position, a set of witnesses, P4). *Lean:* keep `hash` until that layer exists; a richer form is then a new specification.
11. **Which act of the envelope's chain the bake is.** Terms that cite the bake write the bake's act id in both `envelope` and `envelope_at`. *Lean:* as written; whether the layer below gives the bake another id in its chain is that layer's.
12. **The spec hash of a draft that will change.** Draft 24's spec hash names draft 24's file as it stands, with its status line; every later draft is a new specification, and acts under draft 24's hash stay draft 24's. *Lean:* as written: nothing in MOR is updated, and the players' round runs on these bytes.
13. **The report's place.** The report is written under `report-block-3.md`'s hash, as a proposal (E2). When the block is written into the shared language, its reports take that text's hash and type. *Lean:* as written; the alternative, draft 24's hash, would make the report an Agreements type, which draft 24 says it is not.
14. **A resolving update, by its bytes.** E19 excuses `parties`, `authors` and `body` in an act written with `resolves` and `continues`; whether it in fact resolves is the grammar's (G12.3). Draft 24 does not say whether a Terms act written so must also name `replaces` (G12.3: for Terms, its `replaces` names the shared act), nor whether such an act that in fact resolves nothing then lacks required fields. *Lean:* a Terms act written as a resolving update must name `replaces` and `agreement`; one that resolves nothing is read as G12.3 says, and not rejected after the fact.
15. **A resolving Statement's kind.** A Statement that resolves a fork in a signer's line (G12.3, G12.4) must carry a `kind` (assent, counter signature, contest or a K7 kind), and draft 24 does not say which, nor how a power of kind `resolves` matches the Statement made under it. *Lean:* the kind is that of the act it stands for in the line, and a power of kind `resolves` matches any act that carries `resolves`.
16. **The payment or its settlement.** G11.8 says a buyer's contest "names the payment in `settlement`", while section 3.2 says `settlement` names a settlement, and G11.8's own check reads "a payment ... that names the same settlement". Where Money keeps the payment and its settlement as two acts, which does field 9 hold? And a confirmation may name, as its call, the payment or its settlement (G11.7), so two confirmations of one payment can differ in their bytes. *Lean:* `settlement` always holds the settlement; a confirmation names the payment where its signer holds it.
17. **A socket written as text.** Draft 24 makes "a cMIP in a socket that is not named by fingerprint" invalid (G2.1, G2.2). These bytes make a socket that is not `[cmip, params]` rejected (E7), so a socket written as a bare address is rejected, not invalid; only `[address, params]` reaches G2.1. Likewise a power's K7 kind that is not a fingerprint is rejected (E14), though G2.1 says every cMIP named anywhere in Terms must be named by fingerprint. *Lean:* keep the shape rule, and read G2.1 as reaching only what is in a socket's cMIP place; or, if the stricter reading of G2.1 is wanted, read any non-array socket and any non-number power kind as a cMIP not named by fingerprint (invalid).
18. **Two identical roles.** Two parties with the same bytes are rejected (E18), so two seats of one kind, under one cMIP, with the same `what` and parameters, cannot be written twice. *Lean:* as written; two seats are told apart in their spot's parameters, or one role is filled more than once (G10.2).

## What a stranger's reading found

A subagent was given only `draft-24.md` and `encoding-1.md`, asked to write the bytes of a few acts and a checker, and to say where it could not tell what to write. Its points, and what was done:

- **Fixed in the text:** the spec hash's formula (the kind 4 explained; "its final line break removed if it has one"); positions counting from 0 in `authors` and a line's `closes`; which rule the checker names when several cases of invalid are found (the first, in section 4's order) and that a rejection names the first reason met; E22's "five kinds, six forms"; that an act named twice in a report is not refused; that the checker cannot tell `[act]` naming Terms from naming a payment; that the work hash of words that are not text is over their bytes as they are; `relies_on`'s lack is never refused; the depths a verifier stops at are the grammar verifier's; "who said it" in the checker is the signer; Money's "same payment"; no answer about `envelope_at`; that the resolving-update exemption is a rule of the bytes only; what "the same party twice" means.
- **Kept as questions** (above): a resolving update's `replaces` and a resolving Statement's kind (14, 15); the payment or its settlement (16); a socket written as text, and a power's K7 kind (17); two identical roles (18); a receiver that names an agreement's entry (5); a K7 kind's parameters (6).
- **Not changed:** a first Terms act whose `previous` names an act not held gets a note, not an answer; that is G6.1 as written.
