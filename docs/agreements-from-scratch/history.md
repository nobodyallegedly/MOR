### 9.4 Readings of his words that he should confirm

These are Claude's readings, written into draft 2 (and, for item 2, rewritten in draft 3; items 5 to 7 are new in draft 4, items 8 to 13 in draft 5, items 14 to 21 in draft 6, items 22 to 35 in draft 7, items 36 to 49 in draft 8, and items 50 to 57 in draft 9) so that it can be read whole. Each could be wrong.

1. **S8, "To be used only for".** Read as: the future kind is for participants the four kinds of S7 cannot describe in advance (rule 10.4), with the gloss from the brief. Since no verifier can check that, rule 10.4 lays it on the authors.
2. **S12 to S14, what is counted.** Draft 2's reading of S9 (the Terms name who may reject, in `may_reject`) is withdrawn: his words say a rejection was never meant "to be something". Draft 3 reads them so:
   - (a) "never setup to be counted" is read as: the rule the Terms name decides which counts are counted, and nothing outside it is (rule 11.3). No act keeps a count out.
   - (b) "Show me referees that lead to settlement only" is read as a question a rule must be able to ask. So the Statement field `settlement` is kept, read by rule 11 and by K4 rules, rather than removed and left wholly to cMIPs. If he meant the tie to settlement to live only inside cMIPs, the field goes too.
   - (c) "referees" is read as "referrers". He said "Referees might have been a typo... if referrer is used does it makes more sense?", and, asked whether to record it so: "Yes pls." His words are quoted as he wrote them; only the reading changes. A referrer is a spot of kind done (rule 10), not a count of being seen. So his example says of referrers what rule 11 says of counts: of the referrers who fill a spot, only those the agreement's rule sets up to reward are rewarded, and a rule may reward only the referrers whose referral led to a settlement. Draft 3 already allows this without a new rule: `settlement` may be named by any Statement (section 3.2), and a K4 rule may read it. Rule 10 is not changed.
   - (d) The witness of S9 ("seen by the agreed witness") is kept (rules 11.1, 11.2), since S12 to S14 do not withdraw it.
3. **S5, the receiver.** His words say the money goes to "whoever that agreement names as its receiver now". Draft 2 reads that as a field `receiver` in Terms, read by the grammar, rather than a part of the body read by a cMIP. Whether a receiver may itself be an agreement is left open (P8a). **Draft 7:** the receiver stays, for an agreement that points to no service agreement (rules 1.3, 1.4; item 24). **Answered in draft 8** by S28: the receiver may be an agreement (item 36).
4. **S11, reputation.** Read as a task (K13) with no trust and no keeper, rather than a grammar rule: the grammar keeps what reputation reads (signed acts, proven forks, acknowledgments) checkable by anyone, and leaves the measure to cMIPs (S4).
5. **S15, replacing Terms.** His "Yes" answers the MOR lead's question as it was put. Draft 4 reads it so:
   - (a) "latest per the agreement's place" is read through rule 7.2 as it stands: a replacing Terms act counts once the place named by the Terms in force shows it. So an agreement that changes its place does so by Terms that must first be lodged at the old place.
   - (b) "never chosen" is read as holding per place too: a place's order does not pick the earlier of two replacements of the same Terms (rule 4.7, "by position or in any other way").
   - (c) The answer **fork**, which draft 3 gave only to one signer's two acts (rule 6), is also given to an agreement whose Terms branch, since his words call it "a fork that is shown and never chosen". The two forks are told apart by what they are about: a signer, or Terms.
   - (d) The rule is read as about replacing only. Ending Terms without replacing them, withdrawing an assent and withdrawing an open offer are not read into it (P10). **Answered in draft 6**, except withdrawing an assent: ending Terms is a version replacing them (rule 4.9, S21); withdrawing an open offer is closing its spot (rule 10.5, S22).
6. **S16, spots per proof.** A filling is read as a signer together with its proof, the acts the K12 cMIP rests on. A cMIP that says a spot holds one filling is read as also saying which, when two proofs come; whether a later one displaces an earlier one stays open (P7), because his words do not say.
7. **S17, holding.** Read as covering the whole grammar text, so the relay leaves rule 10's plain words, whom rule 10 protects, and section 8; it stays in S7's quoted words (his own) and in the companion. K12's confirmer for holding, which draft 3 gave as "the place or the holder", is read as part of what proves holding, so it becomes "as its cMIP says". The definition of **seen** (which includes "delivery to an audience") is not about holding and is unchanged.
8. **S18, who signs a join.** The MOR lead's words, which his "I think it does" answers, are "the join is signed by whoever could sign the last act both branches share". Draft 5 reads them as: whoever could sign, in that line, the next act after the shared act (rule 12.3). For one signer's line that is the signer; for Terms it is those whose signatures the shared Terms require for their replacement (rule 4.8). If he meant those who signed the shared act itself (for Terms, those who assented to it), the two readings differ wherever the Terms give a power to replace them to fewer than all.
9. **S18, the shape of a join.** The fields `joins` (the first act of every branch) and `continues` (the branch that goes on; absent, the join is the merge), and a join that names `continues` carrying no `parties`, `authors` or `body`, are Claude's way of writing "names every branch and says which continues, or merges them" as data. His words do not give a form.
10. **S19, where the resolver is named.** Read as: in Terms, by the field `resolver`, and the one that applies is named by the Terms in force where the fork stands (for a fork of Terms, the Terms both branches replace). Read as applying to every fork within the agreement, a signer's as well as its Terms', since S18 asks for one shape for all acts.
11. **S19, the other two ways out.** "Resolver makes sense" is read as answering the first of the MOR lead's three ways out. The second ("each party carries on from the last Terms all shared") is not made a rule of its own: for Terms, S20 says the same (rule 4.7); for one signer's line it has no meaning. The third ("what happened stays visible") is rule 12.2 and 12.5.
12. **S20, "invalid".** Read as: a Terms act without the signatures the Terms it replaces require replaces nothing and is not a branch (rule 4.8). No new answer "invalid" is added; such an act is simply not in force as a replacement. **Draft 7** keeps this reading beside the new answer **void** (item 31). **Answered in draft 8** by L: **invalid** is an answer, and is the one for such an act (rule 4.8; item 47).
13. **S18, "what others relied on".** Read narrowly, as an act by someone who signed none of the dropped branch's acts that names an act of it, or is made within or under its Terms (rule 12.5); "stands" is read as keeping the effect these grammar rules gave it, with **disputed** added to every answer resting on it.
14. **S21, one train of thought.** "That agreement can be ended in multiple ways" and "An agreement never ends, it can resolve" are read together, the later refining the earlier: the "multiple ways" are what a resolving version's conditions may say happens to the work and its shares, not ways the grammar lists. "Not sure still pondering" is kept with the words: rule 4.9 is the least they need, and the most this text reads into them.
15. **S21, who signs.** "signed by all signers of previous version" is read through his later answer, "Terms rule, because they were agreed by all signers": a resolving version carries the signatures the previous version requires for its replacement (rule 4.8), which are everyone's unless that version, agreed by all, gave a power to fewer.
16. **S21, resolving is replacing.** No field or act kind marks a version as resolving: any replacing version with new conditions resolves the one before, an amendment as much as what was once called an ending. "« we agree to resolve the previous agreement »" is read as what such a version's signers do, not as a word the grammar makes them write. If he meant a resolving version to be told apart from other versions by the grammar, a field would be needed.
17. **S21, his example.** "the previous agreement says « sorry can’t do this alone »" is read as the last version (the one that made the work public and free) still being in force, so that a lone sale binds no one else (rule 5) and a lone new version replaces nothing (rule 4.8).
