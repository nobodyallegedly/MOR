## In plain words

**A deal's fork, once settled, stays settled (F192, QG4, F196).** When the parties settle a fork, the settlement is final for everything they could see. With no clock, "could see" means exactly what the settlement's own history holds: its line, the tips it names, and what it cites. Anything else is a late version: a version on the dropped branch published later, a version kept in a drawer, a version one party grows alone, a branch nobody named. None of these reopens anything. Only a new version on the line in force changes the deal. A second settlement, by the judge or anyone, that comes after the first changes nothing. The judge of forks now follows the deal's chain of judgment: if it stays silent past its period, the next judge takes over. A settling version that names a version nobody holds is read as an ordinary version, so the deal stays readable.

**Who confirms that a helper helped (F193, F194, QG3).** A relay or a service that wants its share of a sale now needs the buyer to confirm it, and the buyer is the one the payment itself committed to, never the one the split service writes on the receipt. A service's own record of its use pays nothing by itself. Helpers the owners pick in advance get a named share instead. Every verifier now gives the same answer, told or not which specification defines the record.

**An old resignation cannot come back to bite (F195).** A resignation or stepping down that was never registered is used up once the member signs a later version naming them again.

**Money owed back after a broken stretch (QG1, QG2, F197).** It is repaid exactly like any debt: to the refund address the payer gave, or to the pointer their own acts point to, or to their current pointer. A refund to an anonymous payer's one-time key is shown repaid by the collective's own claim with the rail's proof. Money owed to nobody at all (no key given) no longer stops the collective from closing: the closing act names it. For a payer who gave no address, the collective first sends a notice, sealed to them, with a visible deadline; once it passes, the collective may close, the debt named in the closing act. A holder that outlives the closing may be named; the core shows it and requires nothing.

**Forks of a collective (F198, F199).** A payment already made to a pointer that a fork leaves out still counts. Whether a fork took effect is judged by its own history only: a departure registered after it never changes that. The two old failing seeds now pass at 5,000 cases.

**The WebAssembly changed.** The display client must be released again.

## Questions for Nobody, allegedly

Writing these in exposed six questions. None is decided here; what the build does meanwhile is said. They are in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft". One at a time, as you prefer.

1. **QH1. Two settlements of one fork, neither holding the other, one of them the judge's.** A verifier tells a second settlement from the first only by what it holds. When neither holds the other (the judge signs twice, and the parties' version in between did not cite the first; or a judge and the parties settle at the same time), nothing shows which came first. Between two settlements of the parties, DQ3 already answers (both signed by everyone: tangled until one settlement holds both). *As built:* where a judge's settlement is one of the two, the verifier refuses to say which version is in force. Which should hold?
2. **QH2. A branch nobody named, carrying a newcomer (F192 beside DQ5).** Ana and Ben sign a branch adding Carla, then settle for another branch naming only a third. F192 drops what the settlement did not hold, so Carla's branch is gone without her signature; DQ5 protected the parties of the tips a settlement names, not of a branch it leaves out. Her payment stays protected; her place does not. *As built:* F192 as decided. Is this F192's stated cost, or should a settlement whose signers signed a version it does not hold settle nothing?
3. **QH3. The object a relay's record names, and a purchase naming an offer.** QG3 makes every verifier count an acknowledged record without being told its format; F184 also checks that the record's object is what was paid for, which only a verifier reading the format can check. And on a purchase naming the standing offer, the object cannot be read until the offer has a format (Fable's 4b, its test unchanged). *As built:* the object checked only by a verifier told the format. Is the buyer's acknowledgement enough by itself?
4. **QH4. A resignation spent by the very version that registers it (F195 beside F187, 6).** A member who signed a draft and then resigned: when a line puts that draft in force and registers the resignation, the draft is "a later version they signed naming them". *As built:* the version the registering line itself puts in force never brings its signer back. Confirm?
5. **QH5. A one-time key that never gave a refund address (QG1 beside F197).** F197 says QG1 applies to such a payer. *As built:* owed to a key, it still blocks the closing until repaid. Should it instead be left open like money owed to nobody?
6. **QH6. A pointer inside a fork's history whose taking-on the fork left out (F198).** Found by the 5,000-case run once F198 was built. *As built:* F198 read as "judge the pointer as the chain stood without the ending", which covers this too. Confirm?

## Readings taken, to confirm

Also in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft":
- **F192, "holding"**: a settlement holds another where its history does (its line, the tips it names, what it cites, transitively); a version on the kept line holds a judge's settlement only if it cites it. *Suggested, not decided:* a client signing a version of a deal cites the latest settlement of its fork it holds (it would make QH1 rare).
- **F192, the line after a judge's settlement**: the line of the version kept, with what grows on it.
- **QG4, the chain**: the judge of forks, then those field 21 names for it; a link may settle once every link before it let its period pass on that request; whether a period passed is the time reference's answer, which the verifier states; a specification link signs nothing and is stated passed over.
- **F193**: the payer the commitment names is shown by a claim whose rail proof the verifier found valid; on a rail binding no payer, nobody; a referral is counted the same way, named in that claim's field 6.
- **QG2**: a refund rail in the payer's claim is reached by the payment cMIP's `paid-to` naming that claim; "the payer's own acts on the payment" are their claims for it and their acts acknowledging it.
- **QG1**: the collective's claim to a bare key is judged by the rail's proof, its payee field unread.
- **F197**: "deadline passed with no address" is the time reference's answer, stated by the verifier; the notice, an act of the collective, is sealed to every member as well as the payer (rule 35a); the closing names a payment by any receipt or claim of it.
- **F199 for closings**: a closing counts only the departures in its own history when checking its debts, as F143 already says.
- **F195, "named again"**: by a constitutional clone (B10).

## What changed in the code

**Core library** (`core/src/law/`):
- `formats.rs`: the notice (type 24, `Notice`); the closing's field 4 (`OpenOwed`, ascending, none twice).
- `view.rs`:
  - `deal_state` rewritten for F192: what each settlement saw (`fork_histories`), a settlement counting only where it names every tip it saw, one holding another being after it and reopening nothing, the parties' rival settlements tangled (DQ3), a judge's among them refused (QH1); F196, a settlement naming an unheld version read as plain.
  - `arbitrated` follows the chain of judgment (QG4), with a new view input `judges_lapsed`.
  - `payer_acknowledged` replaces `delivery_acknowledged`: any role evidence but a rail Module's counts only acknowledged (or named as referral) by a claim carrying the payment's commitment (F193, F194, QG3).
  - `came_back` and `spent`: a departure spent by any later version its signer signed naming them, registered or not (F195), save the version its own registering line puts in force (QH4).
  - `repaid`, `pointer_holding_at`, `follows_at`: money owed back follows the debt rule (QG2); a bare key repaid where its claim said (QG1).
  - The closing's check: money owed to nobody, or to a payer after a lapsed notice, left open where field 4 names it (QG1, F197), with a new view input `notices_lapsed`.
  - `to_hand_out` and `open_debts`: an ending's debts judged with the departures in its own history only (F199).
  - `rail_not_accepted`: a pointer an ending left out counts for payments already made to it (F198, QH6).

**Bindings** (`wasm/src/lib.rs`): `judgesLapsed` and `noticesLapsed` inputs; `forkSettlementPayload` refuses a repeated tip, as the core does; `noticePayload`; `lawOwedBack` gives `toKind`; `lawClosing` gives `leftOpen`.

**Repo client:** `closingPayload` takes the payments left open; Law type 24; a test time reference.

**Collective client:** shows who money owed back is owed to, with what that means; sends a notice (sealed to the payer and every member) with a deadline; its closing names what it leaves open, and blocks on what it may not.

## Limits, stated

- **This client reads no time reference.** The collective client sends a notice and names it in the closing, but cannot state that the deadline passed: there, the closing does not take effect, and says so. The core decides once a verifier states it.
- **No client signs a judge's settlement or states a chain's periods yet**; the bindings take both.
- **No client pays role shares to relays or services yet**, and the bindings do not yet take the rail's answers (`rail_valid`) that the payer's acknowledgement needs: a client reading a split through the bindings sees every helper's share unevidenced until they do.
