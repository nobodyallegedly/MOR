## What would get it rejected

- **R1. FALSE as marked. Claim 7, first half.** "A thief who changes the flow pointer cannot collect older obligations" is marked Run. No test runs it. The regtest Lightning test covers a receipt and claim, vault limits, refused units and the anonymous refund only (harness/tests/lightning_rail.rs lines 1 to 12). The core library decodes and re-encodes the obligation's pointer field and never checks it (core/src/finance.rs lines 180, 504, 592). Finance carries the protection only as a field comment and italic commentary (spec/MIP-finance-draft-6.md lines 97, 244), with no normative rule. Mark it Reasoned and write the rule.
- **R2. OVERSTATED. Claim 8**, "no fork is undone by anything published after it." Law draft 10 itself names two later events that stop a complete ending counting: a concurrent ending sharing no signer, and an old proposal finished late by members who signed no ending naming it (spec/MIP-law-draft-10.md, Fork section near line 403). The invariant suite counts these cases rather than failing them (ENDING-RACE, ENDING-LATE in docs/law-invariants.md). The claim must carry both exceptions.
- **R3. MISSING. The run/reasoned record does not exist.** Section 7.3 says scenarios are "marked reasoned", notably ballot unlinkability. In the freeze suite every component box is unticked, no scenario carries a run or reasoned mark, and "unlinkable" appears nowhere in the suite or the roadmap. The suite's own freeze rule defers that marking to a future freeze report. The README's sentence saying the same thing is also untrue today.
- **R4. FALSE citation. Claim 2** cites scenario 1 step 5c, which is the flow-pointer step. The stolen-key step is step 5. The claim is in fact run (core/tests/chain.rs lines 432 and 502), but the cited invariant checks only the void half, not the dispute exception.
- **R5. OVERSTATED. Claim 1** (same verdicts in any order) is run only over the Law generators' collective and deal stories (core/tests/law_invariants.rs lines 217 to 276). Identity races, competing rotations, homeless rotations and late objections are never replayed in shuffled orders. The report's own "What this did not test" list also excludes collective rotations, keepers, timed releases and standing offers.
- **R6. OVERSTATED. "328 tests ... all pass."** Today it is 330, and the Lightning test passes without running when no regtest network is present (it passed in 0.00 s here). The end-to-end Lightning result is a manual run on the author's machine (docs/core-pass-v21.md section 5). The 113 client tests are nine clients' tests. The tenth client, site, has 29 browser tests outside the count.
- **R7. MISSING. An unclassified failure is omitted.** docs/law-invariants.md records a 500-case run that failed with its message lost, "not classified", never recurring. Section 7.2 does not mention it. No seeds are recorded, so the large runs cannot be reproduced. Only the shrunk named tests can.
- **R8. OVERSTATED. "The identity chain never branches" (4.2).** Identity draft 11 lets rotations and chain signatures compete for a position, and the gauntlet's step 5.7d shows a chain contested until the operator rotates. It holds only under the chain-key assumption.

## Where readers stop, and what is new

A distributed-systems reader stops at 5.4. "The ending wins" lets any set able to sign an ending void concurrent actions, and the signers choose what counts as concurrent by choosing the line. The paper calls that a stated cost. The reader will call it the absence of a consistency model. Claim 9 promises a deterministic verdict, not safety for the counterparty. A second stop is 4.4's "final" that a later-arriving rotation can overturn.

A cryptography reader stops at 5.2, where agreements are independent signature acts with no fair-exchange treatment, and at the abstract, where "post-quantum offline key" reads as post-quantum identity security while every everyday act, operator key and home receipt is secp256k1.

For the committee: three things are new in combination. A ledgerless pre-rotation scheme whose rotations split an identity's history into kept, void and a third class, disputed, where acts a third party relied on survive a thief's voiding visibly. A two-chain collective model with a deterministic rule for the one race hash order cannot settle, and the trick of ordering governance endings by placing their signatures on each member's non-branching key-event chain. And "legible greed" turned into checkable invariants: splits that balance to the unit, fees as visible payouts, payer claims on equal footing with receipts. The hash-linked acts, relays, happened-before ordering and Ricardian-style terms are not new, and the paper says so. The strongest evidence is the method: invariant hunting that found six flaws in the text and recorded each decision. The weakest point is that oracle and library came from the same process and the same text, which the paper admits.
