# Deals and collectives: one grammar ruling two things

*Fable, 11 November 2026, morning. For the MOR lead. A report of a short conversation with Nobody, allegedly, after `fable-witness-attack.md` (pull request 139) and `fable-small-tasks.md` (pull request 141). The sorting of rule families below is from the table in section 2 of pull request 141, which judged many families from their openings; it is an index, not an inventory. Nothing was checked sentence by sentence, nothing run. What is decided is marked as his; the rest is my reading.*

## In plain words

Nobody, allegedly thinks a source of unneeded complexity in Agreements is that one set of rules tries to govern two different things: an agreement to work or own together where everyone signs everything, and a collective where some may decide for all. I think he is right, and draft 24 half-says so already. It stays one MIR. Inside it, the two handle different parts.

## 1. What Nobody, allegedly said

- "I think we might have found a source of unneeded complexity… attempting to rule collective agreements and work agreements with the same rules. Not needed, collectives benefit from having a constitution and the internal rules book."
- On a deal that grows, which I had listed as a bridge between the two: "3 is not a collective, it's a co-production… it can or cannot turn into one. Legacy prefers to create single film companies, does not mean the cat video needs it".
- On whether this means two texts or two parts: "It's all agreements but they handle different bits."

I read the last as: one MIR, Agreements; deals and collectives as distinct parts of it. He has not said more than that, and the shape of the parts is not decided.

## 2. Why I think it holds

**Draft 24 already separates them, by exception.** It marks rules "Collectives only", and rule 47 says "Majorities, areas and control models belong to collectives". Shared rules then carry a clause for one kind or the other. That is what two things under one grammar look like.

**Sorting the families of pull request 141's table:**

- **Only a collective's:** the collective as an identity and its two chains; an act done once sealed to every member; the line; counting a power; layers; the key grammar; the judicial tier; control models; forks of records; changing members and leaving an area; the four endings; the broken collective. About a dozen of some thirty families, and most of the hard ones.
- **Only a deal's:** almost nothing. Everyone signs everything.
- **Shared:** signing; a new version with a truthful mark; stakes, shares and transfers; publications and the purchase; the split and its guarantees; grants; keepers; time and the judges; termination's shape; succession and the abandonment clause.

**The stranger saw the same divide.** Selling and promising are one kind; a group deciding is another, and "the same" as a person acting for another: a few bind others, within limits. Everything that is "two people signing words to each other" survived both of its suppositions untouched.

## 3. A test for which side a rule sits on

*Does this rule exist because someone can be bound without signing?*

- No: it is a deal's rule, or a shared one. Everyone who is bound signed.
- Yes: it is a collective's.

This follows from his correction. A co-production is a deal with more parties and a shared stake; it needs no constitution. So **owning together belongs to deals**: stakes, shares, transfers and the split all work with no collective. What a collective adds is only *deciding without everyone*: who may act for the group, and what no member can lose when they do.

His two words fit what the texts already have:

- **the constitution:** what no member can lose and no model can decide. Rule 98, the judicial tier, the way out, the key grammar.
- **the internal rule book:** how the group decides the rest. The control model, already a cMIP (rule 99).

## 4. The bridges

I first named three places where the two meet. His correction leaves two.

1. **A collective as a party to a deal.** It signs under its own rules, and the deal must not need to know them.
2. **A publication's owners' side**, which may be a deal or a collective. A buyer must not need to care which.

The third, a deal growing into a collective, is not a bridge. Becoming a collective is a choice, never a stage: parties who want majorities, areas or a shared key found a collective as a new thing, and their deal ends or names it as a party, which is bridge 1.

## 5. Two cautions

- **Guarantees that protect the same person in both kinds are said once, in the shared part.** The holder with no voice; the party who did not sign; the buyer. Splitting the text must not split these into two wordings that can drift.
- **A deal with many parties where one goes silent.** Today the abandonment clause and succession cover it, and both are optional for a deal. I think they stay shared, being a way out and not a way of deciding. This should be checked against the rehearsal's cases before it is relied on.

## 6. What this does to the work in hand

- **The skeleton** of pull requests 138 and 139 (words between people; only once; by when; before a line; a hand) describes the shared part and deals. The line, as section 10 of draft 24 writes it, is mostly a collective's; `fable-witness-attack.md` found the same reading used for a revocation, a deal's declaration and a stake's transfers. So the line's *rule* is shared, and the collective's *use* of it (records, forks, closings) is the collective's.
- **The task list** of pull request 141: task 25, collective control, is the rule book and belongs wholly to the collective's part. Tasks 8 and 17 to 24 are shared. Task 14, absence proof, is shared.
- **The eighteen guarantees** of pull request 141: sentences 13, 16 and 17 are the collective's constitution; the rest are shared.
- **F441's promise is untouched.** This is an arrangement of the text. No verdict moves by it. The vectors already sort this way by their questions ("control answer", "closing", "record", "fork" on one side).
