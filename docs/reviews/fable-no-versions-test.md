## In plain words: what the test finds

1. **What goes.** Of draft 26's 68 numbered rules, 6 go whole, 9 are rewritten from the ground, 27 change in substance, 11 are touched by a word, and 15 stand untouched: 53 of 68 move. Twelve defined words go or are renamed, nineteen change; four fields of a Terms act go and one is added; one task changes its question (K4) and four change their inputs or outputs. The word "update" has 306 uses to remove (COUNTED).
2. **The closing pointer is either a door out of the known parties' scheme, or nothing new.** His 08:58 says the known parties are protected "by their signatures, or the signature scheme they agreed to". The lead's 09:08 reads the pointer as closing the old agreement "for each known party of the old one who signs it, and for no one else". Read together, two of three known parties under a three-of-three ending condition walk out by signing a new agreement that points back, and the ending condition guards nothing against leaving. Read the other way, the pointer closes only where the old agreement's ending condition is met by the pointer's signers, and "for no one else" is wrong. The two readings give opposite answers to the simplest scenario the model has.
3. **Two successors are no fork.** Two new agreements that both close the same old one are two valid parties; nothing orders them, nothing stops them, and nothing calls them rivals. One signer's assents to both are two lines, not a fork of one. The deadlock he and the lead built on 26 November ("we forced the deadlock and push them to resolve it") leaves the core with the fork of Terms.
4. **"Nothing later" loses its only source.** Draft 26 took "nothing later" from the keepers (5.13.5). The model's keeper gives presence and no order; knots never prove absence (5.13.3). So no reader can ever show that an agreement is not ended, that a counter signature does not exist, or that an unknown party's filling is still open: every "not ended" is **as shown** and may flip. The lead's reading 1 ("not there, it does not") makes a keeper's omission a decisive no with no bytes behind it.
5. **A party that is an agreement, and ends, freezes what named it.** A deal that names an agreement as a party can be ended, by default, only with that agreement's signature; an agreement that has ended cannot sign (5.1.2 asks for Terms of it in force). The deal is immortal, and so is every agreement above it in a sequence, unless each ends and re-forms: a change at the bottom re-makes the whole stack. The dual agreement framework is offered as the cure and breaks in its own way: the outer agreement's known parties are its founders, bound for as long as it lives and never its later members; and "the newest inner agreement" is a tip of a chain of agreements, whose forks no grammar rule governs once the fork of Terms is gone.
6. **The unknown party's shield gains one wall and loses two.** She gains the dissolution bar: nobody dissolves while her filling is open. She loses the keeper's record: a dead keeper leaves her filling **unchecked** for every reader for ever, since the model has no signed record to hold a copy of. And a power she gave by a Statement of her own in the old agreement lets an agent sign the new one for her, closing the old one for her, where draft 26 let the same power only change what she earned.
7. **The ending by clock reverses two rules and inherits the seller's clock.** 5.5.7(f) and 5.10.8 say "nothing triggers"; a clock ending triggers. Knots cannot show that an act came before a time, so every act under a power, every filling and every payment made near a clock ending is **not shown** as before or after it unless the clock dates it, and the model has no act that dates another. The clock is the drafter's.
8. **The scenarios.** Of the companion's 39, 17 give the same answer (3 with a keeper variant that now answers worse), 12 a different one, 6 are no longer a case and 4 are a case only in part. Of the 12 different, 9 are worse, 1 is better for the buyer and worse for the seller, 2 are only different. Of the suite's agreement steps, every clone, settlement, late version and termination step is no longer a case; the way out (scenario 9) mostly holds; the fork of a deal (1.7a) and the collective's rollback (3.7w) have no analogue.
9. **The attacks.** Of the fifth attack's ten breaks the model closes 2, half closes 2 and leaves 6 standing; of the sixth's ten it closes 3, half closes 2, leaves 4 standing and sharpens 1. Twelve breaks are new.
10. **What must be added.** An act that meets an ending by signatures; the closing field; four states of an agreement and their answer words; the test of "an open obligation towards an unknown party"; how an act is dated against the clock; the keeper's answer and what "cannot be reached" is; a rule for acts within an agreement after its ending; and a rule for the partial closing and for two successors. Twenty-two questions, in one batch at the end.

The shape under it, as I read it (INFERRED): the model takes out of the core the one place where draft 26 said that two things competing for one state stop each other (the fork of Terms, 5.5.4(e)), and the one place where a reader could learn that nothing came after (the keeper's order, 5.13.5). What it adds, the ending condition and the closing pointer, is each a signature scheme the drafter writes before anyone else arrives. The grammar still reads honestly; it now reads more agreements, each shorter-lived, with less to say about which one stands.

## 4. What must be added

Anything the model needs that draft 26 does not have, each with the reason, and what it needs from another layer. The need and its reason only; no form.

1. **An act that meets an ending by signatures.** Draft 26 has no Statement kind for it: an assent's `terms` "is what it agrees to" (§4.3), and there is no Terms act to agree to for an ending. The ending condition (K4) is asked "of a proposed update" and has nothing to be asked of. The ledger's "by signatures" (his 08:56) needs an act that carries them, and a rule for what it names (the agreement; what becomes of its shares, where the words do not say it in advance). *Reason:* 3.3.1, 3.3.10; without it the default ending is uncomputable.
2. **The closing field.** Its key and form (encoding 3's item 14, open; the lead's 09:08: "a field of its own"); whether it is a knot like any other (5.13.1 says any naming is; the lead's 09:08 says this naming "has an effect no other naming has"); whom it closes for (3.3.1); whether an agent's assent to the act that carries it is a "signing" of it (3.3.6); and whether a first act may carry two (closing two agreements, a merger). *Reason:* 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 3.3.6.
3. **The states of an agreement and their answer words.** Not ended; its ending condition met and not dissolved; dissolved; closed by a pointer for named signers. §3.6's answers (**in force**, **not shown**, **valid**) have no word for the second or the fourth; 5.5.6's "a verifier MUST NOT answer that Terms or an agreement have ended" must be reversed; what each state does to the spot (5.10.5), to an assent (5.4.3), to a payment (5.1.4), to a power (5.6.4) and to a contest (5.11.8) must be said for each. *Reason:* 3.3.3, 3.3.7.
4. **The test of "an open obligation towards an unknown party".** Which cMIP answers it (K1's "still applies", 5.10.8, trusted "format only"), from which acts, and what a verifier answers where it lacks the cMIP or the filling's proof (the lead's "not shown, never a yes", to check). *Reason:* 3.3.3, 3.3.5.
5. **Dating an act against the clock.** Knots show no time (5.13.3, 5.13.5); an ending by clock needs each act near it dated, or every answer about before and after is **not shown**. Draft 26 has no act that anchors another act to the Terms' clock, and §7 still lists "How Terms name their clock" as open. *Reason:* 3.3.9; also the suite's 1.8 and 1.9i.
6. **The keeper's answer.** What a verifier holds when it has asked a keeper (an act? signed by whom? of which layer?); what "cannot be reached" is (the lead's reading 1: **unchecked**); whether "not there" from a reachable keeper is a no (reading 1) or an **unchecked** with evidence (his 09:02); and K9's new question and input. *Reason:* 3.3.4, 3.3.5. From another layer: the identifier of a keeper and the protocol by which a relay says what it holds are Envelopes' (encoding 3's E6, open; the lead's reading 11).
7. **Acts within an agreement after its ending.** Whether a filling can be earned, an assent counted, a contest shown, a payment received, under an agreement whose condition is met, or which is closed for its signers. Draft 26's late update ("An assent to L binds no one", 5.5.4(c)) was the nearest rule and goes. *Reason:* 3.3.7.
8. **The partial closing.** Whether a pointer by fewer than the ending condition asks closes anything, and for whom (3.3.1); and, if it does, what the leavers still owe those who stay. *Reason:* 3.3.1, and every suite step that says "stays a draft until X signs".
9. **Two successors.** Whether two first acts closing the same agreement are rivals, and if so what stops them and what resolves them, since 5.12.4 resolves "a fork of a line only" and two agreements are two lines. The suite's 3.9 had a rule ("neither closes it until one names the other"); draft 26 has none for agreements. *Reason:* 3.3.2.
10. **A signer's line across a closing.** Whether `previous` runs across a closing pointer, from a signer's last act in A to their first in B, or starts afresh (5.7.1 reads "within the same agreement"). If afresh, 3.3.11 stands and no double assent is ever a fork; if across, B's first act names an act of A, and E23 makes an act whose `previous` names "an act of another agreement" invalid. *Reason:* 3.3.2, 3.3.11.
11. **Whose signature on an agreement binds whom, over time.** 5.6.1(b) and 5.1.2 bind the signers of the Terms; a long-lived outer agreement whose actors change needs the core to say whether a power's rule may bind people who never signed the Terms that carry it, and whether a signer who left is still bound. *Reason:* 3.3.8(c). The lead's "asks nothing new of" the core is the claim this item tests.
12. **The receiver of an ended agreement, and a party that has ended.** 5.1.4's receiver chain and 5.5.5(a)'s default signers both reach agreements that may have ended; neither says what then. *Reason:* 3.3.7(d), 3.3.8(a).
13. **The rename.** "update" (306), "replace" (97), "version" in 40 rules, "late", "pin", "gate" (COUNTED); K4's name and row; §0's and §1's narrative sentences on versions and the keeper's where; §3.3 **Never ended** reversed. Not a rule; a cost of the text.

From other layers, beside 5 and 6: the MICS, for the new answer words and for "the report block" that §5.11 still waits on (the fifth attack's 7); Money, for a payment that names an agreement by its root where it named a version (5.13.2: one word, since the root is the one Terms act); Identity, whose rule 34 names a keeper's record that no longer exists in any draft; Development, unchanged, for how a cMIP declares a need.

## 5. The way out

**5.1 Succession.** A party dies. Draft 26: the agreement is frozen "going on in its last version" (5.5.6), no update passing by default; the fifth attack's 5.1. The model: the agreement's ending needs her signature by default, so it cannot end; the others leave by B under 3.3.1's reading (i), and are then "no longer" in A while A, unended, still names them; under reading (ii) they are frozen as before. So the partial closing is the worst break (3.4's first) and the only way out of a dead party in the same stroke: whichever reading he chooses, one of the two is lost. A filling under the frozen agreement stays where it was, as before; a receiver that named the frozen agreement stays pointed at a dead party (3.3.8(a)). For the baker's rights, "not a term of this text" (§3.3 **Succession**): unchanged. INFERRED.

**5.2 Leaving.** Two acts in draft 26 (a dissolution version, then a new agreement); one in the model (B's first act with the pointer), where the ending condition is met by B's signers. What crosses to B: nothing but what B's own acts say: no filling, no power, no call, no receiver, no payment's purpose (3.3.7). Who may leave alone is 3.3.1. What the leavers owe the unknown parties: A's words, for as long as they say, under an agreement the leavers call invalid (3.3.3). INFERRED.

**5.3 Carrying to another system.** Better than draft 26 (the fifth attack's 5.3): an agreement is one Terms act, its words act, its Statements and its cMIPs, with no chain of versions to carry and no keeper's record to translate; what does not travel is the clock's answers and the keeper's "it was there", which the model makes the test of whether any act counts (3.3.4). An agreement carried out of MOR carries its signatures and its knots and loses whether its acts ever counted. INFERRED.

**5.4 Carrying across drafts of MOR.** Better than draft 26 (the fifth attack's 5.4, the sixth's 5.4): a first act under `SPEC_DRAFT_27` with a closing pointer to a draft 26 agreement is the model's own form for any change, and draft 26's E2 ("nothing written under this one is updated") stops being a wall. Whether a draft 27 act's pointer closes a draft 26 agreement for a draft 26 verifier is a question of which verifier reads the pointer; two verifiers, two answers, as before, but now with a form each can at least read as a knot. INFERRED.

**5.5 What the way out costs that it did not.** Every change re-collects every signature of every agreement that named the changed one (3.3.8(b)); the mistake "fixed by killing the chain" (his 08:51) kills the chain for the unknown parties too, who stay on the dead one; and the name people give the thing is, by his 09:13, a human construct, so no act of MOR tells a reader that B is "the same band" as A: the pointer says only that A once existed and is not valid for B's signers. For a reader who holds B and not A, the pointer is a fingerprint of an act not held: **unchecked**, as any missing act (5.13.3). INFERRED.

## Questions
