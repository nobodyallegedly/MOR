**This is a reading and a test review, not a second verifier.** The Rust tests could not be run in the reviewing container (no route to crates.io); the pull request's checks are the test run. **No code was changed in this round.** The decided rules whose code diverges today (F302, F304, F305, and F282's buyers, below) are clear, but each waits on a build session the log already names, and the one divergence I would otherwise fix blind (A2-3's roster) rests on a question this round puts; writing it before the answer would be guessing at a rule.

## In plain words first

The map is right about the shape of the thing. A band and a small cooperative bundle owning and running, and the core's member rules already protect them; a company separates the two, and then everything turns on how owners who do not run things consent together. The five additions (counting by share, the holders' rule, issuance with pre-emption, the owners' matters, the owners' choice of operators) are two patterns and their consequences, not six instances; I come back to that under the seventh objection.

Of the seven objections, three stand as raised, three stand sharpened, and one points at something older than the map. **Votes that unwind (1):** F302 is the right repair for a collective, because an owners' vote completes at the collective's line, which names the signatures and keeps them; the stated cost is the one C5 already states. Chain-key voting is not needed and would make a company with a hundred owners impossible. But a *deal* has no line, and a deal's completed version can already be uncompleted today by any party's later rotation disowning their signature; a holders' rule in a deal inherits that hole. **Pre-emption (2):** the guard is real for proportion and empty for price, as in law; "below the last price" cannot be checked by a verifier that cannot judge a price, and "a right that stays open" is either a trap or a floating claim. What the core can check is that the offer reached every holder; the rest is a stated cost. **The roster (3):** the same answer as F302, turned to transfers: a transfer counts for the collective where the collective's line placed it; a seller and a buyer never both count because the line moves the share once. The code today reads holders from field 7 alone, so F282's buyer is never asked; that is the evidence for the question. **Operators (4):** the deadlock is real and the project lead's reason is wrong in one direction: rule 93 punishes a rotation made *without* the signatures the constitution requires; it cannot compel a rotation *with* them. An owners' vote to replace operators stands complete and unexecuted, visibly, and the owners keep the owners' matters and the exit, not replacement, unless the constitution also puts a share of the chain key where the owners can reach it. That must be the stated cost of F311. **Reserves (5):** a prohibition on paying members from the reserve is not a rule a verifier can decide; what is missing is legibility, since a collective's acts are "done" once sealed to its *members*, and its voiceless owners see none of its spending. **The device (6):** it can and should demand the competing rotation as proof of a contest; it cannot demand proof of a refusal, and never could. **Growth (7):** the additions pass the pattern test; the one instance-shaped rule is pre-emption's period, which is a clock's.

The thing older than the map is the finding to lead with. **F279's way out does not reach a departed holder.** A declaration of absence names "a party to the agreement" (rule 87, the declaration's field 2); a departed holder is no party (field 22). So the only route the core gives against a holder who vanished, "declared absent, the stake moves to its successor", is closed for exactly the holders who have no voice. With F310 making every sale of a work an owners' matter, one departed holder who vanished before any holders' rule existed, and so never accepted one, blocks every sale, every fee change and every fork that touches the plan, for good. The members' collective the core keeps on its own fails the author's condition on that one case today, before any of the new language, because F270 already asked that holder's signature. The fix is one sentence in rule 85: a declaration may name a holder of the collective's stake in itself who is no party, under the clause in force, with the stake-moving outcomes only.

## Findings

Severity: **breaks a guarantee** / **leaves a gap** / **wording**. Kind: **text** / **code** / **instrument**. Pattern: one of the four of round 2, or "fits none", named.

### A2-1. Votes that unwind (objection 1). Text. Stands as raised for deals, answered by F302 for collectives. Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party.

**For a collective, F302 is the repair, and the right one.** An owners' matter is a clone of the collective's agreement (F310: a version), complete when the holders' rule is met (F307), put in force by a record or a rotation that names the signature acts completing it and only those (A2, Flaw M). Those signature acts are what F302 keeps: "an act of another identity that a line of a collective names, registers, or relies on to count what it registers keeps, for that collective, the effect the line gave it". A holder's vote is such an act, named in record field 2. A later rotation of the holder shows the dispute on the line and changes nothing. **Stated cost, the same as C5's:** a thief holding a holder's signing key can vote the holder's share once, and once the line registers it, the holder's rotation does not undo the vote for the collective; the holder's protection is the line's visibility and the equal-treatment rule (a vote the thief casts passes something that hits every holder alike, the thief's victim included, so there is nothing to steal by it but the vote). **Chain-key voting (F303's shape) is not needed and should not be taken:** F303 chose chain signatures for successors on a fork because the fork is the last line, with nothing after it to place anything; a vote is placed by the next record. And a chain-key ceremony per owner per vote would make a company with many owners unable to vote at all, which is the shape the map exists for.

**For a deal, the hole is older than F307.** A deal has no line (Q28: only a declaration draws one). Rule 6: a signature "void or disputed under Identity confers nothing"; rule 47: a deal's version is complete with every party's signature. So today, before any holders' rule, a party who rotates and disowns their signature on a completed version uncompletes it for everyone else (F297: another's voided evidence decides nothing), and the parent comes back into force, with purchases and splits already made under the version. F302 does not reach it, since nothing placed the signature. A holders' rule in a deal (F307 names "a deal's investor buying under a version carrying one") inherits this: a passed change unpasses when one investor rotates.

**Smallest fix.** Two readings. (a) A deal's version, once any party's or keeper's later act relies on it (a purchase naming it, a split under it, a keeper's record of it, a later version cloning it), keeps its completeness for everyone, the disowned signature shown as a dispute: "what others relied on stands", with the relying act as the place; checkable, since the relying act names the version. (b) The holders' rule is a collective's only until keeper records (type 2, format open) give a deal a line. Recommendation: (a) for deals in general, which closes a hole that exists today, and (b) meanwhile for holders' rules, since a deal's owners' matters without a line are the attack above waiting to happen. (Q1.)

### A2-2. Pre-emption against the absent owner (objection 2). Text. Leaves a gap, bounded; a stated cost to state. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check (the price).

**What the guard does.** It keeps every holder's *proportion* available to them on the same terms: a majority cannot issue to itself what the minority could not also take. That is real and checkable: the offers are acts, their terms comparable, and each holder either accepted, declined, or let the period pass. **What it cannot do.** Protect value: a token price dilutes an owner who cannot or does not pay, and the core cannot judge a price (A11's reasoning, kept in F310). Protect an absent owner: the period runs on the agreement's time reference, and an owner who misses it is diluted within the rule.

**The candidate repairs, tested.** *"Issuance below the last price needs every holder":* a verifier cannot compare prices (units, rails, time, a first issuance with no last price, a token last price set on purpose), so two honest verifiers would not agree; and "every holder" is the trap the holders' rule exists to open. Refused. *"An absent holder's right stays open until answered":* either the issuance is not complete until every holder answers, which gives one silent holder the veto back, or the issuance completes and the holder keeps a claim to its proportion at the old terms, a floating option against the collective and the new holders that nothing in the core can value or close. Refused as core; a cMIP may offer it as a term of the issuance. *What the core can check:* that the offer reached each holder, as a declaration must reach the member it names (F189, 8): the offer act is public or sealed to that holder, or it did not count toward the period; and that the period is a point on the agreement's time reference, named in the offer. **Stated cost, to write:** an owner who does not take up its proportion within the period, or has no money for it, is diluted; that is the law's cost too. An owner who vanished for good is reached by F279's way out, which today does not reach a departed holder (A2-6). (Q2.)

### A2-3. Who holds what at the vote (objection 3). Text, and code today. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a lower layer's rule depends on data only a higher layer's client holds, turned around: the collective's rule depends on acts on holders' own chains.

**The question.** A transfer takes effect on the seller's own line (rule 10, F217: the seller's chain signature fixes its place) and completes with the buyer's signature act. Neither is on the collective's chain. For an owners' matter judged at the collective's line, which transfers count? If "every transfer a verifier holds", two verifiers holding different transfers count different rosters, and under F298 one that suspects a transfer it does not hold answers unknown on every vote. If "every transfer, whenever made", a seller who voted and a buyer who bought after the vote both count, or the vote changes as transfers arrive.

**Evidence that the code decides nothing yet.** `pay.rs`, `pay_signers`: the holders a version pays less are read from the parent's field 7 (`st.identified()`), never from transfers. So a holder who bought part of a stake (rule 48 since draft 13, F282: "a holder who bought part of a stake") is never asked to sign; the seller still appears in field 7 and is asked, or, if the seller is a member, nobody is. F282's buyer is text without code. The same function is where a holders' rule would be counted, so the roster must be fixed before it is built.

**Smallest fix, the same shape as F302.** *A transfer counts for the collective, for the owners' matters, where the collective's line placed it: an act of the collective on its chain acknowledges it (Envelopes `acks`, as a member's signature is placed, "Made before, made after" 2), or a record names it in a field for transfers registered, as a record registers departures (field 3).* Until then the seller holds, for the collective, what field 7 and the transfers already placed say; the buyer's protection is to have the transfer acknowledged, which the buyer's client asks for and the collective's client does on sight (as it acknowledges members' signatures). Consequences: the roster at a line is one set every verifier computes from the line's history; a seller and a buyer never both count, because the line moves the share once, at the placement; a vote counts the roster at the line that puts the version in force; a transfer acknowledged but not held is unknown (F298, F304), never silently absent. The split service keeps following transfers as rule 10 says, since that is money, not a vote. For a deal, which has no line, the version carrying an owners' matter names the transfers it counts, as a settling version names the tips it settles (field 26); a transfer it does not name counts nothing toward it. **Cost:** a buyer the collective never acknowledged has no vote until it does, and the collective's members can delay a hostile buyer's vote by not acknowledging; shown, and bounded by the next record the buyer can ask any member to draw. Freeze scenario: a seller votes, sells, the buyer votes on the same version; one of the two counts, by the line. (Q3.)

### A2-4. The owners' choice of operators (objection 4). Text. Leaves a gap: a protection that traps the owners, to be stated. Pattern: a rule that protects the owner also traps the owner.

**What holds.** F311's reasoning from rule 93 is half right. A rotation that moves the keys without the signatures the constitution requires breaks the collective: every act after it counts for nothing until a rollback (rule 93). So operators cannot *defy* an owners' constitution by rotating around it. **What does not hold.** Rule 93 punishes a rotation made; it cannot compel a rotation refused. A constitutional clone that removes the operator-members needs a rotation to come into force (rule 69: "only a rotation declares such a clone"; a record of one "puts nothing in force"), and the rotation needs the chain key, which the operators hold. Complete and never declared, the clone is a draft in force nowhere; the collective is not broken (no rotation failed); it runs on under the old members. A fork needs the members' chain signatures (the operators'); a closing likewise; the recovery path (rule 59, key 3) is for a lost share, under the abandonment authority, not for a refusal. So: deadlock, exactly as the counter-argument says, with one correction: the collective is not frozen and income does not stop; it keeps running under operators the owners voted out.

**What the stated cost must say.** An owners' power over membership binds only through the keys. Where the key holders refuse the rotation, the vote stands complete and unexecuted, visible to everyone who reads the chain; the owners' remedies are the owners' matters (they can refuse every fee, every issuance, every sale) and the exit, not replacement. **The way to make the choice real:** a constitution that counts membership among a stake's holders SHOULD also put a share of the chain key, or the recovery path's custodian (rule 59, `recovery = [0, custodian, grant]`), under the owners' control, so that the holders' rule can produce the rotation; the key grammar is where the core can keep that promise, and a client that founds such a collective warns when it does not (client conformance). Without it, F311's option is a vote the keys can ignore, which was the map's original objection, and it was right about the mechanics even if wrong about rule 93. (Q4.)

### A2-5. Reserves as a side door (objection 5). Text. Leaves a gap (legibility). Pattern: can anyone hide what they take? Today, from the owners, yes.

**The objection, tested.** Raising the collective's own share off the top is an owners' matter (F312); spending it is the collective's business, done under its areas and lanes. Members paying themselves from it (a salary, a contract to a service they own) is lawful today and would be under F312. Is it "re-opening what F280 closed"? Not quite: F280 closed hidden dilution *in the plan*; the reserve is dilution the owners approved, visibly, by their rule. The abuse is in the spending. **A prohibition ("the reserve pays no member without the owners' agreement") is not a rule a verifier can decide:** the reserve is money at the collective's pointer, fungible with every other receipt; a payment to a member's pointer, or to a service a member owns outside MOR, cannot be told from a supplier's invoice by any act. Two honest verifiers would not agree on "paid from the reserve". Refused as a core rule; a cMIP (a budget, an audit) can state it as a term and judge it.

**What the core can give is sight.** Rule 57: a collective's act is done once sealed to every *member*, or public. A departed holder, a successor, a buyer are not members, so the collective's Money acts, its obligations, its payouts to members, are not seen by the owners whose reserve they spend, unless public. That is the legible-greed principle failing for exactly the people the map is about. **Smallest fix:** for a collective whose stake in itself has holders who are no members, an act in its Money lane (a receipt, an obligation, a payout, a payee pointer) is done only once sealed to every holder of that stake, members and others, or public; the collective's client makes them public by default. Cost: more recipients per sealed act, or publicity; a company's accounts are public in most law anyway. Alternative: a stated cost, "owners see the plan and the fees, not the spending". Recommendation: the fix; the right of exit means little to an owner who cannot see why to leave. (Q5.)

### A2-6. F279's way out does not reach a departed holder. Text. Breaks a guarantee (the way out). Pattern: a rule that protects the owner also traps the owner. **The finding to lead with.**

**Sentences.** Rule 48 (F279): "A departed holder who vanishes does not block the collective's fees, because its stake moves: declared absent, to its stake successor, or, with none named, by outcome 1 or 2 (rule 89)". F310: a sale or release of a work, and a fork's successor plans, need the owners who have no say, "F279's way out against a holder who vanished". Rule 87's checks: "the party is a party to the agreement field 0 names". The declaration's format, field 2: "the party declared absent: a party to the agreement of field 0". Rule 85: "Every collective carries one that covers each party with constitutional power". Field 22: the departed members entry lists identities "none of them a party". Rule 83: a stake successor enters the stake by a clone, as a holder, not a party. Rule 10: a buyer is a holder, never a party.

**So.** A departed holder, a stake successor and a buyer are holders who are no parties, and a declaration of absence cannot name them: `declaration_as` checks `lineage[0].1.parties.contains(&d.party)` and refuses. The way out F279 promises, and F310 leans on, does not exist for the holders it was written for. The consequences today, before any of the map's additions: a departed holder who vanished (lost keys, died with no plan) blocks every fee change and every change of the plan (F270, F282) for good. With F310 as decided: every sale and release of a work, and every fork whose successor plan differs, as well. With F307: the holders' rule does not help, because it binds only holders who accepted it, and adding one needs every holder, the vanished one included. **The members' collective the core keeps on its own fails the author's condition on this case**, not because of the new language but because F270 already asked that signature; the map's table ("Still possible, heavier") did not see it because it took F279's sentence at its word.

**Smallest fix.** Rule 85 and the declaration's format: *a declaration may also name a holder of the collective's stake in itself who is no party (a departed holder, a stake successor, a buyer), under the clause in force; against such a holder its outcomes are the stake-moving ones only (succession as rule 89 says; outcome 1 or 2 where the holder's plan names no stake successor), never 0, 3 or 4; the authority and the absence-proof cMIP are the clause's as for a party.* Rule 61's coverage is unchanged (a holder has no constitutional voice to cover). The succession plan a departed holder signed as a member stays attached to the stake (rule 48's "the stake, and its pay with it, goes to the successor"), so the plan exists to execute. Freeze scenario: a departed holder vanishes; declared absent under the clause, its stake passes to its plan's successor, who signs the sale; with no plan, outcome 1 shares it out and the sale proceeds. (Q6.)

### A2-7. Counting by share: the denominator, and flaw C restated. Text, for the writing of F308. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check, unless the text says which.

F308's form, "those counted, holding together at least this share of this stake", is used in two places and needs two denominators. **Among members** (a change rule by share): if the share is of the *whole* stake and departed holders hold half of it, the members can never reach sixty per cent, which is flaw C's trap by another route. The denominator must be the shares held by the parties whose voice remains at the line, and rule 65's flaw-C sentence restated so: where the voices that remain hold together less than the share the rule asks for, all of them together meet it. **Among a stake's holders** (the holders' rule): the denominator is the whole stake, since no holder loses its vote by leaving the membership; a vanished holder's share then counts against the rule for ever, which is A2-6's trap again, and A2-6's fix is what resolves it (the stake moves). "All of these rules" needs its own flaw-C reading: each rule inside it counted as it would be alone. And the seat successor's "voting weight" (plan key 2), which no rule reads, should be given F308's meaning (the successor enters with the departed member's share of the stake in itself, which field 7 already says) or dropped; a weight written as a free number is what F308 refused.

### A2-8. Money the collective keeps for itself: where it rests. Text, for the writing of F312. Wording with a mechanic to fix. Pattern: fits none.

F312 lets the plan name the collective itself off the top. The split service pays each share to its receiver's pointer. The collective's designated pointer for payments *is* its split service's address (field 14: "Its one grantee is the agreement's designated pointer for payments"), so a payout "to the collective" goes back to the service that just split it. The map saw this ("a payout to its own pointer would come back to its own split service"). **The mechanic:** the collective's own share is paid to the collective's *vault* (Money rule 2: declared in genesis or a rotation, never changed with the signing key; distinct from the flow pointer), and a plan naming the collective off the top is invalid where the collective declares no vault. Checkable: the vault is a declared act. It also answers "never split again": money in a vault is the owner's, not a receipt under the plan.

### A2-9. The device cannot see "contested" (objection 6; Identity, F299). Instrument (the Module), and Identity rule 29. Leaves a gap, sharpened. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check, offline.

**Today.** `modules/airgap/src/device.rs`, `standing`: the escape exception (rule 29's second) is granted when the pending rotation is homeless, the online side has prepared an escape endorsement, and the key has not escaped before; the device cannot see that "the homes that count refused" the first rotation, and never could: a refusal is the absence of receipts, which no bytes prove. It tells the human ("Sign this escape only if the homes refused that rotation") and marks the endorsement as not checked. So for the *refused* case, the objection's worry already holds and is bounded by the human and the endorsement, which the current signing key signs on the online device: whoever controls the online device *and* the signing key *and* the air-gapped device is the owner, or has everything a thief needs already.

**For the contested case (F299) the device can do better, and should.** A contest at a self-hosted home is two valid rotations at one position revealing the same chain key. The device holds that chain key's memory (`signed`, with the act bytes) and can check, offline: the competing rotation's signature verifies under the chain key it revealed; its previous act is the same; it is not the one the device signed. That is proof of a conflict from bytes alone, which a refusal never is. **Sharpening:** rule 29's new exception ("or contested at a self-hosted home") is granted by the Module only on presentation of the competing rotation, checked so; the online side's word alone does not open it. The endorsement lists both as abandoned. Cost: none for an honest owner, who holds the thief's rotation (it is what made the contest). (Q7.)

### A2-10. Growth before the freeze (objection 7): pattern or instance. Text. Noted, with one recommendation.

**Pattern.** The five additions are two patterns. *Counting by share* generalises the `rule` form from heads to shares of a stake, one new way of counting, used wherever a rule is counted (members' change rules, the holders' rule, the release rule: F308 fixes the side finding with it, "By head was a real mistake all along"). *The holders' rule* generalises field 24 (a rule among a stake's holders, for one matter) to the owners' matters; what those matters are (pay, issuance, the works, a fork's plans) is one definition, "changes that hit every holder of the stake alike", with F274's list and F310's two doors as its instances. Owner-chosen operators (F311) is an application of the first pattern to a power that already exists. Reserves (F312) is a reading of rule 22. None of these adds a verdict to the vocabulary: a version is still complete or a draft, a stake is paid less or not. **Instance.** Pre-emption's *period* is a deadline on a clock, the one thing here that reaches for a cMIP and the one with a jurisdictional choice behind it (the map: Delaware's default is off). Its *offer* is pattern (an act that must reach every holder, as a declaration must). Recommendation: the offer act and "each holder offered its proportion on the same terms" in the core, as F309 decided; the period, and what silence means after it, as the time reference's answer, stated as a fact the caller states, as every time reference's answer is today (`judges_lapsed`, `notices_lapsed`). That keeps the guard in the core without a clock in the core.

**MOR 2.** Nothing here should wait, on one condition: A2-6 is fixed with it. Without A2-6, every one of the five makes the trap F270 set a little tighter; with it, the members' collective passes the author's condition and the company runs above it. If the author prefers to freeze MOR 1 with companies partnership-shaped, the honest line is: F307 to F312 wait, A2-6 does not, since it fixes a trap F270 set in MOR 1 already.

**The author's condition, tested rule by rule.** A members' collective with no holders' rule, no issuance, no vault and no owners' choice, written exactly as today: founding (field 4 `[0]`, every founder; unchanged), a departure (rule 70, the line; unchanged), a fork (the successors' plans must pay kept holders no less than the original's: with the same plan, trivially met; with no departed holders, nothing to check), a closing (sales before it need the departed holders' consent, by signature: as F270 already made fee changes; and A2-6 is the way out where one vanished). Exactly as today, save the one weight the map named and the trap A2-6 removes.

## The verdict vocabulary

No new verdict. A holders' rule met or not is "complete" or "a draft"; an issuance is a version; an owners' matter is a power in the mark, checked as every power is. A2-5 adds no verdict (done or not done, as today, with more recipients). A2-6 adds none (a declaration counts or not). The one word to watch is "accepted" for a holders' rule (by signing, by buying): it is a fact read from acts (a signature act on a version carrying it; a transfer placed under one), never a status of its own.

## What held under attack

- F302 against a holder's vote: the line names the signatures; a later rotation shows a dispute and moves nothing.
- Equal treatment under the holders' rule: a change that singles out a holder still needs that holder, whatever the rule says; a thief's vote can pass only what hits the thief's victim alike.
- Counting by share against manufactured heads: a holder splitting its share among identities it controls gains nothing by share (the map's side finding, closed by F308).
- Issuance against a majority issuing to itself: pre-emption keeps every holder's proportion available on the same terms; the offer is an act that must reach each holder.
- The broken collective against operators rotating around an owners' constitution (rule 93): defiance is void and visible.
- The members' collective: unchanged by every optional addition; A2-6 aside, as today.
