## In plain words first

Identity is where MOR's promise to be a good ancestor lives, so I attacked the exits first: succession, the homeless rotation, the escape with both keys, closure, the move to a new signature scheme. The design holds where it says it holds. A thief with the chain key alone loses at careful homes and at most contests a position; a stolen operator key alone produces nothing that touches a chain; a used chain key found on an old backup rewrites nothing; a censor cannot make a theft final; an owner who proves both keys leaves any home; a dishonesty verdict never reaches backwards. The gauntlet runs these against real homes, and the library decides them in act-id order, never arrival order.

One exit can be blocked by someone who should not hold that power, and it is the finding to lead with. **An objection, an absence statement, a cosignature or an acknowledgement keeps its force after its signer's own rotation has voided it.** Receipts were fixed for exactly this in round 2 (F95: a voided receipt an accomplice acknowledged "blocks nothing"); the same sentence was never written for the other four. So a thief holding a home operator's signing key signs an objection to the owner's homeless rotation, an accomplice signs a witness act naming it, the operator rotates and disowns it, and the objection stays "valid and shown as disputed", voiding the exit for good. The owner who has lost the signing key, the very owner the homeless path exists for, never leaves. The fix is one principle, stated once: evidence signed by another identity counts only while that identity's chain keeps it; voided, it is shown, never effective.

The rest is about what a verifier says when it cannot tell. The library answers **void** when it cannot rebuild the line to a kept tip, **invalid** when a scoped key's grant is not held or when a chain cannot be resolved; each of these flips to valid, scoped or counts as more acts arrive. That is E11, and it is why the rehearsal's reader showed the author's first post void after one rotation. The MIR already has the right word for this, *unknown*, and uses it for links (F159); it should use it everywhere a verifier holds part of the picture. The shorter proof of kept ancestry you asked about exists (the running summary is a Merkle mountain range root, so a peak-and-path proof is a few hashes) and changes nothing an act carries.

Three smaller things: a self-hosted identity with two rotations at one position is *contested*, not "the thief wins at once" as rule 4 says, and the owner's way out of that contest is the escape, which rule 29's exception does not name; a succession is declared by one side only, so a stolen chain key, or a stranger, can name anyone as successor and clients will show it; and an invalid rotation spends a chain key the Module will not spend again, which rule 29 should not count against the owner.

## Findings

Severity: **breaks a guarantee** / **leaves a gap** / **wording**. Kind: **text** / **code** / **instrument**. Pattern: one of the four of round 2, or "fits none", named.

### I1. Evidence voided by its signer's rotation keeps its force. Text, then code. Breaks a guarantee (the way out). Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party, and an accomplice's acknowledgement keeps it signed.

**Sentences.** Rule 49: "A single valid objection from a live home of the old set voids a homeless rotation." The objection and absence statement checks, 1: "Each is valid as an everyday act of the home's operator or the auditor." Rule 34: an act voided by a rotation but acknowledged "is valid and shown as disputed". Log summaries and audits, 3: "A cosignature counts only if its signer is declared as an auditor." Rule 34 again, for acknowledgements: "another identity acknowledged it". Against these, receipt check 6 (F95): "A receipt voided by the operator's rotation that another identity acknowledged is shown as contesting that chain position. It never counts as support toward a home rule, and it blocks nothing."

**Evidence.** The library applies rule 34's words literally to objections and absence statements (`chain.rs`, `homeless_basis`: `Status::Valid | Status::Disputed`), and to cosignatures and acknowledgements it applies less than that: `cosigned` and `acknowledged` check only that the act's key is bound by a counting act of its signer (`bound`), never whether the signer's later rotation voided it. So:

- *The exit.* A thief holding a home operator's signing key signs an objection naming the owner's homeless rotation; an accomplice signs a witness act naming the objection. The operator rotates and disowns the objection: rule 34 makes it disputed, the check reads disputed as valid, step 3 of the homeless procedure fails, and the homeless rotation never counts. Escape (rule 53) needs the signing key, which is what the owner of a homeless identity has often lost. Cost to the attacker: two signatures, no chain key. Without the accomplice the same objection blocks the exit until the operator rotates, which the text should state as a cost.
- *Audit.* A thief holding an auditor's signing key cosigns a forged log summary; the auditor rotates and disowns the cosignature; the library still counts it (`cosigned` uses `bound` only), so the receipt under that summary is "protected" for good.
- *Reliance.* A thief holding Bob's signing key signs a witness act naming a thief's post under Alice's stolen key. Alice rotates: the post is disputed, not void. Bob rotates and disowns the witness act: the library still counts it, acknowledged or not, so Alice's thief's post stays disputed for ever.

Receipts are the one kind already handled: receipt check 5 voids them with the operator's rotation and check 6 says an acknowledged one blocks nothing. The other four were left to rule 34's general words, which were written for the signer's own acts, not for evidence others rely on.

**Smallest fix.** One sentence, in section 2 or beside rule 34, generalising F95: "An act of another identity that this MIR uses as evidence (a receipt, an objection, an absence statement, a cosignature, an acknowledgement) counts only while its signer's chain keeps it valid. One voided by its signer's rotation counts for nothing here, acknowledged or not: it is shown, as a dispute, and decides nothing." Then the code: `homeless_basis` reads objections and absence statements as `Status::Valid` only; `cosigned` and `acknowledged` consult the act's status, not only its binding. Some fifteen lines and three tests (an objection disowned and witnessed; a cosignature disowned; a witness act disowned). Not done here: whether a *disputed* acknowledgement should still keep the act it names disputed is the one reading only Nobody, allegedly, can take, and the code follows the sentence. Put as Q1 (core business: the way out).

### I2. What a verifier answers when it cannot tell: void and invalid where it should say unknown. Text, then code; instrument for the clients. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check (what the verifier does not hold).

**Sentences.** Rule 33: "If it lies within the rotation's kept ancestry ... it is valid"; rule 34: "If it lies outside the kept ancestry ... it is void". Everyday check 2: for a scoped key, "`binding` names a held act of a higher MIR". "Every verifier gives the same answer ... holding the same identity chain, acknowledgements, keeper records, inclusion proofs and content keys". F292: "an act whose position or summary does not fit the line leading to a kept tip cannot be proven kept". For links, F159: "A link nobody can fetch from those homes is unknown, never invalid."

**Evidence.** Three places where the library's answer is a verdict with partial knowledge, and flips when more arrives:

- `in_kept_ancestry` proves membership by rebuilding the whole line to the kept tip from the act ids held (`line`) and recomputing the summary. One act of the line not held, and a kept act is **void** (then valid once the act arrives; disputed meanwhile if acknowledged). This is what the rehearsal saw: the reader and the display client judge an act alone, without the signer's sequence, and after the author's first rotation every earlier post is void and dubsar.org goes dark, while the library given the linking acts says valid. The "same answer" paragraph does not list the signer's sequence among the inputs two verifiers must share, so the text promises an agreement it does not deliver.
- A scoped act whose grant is not held: everyday check 2 says "a held act", so the library answers **invalid** (`status_cx`: `_ => Status::Invalid`), then scoped when the grant is fetched.
- An identity whose genesis is signed under a scheme the client lacks (the F54 migration, from the other side: a new identity born with a post-quantum signing key, read by an old client) resolves to `Stop::Unknown`; a rotation of it that the client *can* verify, and every act bound to that rotation, is **invalid**, where rule 9 and the encoding section say such things are unknown, never valid, and T3 of the Text review says invalid is for a rule the verifier can apply and sees fail.

None of these breaks a guarantee: nothing binding rests on an act the verifier has not finished judging, and the final verdicts agree once the acts are held. But a verdict that flips from void to valid, or invalid to scoped, as acts arrive is what the plan's order-independence rule exists to catch, and a client that trusts "void" goes dark.

**Smallest fix.** One principle, once, in the paragraph after rule 35: "A verifier that holds the acts a rule needs answers as the rule says. One that does not (a kept tip whose line it cannot rebuild, a scoped key whose installing act it has not held, a chain it cannot resolve) answers *unknown*, never valid, void or invalid; and *void* needs every kept line of the rotation rebuilt and the act on none of them." Then the code: `judge` returns a fourth answer when any kept line cannot be rebuilt (status unknown; acknowledged or recorded acts shown as disputed only once void is established), the scoped branch answers unknown for a binding not held, and `status_cx` answers unknown for an act bound to a chain that stops at `Stop::Unknown`. Some forty lines and three tests. The clients that judge an act alone (barebone `readPost`, the site's `openVersion`) should fetch the signer's sequence as the feed and the release verifier already do: instrument, its own session (the rehearsal's proposed fix). Put as Q2 (core business: a verdict with partial knowledge), with the recommendation above.

**The shorter proof (E11's second half).** The running summary is the root of a Merkle mountain range over the line's act ids. An act's membership is therefore provable by its peak's path (the sibling hashes from its leaf to its peak) plus the other peaks: a few dozen bytes for a long line, instead of every act id of the line. The rotation already carries the root and the length (`kept-tip`), so nothing an act or a rotation carries changes; what changes is that a relay or the owner's client can serve the proof, and the verifier accepts it as an input, exactly as it accepts a home's inclusion proofs for receipts (`add_inclusion_proof`). The MIR lists this under "Still open" ("Efficient proof that an act lies within a kept ancestry"), so nothing in the text stops it. Development layer (the relay transport cMIP serves it) with one core input; it can come after MOR 1, since with Q2 answered the missing proof costs a reader "unknown", not "void".

### I3. A self-hosted identity with two rotations at one position: contested, not "the thief wins". Text. Wording, with one rule to extend. Fits none: a stated cost stated wrong.

**Sentences.** Rule 4: "A thief holding its chain key wins at once, as at a single lax home." "Which rotation counts", step 2: "For a self-hosted home, the rotation the identity itself serves at position n stands in for a receipt; two different rotations served at one position are a conflict." Rule 29's second exception: "after a normal rotation is refused by the homes that count, the owner MAY sign one endorsed homeless rotation".

**Evidence.** At a lax home, first held wins because the home fixes an order. At a self-hosted home there is no third party: "the rotation the identity itself serves" is, for a verifier, whatever it holds. The library (`tally`, `Operator::Own`): one candidate held, it counts; two held, contested, and nothing can end the contest, since the ways a conflict ends (the home's rotation, an audit) do not exist for a self-host. So a thief with the chain key does not win; the owner and the thief both lose the position, and a verifier that reached only one server counts that one until it meets the other. The owner's way out exists: a homeless rotation at the same position, signed with the same chain key and endorsed with the signing key, abandoning the owner's normal rotation (`abandoned` in `step`), counts under section 11 once new homes receipt it. But rule 29 allows that second signature only after a rotation "refused by the homes that count", and a self-host refuses nothing; the air-gapped Module, which enforces rule 29, would not sign it.

**Smallest fix.** Rule 4: "Two rotations served at one position, by a thief and the owner, are a conflict nobody can settle: the position is contested for good, and the owner leaves it by the escape (section 11)." Rule 29's second exception: "refused by the homes that count, or contested at a self-hosted home". The gauntlet's B (self-hosted) gets one more check. Cost: two sentences, one line in the Module. Put with Q3 (core business, small: the Module's rule).

### I4. A succession is declared by one side. Text. Leaves a gap. Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party (only the one leaving).

**Sentences.** Rule 37: "A succession is valid only as part of a valid rotation that counts." Rule 38: "The latest rotation that counts decides the succession." Reasoning: "a thief holding only the signing key can never redirect an identity's followers, agreements or money".

**Evidence.** A link needs both sides (rule 14) "otherwise anyone could claim to be anyone"; a succession needs one. The successor never signs. So a stranger's rotation naming a well-known identity as its successor is valid and counts, and a client following rule 38 shows "succeeded by" that identity; a thief holding the chain key (the stated cost of a stolen chain key, at a lax home) names their own identity and the followers move. The library carries it as declared (`ChainState.successor`), and Agreements reads it: where a party of a collective has no succession plan, "the identity-level succession declared in a rotation (Identity) applies" (Agreements draft 13, rule 84), so a seat, with its stakes and its duties, can pass by fallback to an identity that never agreed to take it. Money reads it nowhere; followers and clients do.

**Smallest fix.** "A succession to a MOR identity is *declared* by the rotation and *confirmed* by the successor: a link confirmation by the successor naming the rotation, or a witness act of the successor naming it (F110's deliberate reliance). Clients show a declared succession as declared until it is confirmed, and follow it only confirmed. A succession to another protocol is confirmed as that protocol's link cMIP says." Cost: three sentences, one check in `successor` for the clients and for Agreements' rule 84, no new act type. Put as Q4 (core business, with a presentation half).

### I5. An invalid rotation spends a chain key the Module will not spend again. Text. Wording. Pattern: a rule that protects the owner also traps the owner.

**Sentences.** Rule 29: "MUST NOT sign a second, different rotation or chain signature with the same chain key", two exceptions. Rotation check 5 (F94): "A home rule the rotation leaves in place must meet them too, against the new set of homes; if it does not, the rotation is invalid."

**Evidence.** The air-gapped device signs one rotation per chain key and refuses a different one except in the two cases (`modules/airgap/src/device.rs`, "this chain key already signed a different rotation; it signs no other"). It cannot check F94: it does not hold the identity's homes. A client that changes the homes and forgets the rule hands the device an inside it signs; the rotation is invalid everywhere (rule 18: no home stores it), never counts, and the device will not sign another with that key. The owner holding the signing key leaves by the escape (the second exception, the invalid rotation listed as abandoned); an owner without it is stuck, which rule 24 already accepts for refusals, but this one is not a refusal.

**Smallest fix.** One clause in rule 29: "A rotation that fails the rotation checks was never a rotation: it can count nowhere, so the owner MAY sign again with the same chain key." Safe, since validity is fixed by the bytes and an invalid act cannot become a competing rotation later; the Module learns to re-sign after a rotation its online side reports invalid. Cost: one clause, a small Module change.

### I6. Device-bound policies: "single or authoritative" understates. Text. Wording.

**Sentence.** Rule 24: "losing that device at a single or authoritative home can mean losing the identity".

**Evidence.** Under a threshold k of n operators, the same loss at any n − k + 1 homes blocks the normal rotation; the homeless rotation is objected to by the live homes; the escape needs the signing key. The cost is the same, at any set of homes enough to block the rule, and rule 25's conformance ("never the default") should say so too.

**Smallest fix.** "at a single or authoritative home, or at homes enough to block the home rule". Cost: a clause.

### I7. "Contested" means four things. Text. Wording.

A position where no rotation counts (rule 40, "Which rotation counts"); a receipt conflict at a position (rule 44, "contested there, not frozen"); a voided-but-acknowledged receipt (receipt check 6, "contesting that chain position"); a routes fork ("the routes are contested from that point"); and "a contested rotation" (rule 42). Two verifiers agree on each, so the admission test is met; a reader of a report is not helped. The library names them apart (`Stop::Contested`, `Resolution::contested`, `Latest::contested`). One word per thing, or a sentence saying which is meant, in the freeze-ready draft.

### I8. The clients judge an act without its sequence. Instrument. Noted; its own session.

The rehearsal's item 1: barebone's `readPost` and the site's `openVersion` look the signer up and judge the act alone; the feed and the release verifier fetch the sequence. With I2 answered as recommended the symptom becomes "unknown" rather than "void", which is honest but still dark; the clients should fetch the line to the kept tip (or, later, its proof). Not Identity's text.

## The verdict vocabulary (attack 7)

For an act: **valid**, **invalid**, **unknown**, **pending** (bound to a rotation that does not count yet), **void**, **disputed** (void but relied on: acknowledged or recorded), **scoped** (a scoped key's act, judged by the installing MIR). For an identity-chain position: **counts**, **pending** (held, none counts yet), **contested** (a conflict keeps any from counting), **end**. For a homeless rotation: **provisional**, **final**, **re-homed without audit** (counts only through the verifier's own attempt; said, and nothing binding rests on it). For a home: **gone** (closure, absence statements, or the verifier's own attempt), **closed**, **outvoted**, **dishonest at a position**. For a receipt: **support**, **void**, **conflict**, **contesting**. For routes, names and links: **counts**, **contested** (routes), **linked / ended / not linked / unknown** (links).

Each is a state two verifiers holding the same acts must agree on, save "re-homed without audit", which the text marks as resting on one verifier's own input and keeps out of everything binding; none restates another under a second name, though "contested" is spread over four (I7). "Scoped" is needed: Identity cannot read the installing MIR, and a client without it must say unknown (rule 8). "Pending" is needed: acts under a key whose rotation has not counted must not be void or invalid. The gap is not a surplus verdict but a missing use of one: *unknown* where the verifier holds part of the picture (I2).

## What held under attack

- **A thief with the chain key at careful homes.** Arriving second, the thief's rotation is refused with the owner's receipt (gauntlet 5.7); at a device-policy home it is refused outright and, held only by the lax home, misses the majority; the owner's counts, the thief's acts are invalid, the owner's history stays valid (5.7).
- **A stolen operator key.** A receipt naming a made-up act changes nothing (receipt check 1; 5.7b); two non-extending summaries change no receipt's standing and stop the auditing (5.7b); a conflict needs two genuine rotations, which needs the chain key; closure needs the operator's rotation, so no signing key closes a home (rule 50; 5.7b). The one hole is I1.
- **Dishonesty bound to a position.** Proven only when both receipts survive the home's rotations or sit under cosigned summaries (`tally`: `protected`); the home's earlier receipts keep counting; its later ones count for nothing (rule 45; F55).
- **Finality.** A homeless rotation is final once the next counts under the rule it declared (`step`, "finals"), never when it rests on the verifier's own attempt (F87; `Basis::OwnAttempt` excluded), and a rotation the old homes held beats it, final or not (F92: the tally runs first). A used chain key found after a closure rewrites nothing (5.7c).
- **The censored reader.** Its own failed attempt re-homes the identity for reading and following only; `binding_status` answers unknown for everything binding until the answer rests on something else (F153; `rests_on_own_attempt`).
- **Escape with both keys.** Objections do not void it, the old homes need not be gone, and the rotation it abandons can never count (section 11; `abandoned`); the lost phone at a strict home with a backed-up seed leaves (5.7).
- **Audit the owner can always drop.** Receipts judged under the requirement the rotation declares (F60; `supports` reads `res.states[k].audit`); absence statements only from the auditors in force before the homeless rotation (F93).
- **Proofs travel (F101).** A reader who never reached the vanished home proves its audited history from the proofs the owner carried (5.7c).
- **F94.** A rule left in place that no longer fits the homes makes the rotation invalid (`apply`), so no rotation quietly weakens a protection.
- **F110 and F128.** A witness act must name an act and carry no objects; an act of a scoped key is scoped, judged by Agreements; a private Identity act of any type but a link is refused (F134); a private link act signed by a scoped key is invalid (F168).
- **Order independence.** Every index is kept in act-id order, never arrival order (`add_with_key`, after IC6); the resolution is a pure function of what is held and of the verifier's own attempts, cached and invalidated on every add. I found no final verdict that depends on arrival order; the flips of I2 are partial knowledge, and converge.
- **The running summary and kept lines.** A private tip's line is rebuilt from the act before it (`line`), so no private act is opened to prove kept ancestry, as the text promises.

## Questions for Nobody, allegedly, one at a time

**Q1 (core business: the way out; I1).** Should evidence signed by another identity (an objection, an absence statement, a cosignature, an acknowledgement) count only while that identity's chain keeps it valid, as receipts already do (F95), so that a voided one is shown and decides nothing, even if an accomplice acknowledged it? I recommend yes, stated once for all five kinds. Otherwise a stolen operator key and one witness act veto a homeless exit for good.

**Q2 (core business: a verdict with partial knowledge; I2).** When a verifier does not hold what a rule needs (the line to a kept tip, a scoped key's grant, a chain it cannot resolve), does it answer *unknown*, with *void* reserved for an act shown outside every kept line it has rebuilt, or does it answer *void* and *invalid* as the library does today, flipping when the acts arrive? I recommend unknown, which is what the text already says for links (F159) and what keeps a reader's screen honest; the shorter proof of kept ancestry can follow later without changing any act.

**Q3 (core business, small; I3).** Does rule 29's second exception extend to a position contested at a self-hosted home, so that the owner's escape with both keys is a rotation the Module will sign? I recommend yes; without it the exit the rules provide is one the Module refuses.

**Q4 (core business, with a presentation half; I4).** Does a succession to a MOR identity count only once the successor confirms it (a link confirmation or a witness act naming the rotation), shown as declared until then? I recommend yes: a link needs both sides for the same reason.

I5, I6 and I7 are mechanics, a clause each, for the freeze-ready draft; I8 is the clients' session.
