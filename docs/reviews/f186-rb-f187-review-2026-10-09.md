## In plain words first

The decisions of the two rounds are in the text and in the code as the findings log records them, and the nine fixes each close what they were aimed at. The rollback still cannot undo a valid act, a key grab by rollback still gains nothing a faction could not already do, the broken clone is refused, a lost act is refused before the rotation, and the last voice is warned across devices. What I found sits beside the fixes, in three places the new work did not look at.

1. **An old resignation can take a voice twice.** A member who resigned, was registered out, and later came back by signing a constitutional clone that names them (B10) counts again. But their old resignation is still a valid act, and a line that registers it a second time, a record or a rollback, counts them out again, with no new signature of theirs. The reading written in on 8 October, "a later record may register the same act again, which changes nothing", is false as built. The collective client's rollback does exactly this by accident: it registers every resignation in its file by a member Law still counts, and nothing ever removes a returned member's old entry. A rollback made in good faith removes a member who came back, and deals them no share of the new key.
2. **A forked deal can be made unreadable by anyone.** While a deal has two branches, the verifier looks for versions that settle the fork before it checks whether they are complete, or even belong to the deal. One party's half-signed draft naming in field 26 a hash nobody holds, or a stranger's terms act naming an unheld parent, and every reading of the deal, its version in force, its purchases, its splits and its releases, ends in an error. Field 26 opened this.
3. **The seller's alarm is silent exactly when it matters most.** The alarm compares the version a payment names with the one the seller holds. When the named version is one the seller has never received, the core answers "no alarm" and the desk shows nothing. That is the fork a seller cannot see, the case the rule was written for.
4. **Settlement is not final in the code.** After a fork is settled, the discarded branch growing by two versions of one version, or by a second settling version, makes the verifier refuse the whole deal, settled version included. The text says the discarded branch never comes back whatever grows on it.
5. **A broken collective can close.** Every member can sign a closing whose line is the last good link before the broken act. Law then reads the collective as closed and as broken at once, and "owes nothing" does not see the payments the stretch owes back.

Three readings are unclear, below; what held is at the end, with what I tried.

## How findings are marked

- **BREAKS**: an attack works, or a common case does not work, as built or as written.
- **CONFLICTS**: the text and the code, or two parts of the text, disagree.
- **UNCLEAR**: the text or the client can be read two ways, or a verifier cannot check it as written.
- **HOLDS**: I tried an attack and it failed; I say what I tried.

Each finding gives the sentence or the code, the smallest story, and a verdict. Ordered by weight.

## 1. A stale resignation, registered a second time, removes a returned member's voice; the collective client's rollback does it by accident. BREAKS (core, rules 37a, 37d, 44d; client)

**Sentences.** Rule 44d: "A party is named again only by a constitutional clone made after the version in which it lost that voice, naming it as a party ..., which it signed by a signature act the collective did not place before the line registering its departure; it counts again under that clone and its descendants (B10)." Law, "Open in this draft", readings taken writing F185 in: "The rollback's registrations stay in effect after it, for every later act and clone, as a record's do; a later record may register the same act again, which changes nothing." Rule 37: "A departing member hands over nothing: the remaining members rotate to keys the departing member never held."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:2460` (`voices`) collects, for each party, every line registering a departure of theirs (`departure_lines`, `view.rs:2332`) and drops a line only where `restored` (`view.rs:2525`) finds a constitutional clone naming them that they signed by a signature not placed before *that* line (`view.rs:2547`). For the first registration of a resignation, the signature on the re-adding clone was placed after it, so the member is restored. For a second registration of the same act, at a later record (`record_eval_inner`, `view.rs:2228`: a resignation naming any agreement in the lineage is registered) or at a rollback (`rollback_departures`, `view.rs:1266`: the same), the signature on the re-adding clone was placed before that later line, so `restored` answers no, and the member is counted out. Nothing refuses registering an act a line already registered. `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1530` (`prepareRollback`): `if (!d.resignation || !all.voices?.includes(d.member)) continue;` registers every resignation in `departedOf(c)` whose signer Law still counts, with no check that the entry already carries a `record`; the declarations loop beside it does check (`d.record || d.rollback || d.rotation`). No path in `actions.ts` removes a `departed` entry when its member joins again ("Change members" only blocks keeping a departed member who is still listed as a member, `actions.ts:1004`). `clients/repo/src/collective.ts:313` (`rollbackRules`) then drops everyone in `leaving` from the clone, and the rotation deals the next safety key to the clone's members only.

**Story, the core.** Ana, Ben and Cy; every party the constitutional rule. Ben resigns; the collective registers it by a record: two voices. A year later the three sign a constitutional clone of the founding agreement that keeps Ben as a party; a rotation declares it with all three signatures: Law counts three voices again (B10; my test confirms it). Then the everyday key's holder, Ana, draws a record registering Ben's old resignation once more. Law: the record is a line, it registers one departure, and the voices are Ana and Cy. Ben, who signed nothing since he came back, has no voice. The same by a rollback: Ana and Cy break the collective on purpose (a clone marked with the clone rule), then roll back to the agreement in force before the break with a clone of it they two sign, registering Ben's old resignation: Law reads the rollback as complete, the collective as working, Ben as departed, and the clone's key grammar need not name him. Reproduced here for both the record and the rollback: `next_voices` answers Ana and Cy after each.

**Story, the client, in good faith.** The same collective, the same return. The relays lose an act and the collective breaks as on the Mac. The members review the rollback. The file still holds Ben's entry from the year before, `{member, resignation, record}`; Law counts Ben among the voices of the agreement before the break; the resignation names the founding agreement, which is in that agreement's lineage; so `registering` takes it, `leaving` holds Ben, the review says "Ben resigned ..., and the rollback registers that act, so they no longer count", the clone leaves Ben out and writes him to the departed entry, the mark names Ana and Cy, `lawRollbackRegisters` is content (the act is held, valid, by a party, naming the lineage), and the rotation deals a two-of-two safety key to Ana and Cy. Ben is out of a collective he rejoined, by a repair nobody meant to remove him with. F187's fix 1 moved the members to Law's voices, which is right; it is fed a wrong `leaving`.

**Also.** `published_resignations` (`view.rs:1477`) lists every valid resignation of the whole voice by a current party naming the lineage, registered or not, so the leaving review (`actions.ts:1300`) treats a returned member as "gone" for the warnings and tells the member leaving that the other "has signed a resignation that no line has registered yet, published from another device": a false last-voice warning in a collective of two where one came back. Over-warning only, but from the same cause.

**Verdict.** BREAKS. A signed act from before a member's return removes their voice again, by a record (the everyday key's holder alone) or by a rollback, and the honest client does it to a returned member by accident. Whether the fix is in the core (a line registers a departure only once: an act a line before it already registered, or one made before the member's restoring signature was placed, registers nothing) or also in the clients (a `departed` entry is closed when its member is named again) is for Nobody, allegedly; the reading "which changes nothing" should then say what the code does. The previous review's HOLDS on "a stale resignation, registered once and followed by a constitutional clone they signed" still stands; it did not try the second registration.

## 2. The settling scan reads before it checks: one party's draft, or a stranger's act, makes a forked deal unreadable. BREAKS (core, rule 45b; field 26)

**Sentences.** Rule 45b: "A split is settled only by a version that names both branches: a complete clone whose parent is the latest version of one branch, and which names, in field 26, the other branch's tip it settles." Law, "Open in this draft", readings taken writing F186 in: "A settling version naming a version this verifier does not hold: refused until it holds it." Rule 45: "a draft changes nothing."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:6783` (`deal_state`): once a version has two complete clones, the scan at `view.rs:6806` walks every terms act this verifier holds that carries a parent and field 26, for any deal. For each, `side(&par)?` (`view.rs:6813`) runs `lineage(par)`, which calls `terms(par)` and fails with `Missing` where the parent is not held; the `?` ends `deal_state` for the deal being read. Where the parent is held and lies on a branch, `view.rs:6814` returns `Unsettled` if field 26 names anything not held. Only after both does `agreement(x).exists` ask whether the version is complete. `latest_version`, `version_for`, `deal_fork`, and through them `purchase`, `split`, `release` and the alarm, all pass through `deal_state` for a forked deal.

**Story (a), one party.** Ana and Ben's deal has two branches, A and B (two devices out of step). Ana, in bad faith or by a careless client, signs a clone of A's tip whose field 26 names a hash nobody holds; Ben never signs it; it is a draft, and `agreement()` says so. From then on every verifier holding that draft answers, for the whole deal: "a version settles a branch of a deal's fork this verifier does not hold: it holds that version before answering". No version in force can be named, so no purchase, split or release under either branch can be judged, and the seller's alarm (`deal.ts`, the `lawDealFork` catch) fires on every payment with that sentence. Ben can do nothing about it: the draft is not his to withdraw, and "it holds that version before answering" waits for a version that does not exist. Reproduced: `version_in_force` and `deal_fork` both return that error after Ana's lone signature.

**Story (b), a stranger.** The same forked deal. Eve, no party to it, publishes a valid terms act of her own whose parent is a hash nobody holds and which carries field 26. The act passes the terms checks (field 26 needs only a parent and no key grammar). Every verifier holding Eve's act now answers `Missing(...)` for Ana and Ben's deal: the scan reached Eve's act, asked for its parent's lineage, and failed. Reproduced: `version_in_force` of the forked deal returns `Err(Missing(...))` once Eve's act is added, while an unforked deal is unaffected (the scan runs only where a version has two complete clones). One act, by anyone, and every forked deal held with it is unreadable until the act is dropped from the verifier, which MOR never does.

**Verdict.** BREAKS. The reading "refused until it holds it" was meant for a complete settling version; as built it is triggered by a draft, and the scan's reach is every terms act held, not the deal's. Field 26 opened this: before it, no field of one act could point a verifier at another deal's reading. The order should be: a version is looked at as settling only where it is a complete clone of a version of this deal (its parent held, in the deal's lineage, `exists`), and only then is what it names in field 26 read. A clone whose parent is not held is nobody's version yet. No DQ is touched: drafts and strangers' acts are not among the shapes DQ1 to DQ4 leave open.

## 3. The alarm is silent when the payment names a version the seller does not hold. BREAKS (client conformance, rule 45b; freeze 1.7a)

**Sentence.** Rule 45b, client conformance: "a seller's client, and a split service, MUST raise the alarm when a payment names a version of the deal that does not descend from the version they hold, showing both branches and pointing to settling the fork." Its reason, written beside it: "a verifier holding one branch only would see no fork at all."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:6907` (`fork_alarm`): `if self.v.get(&named).is_none_or(|l| !self.is_law(l, types::TERMS)) { return Ok(None); }`: a payment naming a version this verifier does not hold raises nothing. `clients/repo/src/deal.ts:89` (`fetchLine`) fetches the named version and its parents from the relays given as hints, and `deal.ts:150`: `if (!a0 || a0.collective) return null;`: where the named version was not found, no alarm. `clients/desk/src/desk.ts:563` shows the alarm only where `sellerAlarm` returns one; its catch covers an exception, not a null.

**Story.** Ana sells a song under a deal with Ben. From Ben's device, both sign version 3B; it reaches Ben's relay, not Ana's (Ana signed from the same device, careless, and her own desk reads her relays only; or Ben publishes 3B nowhere Ana looks). A fan pays Ana naming 3B. Ana's desk: `fetchLine(3B)` finds nothing at Ana's relays, `ag(3B)` is null, `sellerAlarm` returns null; the desk shows the payment with no alarm. A version Ana has never seen is, by the rule's own words, one that "does not descend from the version they hold"; the alarm's reason, "a verifier holding one branch only would see no fork at all", is this case; and the one way Ana could learn of 3B, the fan's payment, is the one the client swallows. The core's own test pins the three answers for held versions and never asks for an unheld one; mine asks, and gets `None`.

**Verdict.** BREAKS as a conformance rule: the alarm is right for every fork the seller can already see, and silent for the fork it exists to reveal. The core should answer with an alarm of its own kind for a version it does not hold ("names a version of the deal this verifier does not hold: fetch it from the payer's relays"), and the desk should show it, with the payer's hints as the place to look. The buyer's protection (A4) is untouched; only the sellers' bell is.

## 4. Settlement is not final: a discarded branch that forks, or settles again, unsettles the deal. BREAKS (core against rule 45b, A3); its second half touches DQ3

**Sentences.** Rule 45b: "Settlement is final (A3): the branch not chosen never comes back, whatever grows on it later; its versions stay shown." Freeze 1.7a: "Final: the second branch grows by two complete versions after it, and never comes back." Core v21: "Settlement is final: the other branch never comes back."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:6827` (`deal_state`, the branch loop): each branch is followed from the split to its latest version before the settling versions are weighed, and a branch with two complete clones of one version returns `Unsettled` ("a branch of a deal's fork that forks again", `view.rs:6845`). The loop does not know yet that the branch is discarded. Likewise two settling versions, one made after the first was in force, return `Unsettled` ("two versions settle one fork"); the build's own test `a_deals_fork_is_settled_by_a_version_naming_both_branches` (`core/tests/law_collective.rs:5435`) pins that reading.

**Story.** Ana and Ben's deal: branches A and B; a complete version on A names B's tip in field 26; the fork is settled, and that version is in force: the verifier says so. Later, every party signs two versions of B's tip (Ana steering Ben's careless client twice, or two devices again: the same precondition as F186 itself). The verifier now answers, for the whole deal, "a branch of a deal's fork that forks again: how it is counted is not decided". The settled version is no longer reported in force; nothing under the deal can be judged. Reproduced: `version_in_force` is the settling version after settlement, and `Err(Unsettled)` after the dead branch forks, while one complete version on the dead branch stays harmless. The text decided the opposite: what grows on the discarded branch is shown and changes nothing.

**Where it touches DQ3.** A second settling version made on the dead branch after the first was in force is "whatever grows on it later" too, and under the text it should settle nothing. As built, the verifier refuses the deal (the build's test shows it, calling it DQ3). DQ3 asks about two settling versions each complete; whether one made *after* a settlement is in force is such a race, or is growth on a dead branch, is for Nobody, allegedly, and I stop there.

**Verdict.** BREAKS for the fork on the dead branch: a shape the text decides (final) is refused as undecided, and it is reachable by the same hands that made the fork. The fix is in the order of the loop: find the settling version first; where one is in force, the discarded branch is not followed at all, and whatever grows on it, forks included, is shown and ignored. DQ1 ("a branch that forks again before the settlement") stays as it is; this is after.

## 5. A broken collective can be closed on the line before the broken act. CONFLICTS (rules 37d, 47a, RB6, against the core); touches RB2's "visible open obligation"

**Sentences.** Rule 37d: "A broken collective cannot fork or close before it is fixed (RB6): a fork or closing made during the broken stretch counts for nothing, then and after the rollback." Rule 47a: "A broken collective cannot fork or close before it is rolled back." Nobody, allegedly: "If they want to push an official act, they have to fix the issue first." Rule 47a, D: a closing "ends a collective that holds nothing and owes nothing".

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:5221` (`at_line`): an ending names any link of the collective's identity chain as its chain act (`position_of`, not the latest), and Law reads the agreement in force at *that* link (`base(col, bf)`). `closing_inner` (`view.rs:5706`) has no other look at the chain's state. The build's RB6 test (`core/tests/law_collective.rs:7790`) lines the closing at the stretch's binding, where `base` fails; it does not try the link before.

**Story.** Ana, Ben and Cy's collective breaks as on the Mac (Ben's resignation lost, a false mark). While broken, Ana drafts a closing whose chain act is the last good link and whose kept tip is the collective's tip at that link, naming the founding agreement; all three sign it by chain signatures. Law: the closing is complete and counts (`closed_by` names it) while `broken()` still answers the broken act's reason. Reproduced: `complete=true, counts=Some(true)`, and `broken=Some(...)` at once. The collective is closed without the rollback the decision requires, and after it no rollback is possible (its keys count for nothing). If payments were received during the stretch, they are owed back (RB2), but `open_debts` sees only obligations in the closing's history, and a refund owed under Finance rule 10a is no obligation act: "owes nothing" holds while the owed-back payments stand.

**What it does not do.** It takes every member whose voice remains, the same signatures a rollback needs, so it is no power gain for a faction: the "break and hold" cost stands as written. It is IT2b's stated cost, "a fork or closing drawn on an old line on purpose", meeting RB6. Also, by MOR's own logic, an act whose line is before the break is made before it, so the code is consistent with "Made before, made after"; it is the words of RB6 that promise more than lines can tell.

**Verdict.** CONFLICTS, medium: either RB6's text says "a fork or closing whose line lies in the broken stretch", with the old-line closing left as IT2b's cost, or the core refuses an ending whose chain act is not the collective's latest link while the collective is broken (one check in `at_line`, and a test). Whether the owed-back payments should block a closing is RB2's follow-up, and I stop there.

## 6. Field 26 names "the other branch's tip" in the rule, "any version of the other branch" in the reading and the code. UNCLEAR (rule 45b, 1.7a, against a reading to confirm)

**Sentences.** Rule 45b: "names, in field 26, the other branch's tip it settles." Freeze 1.7a: "names, in field 26, the second branch's latest version." The terms format, field 26: "the latest version of the other branch". Law, "Open in this draft", readings taken writing F186 in: "any version of the other branch settles the fork, the whole branch then discarded."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:6819`: `side(&other)? != Some(1 - i)` accepts any held version on the other branch, its tip or not.

**Story.** Branch B has grown to B3 (a new price, a new payee), and a sale under B3 is pending. Ana, in bad faith, drafts a settling version on A naming B1, the first version of B, in field 26, and shows Ben "this settles B1". Ben's client shows what it holds; if it holds B1 and not B3, nothing says the branch went on. Ben signs; the fork is settled; B3 and the sale under it are judged under the settling version. The decision chose (b) so that "every signer saw the choice": with the tip named, a signer who holds a shorter branch sees at once that something is missing; with any version allowed, the settling version can be true and incomplete at once.

**Verdict.** UNCLEAR, and listed among the readings for Nobody, allegedly, to confirm, so I do not decide it; I note that the rule's words and the format's comment say the tip, the reading and the code say any version, and that the bad-faith case the decision was made for lives in the difference. A verifier can check "the tip" only as far as it holds the branch, which is finding 2's reason to refuse a settling version naming a version it does not hold.

## 7. Field 26 is not mirrored in the act's citations, so a settled deal is refused by every verifier that fetches by citation. UNCLEAR (terms format; "A clone", `objects`)

**Sentences.** Terms format: "A clone ... names, in `objects`, the parent's chain and the act of that chain it follows: `[[parent, act]]`." Nothing names the settled branch there. Reading: "A settling version naming a version this verifier does not hold: refused until it holds it."

**Code.** `check_terms_inside` and `deal_state`: the settled version is read from field 26 only; `clients/repo/src/deal.ts:89` (`fetchLine`) follows field 11 (the parent) and nothing else; a split service's reading (`lawForkAlarm`, `lawDealFork`) would follow the same lineage.

**Story.** A verifier that is no party to the deal, a split service or a buyer's wallet, fetches the deal's versions by following parents from the version a payment names. It reaches the settling version and its line back to the root; it never reaches the discarded branch, which no citation names. `deal_state` finds the settling version, finds its field 26 unheld, and refuses the deal: "it holds that version before answering". For such a verifier every settled deal is unreadable until it happens to hold the dead branch. The desk escapes only because the seller signed both branches and `sellerAlarm` fetches every version the seller's own signature acts name.

**Verdict.** UNCLEAR, low weight on its own, higher with finding 2: either a settling version cites the settled version in `objects` too (Envelope's chains then carry it to every fetcher), or the reading says that a verifier not holding the settled version reads the settling version as in force on its own branch (its parent is a complete clone of a version of this deal, which the verifier does hold), refusing only what is judged under the other branch. For Nobody, allegedly.

## 8. "Always shown to that party": the client shows what it holds, and nothing makes a declaration reach the member. UNCLEAR (rule 52, RB3; rule 35a)

**Sentences.** Rule 52, client conformance: "a declaration naming a party MUST always be shown to that party, broken collective or not, with the way to contest it." Rule 35a: an act "in a collective's name ... is done ... only once it is sealed to every member". Declaration (type 13): "Signed by the authority the clause names, with its own key."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:1507` (`declarations_naming`) returns the declarations this verifier holds. `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:596` (`lawOf`) feeds the verifier with the acts of the collective, its members and its departed, fetched from the collective's hints. `rollback_departures` (`view.rs:1229`) refuses a rollback registering an act the verifier does not hold.

**Story.** Two of three members, the absence authority by threshold, declare Cy absent during a broken stretch and seal the declaration and their signature acts to each other alone, publishing them nowhere Cy's client looks; a declaration is the authority's act, not one in the collective's name, so rule 35a's "sealed to every member" does not reach it, and no rule says a declaration is public. They roll back, registering it. Cy's client: `declarations_naming(Cy)` is empty, so nothing is shown; `lawOf` reads the rollback as registering "an act this verifier does not hold", so Cy sees the collective still broken, with no rollback. Everyone the two share the declaration with sees a repaired collective without Cy. The attention risk RB3's client rule was written to close is closed only where the declaration reaches Cy's relays.

**Verdict.** UNCLEAR, low to medium. The client rule holds as a rule about what the client holds, and the verifier fails closed as it should; but the text's promise, "always shown to that party", needs a sentence saying how the declaration reaches the party: sealed to the party it names (Envelope `to`), or public, with a declaration that is neither counting for nothing where it is used. Not among BQ1 to BQ6; for Nobody, allegedly.

## What held, with what I tried

- **Complete a key grab by a rollback registering a declaration (RB3).** Two of three, the authority by threshold, break on purpose, declare the third absent during the stretch, and roll back registering the declaration with the other's signature act named beside it: the rollback completes without the third, as the build's test shows. Without the break the same two could draw a record registering the same declaration and refit; the break adds a rotation and a safety-key ceremony, not power. A declaration against the member whose stake the rollback's clone lowers stays a draft without their signature (`must_sign`). HOLDS, as the text reasons; finding 1 is the stale *resignation*, which is different: it needs no authority at all.
- **Undo a valid act by a rollback.** Unchanged since the previous review: the parent is fixed from the agreement in force at the broken act, a rollback where nothing is broken fails rule 37 and is itself a broken act, a rollback naming a later rotation of the stretch is refused. HOLDS.
- **Grants in the stretch (RB1).** A grantee's act citing only its grant during the stretch is not backed (`in_a_stretch`, `view.rs:1551`), and stays void after the rollback even when the collective acknowledges or cites it (`backing` asks `in_a_stretch` before the adoption paths; `consent`'s second judgment runs through the same `backing`). A revocation signed during the stretch does not count (`revocations` asks `consent`), so the grant works again after the rollback as RB1 says; a stretch rotation emptying an area freezes nothing (`freezes` asks `base`). A grant made during the stretch backs nothing then or after. HOLDS. What a grantee signed just before the break that no act of the collective joined is BQ1, and I stop there.
