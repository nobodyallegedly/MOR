# Hostile review of Finance rule 15: theft, anchor or bear the loss

*7 October 2026. Written for Nobody, allegedly, after the whole-set review of the same day (`docs/reviews/whole-set-review-2026-10-07.md`) and the decisions F176 to F178. One target: Finance draft 6, rule 15, with the clock declaration, rules 12a, 14 and 14a as they touch it, and the Envelope's anchoring task, read on main at `4938a17`, against F169 and F175 to F178 in `docs/findings/MOR-findings-log-round-2.md`. Nothing in `spec/` or the code was changed. The code still implements F139 and F146 (`core/src/finance.rs`, `good_faith`); nothing of F169 to F178 is built, so "as written" means the text.*

## In plain words first

The principle is now mostly delivered. The three doors the whole-set review found open are shut: the owner names the clock with the safety key, so a payer can no longer pick a reference the owner never used; the lock change counts from a home's anchored receipt, so an owner cannot anchor a rotation in a drawer; and a debtor who paid a deal the thief re-pointed pays once. I attacked the rule again from the four sides. What remains is smaller than before, and two of the findings are real.

**The payer can still choose the reference, inside the owner's clock.** The clock may name several references. If the owner anchors the lock change on one of them and a payer anchors its claim on another, the rule does not say which reference decides, and a payer can set the lock change's point late on the second reference by anchoring the home receipt there after its own claim. The fix is one sentence: a lock change counts as anchored only once a home receipt for it is anchored on every reference the clock names, and the owner's client is told to do that.

**The owner's own home sets the point before anyone can see the rotation.** The point is "the earliest anchored home receipt". With three homes under a majority rule, one of them the owner's, the owner's home can receipt the rotation on Monday and the public homes can receive it on Saturday. The rotation counts only on Saturday, and payers' clients saw the old vault all week, but the point is Monday. This is the drawer again, one step removed, and it is exactly how the author's own three homes are set up. The fix: the point is when the receipts that make the rotation count under its home rule are all anchored.

Beside these: the owner can un-receipt a payment by disowning its own receipt, so the stated lever is wider than "unreceipted"; a lock change's reach is defined in two sentences that do not agree; rule 12a still judges the rail Module against the chain as it stands now; the clock's entry has no room for the parameters Law's time reference carries; nothing tells the owner's client to anchor anything; and the core and the freeze suite still describe the rule as it was three decisions ago.

What held: everything the thief can reach with the signing key alone (the pointer, the deals, receipts, grants, acknowledgements) is shut out of the rule after an anchored lock change, and the thief cannot touch the clock, the vault or the point. The honest debtor pays once. The pre-written claim on an on-chain rail is bounded by the window. The rest is listed at the end.

## How findings are marked

- **BREAKS**: an attack works, or a common case does not work, under the text as written.
- **CONFLICTS**: two rules, or a rule and the suite, the core or the stated cost, disagree.
- **UNCLEAR**: the text can be read two ways, or a verifier cannot check it as written.
- **HOLDS**: I tried an attack and it failed; I say what I tried.

Each finding gives the exact sentence, the smallest story and a verdict. Ordered by weight.

## 1. Several declared references: the payer picks the one the owner did not anchor on. BREAKS (rule 15, the clock; payer's side)

**Sentences.**
- The clock: "`clock = [ FINANCE, 1, [+ hash] ]` ; the anchoring references the owner names".
- Rule 15: "Anchors are compared on the clock the payee's chain declared before the lock change (F176): a lock change counts as anchored only on a reference that clock names, and after it a claim anchored on none of them is not protected".
- Rule 15 (b): "the payer's claim is anchored before or at the lock change's point, each act judged by its earliest anchor (F178)".
- Rule 15: "The lock change's point is the earliest anchored home receipt for its rotation".
- Rule 15: "Anyone may anchor any act (F169)."

**The flaw.** The clock names one or more references. The rule says on which references a lock change and a claim may be anchored, and that a claim anchored on none of them is unprotected. It does not say what happens when the lock change is anchored on one declared reference and the claim on another, both declared. Then the lock change has no point on the claim's reference until someone anchors the home receipt there, and anyone may.

**Smallest story.**
1. Ana declares a clock naming R1 (a block height through one anchoring cMIP) and R2 (another). Her key is stolen; the thief publishes pointer v3. Ana rotates; her client anchors the home receipt on R1 within the hour.
2. Ben, working with the thief, pays his royalty to v3 after that, writes the claim and anchors it on R2 only. Then he anchors Ana's home receipt on R2.
3. On R2 both are anchored and Ben's claim is earlier: (b) is met. On R1 Ben's claim has no anchor, so nothing there says otherwise. The rule names no reference that decides. Ben's debt is discharged by paying the thief; Ana declared a clock and anchored, and bears.
4. The owner cannot prevent it by anchoring on R2 first only if the rule tells the owner's client to anchor on every declared reference; nothing does (finding 6).

**Smallest fix, for Nobody, allegedly to decide.** Either the clock names one reference, or: a lock change counts as anchored only once a home receipt for it is anchored on every reference the clock names, its point on each being the earliest anchor there; a claim then counts under (b) if it is before or at the point on any one declared reference it is anchored on. The owner's client anchors the home receipt on all of them (finding 6). With that, the payer's late anchor on R2 is never the earliest.

## 2. The owner's own home sets the point before the rotation counts. BREAKS (rule 15; owner's side and home operator's side)

**Sentences.**
- Rule 15: "The lock change's point is the earliest anchored home receipt for its rotation (Identity, "Receipt"; F177): a lock change counts from when a home publicly holds it, not from when it was signed, so one signed and kept back has no point yet".
- Identity rule 7: "A rotation counts once receipts from the homes required by the home rule show they hold it. Until then it is pending".
- Identity rule 10a: "A home MUST sign a receipt for every identity-chain act it accepts, and MUST serve its receipts with the chain."

**The flaw.** "Earliest anchored home receipt" is the receipt of any one declared home. Under a majority rule, one receipt makes nothing count and nothing public: payers' clients that consult the homes (Identity rule 14) still find the old chain at the other homes. A home the owner runs, or one that answers only the owner, gives the owner a point the week before the rotation is visible. F177's reasoning ("when the receiving has been confirmed") wanted the rotation's public moment; the sentence delivers one home's private moment.

**Smallest story (no theft).**
1. Ana's homes: her own machine at an onion address (allowlist, reachable by her alone, as the author's is) and two public homes; majority rule.
2. Monday: Ana submits a rotation setting the unit's limit to 0 to her own home only. It receipts it (rule 10a); her client anchors the receipt on the declared clock. The public homes hold nothing; the rotation is pending; the published vault still says 1,000.
3. Tuesday to Friday: debtors pay the flow, their wallets checking the public homes, writing claims and anchoring them within the hour.
4. Saturday: Ana submits the rotation to the public homes; it counts. Point: Monday. Every claim is after it; no receipt was signed. Every debt is open again; the money is in Ana's wallet.

**Smallest fix.** The point on a reference is the earliest at which the receipts that first make the rotation count under its home rule are all anchored (for a threshold of k, the k-th earliest anchor among its receipts' earliest anchors; for one home or an authoritative home, that home's receipt, whose key security Identity rule 10f already makes the owner's own). Say also that only a receipt passing Identity's receipt checks sets a point, and whether a receipt later voided by its operator's rotation, or from a home later proven dishonest, keeps the point it set; for an identity requiring audit, whether the point is the receipt's anchor or the cosigned summary's.

## 3. The owner can un-receipt a payment by disowning its own receipt. CONFLICTS with the stated cost (rule 15 (a); owner's side)

**Sentences.**
- Rule 15 (a): "the payee's own receipt shows it, a receipt on a line of the payee's that the rotation kept or signed with a key the payee's chain binds after it".
- Rule 15's italic: "Stated costs: the owner, by a lock change after receiving payments, can put in doubt every unreceipted payment it affects that the payer has not yet anchored".
- Identity rotation format, field 5: "disowned: acts within the kept ancestry the owner voids"; rule 16: "or within it but listed as disowned, it is void".

**The flaw.** The cost is stated for unreceipted payments. A receipted one is no safer: the rotation that lowers the limit can list the owner's own receipt as disowned, or leave out the sequence it sits on, and (a) no longer holds it. F178's sentence ("an act the rotation did not keep holds no pointer, signs no receipt") then applies to the owner's own receipt. The rule cannot tell this from an owner disowning a thief's receipt kept by mistake, so it cannot forbid it; it can state it.

**Smallest story.** Ana receives Ben's royalty of 900 and signs the receipt. A month later she rotates: limit 0 for the unit, the receipt disowned. Ben never anchored. (a) fails, (b) fails; Ben's debt is open; rule 10 still shows the money arrived. Visible forever on Ana's chain, as the stated lever is, and defeated by prompt anchoring, as it is.

**Verdict.** HOLDS as the bet the principle makes; CONFLICTS with the cost as written. State it at its size: every payment the lock change affects whose receipt the rotation did not keep and whose claim is not anchored before the point.

## 4. The lock change's reach is defined twice, differently. UNCLEAR (rule 15)

**Sentences.**
- The definition: "A lock change is a rotation after which a payment that followed the payee's chain as published before it no longer follows the chain as published after it: a voided pointer, a vault limit lowered or removed, an entry or source replaced (F178)."
- The reach: "Where it is anchored, a payment it affects (to a pointer it voids, or to the flow above a limit it sets) counts as made only if".
- "Where several anchored lock changes affect a payment, it counts only if the claim is anchored before the first of them (F175)."

**Three readings left open.**
- *A replaced source.* The definition's own example. A fan paid the old node's invoice on an outdated chain. The reach lists voided pointers and limits only; a payment to a replaced vault entry is in the definition and outside the reach. Is it protected under (a) or (b), or always made, or never?
- *A removed vault (a null declaration).* Under the definition, a payment to the vault before no longer follows the chain after: a lock change. Under the reach, nothing. The core says "a null value for kind 0 removes the vault" and rule 14a says the vault that applies is the one the chain declares now. Does a vault payment made before the removal need an anchor?
- *An unanchored lock change followed by an anchored rotation.* Ana has no clock, is robbed, rotates (lock change 1, unanchored: the owner bears, every payment to v3 counts). Rotation 1 declares a clock; rotation 2 follows, anchored, voiding nothing new. Does rotation 2 "affect" the payments to v3 (it is a rotation after which v3 is void) and reopen them against unanchored payers? Under the definition, no: those payments did not follow the chain as published immediately before rotation 2. Under "to a pointer it voids", a reader can say yes. The right answer is no, and it should be said: a lock change affects only payments that followed the chain as published immediately before it.

**Smallest fix.** One reach, from the definition: "a payment it affects, one that followed the chain as published immediately before it and no longer follows it after". Then every example in the definition is covered and the third reading is closed.

## 5. Rule 12a judges the rail Module against the chain as it stands now. CONFLICTS (rule 12a against rule 15)

**Sentence.** Rule 12a: "A receipt or claim counts only if the rail Module its field 0 names is the one named by the rail of the payee's pointer, or the entry of the payee's vault, that the payment was paid to, that pointer being the payee's own and counting for that payment by rules 12 and 14, and that vault being the payee's, as its chain declares it (rule 14a; F164, withdrawing F160)".

**The flaw.** Rule 15 selects the version a protected payment was paid under "from the payee's acts as they stood before the lock change". Rule 12a's pointer clause may follow that through "rules 12 and 14"; its vault clause does not: "as its chain declares it" is the chain after the lock change.

**Smallest story.** Ana's vault entry for satoshis on the Lightning Module is replaced, in a lock change, by an entry on an on-chain Module. A fan paid the old entry's invoice, claim anchored before the point: rule 15 counts it. Rule 12a: the Lightning Module is not the one named by any entry of the vault as the chain declares it; the claim "counts for nothing, whatever its rail's proof shows". The two rules give opposite answers. The citation "F164, withdrawing F160" is also stale (whole-set review, finding 10; not landed).

**Smallest fix.** Rule 12a reads the pointer and the vault as rule 15 selects them for that payment.

## 6. Nobody is told to anchor the home receipt, or to declare a clock. UNCLEAR (rule 15, client conformance; owner's side)

**Sentences.**
- Rule 15: "A payer's client MUST read the payee's clock before paying, as it reads the vault (rule 14a). Anyone may anchor any act (F169). A payer's client MUST write the payer's claim at the moment it pays and SHOULD anchor it promptly".
- The italic: "The owner bears what the stolen key did until the lock change, and reports it by anchoring".

**The gap.** Every conformance line is the payer's. The owner's half of "anchor or bear" has no actor: nothing says the owner's client fetches the home receipts for a rotation and anchors them, on every reference the clock names (finding 1), nor that an owner's client warns an owner who declares no clock that it has no protection, as rule 14b warns about the vault. An owner who declares a clock at genesis and whose client never anchors a receipt has the cost of a clock and the protection of none, and does not know it.

**Smallest fix.** One client-conformance line for the owner's client (after a lock change, obtain and anchor its home receipts on every declared reference), and one rule 14b bullet (before genesis or a rotation that declares no clock, say plainly that a theft's loss will be the owner's whatever it anchors).

## 7. The clock's entry cannot name what Law's time reference names. UNCLEAR (the clock format against Law terms field 6)

**Sentences.**
- The clock: "`[+ hash]` ; the anchoring references the owner names, each an anchoring cMIP and its time reference".
- Law draft 10, terms: "`? 6 => [ hash, any ]` ; time reference: the task cMIP and its parameters".
- Envelope, Tasks: "Two anchors are compared only on the same reference".

**The gap.** One hash names a cMIP. Where an anchoring cMIP serves more than one reference (the same timestamping scheme over a main chain and a test chain), the clock cannot say which, and "the same reference" cannot be checked from the declaration. Law gives its time reference parameters for the same reason.

**Smallest fix.** Mirror Law: `[+ [ hash, any ]]`, or state that an anchoring cMIP names exactly one reference, so the hash is the reference.

## 8. The core and the freeze suite describe the rule three decisions ago. CONFLICTS (editorial)

- Core v21, "Good faith": "counts as made until the owner anchors the lock change (a rotation voiding a pointer or lowering a limit); after it, only where the payee's own receipt or a payer's claim anchored earlier shows it". No clock (F176), the rotation's anchor rather than a home receipt's (F177), the field definition rather than the effect (F178), "earlier" rather than "before or at".
- Core v21, "Flow and vault": "cannot collect older obligations after the owner's lock change" still drops "anchored" (whole-set review, finding 10, listed under F178 and not landed).
- Freeze suite 5c: "the contributor rotates and anchors the rotation: the fan's tip counts as made only if the fan's client anchored the claim before it". The contributor declares no clock in the scenario, so under rule 15 the lock change counts as not anchored and the tip counts; the anchor is the rotation's, not a home receipt's; "before" not "before or at".
- Finance's own scenario list: "Good-faith payment to an invalidated pointer counts as made; a payment that ignored the vault is not protected: 1" names neither the clock nor the point.
- Rule 15's opening, "Where the lock change is not anchored", reads as if anchoring the rotation were enough; what "anchored" now means (a home receipt for it, on the clock) arrives five sentences later. One clause at the first use would spare a builder the wrong reading.

## 9. The thief races the point's coarseness. HOLDS, one tick

**Sentence.** Rule 15 (b): "before or at the lock change's point".

**What I tried.** The thief watches the homes. The moment a home serves the receipt, accomplices anchor prepared claims; on a coarse reference they land in the same block as the owner's anchor, and "at" protects them. The gain is one tick of the reference, after a window the owner bears anyway, and every such claim is visible. Bounded; the price of F178 item 6, which is right for the honest payer. HOLDS.

## 10. A claim written before the money moves. HOLDS under the principle; one note for the on-chain Module

**Sentences.**
- Rule 15: "A payer's client MUST write the payer's claim at the moment it pays"; the italic: "only an anchor obtained at payment proves which side of the lock change a payment fell on".
- Lightning rail Module draft 2: "`ln-proof = [ invoice, ? preimage ]`"; "Pending if the proof carries no preimage"; "The payer alone cannot produce a valid proof".

**What I tried.** Anchor the claim first, pay later. On Lightning a valid claim carries the preimage, which the payer learns by paying, so the claim that counts (rule 4) cannot exist before the payment; a thief's node handing out preimages to accomplices is collusion inside the window, the owner's stated cost. On an on-chain rail the transaction id is known before broadcast: a claim can be written and anchored in the window and the transaction sent after the lock change, and it counts. Only a colluder wants to pay a thief, and the anchor sits in the window, so the principle covers it. The italic overstates by one word: an anchor proves which side the *claim* fell on. The on-chain Module (roadmap step 12a) should say that a claim is valid only once the transaction is confirmed, and that its anchor says when the claim existed, not when the money moved.

## What held, in one list

- **The thief's side.** With the signing key alone the thief cannot declare or remove the clock, change the vault or move the point: all three need the safety key or a home's receipt. The thief's receipts, re-pointing acts, grants to an accomplice service and pointers are outside the kept ancestry after the rotation and hold nothing for money whatever acknowledgement keeps them visible (F178 items 3 and 4); "a grant key whose grant still stands" shuts the accomplice service. A thief's new sequence is outside by Identity rule 17. The thief cannot make the point later; anyone's anchor can only make it earlier.
- **The payer's side.** The honest debtor of a deal re-pointed in the window pays once: the version is selected from the acts as they stood before the lock change (F178 item 3). A payer on a conversion route is protected by rule 9: the last hop's payer is the conversion service, and a hop it does not anchor stays its open obligation. A refund to a bare key is outside the rule (rule 7, F174). A payer's claim judged by its earliest anchor cannot be moved later by anyone else.
- **The owner's side.** A rotation signed and kept back has no point (F177), except through an owner-run home (finding 2). A clock declared in the lock change itself does not serve it, and an owner with no clock cannot buy protection for a past theft by declaring one afterwards: right, since no payer could have known where to anchor. The effect definition covers a first vault declaration, an added entry with a lower limit and a replaced source, and leaves out a rotation that only raises a limit.
- **The home operator's side.** A home cannot sign a receipt for an act it does not hold (receipt check 1), so it cannot predate a rotation it never saw. A slow home under a majority rule is outvoted by the others; a single or authoritative home's delay is the cost Identity rule 10f already states. A thief's accomplice home gains nothing: a late receipt helps payers, not the thief.
- **Finance-only clients.** Rule 15 (a)'s grant-key branch and rule 14's walk through acts on an agreement need Law; rule 1 already shows such receipts as unknown in a Finance-only client, so no new dependency is created.
- **The unanchored lock change.** A payment to the thief's wallet a year later, on an outdated chain, counts where the owner never anchored or declared no clock. That is the principle, chosen by the owner, and the rule says so.

## Does the rule deliver its principle?

- *The owner bears what the stolen key did until the lock change.* Yes, as written, and nothing in the window is reopened by a later rotation if finding 4's third reading is closed.
- *After it, anchor or bear the loss.* Yes for the payer, once finding 1 closes the second reference; no for the owner until finding 6 gives the owner's client something to do.
- *Name your services, name your clock.* The clock is named by the right party with the right key. It needs one reference or an all-references rule (finding 1), and parameters (finding 7).
- *The locks count as changed when a home's receipt is anchored.* Delivered for identities whose homes are not their own; not for the author's own setup (finding 2), which is where it will first be run.
