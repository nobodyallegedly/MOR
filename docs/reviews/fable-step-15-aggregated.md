## In plain words first

Thirty-seven decisions written into six texts at once is where a protocol usually comes apart: one draft says what another no longer has, a word means two things, a rule leans on a fact the layer below stopped guaranteeing. I read for that first. The drafts hold together better than I expected: the three decisions that cross layers (F297 into Identity and Agreements, F298 into Identity, Agreements and Development, F301 into Money and Development) are written on both sides and point at each other; the MICS v28 summarises each draft truthfully, F317's cost and F314's limit included; the verdict words have not grown by instance, they have been *reduced* to one pattern (unknown where the verifier cannot tell) and one family (no line, no fork, no specification: "an act that claims to be X and fails X's conditions is no X").

Two things did come apart, and both are the same seam. **Keepers (G2).** Identity now says every other identity's evidence, "a keeper record" named, counts only while its signer's chain keeps it (F297). Agreements still lets a keeper's record *place* a collective's act before a line ("Made before, made after", 5), lets a keeper's record keep a deal's version complete (F314), and orders a deal's declaration by what "a keeper recorded" (rule 89). None of those is covered by F302, which keeps only what a *line* placed, and a keeper's record is nothing a line names. So a keeper's rotation can un-place an act, flip the tie rule, uncomplete a version: the hole F302 closed for members' acts, open for keepers'. In MOR 1 this bites nobody, because the keeper record (type 2) has no format and no client makes one; but the text promises what the text then lets be undone. Either F302's sentence names keeper records, or MOR 1 says plainly that keepers place nothing until type 2 exists. **Prices (G1).** F310 made selling or releasing a work an owners' matter, and rule 55 still says a release is "like setting a price to zero". Setting a price is operational: the members offer every work at a token price, or at nothing, and the owners' 25% is 25% of nothing again, by the one door the owners' matters do not name. This cannot be an owners' matter (owners cannot vote every price), so it must be a stated cost, under rule 95, where the reader who was just promised the works are protected will look.

Draft 14's new language, attacked as draft 13 was, holds at the pattern level: the holders' rule reaches only what hits every holder alike; a thief's vote can pass only what hits the thief's victim alike; the roster is one line's answer; a proxy can add a voice and never give away what is the owner's alone. Three edges need a sentence each: an issuance take-up placed only by the collective's line hands the members a lever to dilute an owner who paid (G3); "sealed to a holder that is a deal" has no moment at which the deal's version in force is judged (G4); and the proxy's "the holder signed nothing on that version" must say "placed before that line" or the owner's own late vote beats nothing (G5).

On the MICS's test: every rule I read sits on one side of the line, with two that pretend otherwise, named in G7. On the suite: every decision but three has a scenario; all thirty-one new steps are reasoned until draft 14's code exists, and the ones that could be run before the freeze are the ones that do not wait on it. On readiness, in one sentence: the way out is right on paper (A2-6/F313, F303, F305, F317 and the fence), nothing I found needs a new decision beyond G1 to G4, and the freeze-ready drafts should wait for the code of F292 to F328 and one run of every scenario that code can run, because the last two rounds showed that writing finds what reading cannot.

## Findings

Severity: **breaks a guarantee** / **leaves a gap** / **wording**. Kind: **text** / **instrument**. Pattern: one of the four of round 2, or "fits none", named.

### G1. The owners' matters do not name prices, and rule 55 says a release is a price of zero. Text (Agreements 14, rules 55 and 95). Leaves a gap: a stated cost to state. Pattern: a rule that protects the owner has a door beside it.

**Sentences.** Rule 95: a stake's owners' matters are what it is paid, an issuance, "a collective's transfer or release of a stake it holds in a work", and a fork's successor plans; "*Without it, the members sell the works for a token sum ... and a departed holder's 25% ... is '25% of nothing', unsigned.*" Rule 55's commentary: "Releasing is a business decision of the collective, like setting a price to zero". Standing offer (type 6): made by the collective under its lanes, its price one amount, withdrawn by a later version.

**The attack.** The members found a second collective of their own; the original offers each work's publications at one satoshi, or publishes them at no price, or sells a proven delivery under a cMIP at nothing; no stake moves, no release is signed, every holder is paid alike (a share of one satoshi), and the works' income is gone to wherever the members now sell. Rule 95 reaches neither pricing nor publication. Nor can it: owners cannot consent to every price, and the core cannot judge one (F315). This is the sale F310 closed, through the door rule 55's own words point at.

**Smallest fix.** A stated cost under rule 95, beside the sale: *the price of what the collective sells, and whether it publishes at all, are the collective's business under its lanes; owners who have no say are protected by sight (rule 57, F318) and the exit, never by a vote on prices. "Like setting a price to zero" (rule 55) is why a release needs the owners and a price does not: a release ends the claim for good, a price can be raised again.* And rule 55's commentary reworded so it no longer equates the two. One sentence; the reader of rule 95 deserves it where the promise is made. (Q1.)

### G2. Keepers' records under F297: Identity disowns them, Agreements still places by them, F302 does not keep them. Text (Identity 13 rule 34's F297 paragraph; Agreements 14 "Made before, made after" 5, rules 47, 89, F314). Breaks a guarantee (what the collective's line placed stays placed; what others relied on stands). Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party.

**Sentences.** Identity 13: "An act of another identity that a MIR uses as evidence (a receipt, an objection, an absence statement, a cosignature, an acknowledgement) counts only while its signer's chain keeps it valid"; and, two paragraphs up, "A keeper record or acknowledgement counts only alongside the act it names ... and only while its own signer's chain keeps it valid (F297)". Agreements 14, point 5: "The collective's keepers place its own acts a line left out ... when the keepers ... recorded it before recording the line"; rule 89: in a deal, "before then" means "by signatures a keeper recorded before recording the declaration"; rule 47 (F314): a deal's version stays complete "once any party's or keeper's later act relies on it". F302: "an act of another identity that a line of a collective names, registers, or relies on to count what it registers keeps ... the effect the line gave it".

**So.** A keeper's record is not named, registered or relied on *by a line*; it is relied on by a rule to place an act *against* a line. Under F297 a keeper's rotation disowning its record makes the record decide nothing: the act it placed before the line is now after it; the tie rule voids a receipt that counted; a deal's declaration no longer comes after the signatures it came after; a version F314 kept complete through a keeper's record is uncomplete again. Nothing in draft 14 says otherwise, and the code today reads keepers' logs as a fact the caller states, with no act to disown.

**Smallest fix, two readings.** (a) F302's sentence gains "and a keeper's record that a rule of this MIR reads to place an act, or that a version's completeness relies on (rule 47), keeps the effect the rule gave it, whatever the keeper's later rotation disowned; shown as a dispute". It is the same principle: a keeper is a witness the parties chose in advance ("Keepers are chosen up front"), and its record, once used, is used. (b) MOR 1 says plainly, in rules 37 to 44 and point 5, that keepers place nothing and keep nothing complete until the keeper record (type 2) has a format, which is MOR 2's, and the suite marks every keeper step reasoned. Recommendation: (a), one sentence, because the texts promise it now and F314 was decided on it. (Q2.)

### G3. An issuance take-up placed only by the collective's line hands the members a lever to dilute an owner who paid. Text (Agreements 14, rule 96). Leaves a gap. Pattern: a protection that traps the owner.

**Sentences.** Rule 96: "A holder takes up by a signature act naming the offer, within its period, placed by the collective's line as a holder's vote is (rule 94), with its payment where the offer names a price"; the issuance is complete when "for every holder ... the offer reached it, and it either took up its proportion within its period, or its period lapsed". Rule 94's stated cost (F316): "members can delay a hostile buyer's vote by not placing it, visibly, bounded by the next record any member draws."

**The attack.** The offer reaches Dana; she signs her take-up and pays within the period; the members draw no record naming it until the time reference says the period lapsed; her take-up is placed after the lapse, or never; the issuance completes with her proportion gone to the new holders, and her payment sits as a receipt the collective owes back (Money rules 17 to 19) while her share is diluted. F316's bound ("the next record any member draws") is no bound here, because the members are the ones issuing, and the period, not a record, closes the door. For a vote the cost is a delay; for a take-up it is the dilution pre-emption exists to prevent.

**Smallest fix.** One sentence in rule 96, the shape rule 10 already gives a buyer ("the buyer's protection is to anchor the transfer"): *a take-up that the agreement's time reference places within the period counts, placed by the collective's line or not; a holder's protection is to anchor it (Envelopes, anchoring; task 11).* Checkable from the anchor and the offer's period, with the time reference's answer as for the period's lapse; two verifiers with the same anchors agree. Cost: a holder with no anchoring cMIP has only the line's placement, stated. (Q3.)

### G4. "Sealed to a holder that is an agreement" has no moment at which the agreement's version in force is read. Text (Agreements 14, rules 57 and 96; F328 Q13). Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check (which version a deal is on, with no line).

**Sentences.** F328 Q13: sealed "to every party of its version in force; for a collective holder, to the collective". `docs/drafts-after-step-15.md` names the point itself: "a deal has no line (F314), so a verifier cannot place the moment exactly."

**Why it matters.** An issuance offer sealed to a deal's parties "in force" where the deal forked, or where a version completed between the offer and its placement, reaches a different set of parties for two verifiers holding different versions: one says the offer reached the holder, the other that it did not, and the issuance is complete for one and a draft for the other. The same for a Money act under F318.

**Smallest fix.** Make the moment the one Agreements already has for a deal: the version in force *at the act that relies on it* (F314's shape), here the offer or the Money act itself, which names the agreement in `objects` with the version it saw; an act sealed to every party of the version it names has reached the holder, whatever version the deal is on later. Or, simpler and sufficient for MOR 1: where any holder of the stake is an agreement, the offer and the collective's Money acts are public, publicity reaching everyone. Recommendation: the second as the rule, the first as what "sealed" means where a collective chooses to seal. (Q4.)

### G5. The proxy's "the holder signed nothing on that version" needs a place. Text (Agreements 14, rule 97). Wording.

Rule 97: the proxy's signature counts where, among other things, "the holder signed nothing on that version: the owner's own vote always beats the proxy's". Votes count only where a line placed them (rule 94); a holder's own signature act placed after the line that put the version in force counts nothing at that line, so it beats nothing, and the proxy's vote stands. Either the sentence reads "the holder signed nothing on that version that the collective's line placed at or before the line putting the version in force", or the promise "always beats" is a line shorter than it reads. One clause.

## The verdict vocabulary, once, for the whole protocol

The plan counted about sixteen. The six drafts produce these, grouped by what they are; the test for each is whether two verifiers holding the same acts must agree, and whether a module could reach it another way.

**Verdicts on an act, the core's (every layer):** *valid*; *invalid* (a rule the verifier can apply fails; beats unknown); *unknown* (the verifier lacks what a rule needs, or does not implement the specification: F298, F295, F323, rule 30); *void* (outside every kept line of a rotation, rebuilt: Identity rules 15 to 17, F298); *pending* (waiting on something outside the act: a rotation not yet counting, a rail's answer, a condition; three layers, one meaning, G6); *scoped* (signed with a key a higher MIR installs; counts as that MIR says). Six, all pass the test.

**Verdicts on an act that claims a role (Agreements, Development):** *draft / complete / in force / not in force* (a version); *done / not done* (rule 57); *uncited* (rule 58); *counts / counts for nothing* (the umbrella, G6); *backed / not backed* (a grant key's act); *adopted* (rule 76); *no line, no fork, no closing, no ending, no declaration, no specification* (one family, G6); *incomplete / complete and counting for nothing* (an ending, F143, F161); *broken* (a collective, rule 93); *spent* (a resignation); *frozen* (an area); *withdrawn* (an offer); *settled / on its reference* (a deal's fork); *purchase / no purchase / owed back / repaid / ended* (Money and Agreements on a payment); *open / discharged / released* (an obligation); *unclaimed* (a specification, rule 3). Each is decided from acts a verifier holds and names, and none can be reached by a module around the core (a module cannot make a clone complete or an ending final). They pass.

**Records shown, not verdicts (every layer):** *disputed* (void but relied on: Identity rule 34; a line's placed act whose signer disowned it: F302; a receiver's contrary receipt: Money rule 11); *contested* (Identity's four nouns; a sale under a vow); *creator mismatch* (rule 25); *wrong receipt* (Money rule 18: counts for nothing, shown); *bypassing*, *mismatch* (the pointer check, the split check); *not found* (a listing); *deferred* (the suite). These decide nothing and are displays; the drafts say so for most; the MICS should say so once.

**Growth since the plan:** by pattern. *Unknown* was generalised (F298) and *invalid* bounded (F295, T3), which removed verdicts rather than adding them (the library's "void" and "invalid" on partial knowledge are gone); *no specification* and *unclaimed* joined the "no X" family and the records; *pending* replaced "unrecorded" (M3). Draft 14's language adds **no verdict**: a holders' rule met is "complete", an issuance is a version, an owners' matter is a power in the mark, a proxy's vote is a signature act that counts or not. The one place the vocabulary is still by instance is the six nouns of "no X", which are one verdict with six names; naming the verdict once (G6) ends it.

## What held under attack

- F302 against a holder's vote, a resignation, a declaration: the line names what it placed and keeps it; the dispute is shown.
- The holders' rule against singling out: a change that treats one holder unlike another still needs that holder; a thief's stolen vote passes only what hits the thief's victim alike.
- The roster against two verifiers: one line's answer; a seller and a buyer never both count; a placed transfer not held is unknown.
- Pre-emption against a majority issuing to itself: every holder's proportion on the same terms, the offer an act that must reach each; what it cannot do is stated (F315).
- The fence against a proxy running wild: votes only, majority matters only, never the owner's money or property, no sub-proxies.
- The broken collective against operators rotating around an owners' constitution; and the stated cost where they refuse to rotate (F317).
- Endings against another chain's future: successors and members sign by chain key; a closing relies on a release the collective acknowledged; kept holders' consent is acknowledged in the fork's history.
- The way out for an owner who vanished (F313), an identity contested at a self-host (F299), a successor who never confirmed (F300, F305).
- The root (F324): six texts, recomputed; the repository as canonical text.
- The MICS's summary of every draft: truthful, F317's cost and F314's limit included.

## Questions for Nobody, allegedly, one at a time

**Q1 (G1). Core business, a stated cost.** Does rule 95 state that the price of what a collective sells, and whether it publishes, are the collective's business under its lanes, the owners protected by sight and the exit, never by a vote on prices; and does rule 55 stop saying a release is "like setting a price to zero"? Recommendation: yes; the door is real, cannot be an owners' matter, and should be named where the promise is made.

**Q2 (G2). Core business.** Does F302 extend to a keeper's record that a rule reads to place an act or to keep a version complete (F314), the record keeping its effect whatever the keeper's later rotation disowned, shown as a dispute; or does MOR 1 say plainly that keepers place nothing and keep nothing until the keeper record has a format? Recommendation: the first, one sentence; the texts promise it now, and a witness chosen in advance should not be able to unwitness.

**Q3 (G3). Core business.** Does a take-up of an issuance that the agreement's time reference places within the period count, placed by the collective's line or not, a holder's protection being to anchor it, as a buyer anchors a transfer? Recommendation: yes; without it the members issuing are the ones who decide whether an owner's paid take-up is placed in time.

**Q4 (G4). Core business.** Where a holder of a stake is itself an agreement, are the issuance offer and the collective's Money acts public (publicity reaching everyone), "sealed to every party of its version in force" meaning the version the act itself names where a collective seals? Recommendation: yes; a deal has no line, so "in force" needs the act's own naming to be one answer for every verifier.

**Q5 (G6, G8). Recommendation: yes to all; it is the vocabulary's last tidying before it freezes.

**Q6 (G9, G10). Core business, the freeze.** Do the freeze-ready drafts wait for the code of F292 to F328 and one run of every scenario that code can run, the texts corrected by what the build finds, rather than being written now and corrected after?
