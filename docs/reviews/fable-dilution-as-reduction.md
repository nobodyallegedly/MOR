# Dilution as a reduction: issuance under rule 48

*Fable, 4 November 2026. Input: pull request 121 (`claude/s7m-the-trim`), `docs/s7m-3-report.md` (the third hostile reading, its three questions); draft 18's rules 48, 67, 98 (98.2) and 99; the default cMIP's rule 96; F421 and F422; my `fable-s7m-constitution.md`. Read, not run; a recommendation, nothing decided.*

## In plain words

The proposal is right and it is simpler than what it replaces, mine included. Pre-emption was one way of collecting a holder's consent to being diluted, and I wrote it into the core as if it were the consent itself; the stopwatch attack shows the difference. The guarantee was already in rule 48: my share shrinks only with my say, given directly or under a rule I accepted. An issuance is a reduction of every holder's share, so rule 48 judges it, and how consent is collected (first refusal, a period, a price) is a model's way of collecting it, with every holder signing as the default when no model defines one. That is watertight for the percentage. It is not, and cannot be, watertight for the value: a holder who accepted a rule allowing issuance at any price accepted that too, and the core's job there is to make sure they were shown it. The generalisation holds and should be written down as the trim's test: a safe default in the core, explicit choices above it, and whenever the core carries a mechanism, ask whether it is the guarantee or one way of meeting it.

## 1. Is it watertight? The paths, one by one

The guarantee: a stake moves, shrinks or is paid less only with its holder, by signature or under a rule that holder accepted by signing a version carrying it or by buying under it (rules 48, 98.2). An issuance is a reduction of every holder's share. Each path:

1. **Issuance under a holders' rule changed later.** A change of the rule binds no holder without its signature (rule 45, F422 question 4). A holder who did not sign the new rule is bound by the old; an issuance made under the new one does not bind them, so it cannot reduce them, so it counts for nothing (an issuance reduces every holder or none). **Closed.**
2. **A departed founder bound by a rule they were not shown.** They signed the founding that carried it; acceptance is the signature, and "shown" is the client's duty. **Closed by rule 48 plus the conformance line**: at founding, the client shows every holders' rule and every term under which the stake may be issued, Money first. Without the line, the rule holds and the founder's complaint is with their client, which is the stated cost; with it, there is no complaint.
3. **A buyer bound by a rule changed between signing and placement.** F421's question 11 sentence, in rule 48 now. **Closed.**
4. **A stake split or converted rather than issued.** A new claim on the same income (a second stake on the collective itself, a fee, a partner's share, a role share) is "paid less" under rule 48's effect test, and a holder with no voice signs it. A change of field 7 by any route but a placed transfer, a succession, an absence outcome or a consented issuance is a false mark (rule 46). **Closed by rule 48 as written, with one sentence to make the field 7 path explicit** (below, under 3).
5. **Value diluted, not percentage: a cheap issuance to an insider.** Not closed, and not closable by the core without pricing, which is a model's. A holder who accepted a rule allowing issuance at a price the rule sets, or none, accepted that; a holder bound by no such rule signs each issuance and refuses a cheap one. The core's guarantee is the say, not the price. **A stated cost, in the words "you got to define it"**: a holders' rule that allows issuance without naming how the price and the offer are set is a rule the client shows as such, Money first, and the default rule, every holder signs, lets no cheap issuance pass anyone.
6. **The stopwatch** (a one-block period at a huge price): gone with pre-emption from the core; under a model that offers first refusal, the model's own period is its business and its holders accepted it; under none, every holder signs. **Closed.**

So the percentage is watertight; the value is a holder's own choice at acceptance, shown.

## 2. What leaves the core with pre-emption, and what stays

**Leaves:** rule 98.2's pre-emption sentence ("first offered to every holder in proportion, at the same price, for the same period"); the definitions S7m-3 wrote for it, "reached" (public or sealed to the holder), the period on the time reference, and a take-up placed or anchored; rule 8's exception "an issuance a control model allows"; the default cMIP keeps all of it as its rule 96, as its way of collecting the consent.

**Stays**, as the guarantee: *An issuance of a stake is a reduction of every holder's share, and counts only as rule 48 lets a stake shrink: each holder signs it, or it is made under a holders' rule that holder accepted, by signing a version carrying it or by buying under it; a holder bound by no such rule signs; where the terms name no rule, every holder signs. Placed by the collective's line, it changes field 7; a change of field 7 by any other act than a placed transfer (rule 10), a succession (rule 83), an absence outcome (rule 89) or such an issuance is a false mark (rule 46).* The parts a model adds (first refusal, periods, prices) are its terms, and the client shows them.

## 3. The generalisation, applied

"A safe default in the core, explicit choices above it": for each of the third reading's remaining items, whether it is a guarantee (the core, whatever the model), a default (the core, until the founding chose otherwise), or a model's.

- **An area narrowing Money to one member.** A model's, by the founding's explicit choice: field 35 and its parameters are constitutional (F421), so the founders signed the areas. The **default** is the layer's rule (F402), which no area may widen beyond; the **guarantee** is the warning: *client conformance at founding: every holders' rule, every issuance term and every area, Money first, with a warning where a model's area lets one member act alone in Money.* "Narrowing" defined in one clause: an area names, among the layer's actors, who acts for the acts it reaches, and never a rule the layer's rule would not meet with the same signatures plus others; it takes acts out of the layer's rule, not members out of the layer.
- **Placement and adoption through model-governed acts** (record field 3's "an act a model asks a line to place"; an area holder's acknowledgement adopting a grantee's act). A model's, for what the model governs; a **guarantee** for the way out: *the way out, and whether a collective is broken, read placement from the core's own acts alone (records, constitutional clones, resignations, declarations, chain signatures); an act a model asked the line to place, or an adoption under a model's power, places and adopts only for what that model governs.* Two verifiers can then disagree on a model-governed adoption, which is the stated cost of unknown, and never on when a departure took effect.
- **The way out reading parameters only a model understands** (stepping down from an area id; a succession clone "out of every area it held"; a refit's area entry). As the MOR lead would settle it, and F422's question 6 already does: the way out takes effect on what the core alone reads (the member out, the seat passed, the voice gone); its consequences for areas are the model's, read when the model is held, unknown otherwise, and never a condition of the exit. **Guarantee**, one sentence: *no act of the way out waits on a model's answer.*
- **Field 7 changed by operational clones.** **Guarantee**, the sentence under 2: field 7 changes only by the four placed acts; a clone changing it otherwise is a false mark; the way out and a rollback read field 7 as the latest constitutional clone's, updated by those placed acts since. That also answers "which clone a rollback copies": the constitutional one, with the placed acts applied.
- **The default cMIP's parameters 30 and 32.** Parameter 30 ("every holder signs" where a stake names no rule) is the core's **default** restated and should say so, adding nothing to a constitutional change; parameter 32 (what a stake issues under) is a **model's** way of collecting the consent rule 48 asks, and its "every issuance is constitutional" line goes, since an issuance is a reduction judged by rule 48, not a change of the constitution.
