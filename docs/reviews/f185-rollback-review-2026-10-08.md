## In plain words first

The fix for the false mark holds where it was aimed: the collective client now asks Law who counts before it signs a change, asks again before the clone and again before the rotation, and refuses rather than publish a mark Law would call false. I could not make the page sign a change Law refuses. The core's reading of a broken stretch is careful: records drawn in it register nothing, rotations in it place no signature, and a rollback that names the wrong act, too few voices, a clone of the broken clone, or a declaration of absence puts nothing in force. A rollback cannot undo a valid act: its parent is fixed by Law from what was in force at the broken act, and an attempt on a working collective breaks it visibly, as F185 decided.

What I found is in the repair path and in the leaving path, which were built today on top of the fix and did not get the same care.

1. **The rollback can hand a share of the new safety key to a member who left.** The client rebuilds the agreement from the rules it kept on this device when that agreement came into force. A member whose resignation the collective registered after that, before the broken act, is still in that list. The rollback keeps them as a party, names them in the key grammar and deals them a share. The ordinary member-change review refuses exactly this ("would deal them a share of the new safety key"); the rollback review does not. Rule 37's one promise about keys, "keys the departing member never held", is broken by the repair.
2. **The rollback can fail for the same reason the collective broke, and the client then says it worked.** The rollback's two checks cover the mark and the clone, not the resignations it registers. If a resignation signed during the broken stretch never reached the relays, Law refuses the whole rollback ("registers an act this verifier does not hold"), the rotation still moves the keys, and the client, counting home receipts, writes "The collective works again."
3. **The last voice is not warned when the voices leave from separate devices.** The warning is computed from Law's count, and Law does not count a resignation until a line registers it. In a broken stretch no line is drawn, so the other members' resignations are invisible to Law and the client reads them only from its own file. Four members on four devices can each resign, each told the three others remain, and nobody is warned. Narrower, the same race exists in a working collective between two devices.
4. **Leaving while Law cannot be read signs what Law may refuse, and gives no warning.** With the homes away, or a collective broken with no way back, the client names this device's copy of the agreement in the resignation (on the Mac's old collective, the broken clone), draws a record, and says the member left. Law can register neither.

Beside these: the text says the broken clone is "never in force", but the core accepts the broken clone itself as the rollback's clone; the text says a resigning member's signature "counts for nothing" at the rollback, but the core counts it where the collective placed it earlier, as it does at a record; a stretch act can still place a member's signature on a declaration of absence; stepping down during a broken stretch is blocked by the client though the text provides for it; and the repo client's command line still marks a member change from this device's copy, so the step 11b bug is still reachable from there.

What held is listed at the end, with what I tried.

## How findings are marked

- **BREAKS**: an attack works, or a common case does not work, as built or as written.
- **CONFLICTS**: the text and the code, or two parts of the text, disagree.
- **UNCLEAR**: the text or the client can be read two ways, or a verifier cannot check it as written.
- **HOLDS**: I tried an attack and it failed; I say what I tried.

Each finding gives the sentence or the code, the smallest story, and a verdict. Ordered by weight.

## 1. The rollback deals a share of the new safety key to a member who already left. BREAKS (client; rule 37)

**Sentences.** Rule 37: "A departing member hands over nothing: the remaining members rotate to keys the departing member never held, and re-split the safety key among the members who stay or join." Rule 37d: a rollback is "a clone of the agreement in force just before the broken act", its powers "counted among the voices that remain at the rollback".

**Code.** `clients/repo/src/collective.ts:300` (`rollbackRules`): the members of the rollback's clone are `r.members.filter((m) => !leaving.includes(m))`, where `r` is the copy this device kept when `before` came into force (`remember()`), and `leaving` are only the members whose resignations the rollback registers. `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1462`: a resignation is registered only if Law still counts its signer among the voices of `before` (`all.voices.includes(d.member)`). `clients/repo/src/collective.ts:517`: the rotation deals the next safety key to `o.members`, the clone's members. `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1534`: the review lists them as "Members after the rollback".

**Story.** Four members, every party the constitutional rule. Sim Four resigns through the page; the record is drawn and the relays hold it: Law counts three voices from that line. The three refit (Change members, removing Sim Four): the clone is marked for three, signed by three, the checks pass, the rotation is sent and counted. Then one of the three signature acts is lost at the relay, as the removal's record was lost on the Mac; Law reads the rotation's clone as a draft ("a party its mark names has no signature act among those named"), and the collective as broken. The members review the rollback. Law counts the voices of the founding agreement: Ana, Sim One, Sim Two; Sim Four's departure is registered on a line before the broken act, so it is not among `all.voices`, so its resignation is not registered, so Sim Four is not in `leaving`. `rollbackRules` rebuilds the founding agreement with its four members. The review says "Members after the rollback: Ada, Sim One, Sim Two, Sim Four" and "Nothing: the rollback restores that agreement as it was." The rotation deals a two-of-four safety key to all four, and the agreement in force names Sim Four in the key grammar. Law reads Sim Four as departed (the box shows them left), but Sim Four holds a share of the collective's safety key, and with any one member can rebuild it and rotate. Rule 37's promise is undone by the repair; the same guard the page applies to an ordinary change (`actions.ts:986`: "A change that keeps them as a member would deal them a share of the new safety key: remove them in the same change") is missing here.

**Also.** `lawOf` returns no `departed` list when the collective is broken (`actions.ts:591` returns early), so the client has no reading of who left before the broken act; but it already computes `lawRollbackVoices(judicial, [])`, which is that reading. The members of the rollback's clone should be those voices less `leaving`, with every other party of `before` written to the departed entry; the freeze suite's 3.7w does not cover a departure registered before the broken act, which is why 27 of 27 pass.

**Verdict.** BREAKS. The rollback, built to make a key grab worthless, can hand keys to someone outside the collective.

## 2. The rollback's checks do not cover what it registers; a lost resignation fails the rollback, and the client reports success. BREAKS (client)

**Sentences.** "Collectives in the identity chain": a rollback "counts only in the broken stretch … Each act its fourth element names must be a valid resignation or stepping down … a rollback naming any other act there … puts nothing in force." Rule 37d: "a rollback that is not complete, is part of the broken stretch".

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:1201` (`rollback_departures`): `let Some(a) = self.v.get(x) else { return Ok(Err("the rollback registers an act this verifier does not hold")) }`; `rollback_at` (`view.rs:1178`) returns that error, and `chain_state_inner` (`view.rs:1043`) leaves the stretch open. `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1633` (`rollbackHolds`): checks that Law still reads the same broken act, that the mark names voices Law counts for each power, and `lawRollbackAgreement(clone)` for `invalid` and `ready`; it never asks whether the relays hold the acts in `registers`. `actions.ts:1282`: a resignation during the stretch is published by `resign(m, named, col.f.relays)` and kept in the file with `named`, so `prepareRollback` (`actions.ts:1462`) registers it without fetching it. `clients/repo/src/collective.ts:776` (`settle`): "counts" is the homes' receipt of the rotation, an Identity answer; the done message at `actions.ts:1605` then says "The collective works again."

**Story.** The Mac's relay loses an act again, this time Sim Three's resignation signed during the broken stretch. The file says Sim Three resigned. The rollback review counts Ada, Sim One and Sim Two, both checks pass (the mark is right, the clone is valid and signed), the rotation is sent naming Sim Three's resignation, the homes receipt it, `settle` counts, and the page says done: "The collective works again. Members now: Ada, Sim One, Sim Two." Law, reading the relays, finds the rollback registers an act it does not hold: the rollback puts nothing in force, is part of the broken stretch, and the collective is still broken from the first broken act. The keys have moved again, to a safety key dealt by a failed rotation. The next refresh of the box says broken, with the first broken act's reason, and offers the rollback again; nothing tells the members which act was lost. This is the failure the step 11b fix was for, "the client said done while Law read the collective as broken", reproduced in the repair path.

**Verdict.** BREAKS. The fix is the same shape as step 11b's: before the rotation is sent, ask Law from a fresh reading that each registered act is held and is a resignation it would register (there is no binding for that today; `rollback_departures` is private), and after `settle`, say what Law reads, not what the homes counted.

## 3. The last voice can leave unwarned when the voices are on separate devices. BREAKS (client conformance, rule 37a "The last voice"; question 3)

**Sentence.** Rule 37a: "where the member resigning is the last voice that remains in the collective, no other party of the agreement in force (during a broken stretch, of the agreement in force just before the broken act) keeping a voice there as rule 44d counts it, the client MUST say so in plain words before the resignation is signed".

**Code.** `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1279`: `last = n.voices.includes(a.member) && !voices.length`, where `n.voices` is `lawNextVoices(judicial, [])`, Law's count of the voices that remain. In a broken stretch (`actions.ts:1285`): `waiting` is `departedOf(c)`, this device's file, and `lawRollbackVoices(judicial, waiting)` takes those out. `core/src/law/view.rs:2209` (`voices`) counts a party out only through a line registering its departure (`departure_lines`); a resignation nobody registered leaves the voice.

**Story.** Four members, four devices. The collective breaks. Sim One's client: Law counts four voices, the file knows no other resignation, so "A rollback would need all of Ada, Sim Two, Sim Three and Sim One": no warning; Sim One resigns, no record (rule 37d). Sim Two's client the next day: Law still counts four (nothing registered Sim One's resignation, and this device's file does not have it); no warning; Sim Two resigns. Sim Three, the same. Ada, the same: "A rollback would need all of Sim One, Sim Two, Sim Three and Ada." Four resignations are published, each naming the founding agreement, each valid; no client said "last voice"; a rollback registering all four has nothing to meet the rule (`no_rollback_once_every_voice_has_resigned`); the works are frozen for good. The conformance rule was written for this case ("entering danger zone") and, judged "as rule 44d counts it", cannot see it: 44d counts registered departures, and during a broken stretch nothing registers.

**Narrower, in a working collective.** Two members on two devices resign in the same hour; each client, from Law, sees the other's voice remaining; each draws a record; the two records are concurrent lines; both departures are in effect once any later act joins them. Nobody was warned.

**What the client could do without a new rule.** A resignation is a public act at the collective's relays, signed by a party and naming the agreement. Before signing, the client can fetch the resignations by the other parties of the agreement in force (or of `before`) and, where every other voice has one published, say so: "every other member has signed a resignation; if yours is registered with theirs, no voice remains". That is a reading of what the relays hold, which is what step 11b asked every reading to be. Whether rule 37a's wording should count published resignations as well as registered ones is for Nobody, allegedly; it is not one of RB1 to RB6.

**Verdict.** BREAKS as a conformance rule: the client complies with the words and misses the case the words were written for. Hidden today because every member of the test collective lives on one machine.

## 4. Leaving while Law cannot be read: the resignation and the record name this device's copy, and no warning is given. BREAKS (client; rules 37a, 37d)

**Sentences.** Rule 37a: a resignation during a broken stretch "names the agreement in force just before the broken act"; "A resignation naming the clone the broken act declared names an agreement never in force, and no line can register it." Record (type 17): "a record naming no clone names the agreement in force for it" or it is no line.

**Code.** `clients/collective/src/actions.ts:1282`: `const named = b ? b.before : c.f.agreement`, where `b` is `law.brokenAct`, null whenever `lawOf` could not be read (`unread`) or Law reads the collective as broken with no way back. `actions.ts:1392`: in that case a record is drawn with `inForce: col.f.agreement`. `actions.ts:1279`: `n.voices` is empty whenever `counted` has a problem (unread or broken), so `last` is false. `prepareLeave` never checks `law.agreement !== c.f.agreement`, which `lawMark` (`actions.ts:633`) does for every change.

**Story (a).** The Mac's old test collective: this device's copy of the agreement in force is the broken clone (the removal was taken as done). The homes are down for the evening. A member leaves: `lawOf` is unread, `b` is null, the resignation names the broken clone, a record is drawn naming it, the result says "Record …: the collective's line, from which …'s signature counts for nothing." When the homes return, Law reads the collective as broken; the resignation names an agreement never in force, so no rollback can register it (`rollback_departures`: "a resignation from an agreement not in force just before the broken act"); the record is in the stretch and no line. The member must resign again, and nothing tells them so. If they were the last voice, no warning was given either.

**Story (b).** A working collective; another device changed the rules yesterday; this device has not settled. A member leaves here: the resignation names yesterday's agreement (an ancestor, so Law can register it), but the record names it too, and "a record naming no clone names the agreement in force for it" (`view.rs:1950`): no line. The member's voice remains; the page's own box, read from Law, shows them still in, against the result that said they left.

**Verdict.** BREAKS. Leaving must never be blocked, and it is not; but what is signed should name Law's agreement where Law can be read (`law.agreement`, or `b.before`), and where it cannot, the review should say that the resignation may name the wrong agreement and that no last-voice warning can be given.

## 5. The broken clone itself can be the rollback's clone. CONFLICTS (rule 37d against the core)

**Sentence.** Rule 37d: "The agreement chain forks from before the broken act, and the broken clone stays on its own branch, never in force."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:1178` (`rollback_at`): the only check on the clone's lineage is `self.terms(k)?.parent.as_ref() != Some(before)`. The broken clone's parent is `before` (that is how it failed rule 37 by its mark rather than by its parent), so it passes. `clone_at_as(…, rollback = true)` then checks its mark against `rollback_powers`, which for a member removal is the constitutional change rule the broken clone already named.

**Story.** The Mac's case with no judicial change: Sim Four resigned, the record was lost, the rotation declared clone K1 (the three who stay, marked `[0]` with the three) and broke the collective. A later rotation declares `[K1, [the same three signature acts], rot1, [Sim Four's resignation]]`. Law: K1's parent is `before`; its mark names `[0]`, which is what a rollback names; the rollback registers Sim Four, so three voices remain and three signed. Complete. K1, the broken clone, is the agreement in force, on the chain that "forks from before the broken act". The core test covers "a clone of the broken clone" (parent K1, refused) but not K1 itself; step 3.7w says "whose parent is the broken clone", not "which is the broken clone".

**Attack.** None found: the voices still protect the member the broken act removed, and reusing K1 is only possible when its mark happens to equal a rollback's. But the text and the code disagree, and the readings "the broken clone stays on its own branch" and "the rollback … may redo correctly the change the broken act failed" are both satisfied by the code only if "redo" may mean "declare the same clone again". One of the two should give: either the text drops "never in force", or `rollback_at` refuses a clone the broken act declared (one line, plus a test either way).

**Verdict.** CONFLICTS, low weight.

## 6. A resigning member's signature at the rollback: "counts for nothing there" in the text, counts in the code. CONFLICTS (rule 37d, "Collectives in the identity chain", against the core)

**Sentence.** "a member whose resignation the rollback registers is not counted for it, and its signature on the clone counts for nothing there." Compare the record's rule: "a signature it places counts for its departed signer only where an act of the collective placed it before (C2)."

**Code.** `core/src/law/view.rs:2209` (`voices`), called by `clone_at_as` for the rollback as for a record: a party registered out at this line still counts "through a signature act on `target` placed before every line registering their departure" (`sig_before`). The rollback has no special case. So where the collective placed the resigning member's signature on the rollback's clone before the rollback, the member counts, and the rule's number counts them.

**Story.** A constitutional clone K2 of the founding agreement was drafted and signed by all four before the broken act, and the collective acknowledged the signatures (as the text asks a client to, C2); it was never declared. The collective breaks for another reason; Sim Four resigns in the stretch. The members declare K2 as the rollback's clone, registering Sim Four. The text says Sim Four is not counted and the mark names three. The code counts Sim Four's placed signature, so four voices, and a mark of three is "too few signers to meet the power". This client never reuses a drafted clone, so it never reaches this; another client following the text would.

**Verdict.** CONFLICTS, low weight. The record's reading (C2) is the one the core applies everywhere; the rollback sentence should say the same, or the core should make the rollback the exception, and a test should pin whichever.

## 7. A stretch act can still place a member's signature on a declaration of absence. CONFLICTS (rule 37d against the core)

**Sentence.** Rule 37d: every act of the broken stretch "places no member's signature ('Made before, made after', 2)".

## What held, with what I tried

- **Undo a valid act by a rollback (question 2).** The rollback's parent is `before`, computed by Law from the agreement declared at the broken act and the records before it (`in_force_at_rotation`); a clone of anything else is refused (`rollback_at`, first check). A rollback declaration where the collective is not broken fails rule 37 (`rotation_fails`: "a rotation declares a rollback where the collective is not broken") and opens a new stretch whose `before` holds every valid decision until then, as F185's "repair, not undo" decided (`a_rollback_may_change_nothing_and_needs_every_voice_that_remains`, last block). Naming a later rotation of the stretch as the broken act is refused (`rb.broken == col.res.links[*bj].act`). Records before the broken act on a branch its kept tips left out are superseded, as at any rotation (stated cost, "Made before, made after"); the rollback adds no new way to leave one out. HOLDS.
- **Complete a key grab by a rollback.** The removed member's voice is counted in `before` unless a line before the broken act registered their departure, or the rollback registers a resignation they signed (`rollback_departures` checks the signer is a party and the act is their valid type 16). A stale resignation, registered once and followed by a constitutional clone they signed, does not count them out again (`restored`, B10). The grabbers can rotate (they hold the stretch's safety key) but cannot complete the clone without the voices; that is "break and hold", written as a cost. HOLDS. *One caveat, not new to F185 but sharpest here:* the rollback's rotation is made by whoever holds the stretch's safety key, and it deals the next key; a member the clone names in the key grammar holds a share only if that device hands it over. Law reads the key grammar; nothing can read who was handed a share. The repair is thus made by the hand that broke it, and the text does not say so.
- **Place a signature collected during the stretch.** A record in the stretch is no line and places nothing (`record_eval_inner`: `base` is `Err` → `not_a_line`). A rotation in the stretch, the broken act included, places nothing (`sig_before` with `in_broken_stretch`, for the `Line::Rotation` placements). An acknowledgement by the collective's key in the stretch places nothing for a clone (`sig_before`), though it still does for a declaration (finding 7). The rollback's own `signatures` must be signature acts on its clone (`clone_at_as`: "it names an act that is not a signature act on the clone"), and its `registers` must be type 16 by a party (`rollback_departures`); neither list can smuggle a placement of anything else. HOLDS, with finding 7's exception.
- **The client signing or publishing what Law refuses (question 1), on the page.** Every change goes through `lawMark` (powers from `lawClonePlan` on the agreement Law finds in force, voices from `lawNextVoices`), is blocked where this device's agreement differs from Law's, and is checked twice more (`markHolds`) with `lawAgreement(clone).invalid` and `.ready` before the record or rotation is sent. A release in a broken collective is refused before publishing; signing one says the sentence the step asked for. I found no path on the page that signs a clone with a mark Law calls false. HOLDS on the page; findings 2, 4 and 9 are the paths beside it.
- **Readings against F185 as decided (question 2).** The rollback's parent, its powers (constitutional change rule, counted among the voices that remain, plus the judicial tier's rule where a judge changes), its naming the broken act, the stretch shown and void, leaving during the stretch with the resignation registered at the rollback, the last voice's warning, "repair not undo", the two stated costs: each is in the text and in the code as F185 records it. The six readings in "Open in this draft" extend F185 without contradicting it; reading 4 (steppings down) is the one the client does not follow (finding 8). Findings 5 and 6 are places where the text written today and the code written today disagree with each other, not with F185.
