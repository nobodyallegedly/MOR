## 2. What the stranger found

**A first list of fifteen tasks.** Ten for agreeing at all (fix, identify, commit, exchange, order, keep, check, align, free, close) and five for when there is a reward to share (measure, hold, divide, settle, record).

*Hold, divide and record are three tasks there. In MOR they are one service. `fable-task-list.md` repeated the fusion.*

**A "grammar test" over forty-nine tasks.** Every task was tested against five operations a record can perform: state, sign, deliver, order, control; with a sixth it used silently, apply (compute a result from signed terms). Keep and check came out as properties, not tasks. Its verdicts: 33 clean (the operations alone do it), 4 in part, 5 need a witness, 4 depend on a mind, 1 on a hand, 2 are properties. It also stated a closed-world rule (what is not in the record does not count) and named what no record reaches: truth, minds and hands.

**First supposition: free order only.** Twelve tasks change, in two clusters. "Only once": title, transfer, sum. "By when": current, wait, due, count, succeed. In its words, everything that is two people signing words to each other survives untouched. It named three gains: a fork convicts itself; a witness need only say "I saw this entry at this point in my own sequence"; and the closed-world rule becomes local, so that what none of the named witnesses has seen does not count and a thing's ownership is as global as its witness set.

**Second supposition: the record cannot hold value, only statements about value held elsewhere.** Ten tasks change, all of them built on control. Clean falls from 33 to 23. Hold and recover fall to a hand; separate needs a witness; divide, secure, title, transfer, sum, end and leave hold in part. Its four findings: the record falls back from control to proof; a permanent gap opens between record and reality that must be checked continuously; hold collapses into acting for another, so it needs no tasks of its own; and two things carry more weight, apply (who is owed what) and acknowledge (the receiver's signed receipt, the only clean proof that value moved). In place of a posted stake it lists: keep what is held small and brief; make the holder post something of its own elsewhere; use several holders; rely on an enforcer who treats the record as the title.

**Both together.** The record becomes a layer of evidence and accounting that proves who owes what and who lied, and moves nothing. Its only force is what a proven liar stands to lose.

## 3. How I read it against MOR

All of this section is my reading.

**The foundations hold.** Starting from "no shared clock", the stranger arrived at self-convicting forks (Identity), a witness that speaks only of its own sequence (a home's receipt, a keeper's record), and a local closed world (the collective's line; an act counts where it is held; rule 14's admission that the core cannot tie a sale to the one true owner). The second supposition is MOR's actual situation for money: payment happens on outside rails and Money records receipts. Its closing sentence is legible greed.

**The operations are the layers.** State is Text, sign is Identity, deliver and order are Envelopes, control is what Money records, apply is the verifier. Agreements has no operation of its own. It is the list of what must be checked when the others are combined.

**MOR is a hybrid, and should say so once.** Identities, and a party's place in an agreement, live inside the record, so recovering a key and passing a place stay clean or need only a witness. Money lives outside, so everything about money passes through a hand. The stranger's second supposition is too strong for the first half and exact for the second.

**Three kinds of socket.** Every place where the core hands something to a cMIP is one of: trust this witness, apply this rule, trust this hand. What depends on a mind is a stated cost. This replaces the line I drew in `fable-task-list.md`, "fixed at signing, computed at payment".

**A two-question test for whether something is a rule of the core.** First: is there a lie here that signed, ordered text cannot already expose? If not, it is only text. Second: does catching it hurt someone who did not choose the rule? If not, it is a task for a cMIP. The negotiation record (rule 52, type 18) appears to fail the first question and is a candidate to leave the core. I have not checked which vectors would move with it.

## 5. A skeleton for the rewrite

A proposal, to be argued with.

1. **Words between people.** Signing, amending, completing, ending. No witness. The stranger counts nine such tasks that survive every supposition.
2. **Only once.** Anything scarce names its witnesses, and what none of them saw does not count.
3. **By when.** Anything with a deadline or a "latest" names its witness to time.
4. **What a witness may do**, said once for all of them: say what it saw, in its own sequence, and be convicted by its own fork.
5. **What a hand may do**, said once: hold and pay within its scope, show its record, and be convicted by a missing receipt.

Draft 24 has five witness mechanisms, each with rules of its own: homes, keepers, the line, anchors and the time reference. The hypothesis is that they are one thing with one rule, and the agreement picks which. The same hypothesis for hands: the split service, an escrow and a guarantor's pledge are one thing.

## 6. What could go wrong

- **The hypothesis may be false.** If homes, keepers and the line give different verdicts today for reasons that matter, "one rule for all witnesses" changes verdicts, and F441 forbids that. The test is the inventory: every verdict-bearing sentence about each mechanism laid side by side. Where they differ, either the difference is a guarantee and stays, or it is an accident and Nobody, allegedly decides.
- **The stranger does not know the attacks.** Its map says what an agreement needs. The findings log says what a liar tries. Rules that look like excess from outside (the absence clause per holder, the successor's clause, the deal's clone that may not drop an active party) each close a recorded attack. The two-question test must be run on each with its finding beside it.
- **I read MOR into the map.** I already did so once this evening and was corrected. The mapping in section 3 should be read by someone looking for where it is forced.
- **A hand "convicted by a missing receipt" is weaker than it sounds.** Absence is proved only against a named witness and a time, so part 5 leans on parts 3 and 4. That dependency should be written, not assumed.
- **More tasks means more declarations.** Each small task a cMIP fills is one more thing a client must show before signing. The single conformance sentence of F441's first gate decision has to carry that without growing back into the family it replaced.
