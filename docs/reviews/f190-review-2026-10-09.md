*9 October 2026, night. Written for Nobody, allegedly, against main at `dec08b1`: F190 and F184's delivery record as written in and built (`docs/f190-build-2026-10-09.md`, merged `32bb877`), read against Law draft 10 (rules 5, 15a, 19, 22, 37d, 45b; the terms format's fields 26 and 27; the resignation; types 22 and 23; "Open in this draft"), core v21, freeze suite v21 (steps 1.7a, 2.4f, 3.7w), F184 (from "what remains"), F186, F188, F189 and F190 in `docs/findings/MOR-findings-log-round-2.md`, and the code in `core/src/law/formats.rs`, `core/src/law/view.rs` and `wasm/src/lib.rs`. Nothing in `spec/` or in the library was changed. Every BREAKS below is reproduced by a test, kept this time: seven tests appended to `core/tests/law_collective.rs`, named `review_f190_…`, each passing because it shows the attack working as the code stands, or shows what held; the whole file still passes (`cargo test -p mor-core --test law_collective`: 145 passed). QG1 to QG5 are not decided here: the last section gives my reading of each and stops.*

## In plain words first

The build does what F190 decided, and does it carefully: a settlement must have seen every tip, the parties of every tip sign, a version that cites the settlement is harmless, a settlement made after another changes nothing, the judge acts only on a request, a split's number is checked, money paid back is subtracted, a stale stepping down is spent. What I found sits where the new rule meets an older one, and in the one place the build invented a reading of its own: **clean**.

1. **The judge of forks can unsettle a settled deal alone, at any later time**, by signing a second settlement that keeps the other branch. Both of its settlements count as clean, since a judge's settlement is not a version and so never needs to have been *seen*; neither is after the other; the deal is tangled and back on its reference. Worse, **the next plain version on either line then decides which branch wins**, with no tip named and no choice shown: exactly the "harmless change settles the fork" that F186's second round refused. One signature, the judge's, undoes finality; the next signature, steered, picks the winner.
2. **"Nobody can unsettle a deal alone" is false where a party left the deal on the branch that was later dropped.** Ana and Ben settle for branch A; branch B's tip is the version where Ben left, which only Ana is a party to; Ana alone signs a version on it, citing nothing, and the settled deal falls back to the reference. Ben cannot put it back without her. With three parties, two can do it to the third.
3. **The timing of a tangle is in the hands of whoever holds a complete version back.** A version signed by everyone while the fork stood (which the rules say counts) can be kept in a drawer and published after the settlement: the settlement is then unclean and the deal goes back to the reference. The text charges the cost to "those who kept signing two versions"; here the honest signer did nothing wrong. MOR has no clock, and the keepers, as written, order acts against rotations, not versions against settlements.
4. **The delivery record's acknowledgement comes from whoever the split service says the payer is.** The receipt is the service's act, and its payer field is the service's word. For an anonymous payment with no claim of the real payer, the service names a puppet, the puppet's claim acknowledges the record of a relay the service runs, and the relay's share leaves the owners: the "faking usage" F184 set out to stop, moved one step sideways. Beside it: **on a purchase that names the offer rather than the publication, no delivery record ever counts**, however plainly the buyer acknowledges it, since the code reads the object paid for from a publication only.
5. **An old stepping down handed in and never registered is a standing weapon** in the hands of whoever writes the collective's records: however many constitutional clones the member signs as holder afterwards, the day it is registered it takes the area, with no new signature of theirs. QF6 and F189 (1) spend a departure only after a line registered it *and* the member came back.
6. Smaller: a repayment of money owed back may go to any pointer the payer ever signed, valid or not, where a debt's repayment may not; the text names "field 5" for a claim where Finance's field 5 is the disagreement; a complete version naming a hash nobody holds kills every reading of the deal for good; a verifier not told which cMIP is the relay transport cMIP still counts the relay's own word.

QF4 held against everything I tried: only the service can trigger it, against itself. Field 26 as a list held: no settlement drops a tip it did not name, and the parties of every tip sign. The judge cannot overturn a clean settlement of the parties.

## How findings are marked

- **BREAKS**: an attack works, or a common case does not work, as built or as written.
- **CONFLICTS**: the text and the code, or two parts of the text, disagree.
- **UNCLEAR**: the text or the client can be read two ways, or a verifier cannot check it as written.
- **HOLDS**: I tried an attack and it failed; I say what I tried.

Each finding gives what breaks, the smallest story, who gains, the rule and the line, the test where there is one, and a weight. Ordered by weight. Line numbers are of main at `dec08b1`.

## 1. The judge of forks unsettles a settled deal alone, and the next plain version decides the winner. BREAKS (rule 45b: "the first clean settlement holds", "a split is settled only by a version that names both branches"; against F186's second round and A3)

**What breaks.** Rule 45b defines clean as "every complete version of the deal from the reference on is either in its history … or made after it", and says "the first clean settlement holds". A settling *version* is a version: a rival settling version that did not see it leaves it unclean, so two rival versions are both unclean and the deal waits for a third naming both (the DQ3 test shows this working). A **judge's settlement (type 23) is not a version.** Nothing requires another settlement to have seen it. So two judge's settlements of one fork, each naming the tips of its day, are both clean; neither is after the other (`view.rs:7227` to `7230`: a settlement is "after" another only through a version's history or by descent from the kept tip, and a judge's settlement sits in no version's history until a version cites it); the code answers "two settlements of one fork, neither made after the other (DQ3)": tangled, back on the reference.

Then the second half. Once the deal is tangled this way, **any plain complete version on either line decides it.** A version on A's line is after the first settlement (it descends from its kept tip) and was never seen by the second, so the second is no longer clean and the first alone holds: A is in force. A version on B's line does the mirror: B is in force. No tip named, no field 26, no "everyone sees every option". That is "the next complete version on one branch settles it", which the second round rejected ("The bad faith cases push me towards b", F186), back through the judge's door.

**Story.** Ana and Ben's deal names Carla, an arbitrator, as judge of forks. Their devices fork the deal; Ben asks Carla; Carla keeps branch A. Months pass; two versions are signed on A. Ana and Carla come to an understanding. Carla signs a second settlement, on Ben's old request, keeping B and discarding A's tip. The deal is tangled: every reading reports the reference, and the two versions on A are "what the tangled versions changed", lost. Ana now proposes "a small correction" on B, which Ben's client shows as a plain clone of an old version; Ben signs it; B is in force. Ben never saw a choice. Had Ben's client proposed a plain version on A instead, A would have won: whoever steers the next signature picks the line.

**Who gains, and can they pull it.** The judge, alone, for the first half: one signature, no request needed beyond the first one ever made (a request names the reference and is never spent: `arbitrated`, `view.rs:7338`, accepts any number of settlements naming the same request). For the second half, whichever party gets the other to sign anything next. A party of the deal consented to the judge in advance; it did not consent to the judge speaking twice.

**Test.** `review_f190_2_the_judge_unsettles_a_settled_deal_alone_and_a_plain_version_then_decides`: the judge keeps A; A grows; the judge keeps B; tangled; in one world a plain version on A puts A in force, in another a plain version on B puts B in force.

**Text and code.** They agree; the hole is in the reading of "clean" for an act that is not a version. The text's own sentence "a settling version that names or cites a settlement, or a version after one, was made after that settlement" has no counterpart for a judge's settlement that comes after another judge's settlement. Two shapes close it, for Nobody, allegedly, not for me: (a) the judge speaks once per fork: a second settlement by the judge of the same reference, not naming the first, counts for nothing; or (b) a judge's settlement is treated as something later acts must have seen, so that a settlement made without naming the earlier one is unclean, as a settling version is. Either way, the plain-version decision disappears with the double settlement. **Weight: high.** Finality, which QF1 and F189 (4) were about, is undone by one outside signature.

## 2. A party left alone on a dropped branch unsettles the deal by itself. BREAKS (rule 45b, QF1's justification "it takes every party's signature to make such a version, so nobody can unsettle a deal alone")

**What breaks.** A version on a discarded branch "beyond the tips a settlement named, not citing the settlement" tangles the deal (QF1). Such a version needs the signatures of "every party whose voice remains" **of that branch's tip** (`deal_voices`, `view.rs:949`, counts the parent's parties), not of the deal as settled. Where the discarded tip is a version in which a party left (a clone dropping them, which they signed: rule 45), the remaining parties of that tip grow it alone. In a two-party deal, one party grows it alone.

**Story.** Ana and Ben's deal. Two versions from the same parent: A, where both carry on with Ben's share raised; B, where Ben leaves the deal, signed by both as an option they once discussed. They settle: a version on A naming B's tip, both signing. The deal runs on the settlement; Ben's raise is in force. Later Ana signs, alone, a version whose parent is B's tip. She is every party of that tip; the version is complete (`agreement_as`: the voices of a clone of a one-party version are that one party); it cites nothing; the deal is tangled and back on the reference, where Ben's raise does not exist. Ben cannot settle it: a clean settlement needs the parties of every tip, and Ana is a party of both. Only a judge of forks, where the reference names one, can help him. With three parties, Ana and Ben do the same to Carla's settlement by a branch on which Carla left.

**Who gains.** The party who prefers the reference to the settlement and can get, or once got, a version where the other left. The bait branch of DQ5 is a branch the other does not know of; this is a branch the other signed, in good faith, as an exit. A buyer under the settlement stays protected (their payment is judged under the version it names); everything else goes back.

**Test.** `review_f190_1_a_party_left_alone_on_a_dropped_branch_unsettles_the_deal`: B's tip removes Ben; the settlement for A is in force; Ana alone grows B; tangled; Ben's signature on a new settlement changes nothing without Ana's.

**Text and code.** They agree; the justification in rule 45b and in F190's entry ("nobody can unsettle a deal alone") is false in this shape. One shape to close it, not decided here: a version on a discarded branch tangles the deal only where its signers include every party whose voice remains **in the version the settlement put in force**; a version signed by fewer is growth that changes nothing, like one citing the settlement. **Weight: medium to high.** Rare by accident; cheap on purpose.

## 3. A complete version held back decides the timing of a tangle. BREAKS as a story, UNCLEAR as a rule (QF1; rule 45b "a new act may follow either branch, and counts"; "a stated cost, borne by those who kept signing two versions")

**What breaks.** While a fork stands, every party may sign further versions on either branch, and the text says they count. A version is complete the moment the last signature act exists; whoever holds that signature act decides when the world sees it. A version signed before the settlement and published after it is, to every verifier, a version "beyond the tips a settlement named, not citing it": the deal is tangled.

**Story.** Ana and Ben's deal is forked. Ben signs, honestly, a small change on B; Ana keeps his signature act and publishes nothing. Ben proposes settling for A; Ana agrees; the settlement names B's tip as everyone can see it. Under the settlement, Ben delivers what he owed. Ana publishes the old version on B. Tangled, back on the reference; Ana refuses every settlement from then on ("a party of either branch can refuse every settlement", the stated cost). Ben kept double-signing in the sense that he signed B once and A later, which the rules invited him to do.

**Who gains.** Whoever pockets a complete version: a party, or a relay that withholds one party's signature act and releases it later (the signature act is public; a relay that serves it late achieves the same). The text's protection against this is the judge, who "weighs the keepers' record of who signed what, and in what order". But the keepers, as written (rules 7 to 11a), record acts to place them against rotations; nothing in rule 45b or in `deal_state` reads a keeper's log to order a version against a settlement; and a deal without a judge has no one to weigh it.

**Test.** None new: the shape is `f190_qf1_a_version_beyond_the_settled_tip`, read with the question "who chose when". **Weight: medium.** It is the no-clock cost in its plainest form, and the text should own it as such, or give the keepers' order a role in clean, or give the party who did not withhold a way out (finding 2's shape would also help here: a withheld version tangles only if its signers include every party of the version in force, which they do in a two-party deal, so this one stays).

## 4. The split service names the payer, so it names who may acknowledge its own relay. BREAKS (rules 19 and 22; the reading "the payer its receipt names"; Finance, receipt field 2)

**What breaks.** Rule 22 says a delivery record counts "only when the payer's claim for the payment acknowledges it". The reading taken says: the payment itself where it is a claim, "otherwise a claim of the same rail payment by the payer its receipt names". The receipt is signed by the payee of the hop (Finance, "A receipt is signed by the payee of the hop"), which for the owners' incoming money is the split service (rule 19: "for which it signs the receipt"). Its field 2, the payer, is whatever the service writes. The code follows the reading: `delivery_acknowledged`, `view.rs:4905`, accepts a claim whose signer is the receipt's payer, found among the acts sharing the rail proof (`same_payment`), which need only be valid and not shown wrong by the rail. On a rail that binds no payer (Finance rule 10a: "presenting the rail proof proves nothing about who paid"), a claim by anyone carrying the real proof passes.

**Story.** Ana, Ben and Carla's collective pays a relay share. Their split service runs a relay and a second identity, the puppet. A buyer pays anonymously on the rail and publishes nothing, as most buyers will (rule 22's own words: payers publish claims "by choice, so most uses would go unrecorded"). The service receipts the payment naming the puppet as payer; the service's relay signs a record of the song's locked hash; the puppet's claim, same rail and proof, acknowledges it; the split pays the relay share to the service's relay. The verifier reads: evidence held. Nobody but the service signed anything that was not the service's.

**Who gains.** The service and its relay, the relay share of every anonymous payment, taken from the owners. F184's stated cost is "a payer and a relay together", bounded by the share; here no payer is needed. Rule 22's premise, "the confirming signature comes from the other side of the deal", is met in form only: the service appointed the other side.

**Test.** `review_f190_5_the_service_names_the_payer_who_acknowledges_its_own_relay`: the receipt names the puppet; the puppet's claim acknowledges the service's relay's record; `unevidenced` is empty.

**Text and code.** They agree; the reading "the payer its receipt names" is the hole. Finance already has the stronger tool: IT3, "the payment decides": on a rail that binds payee and purpose, the payer's commitment names the claim. A reading that lets only a claim the rail's proof commits to acknowledge a delivery (or the claim of the committed key, Finance rule 10a), and none where the payment committed to no claim, closes this; it also means a buyer who wants a relay paid must commit to a claim, which the cMIP's client rule already asks of them. **Weight: medium to high.** It is the attack F184 was decided against.

### 4b. On a purchase naming the offer, no delivery record ever counts. BREAKS (rule 22, "the object it names is the one the payment was for"; the reading "the locked bytes hash of the publication the payment fulfils")

Finance's receipt field 5, `fulfils`, is "the obligation, agreement, offer or payee-pointer act this hop follows"; the core's own purchase reading accepts a standing offer there (`purchase`, `view.rs:7484` on: `for_offer`). The delivery reading looks only for an Envelope publication in `fulfils` (`view.rs:4889`); for a standing offer it finds no object, and `object != paid_for` fails for every record. The owners keep the share, through no act of anyone's. Test: `review_f190_5b_no_delivery_record_counts_on_a_purchase_naming_the_offer`: the fan's own claim acknowledges the record of the song served; still "evidence of nothing". The offer's format is still open, so the reading cannot yet follow an offer to its publication; the rule should say what it means by "the object the payment was for" when the payment names an offer, or the code should follow the offer once it has a format. **Weight: medium.**

### 4c. The relay's pay depends on when the service splits, and on the payer's client. UNCLEAR (rule 20; rule 22; split plan field 3, `unfilled`; the cMIP's "Clients")

The order of events is: payment, then delivery, then the record, then the claim acknowledging it. Rule 20 asks for a split "for every incoming receipt", and a split "for every payer's claim that shows money arrived without one". A service that splits when the money arrives has no acknowledged record yet: the role share is one "nobody fills", and the plan's option 0 sends it to the owners pro rata. A claim arriving later triggers no split (the receipt already has one), and nothing says the service revisits a split. A plan choosing option 1, "held open", keeps the share as an obligation; nothing says when, or whether, an obligation held open is paid once evidence arrives (rule 29 lists what a service owes, not this). So the honest service that splits promptly pays no relay, ever, and the plan that advertised a relay share pays the owners. Separately, the cMIP states that a payer who acknowledges none pays no relay; it does not say that the client doing the acknowledging is often the owners' own, and that an owners' client that never acknowledges keeps the share for the owners while the plan shows it offered. Smaller still: a record names an object, a size and a nonce, not a payment; one record may be acknowledged by several claims of one payer for several payments of the same object, earning the share each time. **Weight: medium** for the first (a rule for the order of split and acknowledgement, or a stated cost); low for the rest.

### 4d. Two verifiers, two answers (QG3 as built). CONFLICTS (rule 22 unconditional; `is_delivery_record`, `view.rs:4856`, conditional on the `delivery_records` input)

Rule 22 says the record counts only when acknowledged, full stop. The code applies this only where the verifier was told which specification is the relay transport cMIP; a verifier not told reads the record as a third party's act and counts it on the relay's word alone (`role_evidence`, `Role::Other`). No client passes the input yet. The same split is evidenced for one verifier and not for another. My reading is under QG3 below. **Weight: medium** until decided; it is an open question, not a surprise.

## 5. An old stepping down never registered takes the area years later. BREAKS as a story (the resignation format, QF6 and F189 1; rule 37b)

**What breaks.** QF6 and F189 (1) spend a departure "where a line registered" it and a later version the member signed gave the voice or area back; a line "registers only a stepping down of theirs from that area signed after that return". Where no line ever registered the old act, there is no return to measure against: `area_returns`, `view.rs:2646`, looks for returns after each *registering* line; the late registration is itself the first such line, and no clone comes after it. The old act counts.

**Story.** Ana signs a stepping down from Releases for a summer and hands it to the collective; everyone agrees she should stay, and it is never registered. Two constitutions later, each naming her holder of Releases and signed by her, whoever holds the signing key writes a record registering the old act. Releases freezes until a constitutional clone; Ana signed nothing new. The reading taken, "signed after the return is checked by the agreement the resignation names, the only order MOR can see between the two acts", sees the order here too (the act names the founding version; her signatures sit on later ones), and does not use it.

**Who gains.** Whoever writes the records, against any member who once handed in a departure. The holder of the signing key cannot otherwise remove a holder from an area without a constitutional clone.

**Test.** `review_f190_6_an_old_stepping_down_never_registered_takes_the_area_years_later`.

**Text and code.** They agree; the rule's condition "where a line registered" is narrower than its reason. A wider shape, not decided here: a departure is spent by **any** constitutional clone its signer signed that names them, made after the version the departure names, registered or not; or a departure counts only where it names the version in force at the line registering it, or that version's line. **Weight: medium to low.** It needs an act the member signed and the key holder's bad faith, and "do not sign a departure you do not mean" is a fair answer; but a client that signs a stepping down and loses the network before delivering it leaves exactly this act behind.

## 6. Smaller findings

### 6a. A repayment may follow a pointer the payer no longer owns. CONFLICTS (QG2's "any version"; Finance rules 14 and 15, F178: "an act a rotation did not keep holds no pointer"; F169)

`repaid`, `view.rs:7800`, counts a claim where the rail shows the money reached a pointer or vault entry whose act is signed by the payer, and asks nothing else of that act: not that it is valid under Identity, not that it is the version that counts, not the vault rule for older versions. The debt code asks all three (`paid_toward`, `view.rs:6440`, through `pointer_holding` and `paid_where_it_counts`). After a lock change, a thief's pointer is shut out of every payment under F169; here it still receives a refund in the verifier's eyes. Story: Ana's key was stolen last year; the thief published a pointer; Ana rotated and anchored; the collective, owing Ana money back from a broken stretch, pays the thief's pointer, by accident or because the thief is one of its members; repaid in full, the closing goes through. No test: a disowned pointer act needs a larger fixture than this review built; the closure `to_own` shows the gap on its face. **Weight: medium**, and the same decision as QG2.

### 6b. "Field 5" is the wrong field for a claim. CONFLICTS (rule 37d: "field 5 naming the payment owed back"; the QF5 reading; Finance, the claim format)

Finance's receipt field 5 is `fulfils`; its claim field 5 is "the receipt this claim disagrees with", and field 4 is what the claim fulfils. The code reads the claim's `fulfils`, field 4 (`finance.rs:591`; `repaid`). The text should say "the receipt's field 5 or the claim's field 4". **Weight: low**, text only.

### 6c. A complete version naming a hash nobody holds kills the deal for good. UNCLEAR (rule 45b; the F188 reading "a settling version naming a version the verifier does not hold waits until it holds it"; `view.rs:7192`)

Where every party signs a version whose field 26 names a hash that exists nowhere, every reading of the deal errs: version in force, purchases, splits, releases. A later version mends nothing, since the scan errs on the first such candidate before reading anything else. F189 (2) made drafts and strangers harmless; a complete version by a careless client, or by one party's client that slipped a stray hash past the others' review, is not. Test: `review_f190_7_a_complete_version_naming_a_tip_nobody_holds_kills_the_deal_for_good`. A shape, not decided: a settling version naming a tip the verifier does not hold is read as a plain version until the tip is held, so the deal stays forked or tangled and readable. **Weight: low to medium**: all parties must sign it, and there is no way back.

### 6d. The request proves that one party knew of the fork, not the party losing a branch. UNCLEAR (rule 45b, DQ8: "never before a party knows of the fork")

`arbitrated` accepts a request by any party who signed the reference. On a bait branch, the party who made the bait is a party who knows; they request at once, and a friendly judge settles before the other party has seen the fork. Every party chose the judge, so this is a cost of choosing badly rather than a hole; but the text's sentence promises more than the request delivers. **Weight: low**; a wording, or a stated cost.

### 6e. "The tip kept" may be any version of the fork. CONFLICTS, harmless (rule 45b, type 23 field 1; `arbitrated`, `view.rs:7338`)

The code accepts as kept any complete version of the fork, not only a tip; the kept line's later versions are then followed as the line in force. Where the kept version has itself two complete clones, the deal reads as forked again below it, which seems right. The text says "the tip of the branch kept". **Weight: low.**

### 6f. The bindings tidy what the core refuses. CONFLICTS, trivial (`forkSettlementPayload`, `wasm/src/lib.rs:2087`; `ForkSettlement::decode`, `formats.rs:2295`)

The binding sorts and deduplicates the tips a client passes; the core refuses a repeated tip. A client that passes the same tip twice never learns it. **Weight: trivial.**

### 6g. Money owed back to a payer with no pointer blocks a closing for good. UNCLEAR (F189 7; QG1 names the bare key and nobody; the identity case is the same hold)

A payer with an identity but no payee pointer or vault entry ever published cannot be repaid by the collective's claim (`to_own` finds no act of theirs), and may sign no receipt; the money stays owed and the collective never closes. Story: Carla, a departed holder with "no control and no veto" (rule 46b), pays one unit from a fresh identity under an offer of the broken stretch, and publishes nothing, ever; the collective cannot close while her stake keeps earning. A debt already blocks a closing the same way, but a debt is the collective's own choice; this one is bought by a stranger for a penny while the collective is broken. **Weight: low to medium**, and part of QG1.

## Where the text and the code disagree, in one place

- Rule 37d and the QF5 reading: "field 5" of a claim; the code reads field 4 (6b).
- Rule 22: the delivery record counts only when acknowledged; the code: only where the verifier was told the cMIP (4d, QG3).
- Rule 22's "the object the payment was for" and Finance's `fulfils` naming an offer; the code reads a publication only (4b).
- QG2's "any version" of the payer's pointer; Finance rules 14 and 15 and F178: a disowned act holds no pointer (6a).
- Rule 45b: "one naming an older version of that branch settles nothing"; the code reports the deal **tangled** (with the QF1 or DQ4 reason), not forked. The reference is in force either way, and the suite's "never reported settled" holds; the label differs.
- Rule 45b: "the tip of the branch kept" in a judge's settlement; the code accepts any version of the fork (6e).
- Rule 45b and F190: "nobody can unsettle a deal alone"; the code, following the rule, lets the judge (finding 1) and a party left alone on a branch (finding 2) do it.
- The binding deduplicates the tips the core refuses (6f).

## What held, with what I tried

- **Drop a tip some party never saw (item 2).** A settling version naming one of two other tips leaves the deal tangled (the build's DQ2 test); one naming an older version of a branch leaves it tangled; a tip whose branch added Carla counts only once Carla signs (the build's DQ5 test). A tip cannot be dropped silently: the moment it is held, the settlement that did not name it is unclean. HOLDS, by the clean reading; findings 1 to 3 are about who can make a settlement unclean afterwards.
- **Choose or silence the judge (item 2).** Field 27 is read from the version that forked (`arbitrated` reads `at`), which every party signed; terms naming a judge outside field 13, or in a collective, are refused (`formats.rs:1489`, `1494`); a settlement by anyone else, or naming no request, or a request by a non-signer, settles nothing (the build's DQ8 test). A judge's settlement made after the parties' clean settlement, naming it, changes nothing: `review_f190_held_the_judge_cannot_overturn_the_parties_clean_settlement`. HOLDS. Silencing is QG4: a party who refuses to name a judge, or a judge who never answers, leaves the deal on its reference, which is DQ5's stated cost extended to an outsider.
- **QF4 (item 3).** Only the service's own splits enter its numbering (`split_numbers`, `view.rs:1550`, keyed by the signer and the deal's root), so no holder, payer or rival service can make a service's number repeat; a payer-side split is not the service's; a gap is the alarm and no deviation (the build's test); the check is for deals only. A service running two devices out of step repeats a number and breaks its own plan, as two tally receipts citing the same one already did (rule 15a, F171): a stated cost for services, worth a line. A repeated number delivered to different holders is seen by neither until they compare, which rule 15a's "every receipt to every holder" already forbids. HOLDS.
- **QF5 (item 4).** A repayment whose rail the verifier did not check repays nothing; parts sum; a receipt and a claim for one proof count once (the build's test). A third party may repay the collective's debt and it counts: money is money. A payer's own receipt counts wherever it was paid, which is their signature on their money. HOLDS, save 6a and 6b.
- **QF6 (item 4).** An old stepping down registered again after a return takes nothing; one signed after the return counts; a second stepping down naming the first return is spent by a second return (`spent`, `view.rs:2684`, measures against the latest return). HOLDS, save finding 5.
- **F184 (item 5).** The relay's own record is nothing; the relay's own claim acknowledging it is nothing; a record of another object is nothing; two relays' records in one claim both count (the build's test). A relay cannot stop a record from counting; a payer's client can, by not acknowledging, which the cMIP states. A payer who runs their own relay and acknowledges it earns the share back, the self-referral shape, bounded by the share: stated. HOLDS, save 4 and 4b.
- **A version citing the settlement.** It changes nothing, nor does any version after it; a settling version naming the settlement changes nothing (the build's QF1 test). HOLDS.
- **The repo client's field 26.** It sorts the tips as hex strings, which orders as the bytes do, and cites each in `objects` in that order after the parent. HOLDS from reading.

## 7. QG1 to QG5: my reading of each, without deciding

- **QG1, a refund to a bare key, or to nobody.** The bare key already has a pointer of sorts: the claim it signs says where a refund goes (Finance rule 10a, claim key 7). The natural proof of repayment is the collective's claim for the repayment, naming the payment owed back, with the rail's proof showing it reached the rail and address that key's claim named; the code's `to_own` would take `RefundTo::Key` with that claim's refund rail as the payer's "own act", and the payer's receipt path stays unavailable, since a bare key signs no receipt. For money owed to nobody, the choice is stark: if it blocks a closing it blocks it forever, and anyone who pays anonymously without a key under a stretch offer freezes the collective's ending for a penny (6g is the identity twin); if it does not, "owes nothing" would mean "owes nothing anyone can claim", which is what rule 10a already says of such money: "the obligation stays open and visible, and nobody can take it". I read the second as consistent with the texts, and the first as the plain reading of F189 (7); Nobody, allegedly, chooses.
- **QG2, which of the payer's pointers.** Finance's shape for every other payment is: the version that counts is selected from the one paid's own acts, and a disowned act holds nothing. Money owed back has one such act, the payer's claim for the original payment, whose key 7 names a refund rail where it names one. My reading: follow key 7 where the payer's claim names it; otherwise the payer's pointer version that counts as rule 14 selects from the payer's own acts on that payment (its claim, its acknowledgement of the receipt), to the vault for older ones; never "any version", and never an act Identity does not keep (6a). The payer's own receipt counts wherever, as now.
- **QG3, how a verifier knows a delivery record.** As built, two verifiers answer differently for one split (4d), and the rule in Law is in force only for the ones that were told. The general rule, "an act of a cMIP offered as evidence for a role share counts only when the payer's claim acknowledges it, whatever the cMIP", needs no telling and matches the reason given for F184, which is not about relays but about usage: the one other evidence it would touch is "a record signed by the identity running a service" (rule 22, F116), and "paying for usage invites faking usage" applies to it in the same words. The referral is already in the payer's claim. I read the general rule as the simpler and the safer; its cost is that a service's record, too, needs the payer's acknowledgement.
- **QG4, the chain of judgment for the judge of forks.** Field 21 exists for the judge who does not answer, with a period on the time reference after which the next may act (rule 34a). The judge of forks is a judge among field 13. Not following the chain makes one outsider's silence freeze a deal on its reference for good, with no party able to move it, which is more than DQ5 gave any party. I read following the chain as consistent with the texts, with its usual conditions: a time reference, and the request as the start of the period; a deal with no time reference has no chain to follow. Finding 1 should be answered first, since a chain of judges who may each speak twice multiplies it.
- **QG5, Production's other kinds called Modules.** Media types and signature schemes have kinds of their own in the specification format, so the Module definition listing them says two things about one object; the core's table giving "time sources" to verifying modules contradicts its own Law task table, where the time reference is a task a cMIP fills. I read both as wording to align with the change already made for units, with no rule behind them.
