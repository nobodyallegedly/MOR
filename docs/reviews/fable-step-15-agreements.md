## In plain words first

Agreements is the layer where the three principles are tested hardest, because here one identity's act decides something for another: a member's signature completes a collective's clone, a member's resignation draws a line the collective lives by, a keeper's record places an act, a creditor's release lets a collective close. The design's answer to that, F109, is right: for anything a collective does, "before" and "after" are judged on the collective's own chain, and what the collective's own line placed stays placed. The ordering harness checked that answer against a real clock on thousands of worlds, and verifier2 checked the endings against the library on four thousand. Where the text and the code follow F109 all the way, they hold.

**They do not follow it all the way, and F297 now makes that visible.** F297 says: another identity's act counts only while that identity's chain keeps it; voided by its signer's rotation, it is shown as a dispute and decides nothing. Agreements already has the one exception that F109 needs, C5: a member's *signature* the collective placed before its line registering the member's rotation stays valid for the collective. But a member's *resignation*, a party's *declaration of absence*, the signature acts that complete such a declaration, and the member's own signatures that a declaration's field 1 is checked against, get no such sentence. The library applies Identity's current verdict to them, today, at every evaluation. So a member resigns, the collective registers it and lives a year under that line, and then the member rotates and disowns the resignation (a stolen phone, they say): the record is no longer a line, every clone it wrote is not in force, every departure it registered never happened, and every record after it, which follows the agreement chain one clone at a time, finds the wrong parent. One person's theft protection unwinds a collective's year. That is A1, and it is the finding to lead with. The fix is the principle F109 already states, written once for everything a line places or registers, and written into F297 as its exception.

The same question reaches the endings. A fork is final once complete (IT1); the members sign it by chain signatures, which no rotation can void, and that part is sound. But each successor signs for its debts by an ordinary signature act, and a closing's "owes nothing" rests on creditors' receipts and releases: all acts of other chains that F297 lets be voided later. "Final" cannot rest on another chain's future. A2 proposes the two smallest repairs: successors sign a fork as members do, by chain signature; a closing relies on a receipt or release only where an act of the collective in its history acknowledges it, so that A1's principle covers it.

The rest is what a verifier says when it cannot tell. The library refuses to complete a *fork* while an act its history names is not held, and says so; it completed a *closing* in the same state, and verifier2 did not. That is fixed in code (A3), with a failing test and a verifier2 story. Underneath it lies a broader gap (A4): "every other act counts as made after the line, whenever it was really made" is read by the library as *after* even when the line's history has holes, and *after* feeds the tie rule, which says *void*. F298 says unknown there. Rule 84's fallback to Identity's succession is undefined, uncoded, and now needs F300's "confirmed" (A5). The core verifier does not judge Agreements acts against their formats at all, only the view does, so F295 is not yet applied to this layer (A6). And some wording: type 11 is both "the import" and "retired" (A7), the suite still has three sentences about a concurrency rule the text withdrew (A8).

## Findings

Severity: **breaks a guarantee** / **leaves a gap** / **wording**. Kind: **text** / **code** / **instrument**. Pattern: one of the four of round 2, or "fits none", named.

### A1. What a collective's line placed can be unplaced by another chain. Text, then code. Breaks a guarantee (what others relied on stands; the way out). Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party, and a rule that protects that party traps the collective.

**Sentences.** Rule 6 and "Signature (type 1)": "A signature counts only while it is valid under Identity's rules. The exception is a signature a collective placed before its line registering its signer's rotation (rule 6, C5)." "Made before, made after", 4: "A signature act of that member made with the key the rotation replaced, and placed before that line, stays valid for the collective, whatever the rotation kept or disowned, where it signs an act the collective's key holders also signed." Nothing like it is said for the other acts a line registers or relies on. "Record (type 17)", field 3: it registers "a resignation or stepping down (type 16) by a party", "an abandonment declaration removing such a party's voice". "Abandonment declaration (type 13)": "A declaration counts only when it passes rule 87's checks: it is valid under Identity; ... field 1 is the last version of that agreement, back from it, that the party signed by a valid signature act". And the promise these must keep: "Made before, made after", point 3 ("A departure takes effect at the collective's line"), rule 87 ("A later act of the party, or a later contest, never undoes what was put in force"), the reasoning "What others relied on stands (Flaw F)", and F297 as decided for Identity: a voided act of another identity is shown, and decides nothing.

**Evidence.** `view.rs`, `record_eval_inner`: a registered resignation is checked with `!self.valid(&a.id)` → "it registers a resignation that is not valid" → the record is no line; a registered declaration goes through `declaration_as`, which checks `!self.valid(&h.id)` → the record is no line; and `declaration_as` finds field 1 by `sigs_on`, which keeps only the party's signature acts for which `self.valid(&s.id)` holds now. `valid` is Identity's current status: `Status::Valid` only; `Void` and `Disputed` are false. The one place the code softens this is `sig_counts`, for signature acts named in a record's field 2 or a rotation's declaration, which implements C5. The ordering harness models a member's rotation voiding *signatures* only (`harness/ordering/src/rule.rs`, `sig_valid`, on `Kind::Sig`); it never draws a resignation or a declaration that its signer's rotation later disowns, so it could not see this.

**Attack, in three steps.** Ana resigns from the label; the label registers the resignation by a record (its line), signs a clone without her, declares a rotation, and runs a year. Ana rotates her own identity and does not keep the sequence that held the resignation (the resignation is void under Identity rules 15 to 17; a theft, she says). Every verifier now reads the record as no line: the clone it wrote is not in force, the departure never took effect, Ana's voice is back for every rule, and every later record, which must clone "the agreement in force for the record" (rule 72), has the wrong parent and puts nothing in force. The collective is not broken in rule 93's sense (no rotation failed), so it has no rollback either. The same attack runs with a declaration of absence: the authority (a keeper's operator, a third party) rotates and disowns its declaration, or the member declared absent comes back, rotates, and disowns the signature on the version field 1 names, so that field 1 is no longer "the last version the party signed", the declaration fails rule 87 "judged at the point where the record names it", and the record "counts for nothing". Rule 87's sentence "a later act of the party never undoes what was put in force" is contradicted by the code, because a rotation is a later act of the party.

**The principle, once.** F109 already says what should hold: the collective's line is the collective's own act (rule 34's "a signer's own acts keep force, disowned but relied on, visible as disputed", applied to the line), and what it placed, it placed. So, for Agreements, F297's exception is: *an act of another identity that a line of a collective names, registers, or relies on to count what it registers, keeps, for that collective, the effect the line gave it, whatever its signer's later rotation kept or disowned; its status under Identity is unchanged and every client shows it, as a dispute on the line.* This is C5 extended from "a member's signature act on an act the collective's key holders also signed" to every act a record, rotation or rollback names in field 2 or 3, or in its declaration's elements, and to the signature acts a registered declaration's authority needs and the signatures its field 1 is checked against, as placed at that line. It is mechanical: the line names the acts, the verifier holds them, and "placed before the line registering the signer's rotation" is what `sig_counts` already computes.

**Stated cost, to accept or not.** A thief holding a member's signing key can resign the member, and once the collective's line registers it, the member's rotation does not undo the departure for the collective: they come back by a constitutional clone naming them (rule 70's "named again"). That is the cost C5 already states for signatures ("a stolen member key can finish an act the collective signed, never start one"), and a resignation needs no act of the collective to start, so it is a little wider: a stolen key can take a voice out of a collective until the collective puts it back. The alternative is the attack above, which lets one member's chain unwind the collective's. The F109 reasoning chose the collective's chain for exactly this reason.

**Smallest fix.** Text: one paragraph under rule 6 (or "Made before, made after", 4) stating the principle; rule 87's and the record's field-3 checks worded "judged as placed at the line", with "valid under Identity, or voided after the line placed it" where they say "valid"; F297's log entry carrying the exception. Code: `record_eval_inner`, `declaration_as` (`sigs_on`), `recovery_signatures` and `rollback_departures` take `sig_counts`'s test in place of `valid` for registered acts, and show the dispute. Cost: one decision, one paragraph, four functions, one freeze scenario. **Not made in code:** it is a decision (A1's question), not a clear rule.

### A2. An ending's completeness rests on other chains' acts that can be voided after it is final. Text, then code. Breaks a guarantee (a complete ending is final, IT1). Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party.

**Sentences.** "Fork (type 19)": "Every member it lists ... signs it by a chain signature (Identity type 16)"; "Each successor a debt is assigned to adds a signature act naming it (type 1), as for terms (N13)"; "*Reading, to confirm (F132): ... a successor's signature stays a signature act, since two endings sharing a successor share that side's members, whose chains order them.*" "A complete ending is final (F131, IT1)." "Closing (type 20)": "the collective owes nothing ... fulfilled in full by receipts a verifier holds, or ended by its creditor's release (Money type 4, rule 92)"; "*A closing's own conditions (receipts that fulfil its debts, "holds nothing") may still change as a verifier comes to hold more acts.*" Rule 92: a release "counts wherever a verifier holds it, published or not".

**Evidence.** Members' chain signatures are chain acts: Identity section 7 says a chain signature "competes for its position" and "judges no act", and nothing in Identity voids a chain act once it counts; so the members' part of an ending cannot be unsigned later, and the library reads it so (`ending_sigs_inner`). But `fork_inner` checks each successor with `signed_act`, which is `valid_sigs`, which is Identity's current status of the successor's signature act (a collective's everyday act, voidable by the successor's next rotation); and `to_hand_out` finds what a successor inherited through the same `valid_sigs`. `closing_inner` takes `open_debts`, which rests on `paid_toward` (receipts whose status is valid now) and `debt_released` (a release whose status is valid now). Under F297, a successor that rotates and disowns its signature on the fork unmakes the fork's completeness in every verifier; a creditor that rotates and disowns its release reopens a closed collective's debt, and the closing after which "the collective's keys count for nothing" is no longer complete. Neither is an attack on money (the successor still owes what it signed for under rule 34, visibly disputed); both are attacks on *finality*, the guarantee the right of exit rests on.

**Smallest fix.** Two readings to decide, each checkable. (a) A successor signs a fork as a member does, by a chain signature with its own chain key (Identity type 16): the "reading, to confirm" at F132 is reversed; the cost is one chain-key ceremony per successor per fork, in a process the text already calls "slow and heavy, as rotations are". (b) A closing relies on a receipt or a release of another identity only where an act of the collective in the closing's history acknowledges it (Envelopes `acks`), or the receipt is the collective's own: then it is an act the collective's chain placed, and A1's principle covers it; a receipt or release nobody acknowledged is shown, and the closing waits. That matches "a record counts only alongside the act it names", "counterparties wait until a later act of the collective cites", and the collective client's existing habit of acknowledging what it relies on. Cost: a closing needs the collective to acknowledge each settlement before it closes, which its client can do in the closing's own history. The same question touches a stake transfer (completed by the buyer's signature act on the seller's line) and a grant's acceptance (the grantee's signature act); both fit the principle as "placed by the relying identity's own chain" and need one sentence each once A1 is decided.

### A3. A closing completed on a history the verifier does not hold. Code. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check (what the verifier does not hold). **Fixed in code.**

**Sentences.** "Fork (type 19)": "A verifier that does not hold every act that history names cannot tell what it must hand out: for it, the fork is not complete." "Closing (type 20)": "its line holds, and the agreement it names is the one in force there, as for a fork"; "the collective owes nothing: every obligation that binds it and lies before its line, that is, in the history it cites". F298: unknown, never complete, on partial knowledge.

**Evidence.** `fork_inner` calls `line_missing` and refuses while anything the history names is not held. `closing_inner` did not: `at_line` checks only that each kept tip's line can be rebuilt, and `history` records deeper citations it cannot follow in `missing`, which the closing never read. So a closing whose history cites, through an act it holds, an act it does not hold was complete for the library, and the collective closed, while a debt could sit in the unheld part. verifier2 disagrees with the library here (its reading 13 applies to both endings, `agreements_endings.py` line 523), and the comparison never saw it because the reference's generator holds every act ("What this did not cover": "the 'not held' rule is tested only by verifier2's own scenario").

**Fix made.** `closing_inner` now refuses, as `fork_inner` does, naming the count of unheld acts ("not held"). Failing test: `core/tests/agreements_collective.rs`, `a_closing_whose_history_names_an_act_not_held_is_not_complete` (mirrors the fork's last case; fails on main, where the closing is complete). verifier2: a story and test, `test_a_closing_whose_history_names_an_unheld_act`, which passes on verifier2 as it was (its reading was already the text's); run with `python3 -m unittest test_agreements_endings` from `verifier2/`. One wording point stays for A4: both endings say "not complete" where F298 says "unknown".

### A4. "Made after", and so "void", on partial knowledge. Text and code. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check.

**Sentences.** "Made before, made after", 1: "Every other act of the collective counts as made after it, whenever it was really made." Point 7: "an act of the collective a fork's or closing's history does not hold counts for nothing"; "a member's voice a line removes is gone for every act the line's history does not hold". Rule 58: "an action citing no decision ... counts for nothing". Rule 47: "A settling version naming a version this verifier does not hold is read as a plain version until the verifier holds it" (F196). "Grant (type 9)": "a verifier not holding every grant it lists cannot tell, so fails closed."

**Evidence.** `before_struct` answers *false* whenever an act is not in the line's rebuilt history, including when that history has `missing` entries; `before` and `sig_before` turn that into "after", `voices` and `cited_against` turn "after" into "the voice is gone" or "void" (the tie rule), with no unknown on the way. `uncited` does better: an action citing an act the verifier does not hold is `Missing` (unknown), not "counts for nothing", so the code is already ahead of rule 58's words there. The result today: a verifier with one hole in a line's history voids, for itself, every act of the collective it cannot place, and a second verifier without the hole binds them; two honest verifiers disagree on *void*. The text's only acknowledgement is for forks (A3). Rule 47's "read as a plain version" is the same shape with a safer outcome (the deal stays forked for that verifier, nothing is voided), and "fails closed" names no verdict at all.

**Smallest fix.** Text: one sentence in "Made before, made after", 1: "While an act the line's history names is not held, whether an act is before that line is unknown; nothing is void on it." Rule 58: "counts for nothing" → "is unknown while the decision it cites is not held, and counts for nothing where it is held and is no decision of the collective". The fork's and closing's "not complete" → "unknown" where the cause is an unheld act, so a client can tell the two apart (today only the `why` string does). "Fails closed" → the verdict meant (unknown). Code: `history` already carries `missing`; threading it through `before` as `R<Option<bool>>` (or a `Missing` error) is one signature change and its callers. **Not made:** it changes verdicts across the tie rule and voices and needs the text's word first; the question is A4's.

### A5. Rule 84's fallback to Identity's succession is undefined, uncoded, and not yet F300. Text. Leaves a gap. Pattern: a lower layer's rule depends on data only a higher layer holds, turned around: a higher layer's rule rests on a lower layer's declaration it never reads.

**Sentences.** Rule 84: "Where no plan exists, the identity-level succession declared in a rotation (Identity) applies, and otherwise the abandonment clause." Rule 83: "A seat passes automatically ... only if every party whose voice remains signed that version of the succession plan (Q14) ... Otherwise the seat successor is a nomination, approved under the constitutional change rule (rule 64)." Identity rule 39: "Declaring a succession does not end the identity. Both may coexist while followers and agreements move across." F300: a succession counts only once the successor confirms it; clients follow only a confirmed one.

**Evidence.** The library implements succession only through a plan in the terms (mark `[3, party]`, `plan_static`, `plan_at`); `view.rs` reads Identity's succession field nowhere (grep `succession` finds plans only), and no test exercises rule 84. The text does not say what "applies" does: whether the Identity successor takes the seat (automatically? by nomination?), the stake (a stake is property and moves by a transfer the seller signs by chain signature, rule 10; nothing here moves it), or the party's place in the key grammar. Under rule 83's own reasoning the seat cannot pass automatically: an automatic entry needs "consent given in advance, by everyone still counted", and an Identity succession is the party's act alone. And since Identity rule 39 keeps the old identity alive, the stake needs no successor: it stays with the identity and is paid at its pointer.

**Smallest fix.** Rewrite rule 84: "Where no plan exists, a succession the party declared under Identity and its successor confirmed (F300) makes the successor a nomination for the party's seat, approved as rule 64 says; the stake stays with the party's identity, which Identity keeps alive, until a transfer or the abandonment clause moves it; a succession not confirmed nominates nobody." That is checkable (the confirmation is an act the verifier holds), needs no code until a client proposes such a nomination, and keeps "no stranger enters the constitution without the voices that remain". Cost: a sentence; a scenario.

## The verdict vocabulary (attack 7)

The admission test asks that a verdict be something every verifier decides the same way. Agreements uses: *valid / invalid* for formats; *draft / complete / in force* for versions; *counts / counts for nothing* for acts in a collective's name; *void* for the tie rule; *no line / no fork / no closing / no ending* for acts that are something else than they claim; *incomplete* for an ending; *backed / not backed* for a grant key's act; *unknown* for a cMIP's answer it does not hold. Two of these are overloaded. "Not complete" means both "I hold everything and a debt is missing" and "I do not hold everything" (A3, A4); the first is a verdict, the second is F298's unknown. "Counts for nothing" means both "this act is of a kind that can never count here" (a witness act, F156) and "this act's decision is not held" (rule 58), and only the first is final. The library's `AgreementsError::Missing` and `OwnAttempt` are unknowns, and its `why` strings carry the distinction a status should.

## What held under attack

- The members' side of an ending: chain signatures are chain acts, ordered on one line each, never voided by a later rotation; U1, U2, U4, U4b held on 13,580 signatures in verifier2's comparison and hold by reading.
- F109 for the collective's own acts: a backdated fork, an act on a left-out branch, a friend's acknowledgement, a member's forgotten device: each lands where the text says, and the harness's clock agrees.
- The tie rule where the history is held: an act outside an ending's history is void, an adopted one stays adopted, and a departure racing a citation takes no voice off the cited act (IC9, F131, F132).
- "Done" cannot be forged from the outside: the inner `to` is signed.
- The broken collective: no fork or closing while broken, the rollback bounded by the constitutional rule of the agreement before the break, a rollback naming a valid rotation breaking the collective again, visibly.
