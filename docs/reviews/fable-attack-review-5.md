## In plain words: the ten worst breaks, ranked by harm

1. **The unknown party's shield is one line of `powers` wide.** A seller writes into the version Ana buys under a power of kind `assent` whose rule says "the seller may assent for every filler". Ana's assent to that version is her signature on the version that gives the power, and the power is one "they gave" (5.10.7); the seller then assents, for Ana, to version 3, and version 3 reaches her, by 5.10.7's own second clause. The same door stands open in her version's K1 and K5 cMIPs, which are given "the acts held" and may read version 3 from them. The wall of terms comes back through the sockets, and nothing in the grammar bars it.
2. **One signer un-signs an update already in force, and can re-sign it later.** Ben assented to an update; he then signs any Statement naming the same `previous` as that assent. His line forks, his assent is a tip that has stopped, and it is no longer among the assents the gate counts. Two honest verifiers then differ on whether the update is still in force. Ben can resolve his own fork, continuing whichever tip suits him. No keeper helps: a fork of a line is seen "whatever a keeper shows".
3. **The keeper lies safely exactly where its word matters, and its silence is a lock.** A keeper's order is contradicted by knots only where acts name one another; the acts a keeper is there to order are the ones that do not. By holding one act back a keeper picks the winner of every counter signature, every fork and every late update, and signs no lie. A keeper that stops answering stops the agreement for good, since every update is judged per the keepers the version it replaces names, whatever keepers the new version names. His "both keepers are needed" doubles that lock.
4. **In a bare agreement, one needed signer freezes every verifier at "unchecked" for ever, and a withdrawal against a completion can never be resolved.** An assent whose `previous` names an act nobody holds counts for the gate, but every chain through that signer's line is unchecked, so the pin is unchecked and "which Terms are in force" with it. A counter signature that nothing orders against the completing assent is a fork that is not of a line, and draft 26 gives a resolving form "for a fork of a line only".
5. **The pin and lateness by knots give two honest verifiers two answers in ordinary cases.** Whether the author's own Terms act counts as the author's assent; whether 5.5.4(a)'s "an act without which L does not pass O's gate" is read act by act (then an update with three assents under a two-of-three rule can never be shown late); whether 5.5.5(a)'s "in order" sentence makes a second assent's update late when 5.5.4's test does not; whether the acts "that put that version in force" include the pin acts; whether a Text act's `refs` and every act's `prev` are knots (draft 26 says any field; encoding 3 says no); whether two acts with the same `previous` but ordered by `prev` are a fork.
6. **A power widens itself.** An agent assents, for the giver, to a version that widens the agent's power; that assent is counted in her name, and so, by 5.6.1(b)'s own words, is her signature on the version that gives the power. Nothing about her is done without her signature, and her signature is the agent's.
7. **Three hooks hang on nothing.** The version a payment "read" is carried, in Money draft 14, by the receiver's receipt as readily as by the payer's claim, so the seller can name which version the buyer read; a leg report and a buyer's confirmation have no bytes under encoding 3 and no report block in the MICS, so every answer of §5.11 about reports is unchecked today; Envelopes draft 12 has no baker, no transfer and no rule for Terms that name an envelope, so §4.4's case 7, 5.1.4's envelope clause and 5.11.9 point at nothing.
8. **The words can be voided by their author, hidden from the unknown party, or read by a cMIP as something else.** A rotation that disowns the Text act leaves Terms in force whose words are void, and Terms acts cannot acknowledge them; the keeper rescue of Identity's rule 34 names an act type draft 26 does not give. Private words bind a buyer who was no recipient when they were sealed. A K1 cMIP's parameters may say where the real words are.
9. **The hashed text leaves verifier-changing byte choices to an unhashed file, and an agreement cannot be carried across drafts.** The set's label and joining, the keeper field's form, which hashes are `TEXT`, and case 8's declaration are open in encoding 3; two verifiers under one `SPEC_DRAFT_26` may answer "pass" and "not this encoding" to the same bytes. A Terms act under a later draft is "not an Agreements act" to a draft 26 verifier, so every agreement made under draft 26 is frozen at its last draft 26 version when draft 27 comes.
10. **The principle protects too much where it protects an insider's filling, and the purpose promises what §5.8 denies.** Any filling is an unknown party's "whenever it was made", so a known party who fills a spot under version 1 holds something no update by the others can touch. §1 says no grammar rule "needs a particular server, platform or software author to remain"; a named keeper is a particular server, and 5.8.4 needs it to remain.

The shape under them, as I read it (INFERRED): draft 26 moved every rule about how people behave into eight sockets and kept, as grammar, only how acts are read. The guarantees it still makes in its own name (no update reaches a filling; a fork stops both sides; nothing about me without my signature; a keeper's lie is signed) are each defeated by what the sockets, the keeper or one signer's own line may put into the acts the grammar then reads. The grammar reads honestly; what it is given is chosen by whoever wrote the version first.

## 3. What the principle cannot protect, and what it protects too much

**Cannot protect** (each traced above): what the sockets of her own version say (2.1.1 to 2.1.3); her share under a later K6 (2.1.5); anything beyond a sentence on the record once the known parties dissolve and re-form (2.1.4); words she cannot read (2.5.2) or that are voided (2.5.4); her term on a clock and her filling per keepers that the seller chose before she existed (2.10.2, 2.2.3); her standing as a filler at all, where the seller's keeper never shows her proof (2.2.3). In every one, the acts the grammar reads were chosen by the seller before she arrived, and the grammar reads them honestly.

**3.1 Protects too much: the insider's filling.** §3.2: "Any filling of a role is an unknown party's, whenever it was made." **3.2 Protects too much: a defective version cannot be fixed, and a dead clock freezes it.** Sam's V2 words give unlimited use for one unit, by a slip. **5.3 Carrying to another system.** The acts are signed bytes and the rules (Development: RV32IM programs with no clock) travel; a verifier elsewhere needs the identities, the Text act, the cMIPs and the keepers' records. **5.4 Carrying across drafts of MOR.** Encoding 3, E2: "Acts written under a hash stay that text's, whatever is written later; a later draft is a new specification with a hash of its own, and nothing written under this one is updated." A Terms act under `SPEC_DRAFT_27` whose `replaces` names a draft 26 version is, to a draft 26 verifier, "not this encoding": not an Agreements act, never reaching 5.5. ## 6. What the introduction promises that the rules do not keep, and the reverse

**6.1 "No grammar rule needs a particular server, platform or software author to remain"** (§1). 5.8.4: an act counts for order "only once those keepers show it"; 5.4.1: "an act they do not yet show is not yet in force"; 5.5.5(d): a replacement is judged per the keepers the original names "whatever keepers the new version names". A named keeper is a particular server, and an agreement that names one needs it to remain, or stops (2.2.4). The purpose and §5.8 contradict each other in so many words. TRACED.

**6.2 "Every grammar rule is checked from copies of acts alone, plus the answers of the keepers"** (§1). Also plus the cMIPs in eight sockets, which are not acts (Development draft 12 names a specification by content; "A specification is a Text act, named by its id" is queued), plus the clock's answers. Who is bound (K3, K4), who is a party (K5), what the Terms say (K1) and whether they hold the Control task (K1) are not read from acts alone. TRACED.

**6.3 "Agreements is not to be used bare"** (§0). The rules then give a complete bare way of agreeing: unanimity of the known parties for the first version (5.4.1) and for every update (5.5.7(e)), one leg (5.11.5), a party who receives for others as a hand (5.11.2). A verifier answers a bare agreement in full and warns nobody; the warning is a sentence the hash carries and no rule enforces (Q22, the lead's). What "bare" costs is not in the text: no exit, no buyer, no clock, no keeper, each a stated cost in the companion only.

**6.4 "the keeper: the way the drafters of an agreement agree on where the agreement is kept"** (§0). The rules make the keeper decide order, never storage: "Copies of the acts may be lodged at any relay" (5.8.2), and copies elsewhere count for nothing. Where the agreement is kept promises a place for the bytes; the rules give a monopoly on "after". His word was "stored"; the lead read "kept" (the ledger, 27 November, 07:26); the text promises the first and rules the second. TRACED.

**6.5 "Knots ... taken up where this text treats relying on acts (§5.13)"** (§0). §5.13 states what a knot is and the one reading of "shown after"; nothing in the text treats relying (N10 is client conformance, in the companion). A reader of the hashed text alone finds the pointer and no discussion. TRACED (the companion, 4.2, item 4, says so).

**6.6 "who is bound by which Terms, and what is shown, unknown or contradicted"** (§1). The answer words are shown, not shown, unchecked, invalid, valid, not valid, in force, fork, late (§3.6); none is "contradicted". The nearest are "not shown, the keepers disagree", "fork" and "disputed", each narrower.

**6.7 The reverse: what the rules promise that the introduction does not.** "Protect the unknown party" is nowhere in the hashed text; §5.10 applies half of it (the gate) and 5.10.7 undoes the other half (the power). "An agreement never ends" (§3.3, 5.5.6) is a promise with teeth (a dissolution is read as "going on") that the introduction does not make. §3.5 legislates three verbs for every layer. 5.12.9 binds clients. A reader who trusts the introduction expects a small grammar of reading; the rules also carry a theory of agreements (never ended, no exit, fillings untouchable) that the introduction calls "a language, used to decide how to agree".
