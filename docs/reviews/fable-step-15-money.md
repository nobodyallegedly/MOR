## In plain words first

Money's promise is narrow and it keeps it: a payer who followed the published pointer and vault is protected, a receiver cannot hide money without the payer's silence, and a thief holding the signing key alone cannot collect the backlog, cannot open a unit the vault does not cover, cannot take more than a unit's limit in one payment, and cannot make a payer's payment count against the owner after an anchored lock change. I tried the four questions the brief asks and found no new way through. The vault's arithmetic (the smallest limit of several entries, fail closed on an uncovered unit) is exactly the text's; refunds go to the committed key and never to whoever shows the proof; a receipt naming another purpose than the payment's commitment is refused by the rail's own rule; the lock change's point is the anchored home quorum, read per operator; a claim is judged by its earliest anchor; and every verdict I could follow is a function of the acts held, not of their order.

One code finding, fixed. **The core verifier showed a Money act as valid whatever its payload said and whoever signed it**: a payee pointer for Alice signed by Bob, an obligation signed by its creditor, an obligation with a field Money does not define, an act of type 9. Money's own decoder refused all of these, and the Agreements view, which applies Money's rules, refused them too; but the verifier the clients call for an act's standing (`Verifier::status`, the WebAssembly `status`) said valid. F295 decided this: invalid. Now the verifier, told Money's hash as it already is for F110, judges the payload and the signer, with a test that failed before.

What remains is small and mostly at the edges of the core. The fact that decides whether a payment on a push rail is a purchase or a refund, whether the rail is a request rail or a push rail, and whether a rail binds payee and purpose, lives today in each rail Module's code, not in a field of its specification a verifier can read (the text says so: "FORMAT OPEN"). That is a rule resting on a fact nobody can check from the acts, and it must close in Development's format before the freeze. Two words are used for one state ("unrecorded" and "pending"); a payer whose client cannot anchor on the payee's declared clock is not told it is unprotected; and Money's debts are judged only by a client that reads Agreements, where the text says an IOU needs Money alone.

Where Money leans on the Identity questions: on I1 it is already safe, since rule 29 says in so many words that a voided pointer or receipt "holds no pointer, signs no receipt and selects no version, whatever acknowledgement keeps it visible as disputed". On I2 it leans: a payee's genuine receipt on a line the verifier cannot rebuild is void today, so a payment answered only by that receipt is "not made"; with I2 answered as recommended it becomes unknown, which is the honest answer for a debt.

## Findings

Severity: **breaks a guarantee** / **leaves a gap** / **wording**. Kind: **text** / **code** / **instrument**. Pattern: one of the four of round 2, or "fits none", named.

### M1. The core verifier showed a Money act outside its format, or by the wrong signer, as valid. Code. Fixed. Pattern: the thing that decides is signed by the wrong party (and nobody checked).

**Sentences.** Part three, payee pointer: "A payee pointer is valid only if signed by the identity it is for" (F46). Obligation: "An obligation is signed by the debtor. A creditor's statement of what it is owed is a claim, never an obligation" (F66). Rule 7: a receipt "is signed by the receiver of the hop"; "A claim's key 8 MUST verify under the key it names ... or the claim is invalid." F295: an act outside its specification's format, or of a type it does not define, is invalid.

**Evidence.** `money::Payload::decode` is closed and strict, and `money::check_signer` applies the signer rules; both were called only from the Agreements view (`debt_release`, `pointer_holding`, the receipt and claim evaluations) and the payment cMIP. `Verifier::status_cx` decoded Identity payloads only, so for an act under Money's hash it answered valid on the signature and the binding alone. A client asking the WebAssembly verifier for the standing of a pointer, an obligation, a receipt or a release got "valid" for a forged one; the desk and the collective client, which go through the Agreements view, did not, so no client showed money moving on it, but the two answers to "is this act valid" differed.

**Fix made.** `chain.rs`: a verifier built with `with_mirs` (so it knows Money's hash) decodes a Money act's payload and checks its signer when the act is held (`Held::money`), and `status_cx` answers invalid where either fails; a verifier that does not know Money's hash judges nothing of Money's, as before. Test: `core/tests/money_f295.rs` (a pointer by the wrong identity, an obligation by its creditor, a key outside the format, an undefined type: invalid; the same acts by the right signer: valid; a verifier told only Identity's hash: not invalid). Two tests that used an empty-payload Money act as a stand-in for "an act of another specification" (`private_links.rs`) now use a cMIP's hash and an Agreements act, since a Money act outside its format is what F295 makes invalid. The stand-in claim in `chain.rs`'s F110 test (`{0: "a claim"}` under Money's hash) is now invalid as an act; the test still passes because `acknowledged` reads an acknowledging act's binding only, which is I1, and should be made a real claim when I1 is built.

### M2. Request rail or push rail, and whether a rail binds payee and purpose, are read from the Module's code. Text (Development's format). Leaves a gap. Pattern: a rule rests on a fact nobody can check.

**Sentences.** Rule 18: "Which rails are request rails and which push rails, the rail Module's specification says (payment cMIP)." Rule 11: "a rail Module declares whether it binds them (FORMAT OPEN, F140 item 1)." The payment cMIP: "a rail Module declares it, and a client reads it from the Module, never sets it by hand. Format open: the Development specification format has no field for it yet; until it has, each Module's code states what its text declares."

**Evidence.** `RailModule::kind()` and the binding of payee and purpose are constants in each Module's Rust (`modules/lightning`, `modules/onchain`), not fields of the specification an act names by hash. Two verifiers holding the same acts and different builds of a Module, or a Module whose text and code disagree, answer differently on the one question that decides whether a push-rail payment two holders received is a purchase or a refund (rule 18, F128 W4), and on which act decides a disagreement in payee or purpose (rule 11, F151). The text knows it; the freeze does not wait for it unless someone says so.

**Smallest fix.** Two fields in Development's specification format for the Module kind: the rail's kind (request or push), and what its proof binds (payee, purpose, previous hop: a set), read by the payment cMIP from the specification, never from code. Cost: Development's format before the freeze (its segment), and the two Modules' specifications republished with the fields. Put as Q1 (core business: Development's format, before the freeze).

### M3. "Unrecorded" and "pending" name one state. Text. Wording.

**Sentences.** Rule 18: "Until a verifier has the rail's answer, such a payment is unrecorded." Rule 8: the verification answer "is valid, invalid, pending or unknown". The plan's vocabulary (from suite v21) read "unrecorded" as the collective's actions chain not yet recording a sale (F127, W2), which is a different thing.

**Evidence.** The code has `Answer::Pending` and no "unrecorded"; the Agreements view's sale states are its own. A second verifier would not know whether "unrecorded" is the rail's pending answer or the collective's chain not yet having recorded.

**Smallest fix.** In rule 18, "such a payment is pending (rule 8)"; "unrecorded" kept, if at all, for Agreements' sale on a collective's chain. Cost: one word.

### M4. A payer whose client cannot anchor on the payee's clock is not told it is unprotected. Text. Wording (conformance). Pattern: a protection the owner chooses that others bear.

**Sentences.** Rule 29: "A payer's client MUST read the payee's clock before paying"; "a claim anchored on neither [reference] is not protected"; client conformance: "A payer's client MUST write the payer's claim at the moment it pays and SHOULD anchor it promptly". Stated cost (F181): the owner, by a lock change, "can put in doubt every payment it affects ... whose claim is not anchored by the point".

**Evidence.** The clock is a declaration naming an anchoring cMIP and a clock Module by hash. A payee may declare a clock Module no payer's client implements; every payer then anchors on nothing the rule compares, and the owner can later void every unanchored payment by one lock change (F181's cost, with the payer's defence removed). The payer's client reads the clock and pays anyway; nothing tells it to say so. Not an attack on the core's verdicts (they are as written) but on a payer who followed every rule it could see.

**Smallest fix.** One clause under rule 29's conformance: "A payer's client that cannot anchor on any reference the payee's clock names MUST say so before paying: the payment will not be protected against a lock change." Cost: one clause, no code in the core.

### M5. An IOU's discharge is judged only by a client that reads Agreements. Text and code. Leaves a gap (low). Pattern: a lower layer's rule depends on data only a higher layer's client holds, in the build.

**Sentences.** Dependencies: "Money never depends on Agreements ... a Money-only client, such as a wallet that sends tips, is a complete client. Where a Money state depends on Agreements, a Money-only client shows it as unknown." Rule 17: "An obligation with no such act, such as one naming no agreement act (field 4 absent), counts only if paid to the vault, unless the payee acknowledges it with an act of their own that holds a pointer; an Agreements client checks that (F66)."

**Evidence.** Rules 15 to 17 and 29 (discharge, release, which pointer a debt is paid to, good faith) are implemented in the Agreements view (`paid_toward`, `debt_release`, `payment_counts`, `pointer_holding`) and nowhere else; the payment cMIP's `Held::holding` and `payment_counts` return `None` for a client that cannot read them, so a Money-only client shows every debt as unknown, an IOU with no agreement included. The acknowledgement rule 17 names for an IOU is an Identity witness act or a Money act, which any client can read; the text sends it to "an Agreements client" only because that is where the code is.

**Smallest fix.** Either the text says plainly that an obligation's states (open, discharged, released) are shown by clients that read Agreements, and a Money-only client shows them unknown (true to the build; one sentence in rule 15), or a small Money view answers the IOU case (an obligation naming no agreement, paid to the creditor's pointer or vault, acknowledged by the creditor) without Agreements: some two hundred lines. I recommend the sentence for MOR 1, since no Money-only client exists, and the view for MOR 2.

### M6. Where Money leans on the Identity questions. Not a finding; said as asked.

- **I1 (evidence voided by its signer's rotation).** Money is already safe: rule 29's last paragraph says a pointer act the rotation did not keep "holds no pointer, signs no receipt and selects no version, whatever acknowledgement keeps it visible as disputed", and rule 17 selects the version from the payee's own acts on a kept line. A thief's pointer witnessed by an accomplice is disputed and powerless here. Rule 29 (a) also requires the payee's receipt to be "on a line of the payee's that the rotation kept"; a disputed receipt does not count. Nothing to change in Money whichever way I1 goes.
- **I2 (unknown where the verifier holds part of the picture).** Rule 29 (a) rests on whether the payee's receipt lies on a kept line, which the library decides by rebuilding the line; a receipt the verifier cannot place is void today, and the payment then counts only through the payer's anchored claim. With I2 answered as recommended, the payment is unknown for that verifier (the payment cMIP already answers unknown wherever its `Held` cannot read), and a debt it would discharge shows unknown rather than unpaid. That is the better answer; Money's text needs no change for it.
- **I3, I4.** Rule 29's self-hosted case ("the rotation's own anchor is its point") assumes a counting rotation; a self-host contested as I3 describes has none, so no lock change and the owner bears, consistent. Succession Money reads nowhere.

## The verdict vocabulary (attack 7)

For a receipt or claim as an act: **valid**, **invalid**, **unknown** (the act), and its **verification answer**, **valid / invalid / pending / unknown** with the Module's hash and a trusted party if any (rule 8, 9). For whether it **counts** for a payment: counts, or **counts for nothing** (rule 4: a rail the payee never named; rule 18: a wrong receipt), **neither counts** (rule 12: two sharing a payment without a batch), and a **disagreement shown on the receiver** with **the greater amount counting** (rule 11). For a pointer: counts, **contested** (a fork, rule 1). For a payment: **counts as made** or not (rule 29), **protected / not protected**, **affected** by a lock change, **undeliverable** (rule 5), **paid to the flow / to the vault**. For a purchase: **purchase**, **no purchase** (owed back), **pending** (the rail's answer), with Agreements saying which claim and what is owed. For an obligation: **unsigned** (Agreements), **open**, **discharged**, **released**, **past its terms**, **closed by clone**, **redirected** (the last three Agreements'; unknown to a Money-only client). For a refund: owed to an identity, **to the committed key**, or **unclaimable**.

Each is a state two verifiers holding the same acts must agree on, and each is decided from the acts and the rail Module's rule: nothing rests on a relay or a clock nobody declared (a clock is declared with the chain key, rule 29). "Unrecorded" restates "pending" (M3). "Undetermined" occurs only as a negation ("No payment is left undetermined", F128) and in Agreements' deadlines; Money produces no such verdict, and the plan's list can drop it for Money. Nothing else restates another under a second name; "counts as made" is a payment's state and "valid" an act's, and the text keeps them apart.

## What held under attack

- **A thief with the signing key alone.** Cannot change the vault or the clock (chain key, rules 2 and 29); cannot open a unit the vault does not cover (rule 3, fail closed, `destination`: `UnitNotCovered`); cannot take more than the smallest limit of a unit in one payment (F114, `min`); cannot collect the backlog through a new flow pointer, since the version that counts for a debt is the latest the payee's own acts on the agreement hold, on a kept line (rule 17; `pointer_holding`, `rule_14`); cannot re-issue a debt (only the debtor signs one, F66, now also refused by the core verifier, M1); cannot make a payment count after an anchored lock change except through the payer's own anchored claim or the payee's kept receipt (rule 29, `rule_15_by`). What it can do is stated: redirect the flow and the deals during the window, bounded per payment by the vault, until the lock change is anchored (F169, F170).
- **The payer who followed the rules.** Protected while the lock change is unanchored (`LockPoint::NotAnchored` → counts), and after it by the earliest anchor of any of its claims of the same payment (F178, F203: `PaymentClaims::on`, `anchors.earliest`), on the main reference or on the backup with the main counting whatever its point (F179, F182: `claim_in_time`). A payment that ignored the vault is not protected (`flow_followed_vault`).
- **Refunds (F80, F113).** Owed to the committed key, claimed only by a claim naming the same rail, proof, payee and amount, carrying a refund rail and the key's valid signature over the claim and its citations (`claims_refund`, `anonymous_message`); a routing node holding the preimage and the payee cannot claim it; a payment committing to no key leaves it unclaimable (`RefundTo::Nobody`).
- **One payment, one count (F200).** Payments told apart through the rail Module's `payment`, never the proof's bytes; a reorganised transaction is one payment; the commitment is recomputed from the receipt or claim and the rail's rule refuses a wrong one (the payment cMIP, `verify`).
- **Double entry.** A claim's amount is bound by the rail's proof; the greater amount counts until a matching receipt; where the rail binds payee and purpose, the commitment decides (rule 11).
- **The rails the payee accepts (F115).** A receipt or claim on a rail the pointer or vault entry it was paid to does not name counts for nothing (`address` in the cMIP); one implementing another payment cMIP than the agreement names is invalid (`verify_under`).
- **Order independence.** Every answer read here is a function of the acts, anchors and the rail's answer held, with the greater amount, the earliest anchor and the per-operator earliest receipt as the only aggregations, each order-free.
- **Fiat.** The trusted party is named in the answer (`Verification::trusted`).
