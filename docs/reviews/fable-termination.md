Input: F436, item 7 first, on `claude/lead-records`; `docs/s7r-report.md` (F437) and the texts as S7r left them on pull request 131 (Identity 16, Agreements 24, Money 14, Development 12, MICS v37, suite v37), rule 11's "owners' side's exit" above all, with rules 10, 31, 47, 49, 70, 71, 76, 85 to 92, 98, 99, section 22 and Development's task table; my `fable-rehearsal-1.md` (R5). Read, not run; a recommendation, nothing decided.*

## In plain words

The decision is right and the shape is small. Today the core already has terminations in half a dozen places under half a dozen names, and each says, in its own words, the same two things: who may end it, and that everything already owed or placed stands. Define that once, call it termination, give each kind of agreement its default (a publication: the owners' side alone; a deal: everyone; a collective: its own rules), and let parties who want more, a notice period, a minimum term, a veto, plug a cMIP into one new task that answers only "met, not yet met, unknown" about a terminating act. The core then imposes nothing and guards nothing, as decided; it only knows active from terminated.

Two things must be kept apart from it, and the texts as S7r left them blur the first. **Termination is not the way out.** S7r wrote the owners' exit as "the way out, which nothing a cMIP decides reaches", and that sentence must go: a veto on a publication's ending is a deal term the parties may sign, and the owners who signed it still have their way out, which is rule 98's list (a holder sells its share; the work is released; a member resigns; a collective closes), where no termination term reaches. **A party leaving by its own act is not a termination either**: resignation, a transfer of one's whole stake, stepping down from an area stay what they are; the word may describe their result (the place is terminated) but no termination term is ever asked about them. With those two lines drawn, every use of the one definition is safe, and I see nothing in it that a verifier cannot decide.

Two questions are Nobody, allegedly's before it is written: whether termination terms may also *widen* who may terminate (a partner ending its own place, which today no rule allows), and F437.1 (a successor's clause), where I lean against S7r: a stake passes with the clause that bound its holder.

**Ready to write**, as worded in section 2, after those two answers.

## 1. Where termination lives in pieces

So four uses, one definition; everything else keeps its name and its rule. The test that sorts them: termination is one side ending, for the future, what it or another party stands in, by that side's will under a rule the parties signed; whatever is a party's own way out, a judge's outcome, or a change of the constitution is something else.

## 2. The definition, as few sentences as the grammar needs

In Part one, beside "Made before, made after":

> **Active and terminated.** An agreement is active from the act that completes it (a deal, every party's signature on its founding version; a collective, its founding; a publication, every party's signature on its first version) until a terminating act counts; from then on it is terminated. A party's place in an agreement is active from the version that names it, which it signed, until a terminating act for that place counts, or the party leaves by its own act (a resignation, rule 70; a transfer of its whole stake, rule 10), or an outcome of the abandonment clause removes its voice (rule 89); from then on it is terminated. Where a verifier cannot tell whether a terminating act counts, the agreement's or the place's state is unknown from that act on, never active by default (rule 35).
>
> **A terminating act** is a version of the agreement that sells nothing, or that names a party's place terminated, or the clone rule 47 describes for a deal, signed under the agreement's termination rule: the default of its kind (below) where the agreement names no termination terms; where it names them (task 15), the act counts only when the termination terms' cMIP answers met. A party the terminating version newly names in a terminated party's place signs it to accept.
>
> **What stands.** Termination reaches the future only. Every obligation owed before it stays owed and every act placed before it stands; every payment received before it is split and paid as the version then in force says, by the party that received it (rule 31); a payment racing it is owed back (rule 11). Termination moves no stake and changes no holder (rule 98.2): a terminated party keeps what it holds and what it is owed, and its signatures placed before stay valid for what they signed. "Before" and "after" are read as "Made before, made after" reads them, by what each act's history holds.
>
> **The defaults, by kind.** A publication: its owners' side, under its own rule, terminates alone the publication, or any other party's place in it (its split service, a backup, a partner, the measurer); every other change needs every party. A deal: every party whose voice remains, by the clone of rule 47; each party's own way out is its transfer. A collective: its agreement ends as rule 91 says and a member's place as rule 70 says; a collective names no termination terms, and none reaches either.

That is eleven sentences. Rule 11's exit paragraph, rule 31's first sentence and rule 47's ending paragraph then each become one line citing the definition. The glossary gets "Termination" and "Active"; the MICS's Agreements section one sentence.

## 3. The defaults, kind by kind

- **A publication: the owners' side alone.** Agree. It is the owners' offer; a partner, a service or a measurer stands in it by the owners' leave, and is protected for the future by the terms it negotiated, not by a veto the core supplies. A lone seller's publication: its drafter. A collective's split agreement: the collective under its rule for Money, as rule 15 already has it.
- **A deal: every party.** Agree. A deal is what everyone signed, and it unwinds as it was made; a party's own way out is its transfer, which no one blocks. Two notes: a party who is absent is reached by the abandonment clause as today (rule 47's stated cost stands); and a deal may name termination terms too, for a notice or a minimum term before the end clone counts, which costs nothing to allow since the format is the same.
- **A collective's member: resignation alone.** Agree, with the distinction drawn above: resignation is the way out and stays rule 70's; the collective names no termination terms, so there is nothing for one to reach. The collective's own agreement ends by rule 91 alone.

**One default the decision does not name, for Nobody, allegedly:** a party of a publication other than the owners' side (a partner, a service, the measurer) has today no way to end its own place; it leaves only by a version every party signs, or by stopping work and waiting to be terminated. Three readings: (a) leave it, as the stated cost of standing in someone else's offer, the remedy being an obligation term; (b) let termination terms widen the default, so that parties may sign terms under which a named party terminates its own place (the cMIP then answers met for an act the default would not count); (c) make it a default: any party may terminate its own place, for the future. I lean (b): the core imposes no veto and should impose no lock either; what the parties sign is theirs; and it keeps the core's own defaults to the two the decision named. Under (b) the task's grammar does not change, only the sentence "where it names them, the act counts when the cMIP answers met" is read for any terminating act, whoever signed it. Under (a) that sentence is read only for acts the default lets through.

## 4. The task

**Number:** 15, in Development's table (rule 6: "a successor protocol may add tasks with new numbers; the numbers here never change" applies to MOR 1 adding one before its freeze too). MIR: Agreements. Task: termination terms.

**Where an agreement names it.** The task-to-hash maps (terms field 2, publication field 9) carry no parameters, and termination terms need them (a period, a payment, who is protected). The texts already solve this once: the time reference is `[ cMIP, params ]` in its own field (field 6). So: a new field in the publication (`? 13 => [ hash, any ]`, the termination terms: its cMIP, for task 15, and its parameters) and in a deal's terms the same shape under a new key, with the rule that a task-15 entry in field 9 or field 2 and that field name the same cMIP or the version is invalid, as fields 2 and 6 already do for task 10. A collective's terms carrying it are invalid (section 3).

**What it accepts and produces.** It accepts the agreement's version in force, the terminating act, and the acts the terminating act's history holds (the line as data; for a deal, what the act cites), and produces **met, not yet met, or unknown**. "Not yet met" is rule 34's "pending" under the decision's name; one word in the table says they are the same answer. A verifier checks the answer from that data, never on the cMIP's word (section 22's opening sentence), so a termination cMIP's conditions must be things the data shows: a point on the time reference passed (the agreement's own, task 10), a payment receipted, an act of a named party present or absent in the history. A condition no data shows is "unknown", by the same rule as everywhere.

**A verifier that does not hold the cMIP** answers unknown for the terminating act, and the agreement's state is unknown from it on: not active, not terminated. That is the definition's third sentence, and it is the only answer consistent with rule 35; "active by default" would let a verifier's ignorance keep a terminated service in place, and "terminated by default" would let it end what the parties protected. The client side carries the weight, as for absence proof: *a client MUST NOT sign an agreement naming termination terms it does not implement*, and the split service, which signs every version and every receipt, always holds it. The cost is the usual one: a reader without the cMIP shows the publication's state after the act as unknown.

**No other task gates a termination unasked.** Check, task by task, in Agreements 24: the condition cMIP (task 9) is read for an obligation or a payout (rule 34) and, since S7r, for nothing of a publication's split; the time reference (10) is read where a rule names a deadline; anchoring (11) where a rule reads an anchor; grant limits (12) for an act under a grant; absence proof (14) for a declaration; the control model (rule 99) for a collective's acts, and a publication names none. None is read for whether a version counts. One sentence closes the door S7r saw: *Only the termination terms (task 15) are asked whether a terminating act counts; no other task's answer, and no control model's, is read for it.*

## 5. The way out stays apart

Confirmed, with the sentences that keep it so. No termination term reaches: a member's resignation (rule 70: the collective names no termination terms, and the act is not a terminating act); a holder's transfer of its own share (rule 10: a party's own act, not a termination); a release ending a work's claim (rule 55); a collective's fork or closing (rule 91); succession (rule 83); anything rule 98 lists. The definition's sentence "a party leaves by its own act" names the first two as not terminations, and the defaults' last sentence excludes collectives.

A veto signed is a veto kept, and it traps nobody who has the way out: owners under a publication whose termination terms never answer met can still each sell their share (rule 10), or release the work (rule 55), after which the publication sells nothing ("Owners that ended"); a stated cost to write in one line beside the definition, so that nobody reads termination terms as a guarantee the core gives or withholds.

**Where the two blur now, in Agreements 24, to be undone when termination is written:**

- Rule 11: "**No cMIP removes the exit**: it is the way out, which nothing a cMIP decides reaches (rule 99)." Contradicts F436.7 and must go; the way out is rule 98's list, and a publication's ending is not on it.
- Line 1925 (the deal principles) and rule 47: "save a publication's owners' side's exit (rule 11), which no cMIP removes": the same, said twice.
- Rule 99's boundary ("never the way out") must not grow to include terminations; it does not today, and the new sentence in section 4 keeps a control model out of them.
- Rule 49, titled "Exit", and the phrase "the right of exit" in rules 48 and 57: keep "exit" for the principle only; retitle rule 49; use "termination" nowhere the way out is meant.
- Rule 70's "the party keeps its stake ... it is then a departed holder": a terminated place and an active stake are different things; the definition's "moves no stake" sentence says so, and the glossary's "Party's place" should point to rule 98.2.
- The abandonment clause's outcome 2 removes a voice "for the future" in the same words termination uses: say in rule 89 that it is a judicial outcome, not a termination, so the vocabulary does not invite a cMIP in.

## 6. Client conformance

Before a signature on an agreement naming termination terms, or on a version changing them, the client shows: the cMIP's creator and enough of its hash to tell it apart (Development rule 13, as S7r wrote for every specification); which terminations it reaches (the agreement, which places) and whom it protects; and what it adds to the default in the cMIP's own declared summary (a period and on which time reference; a payment and to whom; a signature required), with a plain warning where the terms let no termination count without a named party's signature (a veto) or before a point (a minimum term). The client MUST NOT sign terms naming a cMIP it does not implement. A holder buying into an ownership agreement is shown, before paying, the publications in force over the stake and their termination terms, since it steps into the owners' side bound by them; a buyer of a work is shown nothing, since a purchase accepts the sale and no term of the agreement. A client acting for the owners' side shows, before a terminating act, what the act ends and that everything received before is still split and paid.

## 7. F437

**F437.1, a stake successor under the absence clause.** S7r leans "the version in force where the line placed the succession, by analogy with buying in". I lean the other way: **a stake passes to a successor with the clause that bound its holder**, the version the predecessor last signed or bought under, carried through successions. A buyer accepts something by paying; a successor accepts nothing and signed nothing, it steps into a place. With S7r's reading the members can change the clause between a departed founder's resignation and its succession's placement and bind the successor by it, which is R2's hole one step on; with mine, the clause a stake carries is fixed by the last holder who accepted one, which a verifier finds as rule 87's field 1 check already finds it, following the successions back. The code's "latest placement" for a holder who bought more than once is right: each purchase is a fresh acceptance. Nobody, allegedly's to decide; this is my recommendation.

**F437.3, rule 34 scoped.** What S7r wrote ("a publication's split follows its plan's evidence, never a condition") is the right scope, and coherent with R10: a collective's conditional obligation at closing is an obligation, which rule 34 still reaches; only a publication's split is kept out. "Scoped to deals" would have been wrong; S7r was right not to write it literally.
