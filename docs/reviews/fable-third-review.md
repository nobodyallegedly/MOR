## Does the verdict hold

Yes, and this pass is what makes me say it with fewer words than before. Thirty places where a clear text and the core differed, found by reading the text for sentences no case exercised, all fixed in the core and none in a text: the texts held, and the method caught what two agreeing builders could not. The 47 questions are what reading could not settle alone. Taken one by one, almost every one closes with a sentence in the texts' own voice; the two that need a format change (Q-A10's outcome 2; Q-M2's claim batch, if wanted) are the only one-way doors, and they are named as such above.

Do the questions point at a weakness in one MIR, or where two meet? Two places, and neither is a design fault. The first is Identity's homeless procedure under partial knowledge (Q-I0, Q-I1, Q-I2, Q-I3, Q-I7): five questions, all of the form "what does a verifier say when it holds the acts but cannot judge one of them, or when the acts it holds would decide the position differently from another verifier's". Identity's principle is right (line 442) and its vocabulary is right; what is missing is one device, the two readings, which Money rule 12 already has and Q-I2's sentence borrows. Once "could change the answer" is defined by the two readings, the five questions share one answer. The second is where Agreements meets Money over purchases and payouts (Q-A1, Q-A3, Q-A4, Q-A5, Q-A6, Q-A12, Q-M1, Q-M2, S7f 1 to 3): Agreements says who is owed and Money says what was paid, and the questions are each a sentence one text wrote that the other did not echo. None contradicts the other in substance since F392; the fix is the echo, and the rule I would write down for the pass is that any sentence in Agreements that turns on a receipt's amount, purpose or payee cites the Money rule that reads it, and the Money rule says Agreements decides what it means.

What I would not freeze without: the nine in the first section, for the reasons given there, and Q-A22 and Q-A23 with them, because succession is the way out and the text today leaves a living party no act to trigger it and a verifier "unsettled" on the cases a real plan meets. Everything else is a sentence that makes the texts easier to read alike, or a build that can follow, with unknown as the safe answer meanwhile.

What I checked and what I only reason: every line cited, read; the functions named, read (`text_pointer`, `belongs_into` and the `keepers.rs` walk from the last review, `deal_walk` by S7e's description, the Money payload fields); nothing run. Where I say the core does something, it is S7g's run or my reading, and a session with `cargo` should run each case before changing anything, as F389's working rule already says.
