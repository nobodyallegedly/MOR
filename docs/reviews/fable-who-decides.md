# Who decides: the shape of Agreements before the freeze

*Fable, 3 November 2026. Input: `spec/MIR-agreements-draft-17.md` whole (its rule list, its parts and formats), the MICS's layers, the freeze suite's scenarios, `docs/state-of-mor.md`'s line for 3 November, and the decisions since the redesign (F401, F402, F416). Read, not run; a judgment of shape, nothing decided. Word counts are the prompt's; the parts I classify are the text's own sections.*

## In plain words

The suggestion is mostly right, and the last ten days have already been moving Agreements toward it without naming it: the shares left the split (F398), the collective lost its keys and gained a per-layer rule (F401, F402), and the models of ownership control went to cMIPs with the core keeping only the grammar of consent (F416). What is left to do is to say the shape once and finish the move. But the three-part frame (what we wrote; who decides what we did not; how to stop being bound) is one part short, and the missing part is most of what Agreements is for: **what others relied on stands.** A keeper's record, a version kept complete by reliance, an act placed by a collective's line, a receipt that proves a debt paid: none of it is text we wrote, a decider, or an exit. It is the guarantee that a promise, once acted on, cannot be quietly withdrawn by the one who made it, and it is the part of Agreements that answers nearly every attack the scars record. So the shape I would test against is four things: the grammar of what we wrote; the guarantee that what others relied on stands; the way out; and the deciders of the unwritten, which split in two, the last resort (every party; the holder alone) in the core and every richer model in cMIPs.

Against that shape, about half of Agreements as written is grammar or guarantee and stays; about a third is models of control and of deciding that the admission test puts in cMIPs; the rest is reasoning and scenarios that follow their rules. The way out stays whole. The one thing I would not do is read "who decides what we did not write" as "a cMIP decides": a collective whose last-resort decider lives in a module has handed its constitution to that module, and the thing MOR was built to prevent would be back one layer up.

## 1. Is this the right shape? The parts, by the admission test

The test: core if it is part of the shared language every act is written in, or a guarantee no module may be able to break; otherwise a cMIP's. I add the fourth thing above as a named guarantee, since it is one.

**Part one, agreements as they run.**
- *Making an agreement* (rules 1 to 6: proposal, signatures, cMIPs named, commitments of identities, what you sign is what you saw, positions, chains): **grammar.** Every act is written in it.
- *Claims and ownership* (7 to 10: binding a work, the stake rule, millionths, transfer): **grammar**, with one guarantee inside (no stake moves without its holder's signature, rule 48's root).
- *Publications and vows* (11 to 15): the publication's format and the purchase's link to its tag are **grammar** (Money reads them); "a sale is tied to the seller's agreement" and "money passes layer by layer" are **guarantees** (legible greed: nobody sells what the claim does not give them, and every cut is on the record); a vow's confirmation and contest are grammar. What is heavy here is the number of cases, not the rules.
- *Splits* (16 to 32): the split cMIP is already a cMIP; what the core keeps is **guarantee**: conservation (rule 21), only the plan (27), open obligations (30), the trust that remains (32). Role shares with evidence (22) and attention (29) are **cMIPs'** by the test: how a share is earned is a model, not a guarantee; the core keeps only that a role share paid is on the record.
- *Time, conditions and judges* (33 to 35): time and conditions are already tasks handed out; the chain of judgment is the core giving the language and a cMIP the answers. **Grammar for the language, cMIP for the deciding**; it is the one section already in the suggested shape, and could be the pattern for the rest.
- *Keepers* (36 to 44): **guarantee**, the fourth thing itself. Stays whole.
- *Changing an agreement* (45 to 49, 94 to 97): clone never modify, a false mark, a deal changes only with everyone, no stake reduced without its holder, exit: **grammar and guarantee**. The holders' rule, the owners' matters, issuance with pre-emption and proxies: **cMIPs'**, as F416 already decided for control; the core keeps the holders' rule's grammar (every holder; by head; by share) and the consent check.
- *When something goes wrong* (50 to 54): atomic where possible, failure attribution, the negotiation record, disclosure, standing: **grammar** (small).
- *Public domain* (55): **grammar**, one act.

**Part two, collectives.**
- *A collective* (56 to 61: an identity its members act for; done once sealed and on the chain; two chains; key grammar; layers and areas; every voice covered): the identity, the two chains, "done", the per-layer rule and the key grammar are **grammar and guarantee** (the way out of a collective lives in the key grammar and in "done"). Areas finer than a layer are a **model**; F402 already made them optional.
- *Tiers and clones* (62 to 68): "every field belongs to one tier", "which powers a clone needs", protected clauses: a **model of control**, and the admission test puts it in a cMIP. What the core must keep from it is one sentence: membership and the way out change only with the constitutional rule, every member by default, and a holder paid less signs. Counting a power (65) is grammar.
- *Members joining and leaving* (69 to 72): "a member can always leave alone" is **the way out**, core; changing members under the constitutional rule is core; leaving an area and "every complete clone written on the record" are grammar.
- *Grants* (73 to 80): acting for another within limits is **grammar**; limits (fields 3, 4) are already a cMIP's; import, acknowledgements, fate of acts and evaluation are the **guarantee** that a revoked grant's acts stay visible and what was relied on stands. Keep, shorter.
- *Succession* (81 to 84): "the core gives the language, not the answers" says it: **grammar** for the plan and its trigger, **way out** for what a seat and a stake do when a party is gone. Core.
- *Abandonment* (85 to 89): the clause, the declaration and the contest are grammar; the authority and the absence proof are already a cMIP's; the outcomes shrank to two (F397). Core, as it now stands.

**Part three, rare cases.**
- *Forks of records* (90): **guarantee** (what the line placed stands), small.
- *How a collective ends* (91, 92): **the way out**, core, whole.
- *A broken collective* (93): **the way out** (its way back), core; it is long because it is the one place the texts answer "the rule can no longer be met", and I would keep it and cut its prose.
- *Made before, made after*: the fourth thing for collectives: **guarantee**, core.

**Part four, formats.** Grammar by definition; they shrink exactly as much as the fields of the models move to cMIP parameters (terms field 2 already carries a cMIP's parameters).

So the suggested shape is right once the fourth thing is added and "who decides" is split. Of the eighteen sections, eleven are grammar or guarantee and stay, four are the way out and stay, three are models (tiers, the owners' control models, role shares) that the test sends to cMIPs, and the chain of judgment already shows how a section looks once the deciding has left and the language stayed.

## 2. The scars

Each is an attack or failure the text answers; for each, whether the smaller shape answers it in the core, hands it to a cMIP safely, or loses it.

- **A stolen signing key** (Identity rules 33 to 35, with Agreements rules 41, 42, 47's "relied on" and "Made before, made after"): the owner disowns, what others relied on survives as disputed. **Core, unchanged**: it is the fourth thing. Nothing moves.
- **A race between two payments, or one payment counted twice** (Money rule 12 and its pairs; Agreements rules 21 and 30): **Money's, unchanged**; the split's conservation stays a core guarantee.
- **A member who vanishes** (abandonment, succession, "every voice covered", rule 61): the clause, the declaration, the plan and the two outcomes are core; the authority and the proof of absence are a cMIP's already. **Core, as now.** What a cMIP must not be handed: whether a vanished holder's stake moves; that is the way out.
- **A thief's acts before a rotation, in a collective** ("Made before, made after", the line): **core, unchanged.**
- **A collective whose rule can no longer be met** (rule 93, the broken collective; rule 61): **core**, since the way back is the way out; the model that made the rule unmeetable (a tier, an area) may be a cMIP's, and then the cMIP must say what "broken" means for it, or the core's default applies (a fork of the line resolved under the constitutional rule). This is the one scar where handing a model to a cMIP needs a sentence in the core so that the way back never depends on the cMIP being held: *a collective whose control model's cMIP a verifier does not hold is judged under the constitutional rule alone for its way out.*
- **One member selling a co-owned work** (rule 14, F397's Q-A12): **core, unchanged**; legible greed.
- **A seller who disappears with the money** (rule 30's open obligation, the contest, the refund racing an ending): **core, unchanged**; the remedies that go further (penalty, escrow, arbitration) were always a cMIP's.
- **A pointer that bypasses the split service** (F397's Q-A3): **core, unchanged.**
- **A departed holder whose 25% becomes 25% of nothing** (rule 95's reasoning): the owners' matters is a model; what the core must keep is the guarantee behind it, *no sale or release of a stake a collective holds counts without the consent of holders who have no say*, which is one sentence of rule 95, not its mechanism. **Core keeps the sentence; the mechanism (by share, by head, proxies) is a cMIP's.**
- **A grant key stolen** (rules 76 to 80): **core, shorter**: revocation with the tie rule and the fate of acts are the guarantee; the evaluation of limits is a cMIP's already.

Nothing is lost. What moves to cMIPs is never the answer to an attack; it is the model inside which the attack was answered, and the answer stays in the core as a sentence that any model must satisfy.

## 3. What a smaller Agreements would be

**Parts.** One: the grammar of agreeing (terms, signatures, positions, chains, clones and marks, stakes and transfers, claims, publications and the purchase, contests and standing, the negotiation record). Two: the guarantees (what others relied on stands: keepers, reliance, the collective's line, grants' fate; legible money: conservation, only the plan, open obligations, layer by layer; no stake moved or reduced without its holder). Three: the way out (leave alone; transfer; succession; fork; closing; the broken collective's way back; public domain). Four: the deciders, in two halves: the last resort in the core (every party; the holder alone; a vanished party's two outcomes), and the tasks handed out (conditions; time; the chain of judgment; absence proof; grant limits; the split's computation; role shares; the models of control: tiers, areas finer than a layer, the owners' matters by share, issuance, proxies). Five: formats, reasoning, scenarios.

**Size.** Reasoned from the parts: Part one of today's text keeps about two thirds; Part two keeps about half (56 to 61, 65, 69 to 72, 73 to 89 shortened); Part three stays; the formats lose the fields that become parameters. About 50,000 words with the italics, 35,000 without, from 90,000 and 65,000: the largest MIR still, a third of the whole rather than six tenths.

**A default cMIP must ship with the release**, or a collective founded on day one has no model of control and no absence proof: the one the suite already builds for areas and tiers, carrying today's rules 62 to 68, 94 to 97 as a cMIP's terms, so that nothing a founded collective does today changes; and an absence-proof cMIP (a period on a time reference), which the suite already has. The core's default where a collective names none: every member, by head, under the constitutional rule, which is the last resort anyway.

**Consequences elsewhere.** Identity: none; its collective passages already read Agreements only through the genesis's declaration. Money: none in substance; rule 4's clause and rule 12 read the publication and the tag, which stay grammar. The MICS: its Agreements paragraph says the four things and lists the tasks; its layers are unchanged. The suite: its scenarios keep their steps, and the ones that exercise a model (a judicial tier, a proxy, an issuance) move under the default cMIP's heading, run against it.

## 4. The cost of doing it now

**Texts:** a rewrite of Agreements, two or three sessions of the kind S7h was; the other texts touched in sentences. **Core:** the view (`agreements/view.rs`, which has grown past ten thousand lines) loses the models to a cMIP crate; that is deletion and a boundary, not new logic, and the default cMIP is the deleted code moved. **Vectors:** the 327 cases keep their acts; the ones that exercise a model are re-labelled as the default cMIP's and keep their verdicts, which is the test that nothing moved by accident. **The C verifier:** its third edition meets a shorter text, which is easier, and the models it never built (its own "not implemented" list is mostly them) stop counting against it. **The apps:** Edubba builds on tiers and areas; it runs on the default cMIP unchanged.

**One-way doors.** Moving a field out of the terms format closes the door on its return to the core in MOR 1; it reopens in the cMIP's parameters at once. Keeping a model in the core closes the heavier door: a frozen rule of control that adoption can never replace, which is the third principle's own test ("does this rule let adoption decide?") failed by every tier and proxy rule frozen in. So the direction of the one-way door favours moving now.

**Could it wait for MOR 2 without harm to the way out?** The way out does not depend on the move: it holds in today's text and would hold in the smaller one. What cannot wait is the size itself, because a core cannot lose rules after the freeze: whatever is frozen in stays until MOR 2, and a MOR 1 that ships with its control models in the core has chosen them for everyone. If the move is not made now, the honest alternative is to freeze as is and say so: that MOR 1's collectives carry one model of control, and MOR 2 will open it.

## 5. Where the suggestion is wrong

- **It is one part short.** "What we wrote; who decides what we did not; how to stop being bound" has no place for what others relied on, which is the guarantee most of Agreements' attacks are answered by and the reason keepers exist. Added as the fourth thing, the shape holds.
- **"Who decides the unwritten" is not one thing.** The last-resort deciders (every party of a deal; the holder alone for a stake; the constitutional rule for membership; the two outcomes for a vanished party) are the way out seen from the other side and must stay core; the models of deciding (tiers, areas, proxies, judges, absence proofs) are cMIPs'. Read as "a cMIP decides", the suggestion would put a collective's constitution in a module, and a module that holds the constitution holds the collective.
- **"What we wrote" is not only text.** The core must fix the meaning of what is written tightly enough that two verifiers count a consent alike (rule 65 is grammar, not a model), or cMIPs will diverge on what a signature was worth, and legible greed fails at the count.
- **The size is not all in the models.** The prose that answers one attack at a time (the reasoning, the stated costs, the examples) is a third of the text and belongs to the texts' method; it will not shrink by moving rules, only by saying each rule once. That is a writing cost, not a design change, and it is the cheaper half of the cut.
