## Q2. Who signs a collective's genesis

**Keep S7h's lean (a).** My sentence in the fourth review had the genesis signed by "the chain key it commits to", which would make one chain key sign twice, the genesis and the first rotation, against "revealed and used once" (Identity's glossary, line 37). S7h's version keeps the invariant: the genesis is signed by a chain key revealed in its signature, and field 1 commits the next, exactly as a rotation does. The cost S7h names is right and is no new cost: the key that signs a genesis is unchecked at birth for every identity, since the identity hash is the genesis itself. Lines 87, 117 and 859 as rewritten say it. *Before the freeze:* yes; it is the genesis format, and it is written.

## Q3. A collective's rotations: no signing key, no kept tips

**Keep (a), as written, with one sentence on what replaces the tips.** Kept tips named the latest act of each of the collective's own sequences; a collective with no signing key has no sequence of its own, so the field is empty by construction, and Q15's rule (history never follows a member's `prev` for the collective) is the honest consequence: a member's sequence is theirs, and the collective's line is read through what its decisions and actions cite in `objects`. What the texts must then say plainly, since the tips used to sweep whole sequences into the line and citation places one act at a time: *An act for the collective is placed on its line only where a later act for the collective cites it, directly or through acts that do; one nothing cites is outside every line, and counts for nothing (rule 58's own words). A client acting for a collective cites every head it holds.* The second sentence is conformance and exists; the first makes the cost visible. *Before the freeze:* yes; the rotation format changes with it.

## Q4. A rotation for every constitutional clone

**Take (b), the simpler way, stated precisely.** Rule 69's reason for a rotation at every membership change is in its own commentary (line 1699 of the current text): the rotation "fences off the old rules: whatever the old key signs afterwards is void, whoever signs it", so that former members cannot sign under the old rules later. Under F401 there is no collective signing key and no member key to fence off; a former member's own key keeps signing their own acts, and their acts for the collective after the line count for nothing by "Made before, made after", which the record's line already fixes. What remains of the reason is the chain key: a departed member who holds a share of it must not keep one. So the rotation is needed exactly where the key grammar changes or a holder of a share leaves, and a record suffices otherwise, judged as records already judge the clones they put in force (complete at the record, with the signatures it names). In a shares grammar that is still most departures; in a custodian grammar it is none. *Sentence, rule 69: Changing members takes a clone of the collective's agreement changing the constitutional tier, put in force by a record (type 17) that names the signature acts completing it; and a rotation of the chain key where the clone changes the key grammar or removes a party who holds the key or a share of it, the rotation declaring the clone. A clone put in force by a record where a rotation was needed puts nothing in force.* *Cost of (b):* a record's line, not a rotation's, fixes the departure, which is already true of resignations. *Cost of (a):* a key ceremony for a departure that touches no key. *Before the freeze:* yes, either way; it is the shape of a membership change and cannot relax afterwards.

## Q14. The collective's escape endorsement

**Change it: neither (a) nor (b), but the rotation itself.** As written (line 633), members sign the endorsement under the constitutional change rule and "whether those signers meet the rule is Agreements' to judge". That makes an Identity-chain question depend on Agreements, which is the second review pattern, the one F381 and F389 removed from Identity. The stranger's reading names the MICS sentence it breaks. It is also the worst place to break it: the verifiers that must decide whether a homeless rotation counts are homes, which are Identity-level readers, and a new home that cannot judge the escape cannot know whether to hold the rotation. (b), the key grammar's holders, does not escape the pattern, since who holds the chain key is written in the founding agreement, an Agreements text.

What the escape proves for a person is both keys: the chain key in the rotation, the signing key in the endorsement, so that a thief holding one alone cannot leave. A collective has one key, and that key is already k of n members under its grammar: the threshold is the second factor. So: *for a collective, the homeless rotation, signed by its chain key, is its own escape endorsement; it needs no other, and section 11 applies to it as to an endorsed rotation (steps 3 and 4 do not apply; steps 1, 2 and 5 do).* Identity decides it from its own data: the genesis declares no signing key (rule 1), so the rotation is a collective's. *Stated cost, to write beside it:* an old home's objection never voids a collective's homeless rotation; what protects a collective from a thief is its key grammar, k shares, not its homes. For a person, the homes' objection stands in for the second key; a collective's second key is its members. *Before the freeze:* yes; it is the way out of a hostile home for every collective, and as written an Identity-only home cannot read it.

## Q24. A collective that sold its works by transfer can never close

**Take (a).** "Holds nothing" (line 3857) reads the latest version of each agreement, and a transfer (rule 10) makes no version: the collective stays a holder in every version forever, and the only way to close would be to re-version every work's ownership agreement, which is (b), against rule 10's design that a sale takes effect on the seller's own line. The texts already compute what a seller still holds after its placed transfers (rule 10, "what the seller still held is its share in the version the transfer names, less each transfer of the same stake placed before it"); the closing should read the same. *Sentence, in the "holds nothing" bullet: no stake of a latest version of an agreement a verifier holds names it as a holder, after the complete transfers of that stake the closing's history cites, each placed on the collective's line (rule 94), are applied as rule 10 counts them; other than its own stake in itself; unless the work's release (rule 55) counted with the collective's consent placed before the line.* A transfer not in the closing's history leaves the collective a holder, which is the tie rule as it already stands for debts. *Before the freeze:* yes; a closing is the way out, and as written no collective that ever sold a work has one.
