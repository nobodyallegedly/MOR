## In plain words

The decisions of the last two days pull one way: an agreement is a trap you sign into, the grammar gives no door, and every door is something people build on top. That is coherent. The set does not contradict itself in what it wants.

It breaks in what it leaves beside the door. The door is shut, and four windows are open. Each lets a person out in a way that is worse than leaving would have been: backwards in time, for everyone at once, and with a record that reads as an accident.

1. **A late withdrawal.** Where an agreement names no relay, a signature taken back has no date. "What a race stops" says that when a withdrawal and the last signature cannot be put in order, the withdrawal wins and the Terms never came into force. Nothing in the bytes tells a true race from a withdrawal signed a year later. So most signers of most bare agreements hold a way out for good, and it undoes the agreement for all.
2. **A sale.** "No exception for a sale" moves the burden: until now Terms had to be shown late to fall; now they must be shown early to stand. A holder of the baker's rights who sells, to a friend or to a second identity of her own, can make earlier Terms fall wherever nobody can show they came first.
3. **A fork of one's own line**, and 4. **a key declared lost.** Both were in the earlier attack. Both still stand, and they matter more now that they are the only bare exits.

And two ways remain of holding someone with no signature of theirs on the thing that holds them:

5. **Out of the parties, still bound.** By the letter of draft 25, a version that takes a person out still binds her, and she is never asked again. With "Leaving is an update" and no word for leaving, nothing says when a person stops being bound.
6. **A power she never gave.** "A power given binds its giver" is said of someone who grants. Draft 25 also lets a power over her be added by an update she did not sign. Then others sign for her, and by today's decisions that counts as her own signature, for her baked work included.

The click still has every piece. Two things under it were things only the grammar gave, and one is gone by decision (what a contributor earned), so the click now rewards everyone the current version names, which is not the same as everyone who contributed.

Below: the six questions in the brief's order, then the ranking, then questions for Nobody, allegedly.

## 1. The set against itself

### 1.1 "No default way out" with the two race decisions: they contradict in effect

**His words, read.** "An agreement is there so that you cannot leave it. That's why it exists". And: "Yes, these timing issues when no anchoring is involved should always force whatever was being done not to work". And, to the lean that the coming into force fails and "Carla's withdrawal stands": "Agreed, Carla is the monkey wrench that gets it all stuck".

**Draft 25, read.** G4.10: a counter signature "is available only until every party has signed". With no place, "only citing gives an order": "a counter signature that names every assent the Terms need came after them; a completing assent that names the counter signature came after it"; "Otherwise whether those Terms are in force is **not shown**". Section 7: "the bytes alone cannot tell late from simultaneous".

**The attack, reasoned.** Ana, Ben and Carla sign Terms with no place. Carla signs first; Ben signs last and completes them. A year later Carla signs a counter signature whose `previous` is her own assent and which names nothing else. Her line allows it: it follows her assent directly. It does not name Ben's assent, and Ben's assent, made a year before, does not name it. By G4.10 the order is not shown. By "What a race stops" the coming into force fails, the answer is "not yet in force", and her withdrawal stands.

- **Who can do it:** every signer whose assent the Terms needed, except one who is pinned. A signer is pinned once an act of theirs in that agreement names, directly or through other acts, every assent the Terms needed: a later counter signature must follow that act in their line, so it "came after" and changes nothing. The one who signs last and cites the others is pinned by their own assent. Everyone else is free until they next act in the agreement and cite the completion, and nothing makes them.
- **What they gain:** a choice made later, having seen how things went, between having signed and never having been bound. That is a way out, by default, in every bare agreement: the thing the evening's decision says does not exist.
- **What everyone else loses:** more than a leaving would cost them. The Terms are "not yet in force" for all. Every update, filling and payment since rests on Terms not shown as in force. A payer who named that version is answered "not shown" for who receives (G1.4, read). G12.5 keeps what strangers relied on in a branch set aside; nothing I read extends that to a coming into force that a race un-makes.
- **What the record shows:** an assent, a counter signature, and "not shown, the order is not shown". The same as an honest race.
- **Two readers, two answers:** a verifier that does not hold her counter signature answers "in force, as shown". She chooses who holds it.
- **The same for an update.** Whoever's assent an update needed can do it to the update: the old version comes back, and everything built since stops.
- **What cures it, and what the cure costs:** a named place (the relay then decides the order: section 4 of the earlier attack, which stands), or every signer acting once more after completion. A rule inside the grammar cannot cure it without one of those: the last act of any round is always the one nothing orders.

**In fairness to the earlier attack:** it said that until 17 November "doubt about a withdrawal could arise only before Terms were in force". That was true of the clock and false of the bytes. I was wrong to call the window short.

**Can a person who cannot leave stall every change for ever?** Reasoned, in three cases.

- *She is outside the set the update condition asks* (two of three, and she is the third): no. No act of hers decides the update, so she can make no race against it. What she can do is the attack above against the first Terms, if they needed her assent: she cannot stall the change, she can un-make the agreement.
- *She is inside the set:* yes, for ever, and it costs her less than refusing. She signs each time and takes it back each time with nothing ordering the two. "Whoever wants the change makes it again" needs her signature again, and gives her the same option again. The record shows, each time, a signature, a withdrawal and "not shown". Only someone counting her withdrawals across time would see the pattern, and no grammar rule counts.
- *Strangers stall a bare offer.* Read, G4.8(a): "everyone who signed the original" includes "a signer who fills a role". Under the default condition every buyer of an offer must sign every update of it. A stream of buyers, each one's assent un-ordered against the update's last signature, keeps a bare offer from ever changing. The buyers can be one person with many identities.

**One wording point.** The line says "the change of state fails and the earlier state stays". In each race there are two changes: Carla's assent becomes cancelled, and the Terms come into force. The line lets one stand and stops the other, and does not say by what test. In both cases decided so far (Carla; the sale) the act that binds fails and the act that releases stands. If that is the rule, it should be said, because section 1.2 below has a race where it matters which way it goes.

### 1.2 "A power given binds its giver" with "Delegation is a leftover": yes, a power can be given that nothing ends

**His words, read.** "How does the person give the power away? To assume that there is no way out is to assume a very specific shape for the cMIp". "If you grant powers via a cMIP you take on the responsibilities".

No contradiction between the two: both put the whole weight on the rule of the power (task K3). Three things follow, all reasoned.

**(a) Bare, the power ends only as the Terms can be updated.** G5.5's default ("By default the delegator can end it at any time") goes. A power whose rule says only "Ben may sign for Ana" has no stop in it. It ends by an update of the Terms that give it. Under the default condition that needs Ben's signature, if Ben signed those Terms. The MOR lead named this cost and withdrew it ("the stated cost assumed one shape of cMIP and is withdrawn"). The cost did not go away; it moved into the choice of cMIP. Someone who signs a power under a rule with no stop has given it for as long as the other party chooses, and if that party is gone, for good. Draft 25's section 7 said this of one case ("if the agent is gone, for good"); it is now the general case and deserves the same plain sentence.

**(b) A power to sign for her can be used to keep itself.** Read, G5.6(f): an assent made for a party under a power of kind `assent` "to an update, one that changes the judicial included, is that party's signing where the power's rule says yes", in his words "Your risk, your dial". Reasoned: Ben holds a wide power to assent for Ana. Ben signs, for Ana, the update of the granting Terms that removes whatever stop they had. Under the default "everyone who signed" has signed: Ben for himself, Ben for Ana. Nothing in the grammar keeps a power from signing its giver's consent to its own widening. *The record shows:* a valid update, with Ana's assent made `for` her. *What holds her:* her first signature, two steps back.

**(c) A stop that a cMIP reads is itself a race, and the giver can make one.** If the rule answers "yes, until the giver says stop", the stop is an act the rule reads from the acts held. Ben signs something for Ana that turns out badly. Ana then signs a stop that names nothing of Ben's. No order is shown. If the race rule runs the way it has so far (the binding fails, the release stands), Ben's act does not bind Ana, and whoever relied on it is left with nothing, against G5.7 ("No act of the grantor ... sets aside, voids or undoes an act made under its grant") and against "you take on the responsibilities". If it runs the other way, the giver can never stop a power in time. Either way one side holds an option, unless the rule itself is made to say; and a rule is a module, which brings in section 5.

**A fourth thing, and the worst: a power she never gave.** Read, G5.1(b): the act's `under` names "Terms by which they are bound", and G5.1(c) binds a person to an update "the update condition of those Terms answers valid for". Reasoned: Dana is one of three in Terms with a two-of-three condition. The other two sign a version that adds a power of kind `assent`, held by themselves, for acts made `for` Dana. She is bound by that version without signing it. They then sign, for her, Terms on a work she baked. By "Bound, not named" and "A power given binds its giver" as the "Arrived at" lines have them, her "own signature stands behind" those Terms. His words are about someone who grants: "If you grant powers via a cMIP". Dana granted nothing. Whether a power placed over her by others counts as hers, for the baker's rights or for anything, is not in his words and should be asked.

### 1.3 "Earned rights leave the grammar" with "The two gates"

**Read.** The two gates: "(a). Where the update condition answers valid, the update replaces the version it names." Earned rights: "G10.6 goes. Whoever fills a spot is bound by the Terms' update condition like any signer; a condition that needs fillers' signatures protects them, and that is a choice of the Terms, read before filling."

They agree with each other. Three consequences, reasoned.

- **"Read before filling" protects less than it sounds.** Dana reads condition C1 and fills. C1 lets two of three change the condition itself. They change it to C2, "the maker alone"; the maker then takes her share. Each step is valid. What she read protects her only as far as it forbids changing itself. That is the position of any signer (section 7, "Bound by an update one did not sign"), but a filler is the person draft 25 called "not there to sign", who negotiates nothing.
- **Duties move too, not only rights.** A buyer fills a spot by an assent and is bound "by their own assent" (G10.3). G10.6 only ever shielded rights. Under a maker-only condition an update that raises what the buyer owes binds her by G5.1(c), and there is now no way out. The record would show a stranger who clicked once as bound to pay whatever the last version says.
- **What is left of a filling at all.** After an update, "Who fills a spot under it is said by its own K5 cMIP" (G10.7, read), and the new version can name a new cMIP. G10's own purpose line reads: "Lie exposed. 'I am the one who did it', confirmed by someone chosen afterwards; and 'the terms have changed, so what you earned is gone'." With G10.6 gone the second is no longer a lie, and the first can be done by update. Two sentences beside G10.6 say something different and should not go with it by accident: "a filling already earned stays a filling, shown with its proof" (G10.5) and "nothing is undone" (G10.7). They are about what the record shows, which is not behaviour.

**What the two gates still leave unsaid, from the earlier attack, and now sharper.** Read: "The new version's own `in_force` governs only its own later life, not whether it replaces the old one." G4.10: a counter signature is available "until those Terms are in force". Reasoned: a version can replace by the condition alone while its own `in_force` is unmet. In that state a signer may still take back the assent the condition counted, with no race at all, and the condition's answer changes under everyone. What state "replaced, and not in force" is has to be said before either gate can be built.

### 1.4 Four more meetings

- **"No exception for a sale" with "The holder at the act cited".** They collide. Section 4.3 below.
- **"Leaving is an update" with his own earlier words.** Read: "Leaving an agreement should leave a clear identifiable evidence, yes". Then: "B is impossible. C is not needed". As arrived at, the evidence of a leaving is the difference between two versions' lists of parties and who signed the second. A leaving, an expulsion by a majority, and a redrawing of roles all look like that. Whether that difference is the "clear identifiable evidence" he meant is his to say.
- **"Leaving is an update" with G5.1(c).** Section 3.2 below.
- **"A race changes nothing" with "The two gates".** Read, G4.8: "An update is governed by that one answer, and by nothing else." The race rule makes order a second thing that governs. Both hold only if the condition's cMIP is handed, by the grammar, which assents count, races already taken out (section 5).

## 3. The trap that remains

### 3.1 Who can be held for ever, and by whom

All reasoned from the text; each line says what the record shows.

| Who is held | By whom | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|
| Every signer of Terms with no condition named | any one other signer who will not sign; or one who is dead, gone or without their key | Terms in force; each proposed update "not shown" as a replacement. Read, G4.11(i): "A verifier MUST NOT answer, of itself, that an agreement is locked". So the record never says it. |
| The minority under a named condition | the majority, who can also rewrite the condition | valid updates; the condition's fingerprint |
| A buyer who signed an offer | the maker (a condition of the maker's choosing), or under the default every other buyer | an assent to an offer; later versions she is bound by |
| The maker of a bare offer | every buyer | the same, turned round |
| The giver of a power | whoever holds it (1.2) | Terms with a `powers` entry; "unchecked" for anyone without the rule's cMIP |
| A member of a group | the deals the group signs (G1.2) | the group's signature; not hers |
| A service, an escrow, a receiver | those it serves | Terms in force |
| Anyone, where a place is named | the relay, by not showing the update; or by being gone (G9.1: "unchecked" for everything that depends on it; and G4.8(d): the move to a new place is judged by the old one) | nothing: silence leaves no act |
| Anyone, where a cMIP in `condition` is lost | nobody | "unchecked", for good |

**Does the record show it plainly?** No, and after his words it is not meant to warn: "written agreements have always been about not being able to escape the agreement". But two things are different from warning and are still missing. First, the word. The verifier is forbidden to say "locked", and with no default exit the bare state now *is* held; what a verifier says when asked "how does this change, and who must sign?" for Terms with no condition ("by default: every one of these signers") is the nearest thing the record has, and it is worth checking that it says it. Second, the dead. "For ever" in the first row does not need anyone's bad will. Draft 25, section 7: "If a party is gone and no successor was named in advance ... it is stuck." Bare, every agreement between people ends that way.

### 3.2 Kept in against their own signature: out of the parties, still bound

**Read.** G5.1(c): a person is bound by an act if "the act is a Terms act that replaces Terms by which they are bound, and the update condition of those Terms answers valid for it". G4.8(a): "a party that a version took out is not asked by a later update". Section 8.3, item 4: "Who is bound by an update that does not name them (G5.1(c)). Unchanged".

**Reasoned.** Carla is bound by version 1. Version 2 leaves her out, and version 1's condition answers valid for it. By the letter of G5.1(c) she is bound by version 2: it replaces Terms by which she was bound. Version 3 replaces version 2; she is bound by version 2, so the same clause binds her to version 3; and by G4.8(a) she is not asked. That runs for ever. She has no voice and is bound by whatever each new version says, about her included.

- This is the same whether she asked to go ("Leaving is an update") or was put out by a majority.
- "The grammar has no act or word of its own for leaving", so nothing marks the point where she stops being bound.
- *Who gains:* those who stay. They can write, in the version that removes her, what she still owes, and later change it.
- *What the record shows:* a chain of valid versions that do not name her.
- *What would have to hold* (a need, not a rule): a person is bound by a version only if it names her, or by her own assent; or the text says in plain words that leaving the list of parties does not end being bound, so that people know to write it into the leaving version.

This is the plainest thing left in the grammar that holds someone "against their own signature", and it comes from a clause written before leaving was an update.

### 3.3 Let out against their own signature: the windows

The decisions close the door. These get someone out anyway, each in a worse way than a door.

- **The late withdrawal** (1.1).
- **The sale** (4.3).
- **A fork of one's own line.** Read, G12.2: "While a fork stands, neither tip counts". G12.3: resolved "for one signer's line, by that signer"; a tip set aside "never counted as the next act of the line". P9: "whether the signer of such an assent is bound meanwhile is not said". G4.8(a): the default asks "each party in whose name an assent to the original counts". Reasoned: a signer who makes a second act naming the same `previous` as their assent turns the assent into a tip. While the fork stands the assent does not count; under the default they are then, by the letter, no longer among those asked. Then they resolve it themselves, either way. Draft 25, section 7: an honest fork "looks exactly like a lie".
- **A key declared lost**, as of a time before the assent. Read, section 9 of draft 25: the text "does not say what a change of key does to a line (G6): whether an act the core calls void is still a tip". "Two threads, two owners" gives the lower layer the line; it does not say what Agreements needs back.

**What would have to hold** for "an agreement is there so that you cannot leave it" to be true of the bytes: an assent that has counted toward Terms in force is not un-counted by anything its signer alone does later, in their own line, with their own key, or with what they own. Today four things un-count it.

## 4. The race

**In plain words.** A race is not something that happens to people. Wherever there is no relay, any one person can make one, at any time, by signing an act that cites nothing. The two decisions give a rule for what a race stops. They also, without meaning to, say who wins one: so far, whoever is getting out.

### 4.1 Against coming into force

Section 1.1. *Gain:* a standing option to un-make the agreement. *Record:* assent, counter signature, "not shown".

### 4.2 Against an update

- **By a signer the update needed:** as 1.1, on the update's own assents.
- **By a rival update:** already a stated cost. Read, section 7: "any group it accepts can hold the agreement where it was ... A fork is then a veto. It can reach back, too". "A race changes nothing" ("as with rival tips") confirms it and does not add to it.
- **The redo that forks, reasoned.** "Whoever wants the change makes it again". If they sign the same Terms act again, with fresh assents, nothing is added. If they write a fresh update of the same version, there are now two: the one the race stopped and the new one. A verifier that holds the wrench's counter signature sees the first as "not shown" and the second as valid. A verifier that does not hold it sees two valid updates of one version: a fork, both stopped. The person who made the race decides who holds her counter signature. The line should say that the change is made again *as the same act*.
- **By strangers, on a bare offer:** 1.1.

### 4.3 Against a sale: the burden has moved, and a sale becomes a broom

**Read.** Draft 25, G14.2: "Where neither the knots nor an anchoring show it, the Terms are not late, and are read by the holder at X, however late they may in fact be." And: "Terms made with the holder of the moment keep counting after a later transfer". The decision: "the race rule applies to Terms racing a sale, with no exception: those Terms do not come into force." The MOR lead's ground: "Terms and a sale of the baker's rights are usually ordered, both on the holder's own line; they race only when a power-holder signs Terms for her while she sells".

**Reasoned.** Under draft 25, Terms fell only where they were *shown late*. Under the decision, Terms fall wherever they are *not shown early*. Two things have to be true for that to be as rare as the lead said.

1. The holder's Terms and her transfer sit on one line. In draft 25 they do not: a line is "one signer's acts within an agreement" (G6.1), and a transfer is an act of the envelope's chain. The one line "across their life" is the lower layer's, by "Two threads, two owners"; draft 25, section 8.1, still lists as not known "whether it already gives ... a per-signer line of acts". I did not read that layer.
2. Even on one line, what must be ordered before the sale is the Terms *coming into force*, and that is the last assent, which may be the other party's. If the holder signed first and never acted in the agreement again, nothing on her line shows the completion came before the transfer.

So, where the holder did not sign last and no relay is named:

- **Who:** the holder of the baker's rights; or whoever buys them.
- **How:** she signs a transfer that names nothing of the agreement. To a buyer; to a friend; to a second identity of her own.
- **What she gains:** every earlier agreement on that work whose completion cannot be shown to come first is "not in force", and "whoever wants the deal signs again with the new holder". For a holder who cannot leave an agreement, that is a way out. For a buyer of rights, it is the work delivered clean of its licences.
- **Who loses:** "whoever agreed with the holder of the moment, and every payer under those Terms, whom a later sale does not undo" (G14's own "Protects" line, read). That line was the reason for "The holder at the time", and for "The holder at the act cited" last night.
- **What the record shows:** a transfer, and Terms "not shown" as in force, with nothing to say which came first.

His words were "The less exceptions the better", given on the lead's account that only a power-holder's race was at stake. If the account is too narrow, his words were given on a narrower case than the rule now covers. To be put to him, with the two premises above.

**And in the other direction, the fork.** If the life-long line exists, a holder who signs Terms on one tip and a transfer on the other has forked it. Both tips stop: neither the Terms nor the sale counts until she resolves it, alone. "The sale stands" is then not true while she chooses.

### 4.4 Under a named place

No races: the relay gives positions. Then the relay makes the order, shows or does not show, and can be one of the parties. The earlier attack's section 4 stands whole. The race decisions raise its weight: they make naming a relay the only safe way to sign, so the one sentence that checks a relay (two answers that contradict: unchecked) is the sentence the set now most needs back.

## 5. What "built on top" leaves

## 6. The click

**His words, read, from draft 25's head:** "can I with a single click reward all the people that contributed to the service or good via MOR".

**In plain words.** Every piece is still there, in the grammar or in a named socket. Nothing is missing that a cMIP cannot give, with one exception that was decided on purpose, and one sentence that must not be lost in a move.

| Piece | Where it is now | Sound? |
|---|---|---|
| The offer, and a spot for someone not yet known | grammar (G10.1 to G10.3) | Yes. |
| The click as a signature | grammar: a buyer "signs by an assent to the version they buy under" | Yes; but see below: the click now binds with no way out. |
| The payment names the version it read | grammar (G13.2), and the layer that holds value | Yes, and it is the piece only the grammar can give. Keep. |
| Who receives, through any agreement named | grammar (G1.4) | Yes. |
| For a baked work: is this the holder's agreement | moving to Envelopes | Only if one sentence stays in Agreements: "the answer of G14.3 comes first" (G1.4). Otherwise a click pays a thief and the verifier of Agreements passes it. |
| The receiving side's confirmation | the report block, moving to the MICS, not yet written | Sound if "who may tie" moves with it. |
| Dividing among the contributors | a service's cMIP (K6) | cMIP's, as before. |
| Each further leg and its reports | G11.5, and the block | As before. |
| Seeing that everyone was reached | the data socket (K8), at every leg | cMIP's, as before: "By default the buyer is shown nothing further." "Seeing and being told" changes nothing here. |
| Money arriving under a version since replaced | "What the core is for": a cMIP's | Works as evidence, reasoned: a payment is read against the version it names (G13.1), so that version's call and its Control cMIP are still read, and a payment nobody confirmed is still shown "one side only". | A click that races a change to the offer | section 7: "Who came in time is per the place and the clock the agreement names, and without them only as shown" | A stated cost already. With the race rule, a buyer can pay and not be shown as having filled. A click is safe only with a relay named. |

**What only the grammar could give, and no longer does.** "All the people that contributed." A contributor who earned a spot ("whoever proves they have done X") was, under G10.6, in every later version's reckoning unless she signed otherwise or the condition she earned under allowed it. Now she is in the version the click reads only if those who can update it kept her there. A cMIP can protect her, but only a cMIP the maker chose before she arrived; she chooses nothing. So the click rewards everyone the current version names. Whether that is everyone who contributed is no longer something the bytes promise. This was decided ("Agreed"), on Dana's case. What I would check with him is the click's own sentence against it.

**What the record can still give in its place, if two sentences survive:** that a filling once earned "stays a filling, shown with its proof", and that "nothing is undone". Then a click cannot promise that everyone was paid, but the person clicking can be shown who earned a place and was later written out. That is making greed legible, and it is all the grammar ever claimed.

**The person who clicks.** One click is an assent to an offer. By today's set it binds her to Terms with no default way out, under an update condition named by a fingerprint, with no duty on anyone to show it to her (section 5.2 answers "Nothing is shown to anyone before they sign" with "a body cMIP (K1), and the client"). The single click is now the heaviest signature in MOR, made by the person least likely to read. What a client must show before a click is client conformance, the kind of rule this repository says not to take the convenient path on; today one such duty is written (G12.8) and this is not it.

## 7. Ranked by harm

1. **The late withdrawal** (1.1). Any signer but the pinned one un-makes a bare agreement at any later time, for everyone, payers included. It is the default way out the set says does not exist.
2. **A sale as a broom** (4.3). Earlier Terms fall unless shown early; a holder can sell to herself. It undoes what "The holder at the act cited" was decided to keep.
3. **Out of the parties, still bound** (3.2). No voice, bound for ever, by the letter of G5.1(c).
4. **A power she never gave; a power that keeps itself** (1.2). Others' signatures counted as hers, for her baked work too.
5. **The click that binds** (1.3, 6). A stranger's one assent, no way out, duties that a maker-only condition can raise.
6. **Fork or key instead of leaving** (3.3). Unchanged, and now the quiet exits.
7. **The relay** (4.4). Every attack of the earlier review, with more resting on it.
8. **Modules read the acts raw** (5). Forks, races and "nothing an agent made is set aside" hold only as far as each cMIP chooses to honour them.
9. **Replaced, and not in force** (1.3). A state the two gates make and nobody has defined.
10. **What the contributor earned** (6). Decided; listed for what it does to the click's own sentence.
11. **Sentences that could be lost in the moves** (2, 5): G14.3 comes first; who may tie; who may say unanswered; a filling stays shown; the stated costs.

## 8. Questions for Nobody, allegedly

1. Carla signed, the Terms came into force, and a year later she signs a withdrawal that cites nothing. The bytes cannot tell this from a race. Does her withdrawal stand?
