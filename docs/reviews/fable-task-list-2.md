## In plain words

The new text has three parts: what a deal needs, what a collective adds, and what both share. Placed that way, the list is nineteen tasks: seventeen shared, one the collective's, one the client's own. Two are new since last night: **absence outcome**, decided this morning, and a **share rule**, which the decision "a share changes only as terms its holder signed say" needs in order to be checkable. The deal's part has no task of its own. That is the finding in one line: a deal is the shared part with everyone signing.

The guarantees come to twenty-four sentences: seventeen shared, five the collective's, two the deal's. Three more things the core keeps are not guarantees at all but language (a signature binds; unknown is never rounded; a verdict does not depend on the order acts arrive in). The brief's rule, "a sentence that cannot answer both questions goes to a cMIP", is right for guards and wrong for those three: they are what every guard is written in.

Six things could go wrong, and one of them I would settle before any drafting: **"no one may ever declare me absent" must not reach a collective's constitutional voices**, or a guard written against a recorded attack is lost.

Tasks 8 and 17 to 24 are a publication's. A publication is a deal whose owners' side may be a deal or a collective, so they are shared.

**Task 26, precisely.** Whatever the cMIP, the core applies only the two shapes, and checks two things: that the shares sum exactly, and that the holder signed the clause that produced the answer. For a role, the same two shapes: the place stays (vacant, or held by a named identity) or moves to a named identity. "The successor of the holder's identity" is a move whose target Identity supplies; "redistributed among the remaining holders" is a move whose shares the cMIP computes.

**Task 27 is my proposal, from R-92.** The decision says the rule "must be one a verifier can apply from the signed terms, through the cMIP that names how". That is a task's definition. Issuance under a holders' rule, a recoup schedule and vesting are three cMIPs under it. Without the task, each would need a hook of its own. It also retires the special place issuance had in the control model's query.

### Language, which is neither a guarantee nor a cMIP's

Three sentences fail the first question, because signed text is itself the exposure, and still cannot leave: **a party is bound only by its own signature on the exact terms; a verifier that cannot tell answers unknown, never valid and never void; a verdict and its explanation never depend on the order acts arrive in** (the first of the five laws the text never stated). And the closed world: the core reads only the ties it names. The brief's sorting has no place for these. I put them in the shared part's opening, as what the guarantees are written in.

## 3. Three candidates from the prior art

Run from the candidates as named, without report R1.

### Which of two terms in force wins

*Plain:* contracts carry an order of precedence between documents. *The lie:* "the other document governs."

The first question: signed, ordered text shows which was signed and when. It does not show which wins; that takes a rule. The second: yes, a buyer under a publication chose neither document's insides.

**Already expressible, and it should be said once.** MOR answers it in three places and never as one thing:

1. within one chain, exactly one version is in force for an act, and the line says which;
2. across two agreements nothing wins: each binds only its signers, and a contradiction is shown (rule 14's shape);
3. the core's guarantees decide whatever a cMIP answers (rule 98), and a control model that reaches past them is overreaching.

**Not a task.** A task that picks between two signed terms would let a cMIP decide what its choosers signed: the thing that decides signed by the wrong party. **A guarantee, in the shared part's opening,** made of those three sentences.

### An option one side exercises after signing

*Plain:* one party, alone and later, changes the relation, because all signed that it could. *The lie:* "you had no such right", or "I exercised it in time."

The first question: yes, "in time" is by-when. The second: no, every party signed the option.

**A task, and its instances exist.** By the test it is a cMIP's. MOR already has it in each place where one side may act alone: leaving (task 15), a share that changes by a signed rule (task 27, this morning), an obligation falling due (task 9), an offer anyone accepts by paying. **I would not add a general "option" task.** A general one would let terms pre-authorise any future change by one side, and S1 and D1 exist to stop a version that changes more than was consented to. Each new thing one side may change alone should be its own task with its own declaration, as R-92 just did for shares.

### A suspended state between active and terminated

*Plain:* the agreement is paused, neither running nor ended. *The lie:* "we were suspended, so I owed nothing."

**None, as a state.** A third state would touch every purchase and every "before the line" rule, so it would be the core's, and each rule with two cases would get a third. What it would buy is already expressible in pieces: an obligation not yet due is a condition pending (task 9); a sale paused is a price tag withdrawn; an absent holder's pay is held (task 26, "stays"); a collective whose key moved wrongly is broken until rolled back.

**One place it would earn something, named for Nobody, allegedly and not proposed:** a voice. R-43's stated cost is that a party wrongly declared absent regains its voice only by a later version naming it. A voice *suspended* by a declaration and restored by the party's own next act would soften that. Draft 24's research points both ways (F166, coming back defeats a declaration; rule 88, a contest shows and voids nothing).

## 4. The two questions for Nobody, allegedly

### The negotiation record (rule 52, type 18)

It is only text with receipts. But the receipts are acknowledgements, and no text act and no cMIP's act may carry one (Envelopes rule 18, against F110's attack). The promise changed this morning, so removing a type is now allowed by a decision.

- **If it stays** (my lean): one type and four sentences in the shared part; no verdict reads it; nothing else changes. The cost is a type the core defines that no rule uses, against "nothing enters the core that a cMIP could carry". My answer to that is that a cMIP cannot carry it.
- **If it leaves:** a negotiation becomes text acts replying to one another, and a receipt becomes an Identity witness act. Type 18 is retired, a format change named in the log; the library's negotiation tests retire with it. Two costs: a witness act is public, so that a party relies on some act becomes visible; and Identity says a witness act is never a side effect of another gesture, which a client sending one per message would strain.

My lean is weaker than last night. His own first reaction ("That is just text, no?") and the changed promise both favour leaving. What keeps me at "stays" is the second cost.

### New task numbers before the freeze

Development rule 6: "A successor protocol may add tasks with new numbers; the numbers here never change." So new numbers are free now and closed after.

- **If yes** (my lean): the table gains 17 to 27; terms and publications can name each; a founding cMIP lists several; a later one may fill one alone. That is what "clearer mapping for cMIPs" asked for.
- **If no:** the split stays one task with several answers, replaceable only whole. But absence outcome was decided this morning as a new task, so at least one number is already added. "No" would mean: that one and no other, and the share rule becomes a hook without a number.

## 6. What could go wrong

**1. A guard the inventory holds that this list could lose: rule 61.** "Every party whose signature a collective's constitutional change rule is counted among MUST be covered by the agreement's abandonment clause" (F105). This morning "no one may ever declare me absent" became an allowed answer, with the stated cost that a deal may freeze for good. In a deal, the others still have a way out: each can pass its whole stake on. In a collective, one member with a constitutional voice who answers "never" and vanishes freezes the constitution, and C5 ("can always be ended") is then false. F441 says the decision reaches "a collective's members (in its constitution)". **I would say now: "never" is a deal's answer; a collective's constitution may refuse it for any voice its change rule counts.** This is the one I would settle first.

**2. Two decisions that pull against each other: R-206 and R-238.** By R-206, where a constitution is silent the collective's rules stop binding a stake the day its holder leaves. By R-238, a holder's absence choices decide what happens to that stake. If those choices are "the collective's rules", a silent constitution cancels them, and a departed holder who vanishes holds a stake nobody can ever move: the failure F313 was written against. They agree if **a holder's absence choices are its own terms, travel with the stake, and are never the collective's rules.** The rehearsal's decisions (F436) already have a successor take what the holder held, clause included. The sentence should be written for absence choices.

**3. R-92 and the buyer.** "Terms its holder signed": a buyer signs a transfer, not the agreement. The case "the buyer bait" shows the attack (a rule added after the sale was signed). The sentence needed: *the terms a holder signed are the version its own signature, or the transfer it completed, names; a later version is a new rule.*

**4. A deal of one.** "A deal completes only when each party has chosen." A lone seller's publication is a deal with one party with a stake. Asking it who may declare it absent, and where its stake goes, is a question with no one else to protect. Unless the forced choice is scoped to deals of two or more parties, the smallest act in MOR (sell one thing) gains two questions.

**5. What the way out depends on here.** Three things. (a) **Task 26's "stays" must always be available and must need no cMIP**, so that a party can complete a deal while holding no absence-outcome cMIP at all; otherwise entering a deal depends on a module. (b) **Task 15 and task 27 answering "unknown"** must never block the default way out: a transfer of one's whole stake, a resignation, a default termination. S6 says so for termination; it should be said for all three. (c) **D3 leaves a holder who never signs a receipt, on a rail whose proof does not bind the destination, forever shown unpaid.** That is the decided stated cost; it sits on the hand, and the hand's own way out (leaving by a terminating act) does not clear it.

**6. Nineteen tasks, fourteen declarations.** More tasks means more to show before a signature. The fixed shape of `fable-small-tasks.md`, section 5, is the only thing keeping duty 1 a reading and not a wall. If a declaration cannot be said in that shape, the cMIP declares "not stated" and the client shows that.

## My view

The three parts hold. Nothing in the tasks is the deal's alone and one task is the collective's alone, which is what "it's all agreements but they handle different bits" predicted. The decisions of this morning fit the list with two new tasks and one new field. The risk is not in the list. It is in the four sentences section 6 says are missing: where "never" stops; that absence choices travel with the stake; what "signed" means for a buyer; and whether a deal of one is asked.
