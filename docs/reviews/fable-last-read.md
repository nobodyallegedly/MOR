## 1. The eleven items, item 6 first

**6. Rule 48 and `bought_under`.** Lean (b), agreed. What the two readings differ on is one case only: a seller names a stale version in the transfer, and the holders' rule changed between that version and the seller's signature. Under the text as now written the buyer is bound by that change (it was made before the seller signed); under the core it is not (the window opens at the version named). Reading "made after" as "put in force after the version the transfer names" makes the two the same, and the only party the difference touches is the buyer, who is protected earlier, by the seller's own act. No holder loses by it: the change still binds every holder who signed it, and binds the buyer with the buyer's signature, as rule 48 already says for the window. The clause for rule 48: *"made after" is read against the collective's line: a change put in force after the version the transfer names and before the line placed the transfer*. One clause, no vector moves, no verdict of the published vectors moves. I would not choose (a): it buys exactness in a case the seller controls, at a code session and six releases.

**1. The judicial tier and the constitution.** Lean (a), agreed. Rule 67 already covers field 2's judicial tasks (tasks 9, 10, 11 and 14) and already asks `[4]` with every member whose voice remains; rule 98.3's list omits field 2, so a reader of 98.3 alone could take a change of the condition cMIP for an operational one a model may price. It is not a contradiction, since rule 67 decides it, but it is a two-way reading of 98.3, and the fix is the list entry above. The judicial tier is then plainly part of the constitution, under its own rule, `[0]` added only where another constitutional field changes, which is what rule 98.3's sentence and the cMIP's rule 64.1 already do.

**2. Parameter 32.** Lean (a), agreed, and this one is a contradiction, not a wording: rule 98.3 says field 35's parameters are constitutional, compared whole; the cMIP's rule 62 says parameter 32 is compared never, each version naming its own offer; so an issuance under the default model, with field 18 by head, needs `[0]` by the core's reading and the holders' rule alone by the cMIP's. Two honest verifiers differ. The core must say the exception in its own words, and it can, because rule 98.2 already names the issuance's offer. The sentence is above. Its mechanics: a verifier holding the model knows which parameter names the offer (the model's specification says so, as it says which act types are its own); one not holding the model already answers unknown for every act whose power the model decides (rule 99), and the sentence keeps it there for this clone too, rather than letting it require `[0]` and differ. The bound on what a model may exempt is that the exempt parameter names an act the version follows; a model exempting its areas would be exempting a list, not an act, and the sentence does not reach it. Where field 18 counts by share, 98.2 already makes the issuance constitutional, and the exemption changes nothing.

**3. Three absence-authority lists.** Lean (a), agreed: one list, the format comment's (type 13: the identity the clause names, or, in a collective, a threshold of the other parties); the deal's sentence keeps "never a threshold of the other parties", which is the rule, not a list. The three lists differ only in words today; one list keeps them from differing in substance later.

**4. "Other than its own stake in itself".** Lean (a), agreed, with a preference: the clause has no referent, since "the collective holds no share of itself" makes a collective a holder of its own stake invalid, so the closing's "no stake names it as a holder" is never met by field 7 of its own agreement. Either reword it as the lean says (*a stake whose target is the collective itself (field 7) is none it holds*) or delete it, with the format's sentence cited. I would delete: a clause that excepts nothing teaches a reader to look for the case it excepts.

**5. Leftovers.** Lean (a), agreed: the general sentence ("the order of the list decides nothing") is rule 9's, about payouts, where a reorder must move no money; a fork's division (rule 9's exception, line 301) gives leftovers to the first as listed in an agreement every member signed, which is a different act with a different consent. Scope the general sentence to payouts.

**7. "Made" or "placed".** Keep "made", agreed: it is F421's word, the suite's (step 3.7at) and the core's, and it is the reading that binds the buyer by what existed when the seller signed, which is what a buyer can be shown. With item 6's clause, "made" is read against the version named, which is the only "when the seller signed" the core can see.

**8. Rule 69's sentence on the departing member.** Lean agreed: *which, where the departing member held it or a share of it, the members rotate to one the departing member never held*. Rule 69's own commentary already says a custodian grammar needs none, and `needs_rotation` reads it so.

**9. The tie rule's list and rule 79's.** Lean (b), agreed, and there is no gap under it. Rule 74 ends a grant within an area at the line that puts the new model in force, and "Made before, made after" already says what "after" is: an act for the collective is before a line when the line's history holds it, and every other act counts as made after it. So an act under such a grant that cites neither the clone nor anything citing it is after the line, and not backed; the tie rule's race (an ending and an action citing neither each other) cannot arise, because the ending here is a line. Say so in rule 79 in one clause: *timed by the line alone, as rule 74 says; the tie rule does not apply to it*.

**10. "Each complete clone that makes a constitutional change".** Lean agreed, and this one must close: as written, a verifier reading the identity-chain section requires a rotation for every constitutional clone, while rule 69 requires one only where the key grammar changes or a holder of the key or a share leaves, and says a clone put in force by a record "where a rotation was needed" puts nothing in force. A constitutional clone that changes, say, the keepers, put in force by a record, is in force by rule 69 and not in force by the section's sentence. The sentence above replaces it. The suite's checklist line on members changing should add "(a shares grammar)" where it says the others rotate, as the item suggests.

## 4. Size and readability

The six MIRs are 136,326 words; Agreements is 77,701, of which about 15,400 are italics, so about 62,000 words of protocol. The MICS is 22,491 and the suite 45,906.

Is "the grammar of agreeing" visible? In the MICS, yes: its Agreements section opens with the four parts in one paragraph (the grammar; what others relied on stands; the way out; the deciders), and the glossary and "Who decides" paragraph let a reader find each rule from that map. In the Agreements MIR, the four parts are visible as parts, and inside them the rules are visible as rules; what hides the grammar is the weight of the rare cases around each rule, which is where the attacks were and where the words went. A builder with the MICS as the map and the MIR as the reference will find every rule; a reader starting from the MIR alone will not see the shape in the first hour. That is acceptable for a freeze whose map is the MICS, and the MICS says it stands on its own. I would not cut further before the freeze: the last say-it-once pass took 7,000 words out without a verdict moving, and the next one would be cutting against texts the C verifier has already read; the place for it is MOR 1's companion documents, not the frozen text.

Two readability points that are not size: the "Collectives in the identity chain" section and "Made before, made after" are the two places a reader must hold in mind together to follow any line question, and they are 2,000 lines apart; a one-line pointer from each to the other, in italics, would cost nothing.
