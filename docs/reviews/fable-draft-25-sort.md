## 4. What would be left

**If the five built-on-top clauses left, and nothing else:** almost everything. Agreements would lose the authors' burden, the delegator's default way out, the "next obligation" of whoever holds for others, and a hand-off sentence. No field and no socket would go.

**If the other layers' clauses left too** (all reasoned), Agreements would be about 82 clauses, and would say this:

- two acts, Terms and Statement, and their fields;
- an agreement can be a party, and how it signs (G1);
- everything Terms use is fixed by fingerprint (G2), and who wrote them (G3.1);
- when Terms are in force, and that they change only by an update which their own update condition lets through, by default everyone who signed (G4);
- nobody is bound without a signature path: their own, a power they signed for, or an update condition they signed (G5);
- spots for people not yet known (G10);
- the fields for a receiver, a call, a control cMIP, a data cMIP and a settlement, and the contest as a kind of Statement (parts of G1, G8.5, G11);
- forks of an agreement's own thread, and how they are resolved (G12);
- an act is read against what it cites, and citing gives order (G13);
- one sentence on the place: where Terms name one, every reader takes what is latest from it.

That is close to his sentence: what can be written, who signed, what replaced what. Everything about how a deal is carried out sits in the eight sockets.

## 5. Does the shower's click still happen?

The wish: "can I with a single click reward all the people that contributed to the service or good via MOR". All of this section is reasoned.

**What the remainder still gives the click:**

- the payer finds whom to pay from the work alone: the receiver for the version read, followed through any agreement it names (G1.3, G1.4);
- the payment is read against the version it named, whatever changes afterwards (G13.1, G13.2);
- works that stand on other works are reached, because an agreement can be a party or a receiver (G1.1);
- contributors who arrive later have a spot to fill (G10).

**What the click needs that the remainder does not hold:**

- **Money**, for the payment and its settlement.
- **A `control` cMIP**, for the split. Nothing in draft 25 makes anyone divide either (its stated cost "A service is a hand"), so this is not new. One thing is new: if G11.5 and the empty-socket part of G11.2 leave, a work whose receiver is one of its own owners, with no service, names no one as passing on. Its Terms would have to name a `control` cMIP for the other owners to be seen at all.
- **The report block in the MICS**, for each side's signed word that money arrived and went on.
- **A `data` cMIP**, for the one who clicked to see further than their own leg.
- **Envelopes**, for the click to land on the holder of the baker's rights and not on a copier.

**So:** the remainder lets the click be written and checked, one leg at a time. It does not, bare, show that "all the people" were rewarded. That fits his words tonight: "Agreements is not to be used bare. It's to build on top of." The practical consequence is an order of work, not a hole in the grammar: the click needs at least one `control` cMIP, one `data` cMIP, the report block and Money to exist before it can be shown end to end.

**Three clauses the click cannot lose**, wherever they are placed: G1.4 (who receives for the version named), G13.2 (a payment names the version it read), and G10.6 if contributors who earned a spot are to keep what was offered when they earned it. G10.6 is one of my four "cannot tell".
