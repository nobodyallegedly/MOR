## In plain words first

The exploration is right about one thing and the texts already say it: two complete versions of one deal with the same parent are self-proving evidence that something went wrong, and nobody should build on either until it is sorted. That is what the reference, the alarm and the settlement already do.

Read closely, the explored shape is **today's settlement with one change: finality removed.** A repair "from the last agreed version, naming both tips as closed" is a settling version whose parent is the reference instead of the kept tip, carrying the same field 26. "No branch wins" is a figure of speech: a repair that writes A's words back in has chosen A. What the shape really changes is three things: a version grown during the fork counts for nothing (today it counts, A4); the repair sits on the reference rather than on a branch (a grammar choice, harmless); and **a branch hidden at the split and revealed after the repair breaks the deal again** (today it loses, F192). The exploration calls the third a stated cost and offers anchoring. I read it as the hole, and anchoring does not close it.

1. **Any party holding a hidden complete sibling has a standing option to revert the deal to its reference at a time of their choosing, and hold it there.** Today that option exists only until the first settlement; the shape makes it permanent, through every repair. Anchoring protects the honest forgotten device, not against a party who anchors their hidden version at the split and reveals it later: by anchors it is early, and under the shape early wins. And the shape protects against hidden *growth* (a child of a closed tip counts for nothing) while leaving hidden *siblings* able to re-break: same signatures, same ignorance of the repairers, opposite outcomes, chosen by whoever hides the version. Whoever hides makes it a sibling. **BREAKS, high.**
2. **The shape's own test for "broken" is met by the repair itself.** A repair is a complete version whose parent is the reference, beside the two branch roots; a second repair closing the first repair's tip is, by the same test, a fresh break, or an undo of a valid repair. A collective has F185's answer because one act is the broken one and a rollback naming a valid act is itself broken; in a deal's fork no single act is broken, both branches are valid complete versions, so that rule has nothing to grip. Today a second settlement from the reference naming the first changes nothing (test 4). **BREAKS, high to medium**; it decides whether what a repair wrote in (a newcomer, a re-signed sale) can be taken back by the reference's parties alone.
3. **"Like a collective" voids two valid acts and reverses three settled principles.** A collective's broken stretch counts for nothing because its keys moved without the consent its rules require. A deal's two branches were each signed by every party: valid, complete, true mark. Treating both as "broken" voids valid acts, against F185's own line "a rollback voids only an act Law reads as broken, never a valid one" (repair, not undo); against IT2b's reasoning, "every cure would let something published after a fork reopen it, against IT1", and F199 ("judged by its own history only"); and against A3 and A4, each decided twice in the two rounds ("Settle is final… Matter closed"; "both have signed the deal, so it holds"). **CONFLICTS, high.**
4. **QH2 is answered only under one of the two open signing rules, and "like a collective" picks the other.** A collective's rollback is signed by the powers of the agreement in force before the break; anyone added during the stretch counts for nothing and is signed anew (RB2). Applied to a deal that is F192's answer to QH2 exactly: Carla loses her place. The exploration says the shape closes QH2 "its parties signing, DQ5", which is the other rule: the parties of every tip it closes. Both cannot be "like a collective". **CONFLICTS, medium to high.**
5. **The judge of forks has nothing left to do, or legislates.** With no branch kept, a judge's repair can only close tips, which leaves the deal on the reference where it already was, and the parties still need every signature to move. DQ8's purpose, an outside tiebreak "where the parties cannot agree", is gone, with QG4's chain and F206 as machinery about nothing; or the judge writes in "what continues", terms no party consented to. **CONFLICTS with DQ8, medium.**
6. **What counts during the stretch is not said, and what is said contradicts itself.** "No new version counts" and "payments keep counting under the version they named" cannot both be true of a payment under A3, a version grown during the fork: either A3 counts for that payment or the payment names no version and is owed back (rule 32a). Splits, releases, grants, transfers and a deal's grantee (its split service) under a branch are not mentioned; in a collective they count for nothing (RB1), which would stop the money pipe for every forked deal. **UNCLEAR, medium.**
7. **"Terms broken, do not use this" has no carrier an adversary cannot filter, except the deal's keepers.** Relays are transport; homes hold identity chains, not versions; clients compute from what they are served, and the seller chooses what their relays serve. The pair cannot be faked (it needs every party's signature) and gains little by suppression while payments count; what suppression keeps is a newcomer working on a doomed branch. Timing becomes a weapon under the shape (a reveal after repair reverts), so a relay or keeper that withholds one signature act gains leverage it does not have under F192. **UNCLEAR, and a COST if clients are the carrier.**
8. The rest: what a buyer and a newcomer keep and lose (a cost to state, section 9); a stake sold twice and two endings do not take this shape (section 10); the never-forking deal carries no new field or act, but its exposure to a sibling surfacing late grows, since A2 to A9 would be void as versions rather than counting under A4 (section 11).

What held: nothing grown during the fork or on a closed tip counts, which closes my F190 findings 2 and 3 as I wrote them (F192 already does); finding 1 stays closed only because F192 and F206 are inherited, not because of the shape; the alarm already carries the pair as self-proving evidence wherever money moves.

**My reading, not a decision:** the exploration mistook a settlement that has a parent on a branch for a settlement that "lets a branch win". If the discomfort is that QH2's Carla loses her place, that is F192's stated cost and DQ5's reason meeting; it can be narrowed without giving up finality (section 12 names three shapes from the project's own tools: keepers, knots, anchors with a rule). Removing finality costs every honest party who ever acted on a repair, forever, to spare one newcomer who signed onto a branch nobody else could see.

## How findings are marked

- **BREAKS**: an attack works, or a common case does not work, as written or as the shape would be built.
- **COST**: a stated or unstated cost falls on someone; I say on whom and whether it is understated.
- **CONFLICTS**: the shape and a decided text, or two parts of the shape, disagree.
- **UNCLEAR**: the shape can be read two ways, or a verifier could not check it as written.
- **HOLDS**: I tried an attack and it failed; I say what I tried.

Each finding gives what breaks, the smallest story, who gains, whether they can pull it, the texts, and a weight. Ordered by weight. Sections 1 to 11 answer the eight questions; the map from question to section is at the end.

## 1. The hidden sibling is a standing option to revert any repaired deal; anchoring does not close it; the shape protects against children and not siblings. BREAKS (the stated cost; against F192, A3; questions 1 and 2)

**What breaks.** The shape says: a repair closes the tips of the branches its signers see; a version grown on a closed tip counts for nothing; a whole branch hidden at the split and revealed after the repair "breaks the deal again; it can stall, never win". Three things are wrong with the last sentence.

*It wins.* After a repair, the deal runs on the repair's line, and people act on it: a newcomer is written in, a sale re-signed, a share raised, work delivered. A reveal sends the deal back to the reference and makes the repair "a closed mistake"; everything in it must be written into a new repair and signed again by everyone. A party who prefers the reference to the repair refuses, as DQ5 lets any party refuse. For them the reveal is not a stall: it is the repair undone, at their chosen time, with no signature of theirs needed beyond the one they placed at the split. Under F192 the same version "reopens nothing": nobody who settled in good faith loses anything. The shape moves the loss from the hidden version's holder to everyone who relied on the repair.

*It repeats.* Every hidden sibling is one more reversion. Honest out-of-step devices produce one; a party whose partner's client re-signs on a retry can hold several.

*The attacker chooses the favourable kind.* The shape closes hidden growth: B2, a child of B1 revealed late, "counts for nothing" because its parent is a closed tip. It leaves hidden siblings able to re-break because their parent, the reference, is never closed (closing it would close the repair). The two cases are the same for the repairers, who saw neither; they differ only in which act the hidden version names as its parent, which whoever hides it decides. Whoever hides a version makes it a sibling.

**Story.** Ana and Ben's deal, version 2, 50/50. Ben's client re-signs on a retry, so Ana holds two complete versions 3 with the same parent, A (which both use) and C (in Ana's drawer); a third, B, forks visibly from an honest out-of-step device. They repair: a version from 2 naming A's tip and B's tip as closed, writing A's words in with Ben's share raised to 70% for more work. Ben does the work. Ana publishes C. The deal is broken again; 2 is in force, 50/50; the next repair needs Ana; Ana offers 55/45. Under F192 today, C reopens nothing and Ben's 70% stands: test 1, `review_fdb_1_today_a_hidden_sibling_revealed_after_a_settlement_reopens_nothing`. The hidden child form (B2) and the hidden sibling form (C) lose alike today: test 2, `review_fdb_2_today_a_hidden_child_and_a_hidden_sibling_lose_alike`.

**Who gains, and can they pull it.** The party holding the sibling, alone, for whatever the repair gave the others. Pulling it needs every party's signature on the hidden version once, at the split, which F186's own premise supplies ("two devices out of step, a careless client, or one party in bad faith with the other mistaken"), and the ability to withhold one act, which every party and every relay has.

**Is anchoring a sufficient answer?** No, on three counts. (a) An anchor proves an act existed by a point; it cannot prove a sibling did *not* exist when the repair was signed, so no anchor on the repair alone orders a later-revealed sibling against it. (b) To use anchors a rule is needed: "a version of the fork not anchored before the repair's anchor, on the deal's declared clock, is a late leftover and counts for nothing". That is F192's "could see" rebuilt on a clock, which the exploration set out to drop; it also needs the deal's reference to name its clock (F176: compared only on a declared reference) and makes every unanchored honest version lose, as F192's cost does. (c) The party "with an incentive to make the deal stall" anchors their hidden sibling the day they make it (F173: anyone may anchor any act, cheaply) and reveals it after the repair: by anchors it is early, and under the shape early re-breaks. Anchoring turns the honest forgotten device into a provable case and does nothing against the one case the stated cost names.

**Against the stated cost's wording** ("if you enter a deal where one party has an incentive of making the deal stall and you did not anchor the acts… what can I say?"): the party who could not protect themselves here is the one who *did* anchor the repair; the attacker is the one who anchored. F176's shape, "protection is available; whoever does not take it bears", requires that taking the protection works. **Weight: high.** It reopens, as a permanent feature, the hole F192 closed one day earlier for the case F192 named outright ("a whole branch the settlement never saw… reopens nothing").

## 2. The shape's own test for "broken" is met by the repair; a repair of a repair is undefined, and the texts' one answer does not transfer. BREAKS as a rule, UNCLEAR as written (questions 1 and 3; F185's attempted rollback; A3)

**What breaks.** "Two complete versions naming the same parent are self-proving evidence: the deal is broken." A repair is a complete version naming the reference as its parent, beside the branch roots A1 and B1. So the test must already read "two complete versions naming the same parent, neither closed by a repair", which is fine. Then: a second complete version R2 from the reference, naming the first repair's tip as closed, is either (i) a new break, R1's line and R2 standing as two unclosed children of the reference, so the deal falls back to the reference and the next repair must close R1's tip and R2; or (ii) an undo, R2 counting as the repair of a fork that R1 had already repaired. The exploration does not say which, and neither is A3.

Under (ii) the reference's parties alone take back whatever R1 wrote in, which is finding 4's bait branch run through the repair. Under (i) R2 is indistinguishable from the hidden sibling of section 1 (both are late unclosed children of the reference), so the rule for one is the rule for the other: both re-break. F185's rule for a collective, "a rollback naming an act Law does not read as broken is rejected, and is itself a broken act", needs a broken act to point at; in a deal's fork there is none, each branch being a valid complete version, so a verifier cannot tell a repair from an attempted undo except by whether the fork it closes was already closed, which is exactly the fact R2 contests by existing.

**Story.** Ana and Ben repair for A naming B; the repair writes Carla in. Later Ana and Ben sign R2 from the reference, closing R1's tip and B1 "as mistakes". Today, R2 holds R1 in its history, is after it, and changes nothing: test 4, `review_fdb_4_today_a_second_settlement_from_the_reference_naming_the_first_changes_nothing`. Under the shape, Carla is out under (ii), or the deal is broken under (i) and Carla's return waits on a repair that, under F185-signing, she does not sign.

**Who gains.** The reference's parties, against anyone a repair brought in or bound; or whoever wants the deal frozen. **Weight: high to medium.** It is the one rule the shape would need first, and the texts have no analogue to borrow.

## 3. "Like a collective" voids two valid acts, and reverses three principles decided from first principles. CONFLICTS (F185 (4), repair not undo; IT1 and IT2b's reasoning; F199; A3 and A4, decided twice)

A collective is broken when its keys moved without the consent its rules require (rule 37d): one act, the broken act, is void, and everything bound to its stretch with it. The force of "broken" there is that Identity counts something Law does not. In a deal's fork nothing of the kind happened: both versions are complete, both marks true, every party signed each. Calling the pair "broken" voids two valid acts for being two. That meets:

- **F185 (4)**, decided by Nobody, allegedly: "a rollback voids only an act Law reads as broken, never a valid one", with the gloss "repair, not undo: finality stands". The shape's repair voids both branches and, as section 1 shows, the repair itself whenever a sibling surfaces.
- **IT2b**, decided: "every cure would let something published after a fork reopen it, against IT1"; and **F199**: "what was decided is judged by what its signers could see". The shape's stated cost is precisely something published after the repair reopening it.
- **A3**, in both rounds: "Settle is final… Only one survives… Matter closed"; "settled is final". A repair under the shape is never final against a sibling.
- **A4**, in both rounds: "Both have signed the deal, so it holds. It's not recommended practice but it works"; "they have to count, both versions count, both signed". The shape drops the half that says a new act under a branch counts, keeping only payments; the exploration says so, in its own words.

The exploration's root-cause sentence is right: without a clock nothing tells a branch hidden before a settlement from one grown after it, and every fix chooses which mistake to make. F192 chose the mistake every other finality rule in the project chose (IT1, IT2b, F199, F185 (4)): what the deciders could see decides. The shape chooses the opposite mistake for deals alone, and gives no reason why a deal should be the one agreement whose decisions can be undone by what nobody saw. **Weight: high**, because these are principles, not readings, and the working practice after 6 and 7 October says a principle-level choice is laid beside the earlier decision in its own words before it is put.

Beside it, the analogy to Identity's conflict is borrowed in its language and not its rule. Identity's conflict is **"contested, not frozen"** (rules 10b and 10d, F55): neither receipt counts at that position, and the conflict is settled by an authority that orders, the home's own rotation or the audit log. The shape freezes the deal ("no new version counts") and has no ordering authority. KERI's duplicity, which the exploration cites, is detected and propagated by witnesses that receipt events; the analogue here is the deal's keepers, which brings section 7.

## 4. QH2 is answered only under DQ5-signing, and the collective's rule gives F192's answer. CONFLICTS (question 3; F185; RB2; DQ5; QH2)

**What the exploration claims.** The shape "answers QH2: a hidden branch is not closed, so the deal breaks again and the next repair must close it, its parties signing, DQ5".

**What the two signing rules give.**

*F185's rule, the parties of the last agreed version.* For a deal that is every party of the reference. A branch adding Carla is closed by Ana and Ben alone; Carla is "signed anew" if they wish, as a member added during a collective's broken stretch is (RB2: "what others relied on during the stretch counts for nothing, for good, and is signed anew"). That is QH2's cost as F192 states it: her payment protected, her place gone. Under this rule the shape answers QH2 the way F192 does and the bait branch is open, through the repair instead of the settlement. The exploration presents the opposite.

*DQ5's rule, the parties of every tip it closes.* Carla signs the repair closing her branch; the bait branch is shut, as it is today under DQ5 for a named tip. But a hidden branch Carla is on is not closed by the first repair under either rule, since nobody saw it; under the shape it re-breaks the deal when it surfaces and the next repair needs her. That is the only point at which the shape improves on F192 for Carla, and it is section 1's hole seen from her side: her protection is everyone else's exposure.

**What breaks under each.** Under F185-signing, QH2's bait branch, and section 2's undo by the reference's parties. Under DQ5-signing, nothing new in power (a party could already refuse every settlement), but it is not "like a collective", where nobody added in the stretch has a voice in the way back, so the title's reasoning does not carry it; and a puppet newcomer on a hidden branch gives its controller deniable refusal. Under both, **a party declared absent on a branch during the fork**: the declaration is an act under a void version; a collective's rollback registers such declarations (RB3) and the shape says nothing, so under "every party" an absent party could block the repair for good. **Weight: medium to high**, since the exploration's claim to close QH2 rests on the open question it lists beneath it.

## 5. The judge of forks has nothing left to do, or legislates. CONFLICTS with DQ8 (question 6; QG4; F206)

DQ8 gives the judge one power: "where the parties cannot agree, the arbitrator… may settle the fork", weighing "the keepers' record of who signed what, and in what order". It is the project's answer to the no-clock problem for deals: a human reads evidence a verifier cannot. Under the shape "no branch wins or is dropped", so a judge's repair can only close tips; the deal then sits on the reference, where "while broken" already put it, and the parties who could not agree on a repair still need every signature to clone the reference. The judge adds nothing. QG4's chain of judgment, the request (type 22), the settlement (type 23) and F206's two rules become machinery with no decision behind it.

The alternative is that the judge's repair "writes in what continues": terms of the deal drafted by a judge. Parties consented in advance to a judge choosing among versions they all signed ("a grant to act under specific conditions, here the condition is the existence of a fork"); they did not consent to a judge writing their deal. If the judge may copy one branch's words in, that is choosing a branch, today's settlement, and F192 with F206 ("any judge speaks once per fork") are what keep my F190 finding 1 closed; the shape neither adds to nor removes that.

**Who gains.** Whichever party prefers the reference, since the one outsider who could move the deal off it is disarmed. **Weight: medium.** DQ8 was decided with its reasoning; the shape would need to say it is being reversed.

## 6. What counts during the stretch, and what "payments keep counting under a void version" means. UNCLEAR, CONFLICTS with itself (questions 1 and 5; A4; rule 32a; DQ6; RB1)

- **Payments.** A purchase names "the line at which the payer's client read it current, a version of that agreement" (rule 32a); a payment naming a line that carries none of the agreement's claims is no purchase and is owed back. If A3, grown during the fork, "counts for nothing" as a version, a payment naming A3 either counts under A3 (so A3 counts for it: the version does count, for that act) or is owed back (the buyer is not protected). The shape needs the first; said precisely, it is today's A4 for payments.
- **Splits.** The split service follows the version the payment names (A4, DQ6 keeps the leftover turns per branch). Under the shape the version in force is the reference while broken; if the service splits under the reference's plan, the money is mis-split against the version the buyer paid under; if under A3's plan, A3 counts. Not said.
- **A deal's grantee.** A payee's grant to the deal's split service is a scoped key (Identity, H1). In a collective's broken stretch "a grantee's act counts for nothing" (RB1). Imported, every split of every forked deal counts for nothing until the repair, while payments keep arriving: the money pipe stops for the whole stretch, and rule 20 ("a split for every incoming receipt") cannot be met. Not said.
- **Releases, grants, transfers, succession under a branch.** Today they count under the branch's version (A4). Under the shape they are versions' effects and count for nothing. A release to the public domain signed under A3, relied on by the world, would be undone by a sibling surfacing. Not said.
- **Signatures.** A collective's act of the stretch "places no signature at all" (rule 37d). If a branch version places none, Carla's signature on it binds her to nothing, which is right; if it does (rule 1: "a party is bound only by its own signature act", 45a: signatures "bind those parties"), she is bound while the version binds nobody to her. Not said.

**Weight: medium.** Each is answerable; together they show the shape is a sentence, not yet a rule, and that once answered it collapses toward A4 for everything that involves money.

## 7. "Terms broken, do not use this": what carries it, and what suppressing or faking it gains. UNCLEAR, COST (question 4; rules 7 to 11a; Q28; F188's SHOULD; DQ6)

**What could carry it.**
- *Relays*: transport. The relay transport cMIP makes homes serve whole chains for routes and encryption keys so a verifier sees forks; nothing like it exists for a deal's versions, which are everyday acts at whatever relays each party uses, and a party serves what it likes from its own.
- *Homes*: identity chains. A version is not on one; it would be only if version signatures were chain signatures (F132), which the exploration does not propose and which would put every version through the safety-key ceremony.
- *Keepers*: the one carrier the texts already give a deal. Rule 7 has a keeper record "the deal and the acts around it as they arrive, in its operator's sequence"; Q28 has a deal's declaration ordered against signatures "that the keepers the deal names recorded before recording the declaration", with the line "deals that need protection name a keeper"; DQ8 has the judge weigh "the keepers' record of who signed what, and in what order". A deal with keepers can carry the pair, and can order a sibling against a repair. A deal without keepers cannot, and most deals will have none (field 3 is optional).
- *Clients*: compute from what they are served. The buyer's strong SHOULD (F188) fetches "the seller's latest version from its homes or relays": the seller's. The seller's alarm (DQ7) and the split numbering (DQ6) carry the pair only when money moves on both branches.

So "propagated by everyone who sees both" is a client courtesy with no protocol delivery, exactly as Identity's "anyone may publish the pair, clients SHOULD flag" is; the difference is that an identity's conflict is settled by an authority that orders (the home's rotation, the audit), and a deal's would be settled by nobody.

**Faking.** Impossible without every party's signature on two versions; a party who can get that can fork the deal, which is the thing itself, not a fake. **Suppressing.** A seller serving only A keeps selling; buyers are protected by payments counting, so no money is gained; what is gained is a newcomer kept working on a branch that will be closed, and, under the shape, control of *when* the break is seen, since a reveal after repair reverts (section 1). A relay that withholds one party's signature act and releases it later, my F190 finding 3's relay variant, gains the same timing power. Under F192 timing gains nothing after settlement; under the shape it is the whole game. **Weight: low to medium as a carrier question; medium as a cost**, because it hands relays and keepers leverage F192 took from them.

## 8. HOLDS: what the shape gets right, and what it closes

- **The pair is self-proving evidence, and a verifier holding both must act on it.** True, and built: `deal_state` reports the reference the moment it holds two complete clones of one version, whatever else it holds; the alarm fires on a payment naming a version the seller does not hold (F189, 3). Nothing new is needed for the detection.
- **Nothing grown during the fork or on a closed tip counts** closes my F190 findings 2 (a party alone on a dropped branch) and 3 (a version held back and published late) as I wrote them: both were growth. F192 already closes both (`f192_a_party_alone_on_a_dropped_branch_reopens_nothing`, `f192_a_version_held_back_and_published_late_reopens_nothing`); the shape would close them a second way.
- **Finding 1** (the judge unsettles alone) stays closed only through F192's "any judge speaks once" and F206, inherited as the exploration notes; the shape adds nothing and, per section 5, may remove the judge's reason to exist.
- **"Could see" is no longer needed.** True as stated, and the price is section 1: without "could see", a late sibling is early.
- **The repair's grammar is already there.** Field 26 names the tips closed; the only grammar change is that the repair's parent (field 11) is the reference. Light.

## 9. A buyer and a newcomer joining by growth before the fork is visible. COST (question 5; A4; rule 1; rule 32a; F188's SHOULD)

**A buyer** under A3 keeps the purchase: the payment is judged under the version it named, the goods (a key delivery) are owed, the split follows A3's plan if section 6 is answered as A4 answers it. The buyer loses every reliance that is not a payment: a licence term in A3's words, a release, a grant, any promise the deal made beyond the sale. Today, under A4, those count. The buyer's check (F188) cannot see a hidden sibling; the alarm reaches the seller, not the buyer, and only when money moves on the other branch.

**A newcomer** (Carla, written in as a party on A3, or on a branch nobody else can see) keeps a payment she made and, if the shape makes a void version place no signature, is bound to nothing. She loses her party status, any stake field 7 gave her, her voice, her place in the chain of judgment and in succession, and any grant; and she loses them *retroactively*, the day the sibling surfaces, which may be years later. Work she handed over is outside MOR; she keeps a claim in an outside court, as IT2b says of a creditor. Her only protections are the ones the project already gives: a keeper named by the deal (Q28), an anchor on her version with a rule to read it (section 1 (b)), DQ6's numbering if money moves. Under F192 she has the same exposure before the first settlement and none after it; under the shape she has it for good. **This cost is real under both rules; the shape makes it permanent and does not state it.** Who gains: whoever brought her in on a branch they could later disown. **Weight: medium**, and it is the mirror of QH2: the exploration protects Carla on a hidden branch by exposing every Carla on a repaired one.

## 10. A stake sold twice (2.8) and two endings (IT1) do not take this shape. CONFLICTS (question 7; F217; F132 U1 and U2; IT1)

**A stake sold twice.** Ben sells 400,000 to Carla and to Dan from two devices; each transfer is complete, each paid. The parties differ (Ben with Carla, Ben with Dan); one party, the seller, is common and alone at fault. A repair "from the last agreed version, closing both tips" returns the stake to Ben and voids both sales, with both payments owed back by nobody in any format: that is the formats review's 2.8 complaint, "rule 5 settles for the seller", made into the rule. The direction already taken (2.8's correction; F217, decided 10 October 2026) is the opposite: order the two on **the seller's own line** (a stake transfer as a chain signature), the first confers, the second confers nothing and its payment is owed back by the seller. The double sale has a knot; the shape ignores it.

**Two endings of a collective.** IT1's tie, "two endings made without knowing each other, neither naming the other, are concurrent, and neither counts until one names the other", resolved by a third ending naming both (U2), looks like the shape. Three differences. (a) The knot comes first: U1 orders two endings sharing a signer by that signer's own chain, and since F132 every ending signature is a chain signature, so a true tie remains only between endings sharing no signer; a deal's two versions share every signer, so under the knot principle ("wherever acts must be ordered and no citation orders them, use the knots… before declaring a tie or a cost", Nobody, allegedly, F132) they would be ordered, not tied, if their signatures sat on a knot. (b) The third ending *is* an ending that does something; it is not a return to the status quo with everything to be signed again. (c) IT1's first clause is finality: "a complete ending is final: a later fork or closing of the same collective counts for nothing", which is F192's clause, the one the shape removes. So the collective's endings are the shape's counterexample: the texts resolve two endings by knots and finality, and reserve the stay-on-the-status-quo tie for the case with no shared signer.

**Weight: medium to low** for 2.8 (already decided the other way); the IT1 comparison matters because the exploration offers the collective as the model, and the collective's own fork rules do the opposite.

## 11. The common case. HOLDS for mechanics, COST for exposure (question 8)

A deal that never forks signs no new field and no new act: the repair's grammar is field 26 with the reference as parent, used only in a fork. The buyer's SHOULD, the alarm and the split numbering exist already. HOLDS.

Two costs on the common case, one new. (a) Today a deal that *appears* never to have forked, A1 to A9 in force, keeps every act under A1 to A9 counting when a sibling of A1 surfaces (A4: the deal is forked at the reference, the branch's acts count, one settlement ends it; test 3, `review_fdb_3_before_any_settlement_a_hidden_sibling_already_sends_the_deal_to_its_reference`). Under the shape A2 to A9 are void as versions and every non-payment act under them with them. Every deal carries that exposure from version 1, since any deal may have an out-of-step sibling at any version. New, and against "a common case must work by default". (b) If the KERI analogy is taken seriously, the pair needs witnesses, and the deal's keepers are the only candidate (section 7): every deal that wants the protection names keepers, a weight on every ordinary deal that today only "deals that need protection" carry. Stated, it is a choice; unstated, it is the common case paying for the rare one.

## 12. What the texts already offer instead, laid beside the exploration (readings, not decisions)

The exploration's root is right and shared by F192: no clock, so a hidden sibling and a late one look alike. The project has three tools for exactly that, each already decided somewhere; none is in the exploration.

1. **F192 as it stands, with QH2 answered as its stated cost, narrowed by client conformance.** Carla's client, before she signs onto a deal, checks that the version she joins is the only complete child of its parent at the parties' relays and keepers, warns if not, and anchors her version. This is the F188 SHOULD turned toward newcomers. Cost: Carla bears a hidden bait branch she could not see, as every counterparty of a collective bears a fork drawn on an old line (IT2b: "safe once cited, against everything but the collusion of an ending's signers, which stays visible"). It is the lightest answer and the one the other finality rules took.
2. **Keepers as the deal's clock, where named** (Q28's shape). A settlement is final against every version the deal's keepers recorded after recording it, under their rule; a version recorded before it, which the settlement did not name, settles nothing. Deals that need protection name a keeper, as Q28 already says of declarations; DQ8 already has the judge read the same record. Cost: trust in keepers (rule 11a, stated), and no protection without one.
3. **Knots for settlements** (F132's shape). A settling version's signatures are chain signatures; a party's signature on a sibling placed on their identity chain after their signature on a settlement counts for nothing (U4's rule), and a sibling whose signatures are not on the chain cannot be ordered and so is a late version that loses (F192). Cost: a settlement goes through the safety-key ceremony, as a fork or closing does; a version does not, so only settlements are heavy. It is the project's own answer to "important decisions needing an order no device can blur".

Each keeps finality. Any of them answers QH2 by narrowing who bears, not by making everyone bear. Which, if any, is for Nobody, allegedly; I note only that the exploration's option, removing finality, is the one the texts already rejected in IT1, IT2b, F185 (4), F192 and F199.

## The eight questions, answered in one line each

1. **Does it close F190 findings 1 to 3 and QH2?** 2 and 3 yes, as F192 already does; 1 only by inheriting F192 and F206; QH2 only under DQ5-signing, and not "like a collective" (section 4). **New hole:** the hidden sibling as a standing reversion, and the child-versus-sibling asymmetry (section 1); the repair meeting its own test for a break (section 2).
2. **Is the stated cost only a stall, and is anchoring sufficient?** No: a reversion at the attacker's chosen time, repeatable; anchoring cannot prove absence, needs a "could see" rule to mean anything, and is beaten by an attacker who anchors their hidden version (section 1).
3. **Who signs a repair?** Under F185's rule the bait branch and the undo are open; under DQ5's rule they are shut but it is not a collective's rule; absence during the fork is unanswered under both (sections 2 and 4).
4. **What carries "do not use this"?** Keepers, where named; otherwise clients, served by the parties. Unfakeable; suppression gains a newcomer's labour and, under the shape, the timing of the break (section 7).
5. **A buyer and a newcomer before the fork is visible?** The buyer keeps the purchase and loses every other reliance; the newcomer keeps a payment and loses her place, retroactively, for good (section 9).
6. **What is left for a judge?** Closing tips, which changes nothing, or writing terms, which nobody consented to; DQ8's purpose is gone (section 5).
7. **A stake sold twice and two endings?** Neither: the double sale is ordered on the seller's line (F217), and endings use knots and finality first, the tie being for endings sharing no signer (section 10).
8. **Does the never-forking deal carry new weight?** No field and no act; a larger exposure to a late sibling (A2 to A9 void instead of counting), and keepers on every deal if the pair needs witnesses (section 11).
