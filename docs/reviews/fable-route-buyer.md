## In plain words

The decision is right and the texts can carry it with acts they already have. A route is a chain of hops, each shown by a receipt or a claim naming the hop before it; the purchase is its last hop, and today its buyer is whoever paid that hop, the conversion service. F413 says the buyer is the first payer. What makes that checkable is one match the texts do not yet ask for: the first payer's own claim must say what they were buying, by naming the seller's price tag in the field a claim already has for "what this payment follows" (key 4), and the purchase at the end of the route must name the same tag (receipt field 5). Where the two match along a valid route, the first payer is the buyer; where they do not, the service bought for its own account and the first payer's money went astray, which rule 28 already names. The refund runs back the way the money came, one hop at a time, by the rule each hop already has; nothing new is needed for it, and the direct shortcut can be a cMIP's.

## 1. The sentence, and where it goes

**Money, rule 24, one sentence at its end:** *The first payer of a route is the payer of its first hop (its receipt's field 2, or the signer of the claim that shows it); the first payer's claim of that hop names, in key 4, what the route pays for, as the last hop's receipt names it in field 5.*

**Agreements, "A contest of a purchase", after "The buyer is the payer the payment's commitment names":** *Where the purchase is the last hop of a route (Money rule 24), the buyer is the route's first payer, where the route is valid and the first payer's claim of the first hop names the tag the purchase names; that claim is the buyer's claim of the purchase for field 0. Where the first payer signed no such claim, or committed only a bare key, the purchase has no buyer who can contest it (as above). While the route ending at the purchase is unknown, the buyer is unknown and a contest shows nothing yet; where it is stopped or broken before the seller, there is no purchase at the seller: the hop that failed owes its forward (Money rule 27), and the money is owed back along the route (below). A conversion service carries; it is the buyer of a purchase only where no earlier hop's payer named the tag, or where it paid with no route (receipt field 6 absent).*

**Which act names what.** The first payer: the first hop's receipt, field 2 (an identity, or a bare key), or, where the service signed no receipt, the first payer's own claim. The purchase: the last hop's receipt, field 5 the tag, field 9 the ownership version, field 6 the previous hop. The link between them: the route, read back from the purchase through field 6 and key 10 (rule 24, as S7i built it), plus the match of key 4 against field 5. Every input is an act the verifier holds or can answer unknown for. *Why the claim must name the tag:* without it the only act saying where the first payer's money was meant to go is the service's own receipt (its forward), and a service that forwards a payment to another seller's tag would make the first payer the buyer of something they never chose. With it, a forward that does not match is the service's own purchase and a failed forward of the first payer's, in one reading.

## 2. The refund

**Back along the route, by the rule each hop already has.** A refund owed on a payment is owed to that payment's payer (Money rules 11, 17 and 19); on a route, the purchase's payer is the last service, which is then owed back by the seller, and owes its own payer in turn under the agreement its forward names (rule 28), and so on to the first payer. *Sentence, Money rule 28, one clause: a refund owed on a purchase paid through a route is owed to the last hop's payer, who owes it on to the hop before, each under the agreement that hop's forward named, until it reaches the first payer; each hop's debt is a refund as rule 11 gives one, owed in that hop's unit.* The core checks nothing new: each hop is a payment with a payer and a refund owed on it. **Where a hop in between failed**, nothing reached the seller, so the seller owes nothing; the hop that stopped owes its forward (rule 27) to its next payee's side, and every hop before it owes back what it received, the same way. The direct way (the seller paying the first payer's refund rail named in their claim's key 7, in the seller's unit) is simpler to look at and harder to check (the first payer may have no pointer in that unit, which is why they routed); it is a cMIP's optimisation, not the core's rule.

## 3. What it breaks or leaves open

- **A converter buying for its own account.** Covered by the sentence: a purchase whose receipt names no previous hop, or whose route's first payer named another tag or none, is the service's own. No special case.
- **A route through two converters.** The first payer is still the first hop's payer; the route must be valid through both, each forward matching, as S7i's three-hop vectors already check. The refund runs through both, hop by hop.
- **A forward paid in parts (R1).** Orthogonal: who the buyer is does not change with how a forward was carried. I would take S7i's (b) for R1 on its own merits (the records naming a forward add up to it under rule 12, the stop showing the shortfall), since (a) shows a service that paid 4900 and 100 as owing 5000; but it is a separate sentence in rules 24 and 27.
- **R4's readings** (each hop paid by the previous hop's payee to the forward's next payee, its received amount the forward's): keep S7i's clause; the buyer rule rests on it and adds nothing to it.
- **Standing while the route is unknown.** A contest by the first payer before the rail has answered for every hop shows nothing yet, not nothing ever; the texts should say "yet", or a buyer whose converter is slow will be read as having no standing.
- **One-way doors at the freeze.** None opened: the claim's key 4 and the receipt's fields 5 and 6 exist; no format changes. One door the decision closes knowingly: the buyer of a routed purchase is read from the claims and receipts, so a first payer who paid a converter with no MOR claim at all (a plain Lightning invoice, say) has no standing, which is the same stated cost the texts already carry for a bare-key payer. Say it beside the sentence.

**Before the freeze:** yes; it is who may contest a sale and who is owed back, and both are read from the frozen formats.
