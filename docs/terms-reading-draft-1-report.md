# Terms reading, draft 1: what it could not express

*The Dubsar Suite, 11 November 2026, for Nobody, allegedly, and the MOR lead. On `cmips/cmip-terms-reading-draft-1.md`, written from main (`fc624ab8`) before the core's declarations exist (F441, gate decision 1). Nothing here is decided; each point says what the draft did and what it needs.*

## In plain words

The draft expresses Fable's whole list of facts as data, in a fixed order with the plainest danger first, with the way out beside it and a face saying it is a summary. Three kinds of thing it could not do. Four facts wait on declarations the core has not written. Several of today's warnings are not about terms at all, so a terms reader cannot carry them, and the decided sentence does not cover them. And the task, as decided, accepts less than the facts need.

## 1. The task accepts less than the facts need

As decided, the task accepts "terms and the declarations of the cMIPs they name". To produce Fable's list the draft also needs, handed to it as data (cMIP 1.4): the reader's identity hash ("your place"); the parent, for a clone ("what changes"); the publication and the ownership agreement it names (the split); the publications over a stake a buyer is buying into; the obligations the client holds that arise from the agreement; and the control model's query answer. **Needed:** the task row's "accepts" to name these, or a decision that some facts leave the list.

## 2. Four facts wait on declarations (cMIP section 7)

- **Termination terms (task 15):** who may terminate alone (yes, after a point, never), notice, minimum term, payment owed. Today's text asks a client to show "the cMIP's own declared summary" but gives it no format.
- **Absence proof (task 14):** which acts break absence, for how long, on which time reference.
- **The control model's query:** who may make an issuance offer. Its key 1 gives each stake's holders' rule, not who may issue.
- **A broken vow's refund destination:** not in the core at all, and reached by five players in the first rehearsal.

Until then each reads "unknown", at its place, never as safe.

## 3. Warnings that are not a reading of terms

These are in today's "show before" family but are not before a signature that binds a person to terms, so the decided sentence does not reach them and a terms reader cannot carry them:

- **Before a way-out act:** the last voice and the last constitutional voice (rule 70). The draft offers them as a notice a client may show, never waits on (cMIP 6.2). **A question for Nobody, allegedly: stay in the core, move here as a notice, or go?**
- **Before paying** (a purchase accepts a sale, not terms): the deadline (rule 11), the latest publication and tag (rule 12), a pointer that bypasses the split service, who may read a buyer's claim (F436.4), the evidence a buyer's client fetches. None is a terms reading. They need their own home if the family leaves the core.
- **About acts elsewhere, not terms:** another successor of the version a newcomer joins (rule 47), and one's own devices having signed another successor. They protect against forks and read the network, not the terms.
- **Plain text and bidirectional controls** (rule 4, Text rule 6) stay in the core, as Fable says: they are about bytes, not facts.

## 4. Smaller things found

- **A slip in Fable's section 2:** it gives the holders' rule as terms field 24. Field 24 is the release rule; the holders' rule an issuance counts under is the control model's query key 1 (rule 99). The draft reads both, each from its own place (fact 3.2).
- **What you owe is mostly not in the terms.** Obligations are Money acts naming the agreement; the reader shows the ones the client holds, marked as found, and cannot say there are no others. A debt written only in the terms' words is prose, and the reader does not read prose (the face says so).
- **Publications over a stake,** shown to a buyer, are likewise "as found": nothing proves the list complete.
- **Grants** are in today's rule 4 ("terms, clone or grant") but not in the decided sentence's "binds a person to terms". The draft leaves them out and lists them as open.
- **Development rule 13's worked example** ("on a sale of 10…") is in the draft (fact 3.11) as arithmetic on a stated amount, not a forecast. Whether rule 13 leaves Development with the family is not decided.
- **The order is the draft's own choice.** "Plainest danger first" is fixed by kind of fact, never by judging one agreement against another (cMIP 4.4). That keeps it from being a score, but the order itself is a mechanic and the players should attack it.

## 5. What it did express

All of Fable's list (parties and your place; your share and how it can be paid less; who changes the rules; who may declare you absent, with "decides alone, on no proof" where true; who judges; what you owe; who ends it; the split and where an unfilled share goes; which cMIPs decide), plus three the old warnings carried: what one member may do alone, Money first (rule 60); what a succession plan gives (rule 83); and what a clone changes. The way out shown with every reading, never empty. The face, quoting the Creative Commons deed: "It is not a license and has no legal value." Nothing in a reading is signed, sealed or published (F441: no hash in the signature).
