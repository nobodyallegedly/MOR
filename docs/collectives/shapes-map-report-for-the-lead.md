# The shapes map: the questions in one report, with the leans

*14 October 2026, for Nobody, allegedly, to take to the lead window. It summarises `docs/collectives/shapes-map-draft-1.md` (pull request 57), which holds the full sketches, the quotations and the reasoning. It is a report only: it changes no MIR, MICS, freeze suite or code. Each question names its area; they are in the order to decide them, since each later one leans on the earlier.*

## Where things stand

- **The task (F306):** map a band, a cooperative, a company and an investor-backed venture against Agreements draft 13 as cMIP sketches, and find the common language the core must speak so each can be built above it.
- **The author's condition, with its reading confirmed:** "some form of collective is possible at the core without cMIPs", "only for the collective shape". The transport and payment cMIPs and the rail and key Modules every identity uses are not counted.
- **The form the core keeps on its own: the members' collective** (Fable's "cooperative"; in practice a small cooperative, a band, a partnership). Everyone who runs it is a member with one voice. Its areas, grants, departures, succession, absence, forks and closing need no cMIP. With no split service, it divides money by the stakes in each work, the payer paying each holder (payer-side splitting). *One reading to confirm in the text: nothing in the core divides a stake in the collective itself without a split service, which needs a split cMIP.*
- **What the maps found:**
  - A band (with one agreement per record or tour) and a small cooperative (with member capital paid back as a debt) need nothing new.
  - A company, and a venture with passive investors, are blocked. An owner with no say is protected only by a personal veto: a signature on every change that pays them less (F270, F282), and on every new share (rule 8). So many passive owners block fees, money kept for the business, new shares and any owners' vote. Selling the works pays them less unseen (Fable's A11), and so does a fork's new plan (A10).
- **Fable's notes, tested:**
  - Three of the four places where the core says "one way" are confirmed. The fourth is partly mistaken: removing a director is a constitutional change, not a judicial one, unless that director is also named as a judge.
  - Hook 1 (votes weighted by share) and the project lead's hook 4 (owners without a say agreeing together) become one addition: **counting by share of a stake**.
  - Hooks 2 and 3 add nothing the rest does not give.
  - Making buyers members (hook 4's other form) fails: every trade would need a rotation of the collective's keys.
- **The common language, if every lean below is taken:**
  - counting by share of a stake;
  - a holders' rule per stake (the release rule, widened);
  - issuance, with pre-emption by default;
  - the owners' matters.

  Each is optional, with today's rule as its default, so the members' collective stays exactly as it is. The one exception is that its sales of works and its forks would wait for its departed holders' signatures, as its fee changes already do.

## The questions

### Q1. Can owners without a say agree together? (core business: Agreements, stakes; reopens F279's sentence and F282)

Some owners have no say: a member who left, a successor, a buyer. Today each signs, personally, every change that pays them less. Should an agreement be able to say, for a stake: "these changes pass when owners holding at least this share agree"? The rule would bind only owners who accepted it by signing or buying, and reach only changes that hit every owner of the stake alike.

- **(a) Yes: a holders' rule per stake.** Two forms: "every holder", the default and today's rule; or "owners holding at least this share". Only every holder can change it. It is the release rule's shape (field 24), widened.
- **(b) No.** The personal signature stays the only way, and companies with passive owners wait for MOR 2.
- **(c) Only for buyers.** Members who left and successors keep their veto.
- **(d) Buyers made members.** Each trade would need a rotation of the keys, and members who left would keep their vetoes.

**Lean: (a).** It is the smallest addition that makes a company possible, and it reuses a shape the core already has. The cost: an owner who accepted it can be paid less without signing, when enough of the stake agrees and the change hits everyone alike. Their protection becomes equal treatment, a proportional say, the right to keep their proportion, and the exit. F279's sentence would then read: "no advance permission reaches what one holder is paid unlike the others". Still to settle when it is written: who holds what at the moment of a vote, so that a seller and a buyer never both count.

### Q2. Can a vote count shares instead of heads? (core business: Agreements, rule 65 and the `rule` form; Fable's hook 1)

Today every vote among members is one per member. Should a rule be able to count how much of a stake the people agreeing hold? And should it be able to require two rules at once (a majority of all members *and* every member of a named class)?

- **(a) Yes.** Two new forms of rule: "holding together at least this share of this stake", and "all of these rules". Weights are read only from the stakes, never written as free numbers. One voice per member stays the default. The seat "voting weight" already in succession plans, which no rule reads today, gets this meaning, or is dropped.
- **(b) No.** One voice per member; a vote by share lives only among holders (Q1).

**Lean: (a).** It is the same way of counting as Q1, used among members. Rule 65's counting for when voices are gone (flaw C) must be restated in shares, and rule 61's coverage must reach a member whose share alone can block a rule.

### Q3. Issuance: can a stake take new owners, everyone diluted alike? (core business: Agreements, stakes; reopens rules 8 and 45, with F274)

Today a new owner can join a stake only if every existing owner signs. Should the core name issuance as its own change? New owners come in, and every existing share shrinks in the same proportion. The holders' rule decides it, and each existing owner is first offered their own proportion on the same terms (pre-emption).

- **(a) Yes, pre-emptive by default.** Only every holder can waive the pre-emption.
- **(b) Yes, with pre-emption left to cMIPs.** A cMIP's pre-emption would protect no one the core counts.
- **(c) No.** Every holder signs, as today.

**Lean: (a).** It answers "So what we are missing is the issuance of new shares…", and pre-emption stops a majority issuing to itself. Pre-emption's offer period needs a time reference, a cMIP, but only a collective that chose to issue by vote ever needs it.

### Q4. The owners' matters: what a sale or a fork owes passive owners (core business: Agreements, rules 48, 55, 91; answers Agreements Q7 and Q8, A10 and A11)

There are two side doors. A collective can sell or release the works its income comes from (A11: "25% of nothing"). A fork can found successors whose plans take more off the top (A10). Should both be among the things a stake's owners must agree to, by their holders' rule or, by default, each of them signing?

- **(a) Both.**
- **(b) Only A10;** A11 becomes a stated cost.
- **(c) Neither;** both become stated costs.

**Lean: (a).** "Shared ownership means shared fruits", and company law treats both as owners' decisions; this is also Fable's recommendation. Without Q1, it still holds, at Fable's stated cost: a sale needs every departed holder's signature, as a fee change already does.

### Q5. Can owners choose who runs the collective? (core business: Agreements, the constitutional tier; reopens rules 61 and 65)

In a company, the owners elect the board. In MOR, only members choose members.

- **(a) Yes, as an option.** The power over membership may be counted among a stake's holders, by share.
- **(b) No.** Owners agree on what they own, and leave if they disagree.

**Lean: (b) for MOR 1, held with the least confidence of all the leans.** Changing members needs a rotation of the keys the operators hold, so an owners' vote the keys can ignore is a promise the core cannot keep. A company on MOR is then shaped like a limited partnership; a cMIP can still give owners a key holder answerable to them. (a) is what "a company" means in most law.

### Q6. Money a collective keeps for itself (core business: Agreements, the split plan, rule 22; a reading)

When a collective's income goes through a stake to its owners, how does it keep reserves or reinvest?

- **(a) The plan may name the collective itself off the top,** and that part is kept as its own money, never split again.
- **(b) Another identity off the top,** such as an operating account run under a grant.
- **(c) No stake in itself;** owners are paid by decision, unprotected by the core.

**Lean: (a).** It is one sentence in rule 22. It needs checking first against how a split service treats a payout to the collective's own pointer, which the text does not say.

### Q7. A declared tier (core business: Agreements, rules 62 to 64; Fable's hook 2)

Should the founders be able to raise a field above the tier the core's table gives it?

- **(a) Yes, upward only,** declared in the founding terms, with the mark following it.
- **(b) No.**

**Lean: (b)** if Q1 to Q4 are answered (a). What a company needs raised (issuance, sales, fees) is reached by the owners' matters. Moving a field downward out of the judicial tier would break rule 67.

### Q8. A fork share rule written in advance (core business: Agreements, rule 91; Fable's hook 3)

- **(a) Yes,** with the default unchanged.
- **(b) No.**

**Lean: (b).** Owners who are not members are already kept whole in every successor. The fork act's field 5 already lets the members divide otherwise. Q4 stops a successor's plan paying them less. Share classes are not proposed.

## Side finding, for its own decision

The release rule's threshold (field 24, `[1, k]`) counts **heads** among a stake's holders. Transfers are free, so a holder can split its share among identities it controls and supply the k signatures alone. "Every holder" and "named holders" are not open to this.

- **Options:** count that threshold by share instead (which Q2's form would give), or state the cost.
- **Lean:** count by share.
- **For the findings log.**

## Not proposed, as stated costs

These stay out of the core. Each has a way to be built above it, or a cost to state:

- share classes: a preference becomes an off-the-top recoupment;
- vesting clawback: replaced by issuing shares in stages;
- forced buyouts and drag-along: replaced by a sale of the works, with the owners' consent;
- anti-dilution;
- limits on who may buy;
- a cooperative's asset lock.

## If the leans are taken

The next Agreements draft would carry:

- the two new forms of rule, with rule 65's counting restated;
- the holders' rule per stake;
- issuance, with its offer act;
- rule 48's F279 paragraph rewritten;
- the A11 check on transfers and releases, and the A10 check on forks;
- freeze scenarios:
  - a hundred passive owners raising a fee by holders' rule;
  - a token-price issuance to insiders defeated by pre-emption;
  - the members' collective, with no cMIP, run through founding, a departure, a fork and a closing exactly as today;
  - A10 and A11.

After that, experimental Company and Venture cMIPs would be sketched over the new language. A band and a cooperative need none.
