## In plain words

**Part 1. What Money says and what Agreements decide.** Money's text used to say, in a few places, what someone owes when a payment goes wrong: that a refund past its terms is over, that a payment naming no claim must be paid back, that a seller who sold the same share twice owes the money back, and so on. Those are promises between people, so they belong to Agreements. Agreements already said almost all of them; Money now keeps only the payment itself (its fields, its states, how a refund is paid) and points to Agreements for what must follow. Nothing works differently. One small wording fix: Money rule 7 said "the last three" after a sentence that made it unclear which three; it now names them.

**Part 2a. Money passes layer by layer.** When a fan pays a publisher for a song, the publisher's split service divides the money: the publisher's part, and the song's part. The song's part now goes to the song's own split service, which signs a receipt in its own name and divides it again under the song's agreement (Ana's share, Ben's share). It works the same with three layers: a subscription service's pool, then each publication, then each work. Paying Ana directly for the song's whole part no longer counts as paying the song's agreement: it has to go through the song's service, which answers for dividing it. Where the money arrives in an owner's own bank account instead of the service's, the owner's existing grant lets the service sign for it on the owner's behalf, but only with the bank's (the rail's) proof that the money really arrived. Without that proof, the old protection stands: a service can never sign a payout receipt for someone else. And it can never sign one for a payout of its own split, proof or not.

**Part 2b. Two judges, one fork.** A deal can name a chain of judges: the first, and others who take over if it does not answer in time. Because each request runs on its own clock, the first judge could let Ben's request lapse but answer Ana's, while the second judge answered Ben's: two judges, two different answers. Before, the program refused to say which version of the deal was in force. Now the earlier judge in the chain wins, whichever signed first.

**Part 3. Stepping down names the drafts left behind.** A member who resigns already names the drafts she signed and leaves behind, so none of them can pull her back in later. Stepping down from one area (the Releases area, in the collective client) now does the same: the client finds the drafts she signed, shows them in the review, and the stepping down names them, so none of them can give her the area back later.

### Part 2, F235 with its amendment, and F236

**Library** (`core/src/agreements/view.rs`):

- **F236.** `deal_walk`: the refusal for two links' settlements neither holding the other (question QK1) is gone. Of the first settlements that are all judges', the earliest link's in the chain stays and the later links' are set aside; the parties against a judge (F206 b) is read first, and F206 (a) within one link as before. The internal `Settlement` now carries its link's place in the chain instead of a flag.
- **F235, the split check.** `split`: on a stake held by another agreement, a payout naming that agreement's split service (as its version in force names it: field 14, or its chain of judgment's link for the split service) is read as paid to that holding. `work_payees` (QJ2 a: a payee of the work's agreement) is replaced by `layer_services`; a payout to a payee of the next agreement is now a mismatch.
- **F235, the service's account.** `service_account`: a receipt the service signed in its own name for a layer's share (it names another identity's split that pays the service on a stake held by an agreement naming it) discharges that payout, as any receiver's own receipt does, and is the service's to split under the holding agreement: until a split of its names it, it is in `unsplit`, with that agreement. New helper `layer_share`.
- **F235's amendment, the grant.** `split_key_problem`: a split service's grant key may sign a receipt naming a split's payout only where that receipt is a layer's incoming share (another identity's split, paying this grant's service, on a stake held by an agreement of the grantor's that lists the grant) and the rail's answer for that very receipt is valid; otherwise the H5 and H7 refusal stands. Never a payout of the service's own split. In `service_account`, such a receipt discharges the earlier layer's payout to the service, and is the service's to split.
- **Three layers.** `deal_signed`: a party that is itself a deal signs where every party of its version in force signs, a party that is itself a deal signing the same way, at most eight layers (the F216 build had one level).

**Texts.** Agreements draft 10: rule 16 (QJ2 a replaced by F235: layer by layer, the next layer's split service as receiver, its own-name receipt or, where the rail pays an owner's account, the owner's grant with the rail's proof; H5 and H7 stand otherwise; the three-layer mechanic); rule 18 and "What a split service's grant key may sign" (the amendment's one exception, and why); rule 23 (a stake held by another agreement is paid to its split service); the split plan's checks; rule 34a and rule 45b (F236); QK1 and QK3 marked answered; a header note. ## Questions for Nobody, allegedly

One at a time. Each is a place where writing the rule found the texts silent; nothing here was worked around.

1. **QL1. A layer whose agreement names no split service.** F235 says each layer's split names the next layer's split service as the receiver. Where the next layer's agreement chose payer-side splitting (it names no split service, rule 18), there is no such service. *As built:* no payout matches that agreement's holding, so the split shows a mismatch (it fails closed). Options: (a) such an agreement cannot be a holder in a split-service layer (its owners must name a service to be carried); (b) the earlier layer's service pays the next agreement's stakes directly, as payer-side splitting would; (c) as built, with the cost stated.
2. **QL2. F236 where one link's settlement holds the other's.** F236 answers QK1, which was about two links' settlements *neither holding the other*. Where the earlier link settles *after seeing* the later link's settlement (its history holds it), F192 says a settlement holding another is after it, and the first is final: the later link's. Read literally, F236's "once it has settled the fork, on any party's request, the later judge's settlement counts for nothing" could give the earlier link's. *As built:* F192 decides that case (the later link's, made first, stands); only the neither-holding case changed. Confirm, or say which wins.
3. **QL3. The chain's order for other judges.** F236 decides between links of the judge of forks' chain. Rule 34a's chain serves every judge (arbitrators, keepers' operators, verifiers, the abandonment authority). Whether two links answering the same other question on different requests are ordered the same way is not decided; rule 34a now says so. *As built:* nothing else changed (no such case is built).

## Readings taken under the delegation of mechanics, to confirm

Each respects the decided rule; none changes what a rule does.

- **R1. The receiver in the bank-rail case.** Where the rail pays an owner's account, the earlier layer's split still names the next layer's split service as the receiver (F235's layer rule); the owner's receipt, signed by the service's grant key with the rail's proof, counts as that receiver's receipt for the payout.
- **R2. "The owner may grant".** The grant that carries it is the grant the owner already gave the service as a payee of the next layer's agreement (that agreement's field 14); no new grant kind or field. It reaches only a payout of another identity's split naming that grant's service as the receiver, on a stake held by an agreement of the grantor's that lists the grant.
- **R3. "The rail's proof required on that receipt".** Read as the verifier holding the rail's valid answer for that receipt (the same fact the library already takes from the caller for payers' claims, `rail_valid`); a pending or refused answer is no proof.
- **R4. A layer's share received in the service's own name is the service's open obligation until split** (rules 20, 29), shown with the agreement holding that share; the same for a share received on an owner's behalf.
- **R5. Three layers.** A deal that is a party of another deal signs it where every party of its version in force signs it, a party that is itself a deal signing the same way, at most eight layers deep (the depth payer-side splitting already reads). Replaces the F216 build's "one level, no deal inside a deal".
- **R6. F236 and the parties.** Where a settlement every party signed and two links' settlements are all first, the parties' holds (F206 b), then between the judges the earlier link.
- **R7. Which drafts a stepping down names.** The same as a resignation: every version of the collective's agreement the member signed that is not the one the stepping down names and not one seen in force, as the relays hold them. A version that would not touch the area is named too: naming it costs nothing and keeps one search for both flows.
