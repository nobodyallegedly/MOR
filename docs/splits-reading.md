## The one thing to know first

**A person has one payee pointer.** Money says so ("Only identities have payee pointers", Payee pointer, type 0; the pointer that counts is "the latest of an unbroken, unforked chain", rule 1). Agreements says that where a deal names a split service, a payee's pointer counts for the deal only if every address in it is the service's ("the payee pointer of the identity its publications are made for counts, for Agreements, only if every address in it also appears in the split service's own signed pointer in force", rule 16; "in a deal, each payee whose own grant field 14 lists"). And Money says a payment counts only if it was paid to the payee's own pointer ("that pointer is the payee's own and counts for that payment", rule 4), as the payment cMIP's verifier checks ("For `[0, pointer, rail]`: the verifier holds that payee-pointer act, valid and signed by the payee … its payee is the act's payee", verifying, step 3; in code, `cmips/payment/src/lib.rs`, "paid to another identity's pointer", invalid).

So, as the texts stand, **whoever is the payee of a deal with a split service must point their whole flow at the service**: every tip to them personally would then arrive at the service, which cannot receipt it (its grant key "signs only receipts for money coming in under the grantor's own claims and offers", "What a split service's grant key may sign"). That contradicts Nobody, allegedly's own line: "A tip to a person stays a payment to their own pointer."

There are two ways out:

- **Within the texts (built this round): the deal's own payee.** Not every party need be a payee: field 14 lists "one grant per payee", and a payee is "a party whose own payee pointer receives the deal's incoming money" (glossary, "Payee"); nothing makes every party one. So Edubba makes **one more identity for the deal, held in the proposer's Edubba, whose only job is to be paid**: a party of the deal holding no stake, its pointer the service's rails, its grant the one field 14 lists. Buyers and tippers pay it; the service receipts in its name and splits to the people. Nobody's own wallet changes. *Cost, stated:* the proposer holds that identity's keys, so the proposer can stop the deal's income (by revoking its grant) or point it elsewhere (a bypass every Agreements client shows as such, rule 16's client conformance, and Damgar refuses); the proposer could already block the deal, as any party can (rule 47's stated cost).
- **A MIR change (the MOR lead's): the designated pointer paid directly.** Agreements already says the agreement "designates the one pointer at which it accepts payments, the same whoever pays: a buyer directly, or a layer above" (rule 15), and that a backup "paid directly receipts in the owners' name" at "the one its own signed pointer carries" (rule 17). Read literally, a direct buyer pays the service's own pointer in a payee's name, which Money rule 4 and the payment cMIP refuse. Round 3 already decided that path for a lone seller's one-party deal (F358, 2: "the commitment's payee still the seller and its 'paid to' naming the service's pointer") and built it; **it does not verify**: a probe this round on main's one-party test found the buyer's claim "unknown: the payee pointer it was paid to is not held", and once held it is invalid by the payment cMIP's step 3. Under "For the MOR lead", below.

The rest of this reading is written for the first way, and says where the second would change it.

## 1. A deal between two or three solo identities

**In plain words.** Three people agree, in one signed document, who owns what share of a song (or a vow, or a post), what comes off the top for a reposter, and which split service divides each payment. Each signs it in their own Edubba. It counts once every one of them has signed, the deal's own payee included.

**Precisely.**

- **The parties** (terms field 0): the people, and the deal's own payee (above). A deal is "an agreement that founds no collective: its terms carry no key grammar. A deal exists when every party has signed it" (glossary, "Deal"; rule 1; rule 47: "In a deal, the signing rule and the clone rule are every party"). Field 4 `[0]` and field 5 `[0]` (Terms: "Any deal whose field 5 is not `[0]` is invalid").
- **Each one's stake and share** (field 7): one stake, its object the work, its holders the people, in millionths summing to 1,000,000 (rule 9; "Stakes (field 7) … shares summing to 1,000,000 … Terms breaking any of these are invalid"). The deal's own payee holds nothing: a party need not hold a stake.
  - **A vow** is the one exception: "A stake whose object is a vow is held by the vow's signer … in the version that first writes it" and "Terms writing a stake in a vow held otherwise are invalid, signed by every party or not" (rule 14). So a vow's deal is **two versions signed together**: founding terms writing the vow's stake wholly to its signer, and a clone (field 11 the founding, mark `[[1], every party]`, rule 47) sharing it; the signer "may sell or share what she owns" by "a version its holder signs (rule 48)". The offer is made under the clone.
  - **A plain post**: a text act may not name a deal in `objects` (Text rule 14: "Any other `objects` on a text act … is invalid"), so the post names the deal in `refs` and the deal's stake object is the hash of the post's words (a work hash), never the post's id, which would be circular.
- **The referral off the top** (field 8): a role share `[1, "referral", part]` listed in key 4, off the top (rule 22: "Every share paying anyone but a stake … is paid off the top only"); the rest to the stake (plan key 0's stake share), as the one-party deal writes it today (`repo/src/agreements.ts`, `planValue`).
- **Field 14's split service**: in a deal, "one grant per payee, each naming 'this agreement' by null and signed by the payee" (rule 16; "This agreement"; Grant, "A payee's grant in a deal"). With the deal's own payee, one grant. It "counts only through the deal: once a deal its grantor signed lists it in field 14 and exists, every party having signed it" (Grant).
- **Field 29's backups**: one group per backup service, "one per payee, as many as field 14 lists" (Terms, "The split service's backups"). Not offered this round (the test split service has no second instance, and a direct buyer cannot reach a backup as the texts stand: the split cMIP's open question QS1).
- **How each party signs it in their own app**: Edubba publishes the terms (and the offer, and for a vow the clone), then sends each party a proposal, an Agreements negotiation message (rule 52) sealed to them, as a collective's founding is sent today (`repo/src/proposals.ts`); each party's Edubba reads it in **To sign** from the exact bytes (rule 4: "What you sign is what you saw"), with the deal-assessment tool's reading and risks, and signs a signature act (type 1) naming each act: the terms, the clone, the offer ("under a deal, it is signed by every party", Standing offer, "It counts").
- **When it counts**: once every party's signature act is found (rule 1, rule 47). Until then "An obligation in the terms of a party who has not signed is shown as unsigned, never as open" (rule 1), and the offer counts for nothing ("under a deal, it is signed by every party"). Edubba publishes the work itself only after.

**Verdict: fits as written** (with the deal's own payee; see "the one thing"). Nothing in a cMIP is needed for the terms.

## 2. The split service for a deal

**In plain words.** The service makes one key for the deal's payee; the payee's grant names it; the service signs to accept it. The test service already divides a payment among several holders of one stake; what it lacks is a tip, which names the deal rather than an offer.

**Precisely.**

- **One grant per payee** (the MOR lead's reading, and rule 16's words): here one, the deal's own payee's. "Every grant field 14 lists names the same grantee, the one split service, and is signed by a distinct party. A deal breaking this carries none of them" (Grant).
- **The key the service makes**: "The grant names the public part of a signing key the grantee made and keeps" (Grant, field 9). The test service makes one per grantor (`SplitService.grantKey`), printed by `mor-split-service key GRANTOR`.
- **Its acceptance**: "The split service signs to accept each grant, as every grantee does" (Grant; "The grant counts only once its grantee signed a signature act naming it"). The test service finds the grant at the grantor's places and signs (`acceptGrants`).
- **What it does today**: for a collective, and for a lone seller's one-party deal: answers the invoice request (money messages 4.4), receipts with the grant key, waits for the payer's claim (the referral), publishes the split (type 8) delivered to every holder it pays (rule 20), pays each payout as a Money payment (rule 23), tells every party of a sale (7.2.3). It already reads the parties from the terms (`parties`) and divides one stake among all its holders by rule 9 (`split`).
- **What it lacks for several parties**: (a) a payment fulfilling the deal itself, a tip, whose agreement it reads from the offer's field 0 or a collective's agreement only (`agreementOf`); (b) nothing else: several holders, several parties, one payee are handled. The service as a party is refused in Edubba: "the service is no party to the deal" (Grant, "The mechanic is the collective's founding grant, and the service is no party to the deal").

**Verdict: fits as written**; the test service needs the tip's case (part 2).

## 3. A work, a vow or a plain post under the deal, and how an app finds that a work has an agreement in force

**In plain words.** The offer says which deal it is made under. A post can only mention the deal. An app finds the deal by following what the work names, then looks for later versions at the parties' relays.

**Precisely.**

- **A protected file**: the publication (Envelopes type 0) names nothing of the deal; its offer names it (field 0, "under: the co-owners' agreement (a version) it is made under"), pays a payee field 14 lists (field 3: "a payee the agreement's field 14 lists"), cites it (`objects` `[agreement, agreement]`, Standing offer), and is signed by every party. The publication is made **for** the deal's payee (Envelopes: "Payment for a publication goes to the payee pointer of the identity named in `for`"), so a Money-only wallet pays the same identity. A post names the publication and the offer in `refs`, as today.
- **A vow**: the vow (Envelopes type 5) signed by its owner; its offer `[3, vow]` under the deal's clone (above), signed by every party.
- **A plain post**: the post names the deal in `refs` (Text rule 14 forbids `objects`), so the post does not "belong to" the deal ("An act belongs to an agreement when it names, in `objects`, that agreement or an act of its chain", glossary, "Belongs to"). It only points the tipper's app at it; the tip itself belongs to the deal, through its field 5 (below, 5). *A post mentioning someone else's deal sends tips only to that deal's owners: as a false `for` ("can only send money to the identity it names"), nobody gains by it; the tipper's app shows the parties before paying.*
- **How an app finds that a work has an agreement in force** (the MOR lead's open question). The core answers half: "Its keepers are those of the version in force for the act. For a deal, the latest complete version, on the line of the version the act names, that the act's history holds" (glossary, "Belongs to"), and a purchase names "the line at which the payer's client read it current" (rule 12). Where to look is not the core's ("Where things are found … only guides where to look", the state file's working rules): the client follows what the work names (the offer's field 0; a post's `refs`), then asks for later versions where the parties publish: each version is signed by every party and names its parent as chain (`objects` `[[parent, act]]`, Terms, "A clone"), so any party's relays hold it. *Client conformance already reads this way:* "before paying, a buyer's client finds the offer and verifies that it is truly the latest in its chain … and warns where what it shows is outdated" (rule 12). This round's apps read the version the offer names (no later version is made here), and say so.

**Verdict: needs a cMIP draft** for the finding (a plain post naming a deal in `refs`; the client's walk from a work to its deal and its payee). Written as the money messages cMIP's draft 5, section 8. The acts themselves fit as written.

## 4. Buying a protected file or a vow under the deal

**In plain words.** The buyer's app sees the offer is under a shared deal, says so, and asks the split service for the invoice. The service receipts in the deal's payee's name, publishes the split, pays each person their share and tells each of them of the sale. The proposer's Edubba, holding the key, delivers it.

**Precisely.**

- **Offer field 0**: names the version; "A purchase following it names that agreement" (rule 11; Standing offer: "A purchase following it names its agreement (a version of its lineage), or it is no purchase").
- **The payee in the commitment**: the offer's field 3, the deal's payee (payment cMIP, the commitment's `payee`, "the identity paid"), `paid-to` its own pointer, whose rails are the service's (rule 16's pointer check passes; Money rule 4 holds; the payment cMIP's step 3 holds). The purchase `[deal version, line]` as the commitment's last element (payment cMIP, item 4; Money rule 18; Agreements rule 12).
- **The invoice request**: money messages 4.4 was written for a collective ("Where the payee's agreement in force names a split service (Agreements terms field 14: in a collective, its grant…)"); for a deal, the payee's agreement is the version the offer names, and the service is the grantee of the payee's own grant in its field 14. Damgar already reads it so (`splitServiceFor`, `splitServiceOf`: "in a deal, the grantee of the grant `payee` gave"). The text says "a collective"; draft 5 says a deal too.
- **The receipt**: signed by the service with the payee's grant key: "a Money receipt (type 2) whose payee (field 3) is the grantor, and which is a purchase naming the grantor's own claim (field 9: in a deal, the deal or a version of it…)". Sealed to the payer and every party (the test service already does).
- **The split published**: rule 20, delivered to every holder it pays; conservation (rule 21); rule 9's leftovers; the referral role off the top only with the payer's claim as evidence (rule 22).
- **Each party paid**: rule 23, each payout a Money payment to the holder's own pointer, with the holder's own receipt; each participant's check (rule 18).
- **Each party told of the sale**: the money messages cMIP's sale (type 6, 7.2.3), written for "a collective's sale" ("the service tells each party of the collective's agreement in force"); for a deal, every party of the version the purchase names. Draft 5 says so.
- **Who delivers the key**: the core says "The seller's side delivers the content key" (rule 11) and the deal is complete when "the offer's side either delivers the content key" (rule 11): any party is the offer's side. A buyer's Dub takes a key delivery only from the publication's signer (`dub/src/money.ts`, `keysArrived`: `d.signer === b.seller`), so **the proposer's Edubba delivers**, as the signer; the key is also sealed to every party at publication, so every party can open the work it co-owns (a choice, not rule 57, which is a collective's). *Cost:* while the proposer's Edubba is closed, the buyer waits; any party holding the key could deliver it as their own act, which a buyer's app would have to accept from any party of the deal: a mechanic for a later draft.
- **A vow under it**: bought the same way; the buyer's claim, published, is the sale (rule 13), confirmed or contested by the buyer (Dub's Purchases, Edubba's Vows).

**Verdict: fits as written**, with **a cMIP draft** for 4.4 and 7.2.3 (a deal's payee and parties where draft 4 says a collective).

## 5. A tip to a work under the deal

**In plain words.** A tip on a shared work goes to the deal, not to whoever posted it: to the deal's payee, through the split service, divided like a sale. It stays a tip: it buys nothing and nobody owes it back.

**Precisely.**

- **How the tip names the agreement**: its receipt's field 5 (and its claim's field 4), "fulfils: the act this hop follows (an obligation, agreement, offer or payee pointer, for instance)" (Money, Settlement receipt), names the deal's version. Money judges it: "an agreement or offer to follow" is among the acts "to which a rule of Money or Agreements gives a payment following it a meaning" (Money, "What a payment fulfils"). Through it the tip belongs to the deal ("a Money receipt or payment claim, through … what it fulfils … the agreement itself", glossary, "Belongs to"), so its keepers see it.
- **To which pointer it pays**: the deal's payee's (as a sale, 4). The service may receipt it: "a purchase naming the grantor's own claim … or a payment following it (field 5)" ("What a split service's grant key may sign"; and: "'payments on its offer' are receipts following the deal itself, or purchases of its work").
- **Whether it is then a purchase or stays a tip** (the MOR lead's open question). **It stays a tip.** A payment is a purchase "only if its receipt or claim names the claim it pays under (Money receipt and claim field 9)" (rule 12); a tip names none ("A payment to an identity's own pointer (a tip) or toward an obligation is no purchase and names nothing", Money rule 18). Rule 12's refund ("A payment naming no claim … is no purchase: money received for nothing, owed back") reaches "a payment for a standing offer" and "a payment for a publication carrying a work some agreement claims"; a payment whose field 5 is the deal itself is neither, so nothing is owed back. The service splits it all the same: "For every incoming receipt … the service publishes a split" (rule 20), naming that receipt (split field 0: "the receipt or payer's claim that brought the money to the owners' payee pointer"). *One reading to confirm with the MOR lead: that rule 12's refund does not reach a tip whose field 5 is the deal while the post it was given on carries a claimed work (a protected file's post); the texts above say it does not, in the way they list the two cases. No change asked; a sentence of commentary in rule 12 would settle it.*
- **A tip to a person** is unchanged: field 5 their pointer, paid to their own pointer (Money rule 17: "for a tip, the version of the payee pointer it follows"). With the deal's own payee, the person's pointer never moved.

**Verdict: fits as written**, with **a cMIP draft** for how the tipper's app finds the deal (3) and the service's answer to a tip (money messages draft 5, 4.4 and 8).

## 6. Contest (F386): who may contest, and what each party sees

**In plain words.** Only the buyer may contest their purchase. Every party of the deal (every member of a collective) is now to see the buyer's claim, so each party's Edubba can show the contest beside the sale. The cost: every party sees who bought.

**Precisely.**

- **Who**: the buyer, "the payer the payment's commitment names, an identity (rule 22)" (Contest, "A contest of a purchase"); "the buyer of a purchase, for a contest of that purchase" (rule 54). Parties, stake holders and keepers have standing to contest other acts (rule 54), never as the buyer.
- **What each party sees** (decided with the MOR lead, 23 October): "where the payee of a purchase is a collective or an agreement with several parties, the buyer's client makes its claim readable by every member of the collective's agreement in force, or every party of the version the purchase names (sealed to each, or its key delivered to each)"; a core line of client conformance the MOR lead writes; "stated cost: every member or party sees who bought". The parties are those of the version the purchase names (Agreements: "Its keepers are those of the version in force for the act"; rule 12's purchase names the version); the cMIP addresses them, it does not choose them.
- **How the money messages cMIP does it**: **one sealed container, the claim sealed to every reader at once** (the sealed container carries one content key wrapped to each recipient, Envelopes; `sealFor` takes a list): the service first (it needs the claim for the referral, rule 22), and every party of the version the purchase names (a collective: every party of its agreement in force), left at each one's inbox route. *Why not "sealed to the service, its key delivered to each"*: that needs a second act per reader and the service's cooperation; sealing to each needs neither, and the claim's bytes are the same. Written in draft 5, 9, "following the MOR lead's core line, in its words, once it is on main".

**Verdict: needs a cMIP draft** (money messages draft 5, 9), following the MOR lead's core line. Nothing for a verifier.

## 7. The costs to state

- **What the service learns**: the payer, the amount and what was bought of every payment to the deal ("A split service answers for its collective … it learns the payer and the amount of every payment", money messages draft 4, costs), every party's identity and share (the terms are public anyway), each payout's address. It never sees the work. It holds a key that signs receipts in the deal's payee's name for money coming in under the deal only (rule 16).
- **What a party who leaves or stops answering can block**: a deal "changes only with everyone's signature" (rule 47): one silent party blocks every change, the stated cost there; nobody can lower their share without them (rule 48). They cannot block payments: the service holds the payee's grant, not theirs. **The deal's own payee's holder** (the proposer) can stop payments by revoking its grant ("Each payee can revoke its own grant", Grant) or try to bypass the service by changing its pointer (shown as a bypass by every Agreements client, rule 16); stated. A party who leaves the deal does so by a clone every party signs (rule 47: "A deal ends, or winds down, by such a clone"); there is no resignation in a deal.
- **What a reader whose app does not read Agreements sees**: a post by the proposer, a publication made for an identity they do not know (the deal's payee), and an offer they cannot judge ("Agreements are optional per client, but all or nothing: a client implements it fully, or shows its acts as unknown", Dependencies). A Money-only wallet can pay the publication's `for`, the deal's payee, "but cannot tell whether it bought it: its payment is no purchase, and the money is owed back to it" (rule 12's stated cost); a tip from such a wallet to the payee's pointer arrives at the service, which cannot receipt it under the deal (it follows the payee's pointer, not the deal): it waits, unreceipted, a stated cost. It cannot check the pointer ("a wallet that reads only Money cannot check it, and pays the pointer as it stands", rule 16's stated cost).
- **Every party sees who bought** (6), the decided cost.
- **The proposer's Edubba holds the deal's payee's keys** (the one thing, above).

## For the MOR lead

**One point: which pointer a direct payment under a deal with a split service is paid to, and in whose name.**

*In plain words.* The texts give a deal's money two homes that do not meet. Agreements says the deal designates one pointer, the service's, and that anyone pays it, a buyer directly included (rules 15, 17). Money says a payment counts only at the payee's own pointer (rule 4), and a person has one. So either every payee of a deal points their whole wallet at the service (and their personal tips arrive somewhere nobody can receipt them), or the deal's money goes to a pointer that is not the payee's own, which Money refuses. Round 3 took the second for a lone seller (F358, 2, decided and built), and its receipts do not verify.

*Precisely, the texts it touches:*
- Money rule 4: "that pointer is the payee's own and counts for that payment by rules 1 and 17"; the payment cMIP's step 3 (the suite's), "its payee is the act's payee … or the answer is invalid".
- Agreements rule 15: "The work's agreement designates the one pointer at which it accepts payments, the same whoever pays: a buyer directly, or a layer above"; rule 17: "A backup paid directly receipts in the owners' name, with the grants it already holds … a backup's address is the one its own signed pointer carries, which the pointer check accepts (rule 16)"; the split cMIP's QS1, open since draft 1: "how a buyer paying the work directly reaches a backup, since the owners' payee pointer leads to one service".
- F358 (2), the suite's decision built in round 3 for a lone seller's one-party deal: the service's own pointer paid, the seller the commitment's payee. **Found this round: its buyer's claim reads "unknown: the payee pointer it was paid to is not held" in the buyer's Dub, and invalid wherever the service's pointer is held** (`cmips/payment/src/lib.rs`, `address`, "paid to another identity's pointer").

*My lean:* **(a)** Money rule 4 extended by one clause: "or, where the payment follows an agreement or offer whose terms list, in field 14 or 29, a grant of the payee's, the pointer of that grant's grantee, the agreement's designated pointer (Agreements rules 15 and 17)", judged by Agreements as rule 7 judges a collective's pointer (a Money-only client shows it unknown); then the payment cMIP's step 3 follows (the suite's draft 5), and F358 (2)'s path and rule 17's backups both verify, and no deal needs its own payee. **(b)** Otherwise, the texts as they stand: a deal's payee points its whole flow at the service; the suite keeps building the deal's own payee (this round) and F358 (2)'s path is rebuilt the same way (the lone seller's own pointer stays theirs only if the deal has its own payee). Until you decide, this round builds (b) for deals between people, leaves F358 (2)'s one-party path as it is on main (not this round's to change), and builds nothing on (a).

**A second point, found while building (the collective's case of question 6): a collective's sale is no purchase for the core.** The core reads a payment for a collective's work, under its offer and agreement, as no purchase: "the agreement it names carries no stake in the work it pays for" (`core/src/agreements/view.rs`, `purchase_unjudged`), since a collective's money terms stake the collective itself (object null) and never each work. The glossary says such a stake "is a share of all that collective's income, whoever earns it and whenever". So every collective sale is, for the core, owed back, and its buyer's contest shows beside nothing. *My lean:* the core counts the collective's own stake as a stake in every work it sells under its agreement (a vector for it). Built meanwhile: the claim reaches every member, and Edubba shows the core's words beside the sale.

*No other point.* The tip (5) fits as written, with one reading to confirm in commentary if you wish; the contest line (6) is yours, already decided, and the cMIP follows it.
