**The heaviest three overall.**

1. **A Module signs nothing (F112, F116; Production rule 17; Finance rule 10b; Law rules 19, 22, 28).** Four documents still have a Module signing evidence: the software case study (19, "the module's own signed record"), the musician case study (08, a "session-file module" that signs), the cat video case study (09, "the module reporting it under its own signature", four times) and the advertising case study (15, "claims signed by the metric module that measured them"). The evidence of a rail Module's use is the receipt or claim naming it, which may be the split service's own receipt (F119); the evidence of a service's use is a record signed by the identity running the service.
2. **Buying a claimed work needs a client that reads Law (F126; Finance rule 10c; Law rule 32a; Envelope, "For whom").** The musician case study (08) has a fan buying a claimed track "from the simplest wallet" and "the deal is done"; the adult content case study (18) and the gaming case study (13) describe purchases the same way. Since F126 a payment for a work under a Law claim is a purchase only if it names the claim it pays under; a Finance-only wallet's payment is no purchase and is owed back. One spec question sits under this (below, 08, issue 2).
3. **"Shared" attribution promises what the core cannot carry (F184, open, high priority).** The advertising case study (15) divides a sale share "among every referrer the buyer's client recorded", while Finance's claim carries one referral (claim key 6). F184 records this conflict and leaves it open with the rest of the incentive layer; it is flagged here and not resolved. The same single slot is behind lighter flags in the commerce case study (16, a storefront's share) and the software case study (19, nothing wrong there).

**Two further patterns worth seeing as one each.** Per-party protected clauses (F71) were replaced by one judicial tier for everyone (F121, Law rule 46a) and departed holders are judged under the clause in force (F121 reading 7, rule 46b): the musician (08) and software (19) case studies still say "the version they signed is the one that judges them". And grants now hand the grantee a grant key of the grantor (F128, F129; Law rules 38 to 44): the musician case study's label (08, "the manager's branch is sealed", "imported") and the AI case study's agent (20, "never holds Lena's keys", "the safety key can revoke the grant", "the vault keeps anything beyond the agent's limits out of its reach") describe the old mechanics.

**Questions for Nobody, allegedly, found on the way** (listed in full at the end): what a purchase names when a sole creator's work carries a work claim but no agreement; how F126 reads a buyer's payment for a lone co-owner's publication; whether case study 11's proposed correction rule is taken up or dropped; the relay's "signed delivery record" that Law rule 19 names and nothing defines (F184, point b); stakes written in works that do not exist yet; the pending rename of the safety key to the chain key (F132).

## 07 MOR in one page v6

1. **Medium.** "A service that divides money, a deal's or a group's, holds a key from each owner that can sign only for money coming in … and each owner can take back their own." Against Law rule 18, terms field 14, F129 H4 and F130 (reading 5): in a deal the service holds one grant key per payee, each payee revoking its own; in a collective it holds one grant of the collective (field 14 is one hash, "its one payee being the collective"), which the collective revokes under its own rules, never a member alone. Why: a member of a group cannot take back a key the group granted. Fix: keep "from each owner" for a deal, and say that a group grants one key and takes it back by its own rules.
2. **Light.** "Relays carry acts, and cannot read the ones that are locked." Against Envelope, "The act": every act is locked; a public act carries its key on the outside, a private one does not. Why: "the ones that are locked" suggests some acts are not. Fix: say that relays cannot read private acts.
3. **Light.** "A work can also be given to everyone, now or on a date, if everyone who owns it signs." Against Law rule 17 and terms field 24: every direct owner signs unless the release rule set at founding says otherwise. Why: the founders may set a lower rule. Fix: add "unless the owners agreed another rule when they founded".
4. **Light.** "Money arrives at an address the owner controls." Against Law rule 18 and F123: where the owners name a split service, the addresses in their pointer are the service's, vouched for by the service's own signed pointer. Why: the service, not the owner, controls that address. Fix: say the money arrives where the owner pointed it, at an address the owner or the service it chose controls.
5. **Light.** "each side first starts its own new group, the members sign the split as the group's own rules for its constitution require, and the old group closes." Not wrong; since F132 each member signs a fork or closing with the safety key, on their identity chain (Identity type 16), which the texts state as a cost: forks and closings are slow and heavy, as rotations are. Fix: optional, one phrase saying the split is signed with the offline key.
6. **Light.** "someone it hires, who signs with a key of the group limited to the job, which the group can take back at once." Against Law rule 40 and the tie rule: a revocation ends the key for everything after it and for acts racing it, but an act the group already cited stays adopted (F131 IT2a). Why: "at once" is true, but the group keeps what it already took on. Fix: none needed; a note that what the group already acted on stays, if the author wants it.

*Not an issue, but a note:* F132 decided a rename, the safety key becoming the chain key and the everyday key the signing key, "to apply after the session writing F132". The spec texts still say "safety key" throughout, so every document checked here is consistent with the spec as it stands. The question is when the rename pass runs (listed at the end).

## 04 Augmented democracy v3

1. **Heavy.** "An anonymous ballot is not a signed act, because every act names its signer; it travels as a sealed container, the wrapper the Envelope MIP uses for private senders, whose inner form the voting cMIP defines." Against Envelope, "Sealed containers" and rule 10a (F99, written after this draft): a sealed container's contents are an act, signed by its sender, checked against its outside on opening; a container whose contents fail that check is invalid, and its inner act is not delivered. Why: the container hides the sender from relays, never from the recipient, and it cannot carry something that is not an act. Fix: a ballot is an act signed by a fresh one-time identity (a nym, as the journalism case study does for a source), carrying the membership proof and nullifier as its payload; or the ballot is not a MOR act at all but an object the commune's relay accepts under the voting cMIP, outside the core. Which one is the author's choice.
2. **Medium.** "Delegation is a grant: 'my 40 points on events go to Sam', revocable until the round closes." Not wrong, but since F128 a grant hands Sam a grant key of the delegator, scoped to the grant, and what Sam signs with it is the delegator's own act (Law rule 38, "every identity grants keys the same way"). Why: a delegate's ballot would then be signed in the delegator's name, which changes what "delegates are public" (section 3) means: the grant is public, and the ballot shows the key it was signed with. Fix: say that the delegate signs the delegator's ballot with a key the delegator granted, revocable by an everyday act.
3. **Light.** "Membership is a signed list in the founding agreement, changed by cloning it." Against Law rule 37: members change by a rotation of the collective plus a constitutional clone. Fix: add "and rotating the association's keys".
4. **Light.** "The association's budget is split across projects in proportion to the result, through a split plan in the association's agreement." Against Law, "Splits" (rules 19 to 23): a split plan divides each incoming payment by stakes, roles and named receivers; it does not disburse a budget the association already holds, and shares that change every round mean a new version of the agreement each round (operational, under the clone rule or the area that holds the plan). Fix: say that the association pays each project under an obligation or offer, the amounts set by the result, each payment with its receipt; or say that a new version of the plan is signed each round.
5. **Light.** "Law: … time references and anchoring for rounds and deadlines." Against F173: anchoring is an Envelope task now. Fix: move anchoring to the Envelope line.
6. **Light.** "Finance: … points counted in their own unit, defined by a unit module." A unit is a specification of kind 2 (Production), not a Module. Fix: "a unit specification".
7. **Light.** "two of five board members sign everyday acts, and four of five rotate its safety key." Not wrong; since F105 every member with constitutional power must also be covered by an abandonment clause, or the founding agreement is invalid (Law rule 36b). Fix: none needed, or one phrase on the abandonment clause the founders name.

## 08 Musician to label v2

1. **Heavy.** "her track's split carries a small share for 'tools used', evidenced by her session file, which the session-file module signs." Also section 2, "evidenced by the session-file module's signed record", and section 5, "A session-file module: recording, with its own signature, which works … went into a release, as evidence for role shares." Against F112 and F116 (Production rule 17, Law rules 19 and 22): a Module is a specification and signs nothing; evidence comes from a party. Also against Law rule 22: a role share's evidence is signed by someone other than the split service and the payee, and Lea is the payee, so her own session file cannot evidence a role share in her own split. Why: the preset maker is known when the track is published, so this is not a role share filled at payment time but a share for a named receiver (share rule `[2, receiver, part]`), which needs no evidence at all (F184, "what the core already gives"). Fix: write the preset maker into the plan as a named receiver; drop the signing module; if a session file is still wanted as a record, it is a record Lea signs, not evidence for a role share.
2. **Heavy.** "A fan pays from the simplest wallet; Lea's side delivers the key, and the deal is done." Against F126 (Finance rule 10c, Law rule 32a, Envelope "For whom"): a payment for a work under a Law claim is a purchase only if it names the claim it pays under; a Finance-only wallet can pay a claimed work's publication, but its payment is no purchase and is owed back. Why: Lea bound the track to herself with a claim, so the simplest wallet can tip her but cannot buy it. Fix: say the fan buys with a client that reads the track's claim, and that the simplest wallet can only tip. *Question for Nobody, allegedly:* Finance's purchase field names "the work's claiming agreement"; where a sole creator has a work claim (Law type 3) and no agreement, what does the purchase name, the claim act itself, or is such a work "unclaimed" for rule 32a, so the simplest wallet still buys it? The texts do not say.
3. **Heavy.** "And a clone the missing member never signed cannot change that clause for them: the version they signed is the one that judges them." Against F121 (Law rule 46a, one judicial tier for everyone) and F121 reading 7 corrected (rule 46b): the abandonment clause changes only with every member's signature while they are a member, and a party whose voice was removed, or who left, is judged by the clause in force, not an older one. Why: the per-party version of F71 was withdrawn. Fix: say that the clause cannot change without every member's signature, and that whoever left is judged by the clause in force.
4. **Medium.** "Any tiny leftover from dividing goes to the first party listed." Against F150 and F165 (Law rule 15a): leftover units go to the holders with the largest remainders, ties take turns counted along the split service's own receipts, and the order of holders decides nothing. Fix: say leftovers go by largest remainder, ties taking turns.
5. **Medium.** "If the label later revokes that manager's grant, the manager's branch is sealed. Deals the label accepts are imported into its own chain; the rest do not bind the label, and those the other side relied on stay visible as disputes. The label cannot seal before a deal it already collected on … Counterparties who want certainty ask for import before they perform." Against F128 and F129 (Law rules 38, 40, 41, 43, 44): the manager signs with a grant key of the label, so the deals are the label's own acts; a revocation names the grant, never a place on its strand (no seal); the handover is withdrawn; an act the revocation's history holds binds, one racing it or after it is void unless the label adopts it by an act of its own key (acknowledging, citing, paying on or importing it); an unadopted act is void, not "a dispute"; counterparties wait until a later act of the label cites the deal (rule 43). Fix: describe the grant key, the revocation as a decision ending powers, adoption by the label's own act, and "wait until the label cites your deal".
6. **Medium.** "two of three are needed to publish a release; all three are needed to rotate the band's safety key." Not wrong in effect, but since F103 which members must sign which acts is an area of the agreement (holders and a number, by layer or act type), not part of the key grammar (its listed act types were retired, Law key grammar key 2). Fix: say the agreement gives publications to an area that two of three hold, and that the key grammar says how the keys are held.
7. **Medium.** "a fourth share of the safety key held in escrow by a custodian under the band's grant, released only by the abandonment authority." Against Law, key grammar: the recovery path is either a custodian holding a share under grant (`[0, custodian, grant]`) or an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority (`[1, authority]`); the sentence mixes the two. Fix: pick one.
8. **Medium.** "Escrowed key shares: a custodian Module holding a collective's recovery share under grant." Against Production, "Module" (signs nothing, holds nothing): a custodian is an identity, a party under a grant; the dealing of shares is the air-gapped signer Module's business. Fix: "a custodian service holding …", with the Module naming only how shares are dealt and checked.
9. **Light.** "A publisher withdraws its own publication; the label can end its agreement with one that does not sell, contest a publication it no longer authorises, and publish elsewhere." Against Envelope rule 15 and F68: a withdrawal is valid when signed by the publication's signer or by the identity in its `for` field, and these publications name the label in `for`. Why: the label can withdraw them itself. Fix: say the label withdraws a publication made for it.
10. **Light.** "the stakes: for example, the label takes a share of new releases' publication, or of the works themselves." Against Law terms field 7: a stake names its object by hash (a work, a publication, or the collective itself); a work that does not exist yet has no hash. Fix: a stake in the band's own income (field 7, the collective as object, F121 Q8), or a clone per release. *Question for Nobody, allegedly:* is there a way to write a stake in future works other than a stake in the collective itself, or a clone when each work exists? (Also raised by 09 and 20.)
11. **Light.** "who judges absence, over what period, and what follows." Against F172 (Law rule 51): the core checks no period; absence is the named authority's judgment unless the clause names an absence-proof cMIP. Fix: "and, if they chose one, which module proves it".
12. **Light.** "The band's payee pointer points to a split service named in their agreement through a grant." Not wrong; since F123 and F124 P2 the band's pointer counts for Law only if every address in it, and every entry of its vault, also appears in the service's own signed pointer and vault. Fix: optional, one phrase that the service vouches for those addresses.

## 09 Cat video to coproduction v2

4. **Light.** "Nico keeps his flow pointer on his everyday key for convenience, and sets a vault behind his safety key for anything large, per currency he accepts." 5. **Light.** "Subscription money is shared visibly. The streamer's subscription revenue is divided among the works on its service by a split module." ## 10 Migration to MOR 2 v2

1. **Medium.** "It will go through several MOR 0.x releases, each one tested, attacked and fixed while changing the rules is still allowed. Then MOR 1 freezes." Against F84 and F85: the first release is V1, "frozen at inception"; there are no 0.x releases, and the freeze report says scenario by scenario what was run and what was reasoned. Fix: say V1 is the first release, frozen when it is presented, with its freeze report.
2. **Medium.** "Anchoring is always a choice: each party decides what is worth anchoring, their risk, their dial." Against F148's working rule, F169, F176 and F181 (Finance rule 15 and 14b): anchoring stays optional in the core except where an act expresses a time; for money, a payee that declares no clock, or does not anchor its lock change, bears the theft window, and a payer's client that does not anchor its claim bears the loss after an anchored lock change (client conformance, MUST and SHOULD). Why: it is still a choice, but one with a stated loss, no longer only a dial. Fix: say anchoring is optional but that money has a rule: name a clock and anchor, or bear the loss.
3. **Light.** "An anchoring module, for example wrapping OpenTimestamps." Against F173: anchoring is an Envelope task, filled by a cMIP; the clock declaration names an anchoring cMIP (Finance, "The clock"). Fix: "an anchoring cMIP".
4. **Light.** "Like any specification, MOR 2 names MOR 1 as its predecessor." Fine for a specification's field 8 (Production rule 3a: a predecessor is a claim); MIPs have no creator and a successor changes the tag prefix (Production rule 21). Fix: none; "and changes the tag prefix" if wanted.

## 12 Gig economy v2

1. **Medium.** "The simplest answer is for the rated person's client to acknowledge every rating it receives, good or bad, which pins it; a receipt-bound rating cMIP can require exactly that." Against F110 (Envelope rule 4a, Identity rules 18b and 18c): only Identity, Finance and Law act types carry acknowledgements; a rating is a cMIP act, so "acknowledging" it means a witness act, which a client must never sign as a side effect of another gesture and must explain before signing. Why: a client that acknowledges every rating automatically breaks the witness act's conformance rule. Fix: say the rated person pins a rating deliberately with a witness act, or the rating cMIP names a keeper that records ratings as they arrive (Law rule 11).
2. **Medium.** "Proof of delivery. The customer's client signs an acknowledgement when the order arrives." Against F110: a delivery cMIP's act cannot carry `acks`; what the customer signs is a Law act (a signature act on the delivery, or a delivery confirmation where the cMIP defines one), a witness act, or a plain signed statement of the cMIP that acknowledges nothing in the Envelope sense. Fix: "signs a delivery act", and let the cMIP say which.
3. **Light.** "Late payment is visible. A payment due 30 days after delivery appears as an open obligation past its terms on the time reference." Against Law rule 33: showing that something did not happen in time needs an anchor or a named keeper; without one the answer is undetermined. Fix: add "where the deal names a keeper or is anchored".
4. **Light.** "a mutual pool … funded by a small role share in each of their settlements"; "contributions as a role share: each settlement carries a share to the relevant social insurance fund's payee pointer." A role share is a share for a role filled at payment time, with third-party evidence (Law rule 22); a receiver known in advance is a named receiver (`[2, receiver, part]`). Fix: "a share to a named receiver".
5. **Light.** "Under a receipt-bound rating cMIP, fraud requires real, paid, visible jobs." Receipts are private by default (Finance, act formats); visible to the parties and to whoever they show them to. Fix: "real, paid jobs with receipts".
6. **Light.** "From the core, nothing new. Identities, standing offers, agreements, time references, custodians, keepers …" Custodians are not core. Fix: as 11, issue 5.
7. **Light.** "A rating is a signed act pointing at another act." Fine (Envelope `refs`); worth saying it is a reference, not an acknowledgement, since F110.
