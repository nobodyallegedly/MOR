## What changed, text by text

### Envelopes, draft 10

*In plain words.* Every everyday act carries a page number and a running fingerprint of the pages before it; these are now judged only when a whole line is rebuilt, and an act that does not fit simply cannot be proven kept (F292). The sealed envelope loses its throwaway outside signature, which never did what the text said; the text now says plainly that the outside of a sealed envelope proves nothing, by design, and everything that matters is proven by the signed act inside (F293). A relay's "what I hold" statement leaves the core for the relay transport cMIP; its rule and type number are retired, never reused (F294). An act outside its specification's format is invalid; one whose specification a client does not know is unknown; and where a verifier can see a rule it knows broken, invalid wins over unknown (F295, F323, T3). Fable's small fixes: the "previous" field required on every everyday act, which copy of an act is kept, a key delivery with a wrong key, both opening steps named, floats in their shortest form.

*Precisely.* Rule 13: Fable's sentence, no client-conformance warning (F292); E1 (key 2 required on every everyday act, absent invalid). Section 3 and the glossary: the outside of a sealed container is unauthenticated by design; `sealed = [to, locked-act]`; the container's step 3 becomes "No signature", rule 12 and step 4 no longer check one (F293); E10: the core hashes nothing under `MOR/sealed` now, the relay's container id alone uses the tag, so no rename is needed. Rule 28 and type 2 retired in place (F294); a "Withdrawal (type 3)" format, `withdrawal = {}`, carrying the entry `[publication, publication]`. Rule 30: F295 and F323 by pointer to the MICS's common conventions, and T3. E4 (format step 3, rule 27), E7 (under rule 11), E8 (rule 12), E9 (rule 1). Freeze lines, open parameters and the old-to-new table updated.

### Text, draft 8

*In plain words.* The list of what a text act carries is now a rule, `to` included for a reply or an addressed message; a text act carrying `acks` or `objects` is invalid; a chain of texts (a thread, a co-written document) is a cMIP's, by a companion act, so every message stays a plain readable text act (F296). The text act's content is read strictly: the text in field 0, an optional format in field 1, nothing else (F295). The bound on what a format may hide is checked by the client implementing the format, not by a verifier of acts (T4).

*Precisely.* New rule 14 (F296, T2's list); section 2 points to it. Part three: the strict decoding (closed map, field 0 a text string, field 1 a 32-byte hash), pointing to the MICS and Envelopes rule 30 (F295). Rule 1: T3. The format task: T4's wording. Reasoning, freeze lines and the table updated.

### Identity, draft 13

*In plain words.* Someone else's signed statement used as proof (an objection, an auditor's countersignature, a "this home is gone" statement, a receipt, an acknowledgement) counts only while its signer stands by it; disowned by a rotation, it is shown as a dispute and decides nothing, even if a friend vouched for it. A person's own posts and promises are as before (F297). A verifier missing something a rule needs now says unknown, never void or invalid, so a reader no longer shows a genuine post void because it has not fetched the line yet (F298). At a self-hosted home, a thief and the owner serving rival rotations at one place leave it stuck for good; the owner leaves by the escape, and the offline signing device signs that escape only when shown the thief's rotation, checked by itself (F299, Agreements round 2 Q7). Naming a successor is only a declaration until the successor confirms (F300). Fable's I5 (a rotation that failed the checks never counted, so the owner may sign again), I6 (a device-bound policy is as risky at homes enough to block the rule), I7 (the four meanings of "contested", named).

*Precisely.* F297 under its own heading after rule 34, rule 34 unchanged for a signer's own acts, pointers from rule 10, receipt check 6, the objection check, rule 49, the cosignature check, and one sentence pointing to Agreements' F302 and F303. F298 after the "same answer" paragraph (which now lists the signer's sequences). Rule 4 rewritten; "Which rotation counts" step 2; rule 29's second exception "or contested at a self-hosted home", with the Module's offline check; section 11 and the self-hosting reasoning no longer say "wins at once". F300 in section 8, with the glossary, "Which value counts" item 2, the succession confirmation's format and the client presentation note. I5 in rule 29; I6 in rules 24 and 25; I7 as a glossary entry. No rule renumbered. Freeze lines 5.5c, 5.7e, 5.7f, 5.7g, 5.8b, 8.4.

### Money, draft 8

*In plain words.* Whether a rail is a request rail (the seller asks for each payment) or a push rail (the buyer pays a published address), and what its proof locks in, are now read from two fields of the rail's published specification, never from its code (F301). One word, "pending", for a payment the rail has not yet answered (M3). A payer's app that cannot use the payee's clock says so before paying (M4). A debt's state (open, paid, forgiven) is shown by apps that read Agreements; a Money-only wallet shows it unknown; a Money-only view is noted for MOR 2 (M5).

*Precisely.* Rules 11, 18 and 24, the "Rail Module" entry, the payment task and the freeze lines point to Development's fields 11 and 13 (F301). M3 in rule 18, M4 in rule 29 (client conformance), M5 in rule 15 and Dependencies. A freeze line for F295 (Fable's M1). F297 and F298 checked: nothing to change (rule 29 already counts a receipt its rotation did not keep for nothing; no Identity citation says void or invalid where a verifier lacks something).

### Agreements, draft 14

*In plain words.* What a collective wrote onto its own chain (a resignation, a declaration of absence) stays true for the collective even if its signer later rotates; the dispute is shown, and a year of the collective's life no longer unravels (F302, its stated cost written). A successor signs a fork with its own chain key, and a closing counts a creditor's receipt or release only once the collective has acknowledged it (F303). Missing a piece of the history now means unknown, never "after" or void; "fails closed" and its kin now say unknown (F304, Q6 taken). A member with no plan and a confirmed successor gets a nomination, not a seat (F305). The old import act is gone for good; Money is cited by draft 7's numbers (Q5, Q6 taken).

Then the collectives' common language. A stake may carry a **holders' rule**: owners holding a set share agree for all, only on changes that hit every owner alike, binding only owners who accepted it (F307). Votes may be **counted by share** and rules combined; the release rule's threshold is counted by share (F308). **Issuance**: new shares, everyone diluted alike, each owner first offered its proportion; the core checks the offer reached each owner and names its deadline (F309, F315). Selling or releasing the works, and a fork paying kept owners less, are **owners' matters** (F310). Founders may let owners choose the members who run the collective; it binds through the keys, and where the key holders refuse, the vote stands complete and unexecuted, visibly (F311, F317). A collective may keep part of its income for itself, off the top, paid to its vault (F312). A passive owner who vanished can be declared absent, so the stake moves (F313). A deal's version someone relied on stays complete (F314). A share sale counts for the owners' votes once the collective's chain placed it (F316). Passive owners see the collective's money acts (F318). A grant's limits may include an expiry; exclusivity, conditions, licences, leases and trusts are not grants (F320). A passive owner may hand its vote to a **proxy**, inside the fence (F327).

*Precisely.* New rules 94 (the holders' rule), 95 (the owners' matters), 96 (issuance with pre-emption) and 97 (proxies), placed after rule 48, with no old number. New act types 26 (an issuance offer), 27 (a proxy), 28 (a proxy's revocation); new terms fields 30 (holders' rules), 31 (the transfers a deal's version counts), 32 (the offer an issuance follows). Rule forms "by share" and "all of"; a clone's mark may name a stake's holders' rule; field 24's head count `[1, k]` is now invalid, counted by share. Rule 77 (type 11, the import) retired in place. Rules 22 (F312), 47 (F314), 48 (F279's sentence: "No advance permission reaches what one holder is paid unlike the others"), 57 (F318), 58 (F304), 61 and 65 (F308, F311), 64 (F305 approval; F319's one sentence), the grant's format and reasoning (F320), 84 (F305), 85 and the declaration's format (F313), 91 (F303, F310), 93 (F311, F317), and C5 extended (F302). Every Money citation in draft 7 numbers; every "fails closed", "a verifier refuses it", "refused rather than guessed" now unknown. Freeze lines added per decision.

### Development, draft 10

*In plain words.* The MICS document, a test suite and a MIP (a proposal toward MOR 2) each get a kind of their own; a MIP names its author and what it forks (F291, F325). A rail Module says in two fields whether it is a request or push rail and what its proof ties down (F301). For each kind, the text lists which fields are required; act types and dependencies only help people find things; two publications of one text that differ in optional fields are two specifications, shown as versions of one text (F322). A specification that breaks the format is no specification, and acts naming it are unknown (F323). The root of trust is the six frozen texts themselves; a client ships them and computes their hashes; the repository stores them exactly as published (F324). "Anyone can run the same rule" stays a promise; the machine it runs on is a specification published after the freeze; until then every founding rule says it is a reference implementation (F326). If the creator disowns a publication, the specification is unclaimed (D7); a successor protocol's specifications get new hashes and agreements move by cloning (D9).

*Precisely.* Kinds 6, 7, 8; a MIP's creator and predecessor (field 8) required. Field 11 is the rail's kind alone (0 request, 1 push); new field 13 the set its proof binds (ascending from 0 payee, 1 purpose, 2 previous hop; may be empty); both required on a Module whose field 5 names the payment cMIP; the task 6 line reworded. "What each kind must carry" in Part three; the publishing rule; rule 26 "names one of them, or a specification whose text depends on them" and the root as the six texts. F323's verdicts. Rule 22 and F326. D7's sentence in rule 3, D9's in rule 27. F290 kept.

Every section summarises its layer's decisions, and Agreements gains a group of its own for the owners ("what a stake's holders decide together"). The freeze suite gains the scenarios the reviews asked for, for every decision taken.

## Readings and mechanics taken while writing (for Nobody, allegedly, to see; any may be overruled)

None of these changes a decided rule; each is a detail the decided rules bound, taken so the text could be written, and listed so it is seen.

1. **Envelopes, E1:** an everyday act without `prev` is invalid. The code and the published vector already treat it so; no verdict the library gives changes.
2. **Envelopes, type 2:** retired, so by F295 an act of type 2 is invalid (of a type the MIR does not define). Nobody makes one.
3. **Envelopes, withdrawal:** it "carries" the entry `[publication, publication]`, not "one entry", so a collective's withdrawal can also carry its own chain entry.
4. **Envelopes, T3** written in rule 30, not the MICS: rule 30 is where unknown is given as a verdict; Text rule 1 and the MICS point to it.
5. **Development, field 13 may be empty:** needed for the push rail that binds nothing, which Money rule 11 already decides.
6. **Development, a MIP's predecessor is field 8**, required on kind 8.
7. **Development, a rail Module missing field 11 or 13 is "no specification"** (F323's word) where Development Q6 said "invalid".
8. **Payment cMIP, field 6 (a rail's rule as a published program) required only where present**, since by F326 no founding rule is yet such a program.
9. **Agreements, eleven format details** (`notes` to the draft's section "Mechanics taken", each marked "mechanic, draft 14's" in the text): the numbers of types 26 to 28 and fields 30 to 32 and their shapes; an issuance is a version carrying field 32 (its offer), the offer type 26; the offer states the new shares' part of the stake, one price, each holder taking up its whole proportion by a signature act with a Money receipt or claim, no decline act; the offer reaches the roster where it is placed, a later placed transfer passing the seller's place to the buyer; "by share" written `[3, share]` (holders) and `[3, stake, share]` (members), **field 24's head count `[1, k]` made invalid rather than silently re-read as a share**; "all of" lists two rules or more, none nested, the forms by share for fields 5 and 18 only; a declaration against a holder who is no party names the clause in force, and may carry no outcome where the holder's plan names a stake successor; proxies and revocations count only public or sealed to the collective, signed by the holder, the later placed replacing the earlier; a successor's chain signature is "before" a later fork when it lies in its chain before the later fork's chain act; record field 3 registers transfers, proxies and revocations; F302's test written as C5's.

The one in bold changes how existing terms read (an agreement written with a head count for field 24 becomes invalid, not re-read); it follows F308 ("counted by share"), but it is the reading most worth the author's eye.

## The questions, one at a time

Sixteen, in the order suggested: the ones on the core's verdicts first, then the formats that freeze, then the rest. Each names its area. The label in brackets is the one the draft's "Open" marker uses.

**Q1 (Envelopes, rule 13; core business, a verdict) [QE1].** *An everyday act carrying two entries in `prev`.* The text has said "at most one" since F24 and gives no verdict; the code judges it only when a line is rebuilt, so such an act is valid act by act today. F292 put position and summary off to the rebuild because judging them needs the predecessor; the number of entries needs nothing but the act. (a) Invalid act by act, a shape rule like a missing `prev` (E1); every verifier gives the same answer in any order; a few lines of code and one test. (b) As the code does: judged at rebuild, where such an act cannot be proven kept; no code; but the field's two bad shapes (absent, two entries) get different verdicts. *Recommendation: (a).*

**Q2 (Identity, rule 49; core business, a stated cost) [Q2 (Identity)].** *An objection signed with a stolen operator key.* F297 takes away "for good" (an accomplice's acknowledgement no longer keeps it alive), but until the operator notices and rotates, the objection still voids the owner's homeless rotation; an owner holding both keys leaves at once by the escape. The text does not say so. (a) State it in rule 49's commentary as a stated cost, bounded by the operator's rotation. (b) Leave it unsaid. *Recommendation: (a)*: a stated cost is the author's to accept, and saying it shows it is not an oversight.

**Q3 (Identity, rule 29; the Module, and client conformance) [Q1 (Identity)].** *What may the air-gapped Module accept as showing that a rotation it signed was invalid (Fable's I5)?* I5 lets the owner sign again after a rotation that failed the checks; the Module cannot run the home-rule check (F94) without the identity's history, and Q7 of round 2 set the standard that the online side's word alone opens no exception. (a) The online side's report: simple; a compromised online side could get a second valid rotation signed, the very thing rule 29 prevents, stopped only by the human reading the screen. (b) Only bytes: the identity chain from genesis to the rotation's predecessor, checked offline, the Module running the check itself; a heavier Module. (c) Drop I5, leaving the owner to the escape. *Recommendation: (b)*, the round 2 standard applied again.

**Q4 (Money, rule 11 with Development field 13; core, which act decides) [Q-Money-1].** *A rail that binds part of the set.* Field 13 is now a set, so a rail can bind the payee without the purpose, or the reverse; rule 11's two cases do not say how they apply, nor who decides a disagreement about the payee when it is not bound. (a) Element by element: for each of payee, purpose and previous hop, the proof decides where field 13 holds it, the payer's claim where it does not, the contrary receipt shown as a dispute; where the payee is unbound, the payment address (rule 4) decides whose payment it is. (b) All or nothing: the proof decides only where it binds both payee and purpose. (c) Development allows only the sets {}, {payee, purpose} and {payee, purpose, previous hop}. *Recommendation: (a).*

**Q5 (Money rule 18 with Development fields 11 and 13; core) [Q-Money-2].** *A request rail whose proof does not bind the purpose.* Rule 18 settles a request rail's purchase by "the claim the seller's request committed to", which only the proof's binding of the purpose can show. With kind and binding now separate fields, a request rail could declare no purpose. (a) Development's format requires field 13 to hold purpose (and, by FR11 of the formats proposal, payee) where field 11 is 0, the specification no specification otherwise. (b) Settle such a rail as a push rail. (c) Leave it to the payment cMIP. *Recommendation: (a)*, with both payee and purpose, as FR11 already reads "a request rail always binds payee and purpose".

**Q6 (Development, Part three; core business, what a specification carries) [Q-Development-1].** *A Module with no cMIP to name.* F322 requires field 5, "the cMIP a Module implements", on every Module; but the seed Modules, the two air-gapped signers, the carry-words Module and the JPEG and video Modules work under a MIR or a task, with no cMIP. (a) Field 5 may name a MIR too (the glossary already calls a Module "an implementation or extension under a MIR or cMIP"). (b) Field 5 required only for a Module under a cMIP; such Modules unplaced in a listing. (c) Republish them as cMIPs. *Recommendation: (a).* It must be answered before those Modules can be published at step 17.

**Q7 (Development, rule 22's format; core business) [Q-Development-2].** *How a rule names the machine it runs on.* F326 makes the machine profile a specification that evolves, but `rule-ref = [bytes-hash, size, budget]` names no profile, and it freezes. (a) `rule-ref` gains the profile's spec hash. (b) A specification names its profile once, in a new field. (c) Leave it unnamed; later profiles kept backward compatible, which breaks "the same answer everywhere". *Recommendation: (a)*, before the freeze.

**Q8 (Development, the kinds; core business) [Q-Development-3].** *What kind of specification the machine profile is.* (a) A Module whose field 5 names the Development MIR (needs Q6's (a)). (b) A kind of its own, 9. (c) Left to the profile's author, which in a frozen format means (a). *Recommendation: (a) if Q6 is (a); else (b).*

**Q9 (Development rule 3 with Identity rule 34; core business) [Q-Development-4].** *A disowned publication someone relied on.* D7's sentence: a publication the creator's rotation disowned leaves the specification unclaimed. But under Identity rule 34 a signer's own act that another identity relied on stays valid, shown as disputed; so a thief's publication acknowledged by an accomplice would stay the creator's. (a) Unclaimed whether relied on or not, the publication shown as disputed: a special case beside rule 34. (b) Unclaimed only if not relied on. *Recommendation: (a)*: authorship is the creator's own claim, and the rotation withdrew it.

**Q10 (Development, kind 8; core business, the path to MOR 2) [Q-W1].** *A MIP proposing a new layer forks nothing.* F325 requires every MIP to name what it forks; F286 says a new layer comes with its own MIP. (a) The predecessor required only for a MIP that forks; none means a new layer, though forgetting then reads as intent. (b) Required always; a new-layer MIP names the MICS document, where layers are added. (c) It forks the nearest MIR; misleading. *Recommendation: (b).*

**Q11 (Agreements, rules 61 and 85; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q2].** *A vanished owner who votes on membership (F311).* Rule 61 covers every party the constitutional rule counts by the abandonment clause, so a lost voice never freezes the constitution; owners who are no parties can only have their stake moved (F313). (a) Where a collective counts membership among a stake's holders, its abandonment clause must allow moving a vanished holder's stake (outcome 1 or 2), or the terms are invalid, as rule 61 does for parties. (b) A stated cost: the constitution frozen while such a holder's stake does not move. *Recommendation: (a)*, F105's promise carried to owners.

**Q12 (Agreements, the fork's finality; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q4].** *How kept owners' consent to a fork is placed (F310 with F303).* Their consent is a signature act, which a later rotation could void (F297), unmaking a final fork, the hole F303 closed for successors. (a) Their consent counts only where an act of the original in the fork's history acknowledges it (F303's reading for closings). (b) They consent by chain signature: a key ceremony per owner per fork. *Recommendation: (a).*

**Q13 (Agreements, done and reaching; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q6].** *Sealing to an owner that is itself an agreement (F315, F318).* An issuance's offer and a collective's money acts must reach every holder, but a holder may be an agreement, and a deal has no identity to seal to. (a) Sealed to every party of its version in force (a collective holder: to the collective). (b) Sealed to its pointer's identity (its split service), which then reads it for the owners. (c) Reached only by publicity. *Recommendation: (a).*

**Q14 (Agreements, issuance; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q5].** *A standing waiver of pre-emption (F309).* The draft writes the waiver per issuance only (an issuance every holder signs needs no offer); the shapes map said "at founding or later". (a) Per issuance only. (b) Also a standing waiver in the stake's holders' rule, set or removed only by every holder, a buyer accepting it by buying. *Recommendation: (b)*: still "only by every holder".

**Q15 (Agreements, the succession plan's format; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q1].** *A seat successor's "voting weight" (rule 81, plan key 2).* F308 said "given this meaning or dropped"; under counting by share a successor weighs what it holds. The draft now says the number decides nothing. (a) Drop it from the format, before the freeze. (b) Keep it, deciding nothing: a number in signed terms that looks binding and is not. *Recommendation: (a).*

**Q16 (Agreements, areas; core) [Agreements draft 14's Q3].** *Does counting by share reach an area's threshold (F308)?* The map said change rules and area thresholds; the decision named the forms "of rule", and an area's threshold is a number. (a) Areas keep counting heads in MOR 1; a cMIP can put a by-share decision behind an area's single holder. (b) Give areas a by-share form. *Recommendation: (a)* for MOR 1.
