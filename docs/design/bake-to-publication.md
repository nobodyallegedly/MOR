# From bake to publication: how it should be

*A design note, 24 to 28 October 2026, drafted by the MOR lead from conversations with Nobody, allegedly ("Let's forget how it is for a minute and let's focus on how it should be"), and his hand test. For Fable's review against the way out; then the core round writes it into the texts. Nothing here is in the texts yet. What he said is quoted; what the lead proposes or takes under delegation is marked so.*

## Decided by Nobody, allegedly (24 October 2026, after a walk)

**The diagnosis:** "Feels like complexity and out of place info came out of wanting to allow splits without a publishing envelope. Ownership is one thing, distribution is another. The same owned item can be published in various places."

**The decision:** "I think it's best, the publication is the offer." And the four sentences, his:

| Step | Act | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Bake** | "I uploaded this." |
| 2 | **Claim** | "I claim to have made this." |
| 3 | **Ownership agreement** | "We have agreed that this is how we claim joint ownership." |
| 4 | **Publication** | "This is how we agree to publish it with these specified partners." |

So: **splits live only in a publication; one work has one ownership and may have many publications**, each with its own price, partners and split, the owners' shares the same in all. **A tip to a work goes through the publication where it was found**, which pays its partners and gives the owners their line. **"Unless there is a publication, the tip goes to the uploader then with no split possible."** **The price tag follows the same rule:** without a publication, a price tag pays the uploader, unsplit; with one, the publication names its price tag and splits what comes in, the price staying one Money act any wallet can pay. *Stated cost, to write in the texts:* a fan who re-uploads someone else's work receives the tips and tag payments on that bake; the owners' defense is their claim and their own publication. "Absolutely, piracy cannot be stopped. But truly rewarding creators can be made easy."

## From his hand test (28 October 2026; H101, H120; the suite's items 10 to 12)

- **No offer step.** "The offer is gone, it's in the publication envelope. Ownership is defined and then carried into future publishing deals." The standing offer (Agreements type 6) does not survive as a separate act.
- **The ways in** (the apps' Publishing tab, his): upload (then, optionally, a price tag on the bare bake, or a claim, then optionally a publication); a claim alone; an ownership agreement set up; a publication built from an ownership agreement. *So the claim is its own act, never a mark on the bake* (the earlier open question 1, closed by his flow).
- **Partners are any identity.** "The publisher is undefined in the core, it could be a curated relay, and client…" **The split service pays everyone; no partner holds others' money.**
- **Withdrawals belong where the act lives.** "Sounds like this should not be in envelopes but agreements, and that the whole offer does not exist anymore, it's the publication envelope now (which is an agreement or set of)." And: "move claim where it belongs" (claims stay in Agreements).

## Collectives (24, 26 and 28 October 2026)

- **A collective always has shares:** "A collective has shares regardless of whether money has been setup or not. It's the same as the ownership agreement.." Its founding and every member change carry the members' shares (a stake on the collective itself), required.
- **One act, two targets** (Nobody, allegedly, 28 October 2026, confirmed by the MOR lead): "So both shared works and collective are ownership agreements… one points at an envelope, the other at an identity." A work's ownership agreement points at the work (its claim, which names the bake); a collective's points at an identity, and founds it, so it also carries the members' areas and powers.
- **The split service is an agreement pointing to the ownership:** "The split service is an agreement that points to the ownership of the collective to attribute shares, same as with the publication." Shares read by reference, never copied. **One wallet is allowed:** "If people want to operate with a single wallet it's their choice."
- **One ID among owners:** "The collective is simply one ID in the co ownership agreement. It adds one layer of splitting when it gets to that id pointer."
- **No signing key of its own, no member keys (F401):** "Collectives only have chain keys and grant powers to the individual keys of their member." "There are no members key anymore." The collective grants powers to its members, not to keys; a member acts for it with their own key, the act naming the collective; with no area named, every member acts alone for it. The chain key stays as shares (the way out).

## Answers to the suite's questions (the MOR lead, under delegation, 28 October 2026)

- **Item 10, partner labels: in the publication's own format.** Each partner entry carries an optional short label in canonical text ("client", "relay", "split service"), shown to readers; no rule and no payment reads it. *Why core, not a cMIP:* naming who takes what is the legible part of distribution, so every reader should see it the same way; it costs one optional field and decides nothing.
- **Item 11 (a):** in the redesign, a collective's founding carries its shares and no split at all, by design (the split is a separate agreement pointing to it), so the question becomes how a member change that changes shares is complete: the constitutional change rule (membership is constitutional), and every holder whose share it lowers (rule 48), as today; nothing more.
- **Item 11 (b):** a collective's constitution **is** an ownership agreement (the shares in the collective itself, and how its owners decide) whose target is an identity it founds, with its members' areas and powers. The texts name the relation once: one act, the ownership agreement, pointing at a work or at an identity.
- **Item 11 (c):** yes, the design's own shape: a split agreement (a publication's, or a collective's own split service) reads the shares by reference to the ownership agreement it names, the version in force at each payment, for collectives and works alike.
- **Item 12, withdrawals, one per layer:**
  - **A publication is ended in Agreements**, by a later version of it that sells nothing (as an offer is withdrawn today: a later version, field 1 empty); past sales stand, a payment racing the ending is owed back.
  - **A price tag is withdrawn in Money**, by its signer, so a sole uploader can stop selling.
  - **A bake is never withdrawn by the protocol:** "I uploaded this" stays true. Taking its bytes off homes and relays is their policy (the vanish request, a relay transport cMIP, after the freeze); a witnessed act stays with whoever relied on it (F400).
  - **Envelopes' withdrawal (rule 24) retires** with the offer it served.

## Each step

**1. The bake.** The file, locked and stored (today's Envelopes type 0, without its price and `for`). "The bake does claim one thing: uploaded by." Anyone may bake the same file. Money sent to a bare bake (a tip, its uploader's price tag) goes to its uploader, unsplit.

**2. The claim.** "Claim is used to build agreements and as a defense." Its own act, in Agreements. It records who says they made a work, and in what order; never legitimacy. "If no claim is made, and no agreement is attached building from the claim, then it's the far west. And those even with an anchor only prove 'first on MOR', but MOR is not a court."

**3. The ownership agreement.** Built from the claim, or founding a collective. Ownership only: who owns what share, and how the owners decide (change rules, keepers, judges, succession, absence). No split, no fees, no pipes. It points at a work, or at an identity it founds (a collective, which adds its members' areas and powers).

**4. The publication, which is the offer.** The public face and the whole of distribution: what is sold, at which price (it names its price tag), on which terms (delivery, refund, contest); its partners, any identity, each with an optional label; the split service, which pays everyone; and the full split of the price paid, partners first, then the owners' line divided by their shares, read by reference to the ownership agreement. Self-publishing included. Ended by a later version that sells nothing.

## What changes from today, roughly (for the core round)

- Envelopes: type 0 becomes the bake (fields 7 and 8 leave); withdrawal (rule 24) retires; "upload" as the verb.
- Money: the price tag, and its withdrawal; Money rule 7's collective passages (F401).
- Agreements: the standing offer (type 6) and rule 15's "publication's agreement" become one act, the publication; the split plan, the split service, fees and role shares leave the ownership agreement (terms fields 8, 14, 28 and 29; rules 15 to 30 as they bear on it) for the publication and for a collective's own split agreement, each reading shares by reference; a collective's constitution written as an ownership agreement whose target is an identity; no collective signing key, no member keys (F401; rules 57 to 60, 73 to 76); partner labels; a witness act's copy kept (F400 is Identity's).
- Identity: a collective's genesis names its founding agreement in place of a signing key (F401); the witness act's kept copy (F400).
- Text: rule 14's collective cases follow F401.
