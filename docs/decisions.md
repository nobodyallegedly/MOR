# What Nobody, allegedly, and the MOR lead arrived at

*This file records what Nobody, allegedly, and the MOR lead arrived at together, and nothing else counts as his direction. He said, on 12 November: "My words matter less than our words within that conversation do." So each entry records the exchange: what the MOR lead (or Fable, or a session) asked or proposed, labelled as theirs; his words, quoted exactly; and one line of what was arrived at, written by the MOR lead and marked "to check" until he has read it. Each stint opens with his wish, kept at the top for the whole stint. A new wish starts a new stint; the old one moves to the history below, and nothing from it carries over unless he says so. Everything else in the repository (findings, reviews, prompts, reports) is a proposal or a record of work, labelled with whose it is.*

*How his words are read: as one train of thought, later answers refining earlier ones; never one isolated sentence given more weight than the exchange gives it ("Be mindful of taking isolated sentences out of context and give them too much weight", 12 November).*

*Typos in his quotes are corrected only with him: on 12 November he corrected "decoifying" (defined), "pints" (points), "spit" (spot), "ment" (meant), "tat" (that), "it!s" (it's), "PERSONALY" (personally), "the the cMIP" (the cMIP), "cruelly" (really), "they'd were" (they were), and confirmed "referees" as referrers.*

**What to build first.** The MOR lead asked whether the Dubsar Suite or the lead should build the first cMIPs on draft 1.

**What must hold** (after the first shower test, pull request 151).
- He: "The shower is an idea, the exact text does not need to hold. What needs to hold is: can I with a single click reward all the people that contributed to the service or good via MOR"
- *Arrived at (to check):* the test of the grammar is one click reaching every contributor.

**A work as a party.** The lead asked whether someone in an agreement can be a work (another agreement), the money going to whoever that agreement names as its receiver now.
- He: "I think it is essential… it ensures a series of split is possible." "It’s already there in some shape. The collective agreement feeds its share structure to the collective deals." "With lineage it’s sort of the same. The new agreement points to the other agreement as one party, no collective involved"
- *Arrived at (to check):* a party can be an agreement; what reaches it is passed on by that agreement's own terms; no collective is needed for it.

**Participants who arrive later.** The lead said the payer is in neither agreement.
- He: "That was a problem when we first look at it… some participants are not tied to a specific ID when the agreement is drafted, they are tied to a role that will be linked to an ID in the future. (The repost was the easy one… but there are more)" The lead proposed research on such roles; it went to a stranger (`docs/strangers-2026-11/late-roles.md`).
- On the four holes the lead drew from it (attention roles; shared pools; the middle of the chain; the ones nobody anticipated): "1. Yes, but seen by the agreed witness and with the possibility to reject all that is not tied to a settlement." "2. Sadly some rails will need pools… this headache won’t be easy." "3. Is the reason reputation tracking no platform owns or computes is important." "4… Mor won’t force the shower, it will sustain it, that should lower the difficulty for this bit"
- He: "Maybe a customizable “future participants” with the cMIP specifying how that participant is defined and confirmed" The lead agreed and mapped it to the research's kinds. He: "To be used only for what point 4 covered, the rest is defined with the list. What do you think?" The lead proposed the list be kinds, not named uses. He:
  > "Prove that you have done X and earn spot 1.
  > Prove you have received Y and earn spot 2.
  > Prove that you are holding Z and earn spot 3 (relays for example)
  > Prove that your ID fits the conditions in W and earn spot 4"
  And: "Looks elegant and powerful. To be tested…"
- *Arrived at (to check):* four spots (done, received, holding, fits), each a proof whose test and confirmer a cMIP defines; a fifth, "future", only for those nobody could anticipate; attention counted only by the agreed witness; pools open; reputation computed by anyone, owned by no one; MOR sustains the shower, never forces it.

**Old Terms stopping** (after the second shower test, pull request 154).
- He: "Old terms stopping is a headache I did not get to understand quite yet… that’s where a whole lot of special cases were born if I remember right" The lead proposed: Terms stop when Terms replacing them are in force, latest per the agreement's place or "as shown", two competing replacements a fork shown and never chosen. He: "Yes, less likely to bloat and overdefine."
- *Arrived at (to check):* one rule for replacing, no cases.

**Spots and holding.** The lead proposed a spot earned per proof, not once, its cMIP saying one or many. He: "Yes, it leverages the above discussion as intended I think" The lead asked where a relay stops holding and starts being seen. He: "Relay was just an example. I don’t think it should be defined, what matters is “HOLD” with cMIP defined what the proof is, no?"
- *Arrived at (to check):* a spot may be filled many times, each filling tied to its proof; the grammar names no use of holding; the proof is the cMIP's.

**Forks.** He: "Can we attempt  to solve incompatible forks (a fork that should not exist) for all sorts of acts that need it?" The lead proposed one shape (shown, never chosen; ended by a join naming every branch, signed by whoever could sign what the branches shared; what others relied on stands, shown as disputed). He: "I think it does. Let’s focus on unresolved forks…" To the lead's three ways out: "Resolver makes sense, the problem potentially exists, so deciding in advance how to solve it makes sense, and this way does not force a single way to do it." On the lead's flawed example: "Both term 2a and 2b are invalid as they do not carry the required signatures that were in term 1." The lead corrected it (a real fork only where the Terms let fewer than all replace them, or the same people sign twice) and proposed that the Terms both branches replaced stay in force until settled. He: "It does, and more sense than anyone ending up stuck in the first one"
- *Arrived at (to check):* one fork rule for every act that comes in a line; a join, or a resolver named in advance; meanwhile the last shared Terms stay in force, so nobody is stuck.

**Ending.** The lead proposed ending as replacement by Terms saying "ended". He: "That agreement can be ended in multiple ways… what happens to the work and its shares draws different ends." "Not sure still pondering. An agreement never ends, it can resolve. It’s a new version of the agreement with new conditions signed by all signers of previous version" "« we agree to resolve the previous agreement » is really what happens. The agreement stays and is useful. Imagine they all agree to make the work public and free, then one person attempts to sell it again but individually, the previous agreement says « sorry can’t do this alone, the other two signatures are needed » so the cheater has to modify and bake again…" The lead asked whether resolving needs everyone who signed the previous version even where the Terms let fewer change them. He: "Terms rule, because they were agreed by all signers" On withdrawing an open offer: "The offer does not end, it is simply not open to new IDs anymore. Whoever bought the rights should still be able to point to a valid agreement that says so"
- *Arrived at (to check):* an agreement never ends; it resolves into a new version under the Terms' own rule, whose conditions say what happens to the work and shares; the last version keeps protecting those in it; an offer closes to new identities, and whatever was earned keeps a valid agreement to point to.
