**The frame under test** is his sentence, "money truly is two things « pay this pls » « this has been paid »". The assignment turned it into a claim to try to break: every moment at which a use touches money itself is either someone asking that money move or someone saying that it has moved; everything else is a rule, a promise or an account between people. That wording of the claim is the assignment's, not his.

## How to read the marks

- **counted**: computed by a script from the table in this file.
- **traced**: shown by the order of moments in a named use.
- **sourced**: taken from a source in the list at the end.
- **disputed**: sources disagree; who and on what is said.
- **inferred**: my reading, with what it rests on and a confidence range out of 100.

Three things are kept apart throughout: **money moves** (something leaves one holder and reaches another); **words** (someone says money moved, or asks that it move); **a claim** (a debt, a share, an entitlement exists between people, and nothing has moved or been asked yet).

## Plain words first

**The ways of touching money, in ten lines.** (The list is inferred from 63 uses; the numbers are counted.)

1. **Ask.** Someone asks to be paid. In 35 of 63 uses.
2. **Order.** Someone tells whoever keeps their money to pay. In 7.
3. **Permit.** Someone lets another person, or a clock, ask later and be obeyed. In 4.
4. **Move.** Money leaves one holder and reaches another: plainly, or into the hands of someone in the middle, or out of them, or back, or onward, or taken. In 61.
5. **Say it moved.** Someone tells a person who could not see it that money moved. In 11 as a step of its own.
6. **Say it did not move.** Someone says a payment failed or never came. In 3.
7. **Set aside.** Money is fenced off where it lies; it has not moved and its holder cannot use it. In 4.
8. **Make and unmake.** A promise is written and used as money, and later comes home and ends. In 2.
9. **Cancel against.** Debts are set against each other and are settled, and nothing moves. In 6.
10. **Time runs out.** A day passes, nobody acts, and because of that someone now owes, or loses, something. In 9.

That is eleven kinds of moment (line 8 holds two). Almost everything else people do with money (lending, pooling, budgeting, sharing out, insuring, guaranteeing, counting plays) is a promise, a rule or an account, and touches no money until one of these moments comes.

**Whether the frame held.** Mostly, with three stretches and three breaks.

- Of 197 moments, 112 are plainly "pay this please" or "this has been paid" (counted).
- 65 more are one of the two only if the sentence is stretched (counted). "This has been paid" must be able to say *for whom it is held*, *what it returns or passes on*, or *by whose power it moved* (55 moments). It must be allowed to be true when debts cancelled and nothing moved (6). And "pay this please" must be allowed to be said long in advance, for someone else to fire (4).
- 20 are neither, or I cannot tell (counted). The clear neithers: money **set aside** without moving (a card's authorisation, a lock, a freeze); someone saying a payment **did not** happen; and **time running out**, where the moment is that nothing happened. The doubtful ones are money being **made** and **unmade** (a voucher issued and redeemed, a mutual-credit balance): they can be told as "handed over" and "handed back", or as something coming into being and ending, and the map alone does not decide which.

One thing runs under several of these (inferred, 65 to 80): the frame's two sentences are both about money in motion. Most of what does not fit is about money, or a debt, at rest.

## The map, family by family

**This map is my own description of practice**, written from what I know of how each use works, before reading any prior art for this session (Part 1 was committed on its own first). Nothing in it is sourced. Each use is one ordinary case told once; real practice varies, and nine uses are marked "less sure of the workings".

How each use is laid out: a short story; **a.** the verbs; **b.** the moments that touch money itself, in order, each tagged in brackets with which of the three things it is and how it sits with the frame; **c.** what the use does without touching money; **d.** who else is there. The tags are explained in full under "The ways of touching money". In short: "request" and "statement" fit the frame plainly; "statement+" is a statement that money moved with something added; "statement−" is a statement of payment with no move behind it; "neither" is neither.

A plain payment is usually also said ("here is your 3", a slip, a line on a statement). "Say it moved" is listed as a moment of its own only where the saying is a separate step that someone relies on.

### Family 1: Finance

#### 1.1 A loan and its repayment
Ben needs 100 to mend his van. Ana lends it for a year; he is to give back 110.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, promises it, delays it, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben asks Ana to lend him 100. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana hands Ben 100. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move back.** A year on, Ben hands Ana 110: the 100 returned and 10 more. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** for a whole year the loan is only Ben's debt and Ana's claim; the 10 of interest is a rule that makes the claim grow; the due date is a rule.
- **d. Others:** nobody, unless a witness or a note is wanted.

#### 1.2 Instalments
Ana buys Ben's bicycle for 300 and pays 100 a month for three months.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, delays it, splits it (over time), counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana pays Ben the first 100 and rides away. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move.** A month later Ana pays the second 100. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move.** A month later Ana pays the last 100. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the price, the schedule, the balance still owed after each payment (an account), and any rule that the bicycle stays Ben's until the last payment.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 1.3 A deposit (money left with a keeper)
Ana leaves 500 with Carla, who keeps money for the whole street. Later Ana tells Carla to pay Ben 100 out of it, and takes 200 back in coin.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, counts it, returns it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana hands Carla 500. From now on what Ana has is Carla's promise to give it back. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Order.** Ana tells Carla: pay Ben 100 from mine. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move.** Carla rewrites her book: she owes Ana 100 less and Ben 100 more. No coin leaves Carla's box. *(moves · statement)*
   4. **Say it moved.** Carla tells Ben he now has 100 with her. *(words · statement)*
   5. **Order.** Ana asks Carla for 200 in coin. *(words · request)*
   6. **Move back.** Carla hands Ana 200. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** Ana's balance is an account of what Carla owes her; whether Carla may lend the coins out meanwhile is a rule between them.
- **d. Others:** Carla the keeper. After moment 1 every payment Ana makes from her deposit is a change in Carla's book.

#### 1.4 Escrow
Ana buys Ben's old car for 1,000. Neither trusts the other to go first, so Carla stands in the middle.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, delays it, passes it on, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays 1,000 to Carla, to be held for Ben until the car is handed over. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Say it moved.** Carla tells Ben the 1,000 is with her. *(words · statement)*
   3. **Order.** Ben delivers the car; Ana tells Carla to release. *(words · request)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays Ben 1,000. (Had the car been a wreck, a "move back" would have returned it to Ana instead.) *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the condition for release; whose the money is while Carla holds it; who decides if they quarrel.
- **d. Others:** Carla holds, tells and releases; she owns none of it.

#### 1.5 An insurance pool and a claim on it
A hundred households each put 10 a year into a pool Carla keeps. Ben's roof burns.
- **a. Verbs:** pools it, holds it, asks for it, counts it, earmarks it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Carla calls for the year's 10 from each household. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move into the middle.** Each household pays its 10 into the pool. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   3. **Ask.** Ben claims 400 for his roof. *(words · request)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** Carla, having looked at the roof, pays Ben 400 from the pool. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the promise to every member (most never collect); the assessment of the damage (a measure); what happens to a surplus or a shortfall (a rule).
- **d. Others:** Carla keeps the pool and judges claims; ninety-nine members pay and receive nothing but the promise.

#### 1.6 A bond or stake posted in advance
Ben is to build Ana's wall by June. To show he means it he posts 200 with Carla: his again if the wall stands, Ana's if it does not.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, earmarks it, returns it, forfeits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Ben pays 200 to Carla, held against the wall. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Time runs out.** June ends and there is no wall. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   3. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays the 200 to Ana. (Had the wall stood, a "move back" would have returned it to Ben.) *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the promise to build; the test of whether it was kept; the forfeit rule.
- **d. Others:** Carla. Where a machine holds the stake under a rule and no person can touch it, moment 1 is a "set aside" in place of a move into the middle, and the forfeit may destroy the money rather than pay it to anyone.

#### 1.7 A dividend
Ana owns six tenths of the bakery and Ben four tenths. The year leaves 1,000 over and they agree to share out 500.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, splits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** The bakery pays Ana 300. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move.** The bakery pays Ben 200. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the count of the surplus; the vote to share out; the shares. Between the vote and the payment each owner holds a claim on the bakery.
- **d. Others:** whoever keeps the bakery's books and till.

#### 1.8 Mutual credit
Ana, Ben and Carla keep one book and all start at nought. Ana buys bread from Ben for 10; Ben buys a haircut from Carla for 10; Carla buys eggs from Ana for 10.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, promises it. A verb is missing from the list: **makes it** (and **unmakes it**), because the money here does not exist before the first purchase or after the last.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Make.** The book is written: Ana 10 below nought, Ben 10 above. Ben's 10 did not exist a moment before. *(a claim is born as money · neither?)*
   2. **Move.** Ben pays Carla with his 10: the book moves it from his line to hers. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Unmake.** Carla pays Ana 10: Ana is back at nought, and the 10 is gone. *(a claim used as money ends · neither?)*
- **c. Without touching money:** each one's limit below nought (a rule); the standing duty of whoever is below nought to give something to the circle some day.
- **d. Others:** the book and whoever keeps it. Each purchase is at once a payment and a statement of it; there is nothing else to move.

#### 1.9 A voucher issued, passed on and redeemed
Ben the baker sells Ana a voucher for one loaf, for 3. Ana gives it to Carla for trimming her hedge. Carla brings it to the bakery.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, passes it on, converts it, makes it, unmakes it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Make.** Ben writes the voucher and hands it to Ana: a promise of a loaf, new that morning. *(a claim is born as money · neither?)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays Ben 3 for it. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move.** Ana pays Carla with the voucher. *(moves · statement)*
   4. **Unmake.** Carla hands the voucher to Ben and takes her loaf; the voucher is spent. *(a claim used as money ends · neither?)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the promise itself, whoever holds it; any date after which Ben will not honour it (a rule).
- **d. Others:** every holder between issue and redemption. While it passes from hand to hand it is used as money; only the last holder meets the baker.

#### 1.10 A rotating savings club
Ana, Ben and Carla each owe the club 10 a month. Each month one of them takes the whole 30: Ana first, then Ben, then Carla.
- **a. Verbs:** pools it, promises it, delays it, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Month one: Ben and Carla each hand Ana 10. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move.** Month two: Ana and Carla each hand Ben 10. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move.** Month three: Ana and Ben each hand Carla 10. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the order of turns; the duty to keep paying after one's own turn. Ana, who took first, has in effect borrowed; Carla, who took last, has saved; nobody said "loan".
- **d. Others:** often an organiser who collects and hands over, and then each month is a "move into the middle" and an "move out of the middle". In the plain form the pool never rests anywhere.

#### 1.11 A guarantee given by a third person
Ana lends Ben 100 only because Carla says: if he does not pay, I will.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, asks for it, passes it on (the debt, not the money).
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana hands Ben 100. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Time runs out.** The day comes and Ben pays nothing. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   3. **Say it did not move.** Ana tells Carla that Ben has not paid. *(words · neither)*
   4. **Ask.** Ana asks Carla for the 100. *(words · request)*
   5. **Move.** Carla pays Ana 100. *(moves · statement)*
   6. **Ask.** Carla asks Ben for the 100 she paid for him. *(words · request)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the guarantee, which touches nothing at all if Ben pays; after moment 5, Ben's debt is to Carla.
- **d. Others:** Carla, who is nowhere in the money unless Ben fails.

### Family 2: Trade and work

#### 2.1 Buying a good
Ana takes a loaf to Ben's till.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben says: 3, please. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana hands him 3. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Ben hands her a slip saying 3 was paid. *(words · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the price on the shelf, which is an offer to anyone until someone comes to the till.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 2.2 Buying a service
Carla cuts Ana's hair; Ana pays when it is done.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, delays it (by minutes).
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** The cut done, Carla says: 20. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays 20. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** for the length of the haircut Ana owes for work already begun; the price list.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 2.3 A subscription
Ana takes Ben's monthly paper at 5 a month and lets him collect it from her account until she says stop.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, asks for it, delays it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Permit.** Ana tells Ben and her bank: Ben may collect 5 each month. *(words · request given in advance)*
   2. **Ask.** Each month Ben's collection goes to Ana's bank; Ana does nothing. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move.** Each month 5 goes from Ana to Ben. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the term, the price, the right to stop. Stopping is words that end the permit; no money is touched.
- **d. Others:** Ana's bank, which obeys Ben's request because of Ana's earlier word.

#### 2.4 An invoice paid in parts
Ben builds Ana a shed and invoices 900. She pays 300 now and 600 next month.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, delays it, splits it (over time), counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben sends the invoice for 900. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays 300. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Ana notes with the payment which invoice the 300 is for. *(words · statement)*
   4. **Move.** Ana pays 600. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the 600 still open between the two payments (an account); the terms of payment.
- **d. Others:** nobody. Without moment 3 Ben has money and cannot tell what it settles.

#### 2.5 A refund
Ana bought shoes from Ben for 60. They pinch; she brings them back.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana paid Ben 60 (the sale). *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Ask.** Ana asks for her money back. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move back.** Ben pays Ana 60, against the sale. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the right to return and its time limit (rules); between moments 2 and 3 a claim.
- **d. Others:** nobody. The refund is a new move the other way, tied to the first; the first is not unsaid.

#### 2.6 A wage
Ana works the month in Ben's workshop for 2,000, of which 400 is tax that Ben must send to the town.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, delays it, splits it, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** At month's end Ben pays Ana 1,600. She does not ask; the day does. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Taken.** Ben pays 400 to the town as Ana's tax. It was never in her hands. *(moves, by another's power · statement+)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Ben gives Ana a slip saying what was paid to her and what to the town for her. *(words · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** through the month Ben's debt to Ana grows day by day and nothing moves; the rate; the tax rule.
- **d. Others:** the town, which is paid "by Ana" with money Ana never held.

#### 2.7 A commission
Carla sells Ben's painting to Ana for 1,000 and keeps a tenth for her trouble.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, splits it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana pays Carla 1,000. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move onward.** Carla pays Ben 900. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the tenth. Carla's 100 never moves as a payment of its own: she earns it by not passing it on.
- **d. Others:** Carla. Whether the 1,000 was hers for a moment or Ben's all along in her hands is settled by their agreement, not by anything visible in the money.

#### 2.8 A split among those who made something
Ben, Carla and Dan made a record together, in equal shares. Ana buys it for 12.
- **a. Verbs:** splits it, passes it on, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana pays 12 into the band's till, which Ben keeps. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move onward.** Ben pays Carla 4. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   3. **Move onward.** Ben pays Dan 4. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the shares (a rule); the count of what was sold; between sale and sharing, Carla's and Dan's claims on the till.
- **d. Others:** whoever keeps the till. If the 12 were divided at the door there would be three "move"s from Ana and no till.

#### 2.9 A payment passed on down a chain of receivers
Ana buys a book in Ben's shop for 10. Ben owes his wholesaler Carla 6 of it, Carla owes the publisher Dan 4, Dan owes the author Eve 1.
- **a. Verbs:** passes it on, splits it, delays it, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana pays Ben 10. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Ask.** Carla invoices Ben for the month's books. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move onward.** Ben pays Carla 6. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   4. **Move onward.** Carla pays Dan 4. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   5. **Move onward.** Dan pays Eve 1. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** each link's share; the count of copies; the weeks or months each link may wait. Each link usually pays for many books at once, so no coin can be followed from Ana to Eve.
- **d. Others:** every link is both receiver and payer.

#### 2.10 A payment on delivery
Ana orders a stove from Ben. Carla the carrier brings it and takes the 200 at the door.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, holds it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** At the door Carla asks for 200. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays Carla 200, for Ben. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Carla gives Ana a slip and leaves the stove. *(words · statement)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** At week's end Carla pays Ben 195 and keeps 5 for the carrying. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the rule "no money, no stove"; Carla's debt to Ben while she carries his money.
- **d. Others:** Carla, who holds both the stove and the money and lets go of one for the other.

#### 2.11 An advance
Ana publishes books. Ben asks for 500 now to live on while he writes; it is to be earned back from his royalties.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, delays it (backwards: paid before it is earned), counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben asks for the advance. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays Ben 500. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Cancel against.** The book earns Ben 800 in royalties; the first 500 are set against the advance and nothing moves. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
   4. **Move.** Ana pays Ben the remaining 300. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the running account of royalties against the advance; the rule for a book that never earns the 500 back.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

### Family 3: Media and attention

#### 3.1 Buying a work
Ana buys Ben's song for 1 from Carla's shop.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, splits it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Carla's shop shows the price at the checkout. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays Carla 1. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move onward.** At month's end Carla pays Ben his 70 hundredths, with his share of every other sale. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** what Ana may do with the song (a permission, not a thing); Carla's count of sales.
- **d. Others:** Carla the shop.

#### 3.2 Paying per play or per use (less sure of the workings)
Ana pays Carla's music service 10 a month and plays Ben's song forty times.
- **a. Verbs:** pools it, counts it, splits it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Permit.** Ana lets Carla collect 10 a month. *(words · request given in advance)*
   2. **Move.** Each month 10 goes from Ana to Carla, into one pot with every other listener's. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move onward.** Carla counts all plays and pays Ben the slice of the pot that his plays come to. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** every play. A play is counted, never paid: it moves nothing when it happens. The rate per play is worked out afterwards from the pot.
- **d. Others:** Carla, and often a label between Carla and Ben that takes a part and passes the rest on. Where a use really is paid one at a time (a coin in a jukebox) each use is a plain "move".

#### 3.3 A tip
Ana leaves 2 on the table for Ben, who served her.
- **a. Verbs:** none of the list fits well; nearest is passes it on. She simply gives it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana leaves 2; Ben picks it up. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** custom. Nothing was owed before and nothing is owed after.
- **d. Others:** nobody, unless the staff share tips, and then the 2 goes "move into the middle" to a jar and "move out of the middle" by a rule.

#### 3.4 Advertising (less sure of the workings)
Ben wants readers of Carla's paper to see his shop. He puts 100 with Carla, who charges him a little each time his notice is shown.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, holds it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ben pays Carla 100 ahead. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Cancel against.** Each showing is counted and its price set against Ben's 100; nothing moves. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
   3. **Move onward.** Carla pays Dan, on whose page the notice appeared, his part. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the count of showings, which only Carla sees; the price of each; Ben's remaining balance.
- **d. Others:** Carla in the middle; Dan the page owner; and Ana the reader, whose attention is the thing sold and who touches no money at all.

#### 3.5 A royalty
Carla publishes Ben's book and owes him a tenth of what it brings in, counted twice a year.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, delays it, splits it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move onward.** Twice a year Carla pays Ben a tenth of what the book brought her. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the statement of sales that comes with it (a count, made by the one who pays); the rate; the six months each sale waits.
- **d. Others:** sometimes a society that collects for many authors and divides one pot among them.

#### 3.6 A licence
Ana wants Ben's photograph on her café's menu for a year.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben names 50 for the year. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays 50. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the permission itself and its limits. A licence priced per copy is a royalty (3.5).
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 3.7 A pay-what-you-want price
Ben puts his album out and says: pay what you like. Ana pays 7.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it (with the amount left open).
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben asks everyone for whatever they choose. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays 7. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** nothing before; no debt ever exists. The amount is set by the payer at the moment of paying.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 3.8 A crowdfunded work paid only if enough people pledge
Ben needs 1,000 to press a record. Carla runs the collection. Ana and others pledge; nothing is to be taken unless the pledges reach 1,000 by the end of May.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, promises it, pools it, holds it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben asks the crowd for 1,000. *(words · request)*
   2. **Permit.** Ana lets Carla collect 20 from her if, and only if, the goal is met. *(words · request given in advance)*
   3. **Time runs out.** The end of May comes. If the pledges fall short, every permit dies here and no money was ever touched. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   4. **Move into the middle.** The goal was met: Carla collects each pledge. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   5. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays Ben the total less her fee. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** every pledge until the day; the tally. In the other common practice the money is taken at once, held, and sent back if the goal is missed: then moment 2 is a "move into the middle" and failure is a "move back".
- **d. Others:** Carla, who collects, counts and holds.

### Family 4: Law and collective life

#### 4.1 A membership fee
The garden club asks each member for 20 a year; Carla is treasurer.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, pools it, holds it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Carla sends each member the year's request. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays 20 to Carla, for the club. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** membership and what it gives; the rule that an unpaid member lapses.
- **d. Others:** Carla holds for the club. The money is no one person's.

#### 4.2 A fine or penalty
Ana leaves her cart where carts are forbidden. Ben the warden writes a ticket for 50, due in thirty days.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it (with force behind the asking), forfeits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben's ticket demands 50 for the town. *(words · request)*
   2. **Time runs out.** Thirty days pass unpaid; by rule the fine is now 80. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   3. **Taken.** The town has 80 taken from Ana's wage before it reaches her. (Had she paid in time, a plain "move".) *(moves, by another's power · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the rule broken; the scale of fines; the growth for lateness.
- **d. Others:** the town; and Ana's employer, made to pay the town in her place.

#### 4.3 A deposit lost to a dispute
Ana rents Ben's flat and leaves 500 with him against damage. When she goes, Ben says the floor will cost 200. Carla, asked to judge, agrees with him.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, returns it, forfeits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays Ben 500, to be held against damage. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Ask.** Leaving, Ana asks for it back. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move back.** After Carla's ruling Ben returns 300. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the 200. It never moves: it was in Ben's hands as Ana's and, by Carla's word, is in the same hands as Ben's. The forfeit is a change in a claim only.
- **d. Others:** Carla the judge, who moves nothing. Here the holder in the middle is one of the two sides.

#### 4.4 A tax or levy
The town levies 100 a year on each house, and takes a twentieth of every sale in Ben's shop.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, asks for it, passes it on, pools it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** The town sends Ana a demand for 100. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays the town 100. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it moved.** The town gives a receipt. *(words · statement)*
   4. **Move onward.** Each quarter Ben pays the town the twentieth he collected from his customers inside his prices. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the assessment (a count of houses, of sales); the rule itself. Ana pays the sales levy without knowing the moment: it rides inside the price.
- **d. Others:** Ben, who collects for the town from people the town never meets.

#### 4.5 A grant
Carla's foundation offers money for village libraries. Ben applies for 1,000.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, earmarks it, counts it, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben applies: 1,000, for shelves and books. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Carla pays 600, to be spent on the library and nothing else. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Ben reports what he paid out and to whom. *(words · statement)*
   4. **Move.** Carla pays the last 400. *(moves · statement)*
   5. **Move back.** Ben returns the 50 he did not need. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the purpose that rides on every coin (an earmark); the panel's choice; the duty to report.
- **d. Others:** the people Ben pays in turn. Moment 3 is Ben saying that money moved onward, to someone who was not there to see.

#### 4.6 A prize
The town promises 100 for the best pie at the fair. The jury picks Ana's.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it (to nobody yet).
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** The town pays Ana 100. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the announcement, a promise with no one on the other end until the jury speaks; the judging.
- **d. Others:** the jury, who decide and move nothing.

#### 4.7 A budget a group votes
The allotment society has 1,000 with Carla. The members vote: 400 for tools, 300 for the fence, 300 kept back.
- **a. Verbs:** earmarks it, counts it, holds it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Weeks later Ben brings the bill for the tools: 380. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays it from the tools line. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the whole vote. At the meeting nothing moves and nothing is asked; 1,000 sits where it sat with three names written over it.
- **d. Others:** Carla, and the meeting.

#### 4.8 Damages awarded
A court finds that Ben's dog ruined Ana's garden and says he must pay her 500.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, asks for it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ana, judgment in hand, demands 500 from Ben. *(words · request)*
   2. **Time runs out.** The time allowed passes and Ben pays nothing. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   3. **Taken.** An officer of the court takes 500 from what Ben has and pays Ana. (Had Ben paid, a plain "move".) *(moves, by another's power · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the judgment itself. It measures a harm in money and makes a claim certain; it is neither Ana asking nor anyone paying.
- **d. Others:** the court, which speaks; the officer, who takes.

#### 4.9 A common fund and who may draw on it
The village keeps a fund for the hall. Any two of the three wardens together may spend from it.
- **a. Verbs:** pools it, holds it, earmarks it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Villagers pay into the fund. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Order.** Carla and Dan both sign: pay the roofer. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move out of the middle.** The fund pays Ben the roofer 300. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** who may draw and how many must agree; what it may be spent on.
- **d. Others:** the wardens. The request to move the money is not valid from one voice alone.

### Family 5: Gifts and chance

#### 5.1 A donation
Carla's shelter appeals for help. Ana sends 20.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, earmarks it (if the giver names a purpose).
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** The shelter's appeal goes out to everyone. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays the shelter 20. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it moved.** The shelter sends a receipt, which Ana may show the tax office. *(words · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** nothing is owed either way. If Ana says "for blankets", a purpose rides on the money.
- **d. Others:** the tax office, which reads the receipt and was at neither end.

#### 5.2 A gift
Ana gives Ben 50 for his birthday.
- **a. Verbs:** none of the list fits. A verb is missing: **gives it**, a move with no claim before it and no claim after.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana hands Ben 50. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** whatever the gift means between them, which no account in money holds.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 5.3 An inheritance (less sure of the workings)
Ana dies. Her will leaves what she had to Ben and Carla in halves; Dan is to carry it out.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, counts it, splits it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Dan shows the will to Ana's bank and gathers her 1,000 into his hands, for the estate. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Ask.** The grocer claims the 100 Ana owed him. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move out of the middle.** Dan pays the grocer 100. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** Dan pays Ben 450 and Carla 450. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the will; the shares; from the death until moment 4, Ben and Carla hold only claims.
- **d. Others:** Dan, the bank, the grocer. The one whose money it was takes no part in any moment: every move is made by others on the strength of a paper.

#### 5.4 A bet
Ana says the red boat will win, Ben the blue. They put 10 each with Carla.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it, promises it, forfeits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Ana and Ben each pay 10 to Carla. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Move out of the middle.** The red boat wins; Carla pays Ana 20. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the wager. With no Carla it is two promises, and the only moment is the loser paying, which in many places no court will make him do.
- **d. Others:** Carla the stakeholder.

#### 5.5 A lottery
Carla sells a hundred tickets at 1. One wins 60; the other 40 goes to the school.
- **a. Verbs:** pools it, holds it, asks for it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** A hundred people each pay Carla 1. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Ask.** Ana brings the winning ticket and asks for the prize. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays Ana 60. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** Carla pays the school 40. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   5. **Time runs out.** Had nobody brought the winning ticket by the last day, the 60 would have stayed where it was and the claim on it died. *(nobody acts · neither)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the ticket, a claim on a chance, good in whoever's hand holds it; the draw.
- **d. Others:** Carla; the school.

#### 5.6 Alms (less sure of the workings)
Ben sits at the gate and asks. Ana gives him a coin. Each year she also gives a fortieth of what she holds to Carla, who shares it among the poor.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, counts it, pools it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben asks Ana for a coin. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana gives it. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays her yearly fortieth to Carla. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   4. **Move out of the middle.** Carla gives to those in need. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the duty; the count of what Ana holds, on which the fortieth is reckoned. Here it is money at rest that is counted, not money moving.
- **d. Others:** Carla the collector.

#### 5.7 A dowry or bridewealth (less sure of the workings)
Ben is to marry Ana. The elders of the two families agree what Ben's kin will give Ana's, over three years.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, promises it, delays it, splits it, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ana's elders name what is to be given. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ben's kin pay the first part, and later the others. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Move onward.** Ana's father shares what came among her uncles and brothers. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   4. **Move back.** If the marriage ends early, part is given back. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the bond between the families, which the payment makes and does not buy; the long-running account of what is still to come.
- **d. Others:** two whole families, givers on one side and sharers on the other. What is given is often a special kind of wealth that is not used to buy bread.

### Family 6: Machines

#### 6.1 An agent paying on someone's behalf
Ana tells her agent, a program: get me a seat on the morning train, 50 at most. Ben sells seats.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, earmarks it (the limit), passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Permit.** Ana lets her agent order payments from her account, up to 50, for a seat. *(words · request given in advance)*
   2. **Ask.** Ben's machine asks the agent for 40. *(words · request)*
   3. **Order.** The agent tells Ana's bank: pay Ben 40. *(words · request)*
   4. **Move.** 40 goes from Ana to Ben. *(moves · statement)*
   5. **Say it moved.** The agent tells Ana what it paid, and for what. *(words · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the limit and the purpose, which bind the agent and not the money.
- **d. Others:** the agent, which asks, orders and reports, and holds nothing.

#### 6.2 One machine paying another (less sure of the workings)
Ana's car draws current from Ben's charging post, and pays as the current flows.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, holds it (locked), promises it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** The post states its price for each unit. *(words · request)*
   2. **Set aside.** The car locks 20, so that the post can be sure of it and the car cannot spend it elsewhere. *(no move · neither)*
   3. **Move.** When the car is full, 12 of the 20 goes to the post in one payment; the other 8 is free again. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** every tick of the meter; and between the ticks, the car's signed notes saying "of the 20, so much is now yours", each replacing the last. These are promises; only the last one is ever turned into a move.
- **d. Others:** whatever keeps the lock; no person at either end.

#### 6.3 A relay or service paid for what it carries
Carla's relay carries Ana's messages. Ana puts 5 with Carla and each message carried uses a little of it. Carla also carries payments, and keeps a hundredth of each.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, holds it, passes it on.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana pays Carla 5 ahead. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Cancel against.** Each message carried is set against the 5; nothing moves. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
   3. **Move onward.** Ana sends Ben 100 through Carla; Carla passes on 99. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the count of messages; the balance left; the fee, which (as with the commission) is kept back and never paid as such.
- **d. Others:** Carla is the third party in both halves.

#### 6.4 A metered service paid as it is used
Ben supplies Ana's electricity. A meter counts; a bill comes each month.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, asks for it, delays it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben bills Ana 42 from the month's reading. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana pays 42. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the meter, which turns use into a debt minute by minute with nobody watching. With a meter paid ahead the order turns round: a "move" first, then the use is set against it ("cancel against") until nothing is left.
- **d. Others:** the meter.

#### 6.5 A standing instruction that pays by itself
Ana tells her bank once: on the first of every month, pay Ben 300.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, delays it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Order.** Ana gives the bank the instruction, once. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** On each first, 300 goes from Ana to Ben. Nobody asks and nobody acts that day. *(moves · statement)*
   3. **Say it did not move.** One month the account is empty; the bank tells Ana nothing was paid. *(words · neither)*
- **c. Without touching money:** what the 300 is for, which the instruction need not know.
- **d. Others:** the bank and its clock.

### Family 7: What the list missed

#### 7.1 Exchange of one money for another
Ana has 100 in the money of her own country and needs the money of Ben's. Carla keeps a booth.
- **a. Verbs:** converts it, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Ana hands Carla 100 of one money. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move.** Carla hands Ana 105 of the other. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the rate (a measure); Carla's margin, which is inside the rate and is never paid as such. If one side hands over first, the other owes until the second move.
- **d. Others:** Carla is the other party, not a third one.

#### 7.2 A remittance
Ana works abroad and sends 200 home to her brother Ben. Carla has a counter where Ana is; Dan has one in Ben's town.
- **a. Verbs:** passes it on, converts it, holds it, counts it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move into the middle.** Ana pays Carla 200 and a fee, for Ben. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   2. **Order.** Carla sends word to Dan: pay Ben 200. *(words · request)*
   3. **Say it moved.** Ana tells Ben the money is sent, and the word he must give. *(words · statement)*
   4. **Ask.** Ben gives the word at Dan's counter and asks for the money. *(words · request)*
   5. **Move out of the middle.** Dan pays Ben from his own drawer. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   6. **Cancel against.** Carla and Dan set this against payments going the other way; only now and then does a balance move between them. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
- **c. Without touching money:** what Carla owes Dan in the meantime (an account).
- **d. Others:** Carla and Dan. The coins Ben receives are not the coins Ana gave; for this payment nothing crossed the border.

#### 7.3 Rent
Ana lives in Ben's house and owes 300 on the first of each month.
- **a. Verbs:** promises it, delays it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** On the first, Ana pays Ben 300. Nobody asked; the lease and the calendar did. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Time runs out.** One month the first passes unpaid. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   3. **Ask.** Ben writes to demand it. *(words · request)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the lease; the debt, which is made new each month by time alone.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 7.4 A pledge or pawn
Ana needs 50 for a week. She leaves her ring with Carla, who gives her 50 and a ticket.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it (a thing, not money), returns it, forfeits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** Carla hands Ana 50. *(moves · statement)*
   2. **Move back.** Ana brings 55 and the ticket and takes her ring. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   3. **Time runs out.** Or she never comes: the last day passes, the ring is Carla's to sell, and the debt is over. *(nobody acts · neither)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the ring's value (a measure); the ticket; the forfeit rule. Here the thing held in the middle is not money at all.
- **d. Others:** whoever buys the ring.

#### 7.5 A ransom or a bribe (less sure of the workings)
Ben the clerk lets it be known that Ana's permit will come sooner with 100 in an envelope.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it; and a verb is missing: **hides it**.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** Ben hints; he never names it. *(words · request)*
   2. **Move.** Ana passes the envelope. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** everything else, on purpose. No slip, no account, no word after; both will say nothing moved. A ransom is the same with a threat in the asking and a stranger at the receiving end.
- **d. Others:** often a go-between, so that the two never meet. This is the one use whose point is that the move is never said.

#### 7.6 A float or a tab between people who settle later
Ana, Ben and Carla share a house and each buys things for all three. They keep a list on the kitchen door and settle at month's end.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, delays it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Cancel against.** At month's end the list is added up: most of what each owes the others cancels out. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
   2. **Ask.** The list shows Ben 12 short; Ana, who is 12 over, asks him. *(words · request)*
   3. **Move.** Ben pays Ana 12. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the list all month: thirty purchases among themselves and not one payment.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 7.7 A household's envelopes
Ana brings home 1,000 and deals it into envelopes: rent 400, food 300, school 100, kept 200.
- **a. Verbs:** earmarks it, counts it, holds it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Move.** On the first she pays the rent from the rent envelope. *(moves · statement)*
- **c. Without touching money:** all the dealing out. The money goes from Ana to Ana; no holder changes. When food runs short and she takes 20 from "school", she owes it to herself.
- **d. Others:** nobody; or a husband or child handed an envelope, which is then a move with a purpose riding on it.

#### 7.8 Paying by card in a shop (less sure of the workings)
Ana fills her tank at Ben's pump and pays 80 by card. Carla is Ana's bank and Dan is Ben's.
- **a. Verbs:** asks for it, holds it (set aside), promises it, delays it, passes it on, returns it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Ask.** The pump shows 80. *(words · request)*
   2. **Order.** Ana taps her card: an instruction to Carla, carried by the network. *(words · request)*
   3. **Set aside.** Carla sets 80 of Ana's aside and answers "approved". Ben is told he will be paid, not that he has been. *(no move · neither)*
   4. **Ask.** That night Ben's machine presents the day's sales for payment. *(words · request)*
   5. **Cancel against.** The banks set what they owe each other against what they are owed. *(claims end, no move · statement−)*
   6. **Move.** Only the difference moves between Carla and Dan. *(moves · statement)*
   7. **Move onward.** Dan pays Ben 79 and keeps 1. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
   8. **Taken.** Weeks later Ana disputes the charge; 80 is taken back from Ben and returned to her. *(moves, by another's power · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** "approved" (Carla's promise); the network's rules on who bears a dispute; the fees.
- **d. Others:** Carla, Dan and the network. Ana's money does not leave her at the pump: it is set aside there, and taken a day or two later.

#### 7.9 Money taken by order
A court has found that Ben owes Ana 500. It sends an order to Carla, who keeps Ben's money.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it; and a verb is missing: **takes it**.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Set aside.** On the order, Carla freezes 500 of Ben's: still his, and he cannot move it. *(no move · neither)*
   2. **Taken.** On a second order, Carla pays the 500 to Ana. *(moves, by another's power · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the judgment; Ben's right to object between the two orders.
- **d. Others:** the court and Carla. Ben neither asks nor pays; Ana does not ask Ben.

#### 7.10 A hoard
Ana keeps 500 under the floor for a year, against bad times.
- **a. Verbs:** holds it.
- **b. Moments:** none.
- **c. Without touching money:** the whole use. Its point is that nothing happens.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 7.11 A debt forgiven
Ben owes Ana 100 and cannot pay. Ana says: forget it.
- **a. Verbs:** none of the list fits. A verb is missing: **lets it go**.
- **b. Moments:** none.
- **c. Without touching money:** a claim ends, by a word. A book-keeper may still write it as though Ana had given Ben 100 and Ben had paid it straight back.
- **d. Others:** nobody.

#### 7.12 A bankruptcy (less sure of the workings)
Ben owes Ana 100 and Carla 300 and has 100 in all. Dan is appointed to wind things up.
- **a. Verbs:** counts it, holds it, splits it.
- **b. Moments:**
   1. **Time runs out.** Ben's debts fall due and are not paid. *(nobody acts · neither)*
   2. **Say it did not move.** It is declared that Ben cannot pay. *(words · neither)*
   3. **Set aside.** From that word, what Ben has is frozen: nobody may take it and he may not spend it. *(no move · neither)*
   4. **Move into the middle.** Dan gathers the 100 into his hands, for the creditors. *(moves, with a claim · statement+)*
   5. **Move out of the middle.** Dan pays Ana 25 and Carla 75. *(moves, with a link · statement+)*
- **c. Without touching money:** the list of who is owed what and in what order; the remaining 300 of claims, which are wiped out and never paid.
- **d. Others:** Dan; the court.


## The ways of touching money

The smallest list I could make holds **eleven kinds of moment**. One of them, the move, comes in six flavours that differ only by a claim or a link that rides on it. (Inferred from the 63 uses; that the list is the smallest possible, 55 to 75: another hand might merge Ask with Order, or split Move further.)

| # | Moment | In one line | The three things | Against the frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ask** | The one to be paid, or someone for them, asks that money move: a price at the till, a bill, a demand, a claim, an appeal. | words | a request |
| 2 | **Order** | The one who has the money tells whoever keeps or carries it to move it. | words | a request |
| 3 | **Permit** | The one who has the money lets someone else, or a clock, ask later and be obeyed. | words | a request given in advance |
| 4 | **Set aside** | Money is fenced off where it lies: it has not left its holder, and the holder can no longer use it. | none of the three cleanly | neither |
| 5 | **Move** | Money leaves one holder and reaches another. | money moves | told whole by "this has been paid", in its plain flavour |
| 6 | **Say it moved** | Payer, receiver or carrier says that a move happened, to someone who relies on the saying. | words | a statement |
| 7 | **Say it did not move** | Someone says that a move that was due, ordered or assumed did not happen. | words | neither: the statement turned round |
| 8 | **Make** | A promise is written and at once used as money; it did not exist before. | a claim is born | neither, or a statement with something added |
| 9 | **Unmake** | A promise used as money comes home and ends. | a claim ends | neither, or a statement with something added |
| 10 | **Cancel against** | Debts are set against each other and are settled; nothing moves. | claims end, words | a statement of payment with no move behind it |
| 11 | **Time runs out** | A day passes and nobody acts; because of it a claim changes. | nothing; a claim changes | neither |

The six flavours of the move:

| Flavour | What is added to "money moved" |
|---|---|
| **Move** (plain) | nothing: it is now the receiver's own |
| **Move into the middle** | a claim: the receiver holds it for someone else or until something happens |

## Where the families surprised me

All traced from the uses named; the word "surprise" is my reading.

1. **Paying per play pays nothing per play** (3.2). A play is counted and no more. The money moved once, weeks earlier or later, as a monthly sum; the rate per play is arithmetic done afterwards.
2. **A commission is never paid** (2.7, 6.3, 7.1). The agent's fee, the carrier's cut and the changer's margin are the part of a payment that was not passed on. There is no moment at which they move.
3. **Paying by card does not pay at the till** (7.8). At the pump Ana's money is set aside and Ben is given a promise. The move happens later, between two banks, for the difference of everything they owe each other that day.
4. **A remittance sends no money** (7.2). Word travels; the receiver is paid from a different drawer in a different money; the two counters cancel their debts and rarely move a balance.
5. **A forfeited deposit does not move** (4.3). The 200 lost to the dispute stays in the hands that already held it. What changed is whose it is.
6. **A budget touches nothing** (4.7), and neither do the envelopes (7.7). A great deal of what a group or a household "does with its money" is naming.
7. **A third of the uses that move money have nobody asking** (21 of 61, counted). The wage, the dividend, the rent, the standing instruction, the gift and the tip all move without a request on the day. Where there is a request, it is sometimes years old (the lease, the instruction).
8. **A savings club is a pool that is never anywhere** (1.10). In its plain form no pot exists at any moment; and it is a loan to the first taker and a saving for the last without either word being used.
9. **In an inheritance the owner makes no moment** (5.3), and in a wage tax the taxpayer never holds the tax (2.6). "Ana paid" is said of money that others moved.
10. **A deposit turns money into a promise, and the promise is then used as money** (1.3). After the first move, every "payment" Ana makes is a line changed in the keeper's book. The same thing is true, with no first move at all, of mutual credit (1.8), and of the voucher (1.9). The line between "money" and "a claim" is the line the three things are meant to keep apart, and in these three uses it does not stay still. (Inferred; that this is a real difficulty for the three-way split and not a quirk of my stories, 70 to 85.)
11. **The bribe is defined by a missing statement** (7.5). It is the one use whose point is that "this has been paid" is never said.
12. **The hoard is a use of money with no moment** (7.10).

## Whether the frame held

**Counted, over the 197 moments in the 63 uses:**

| Against the frame | Moments | Which |
|---|---|---|
| A request, plainly | 46 | Ask 38, Order 8 |
| A request with something added | 4 | Permit 4 |
| A statement that money moved, plainly | 66 | plain Move 55, Say it moved 11 |
| A statement with something added | 55 | into the middle 14, out of the middle 15, onward 14, back 7, taken 5 |
| A statement of payment with no move behind it | 6 | Cancel against 6 |
| Neither | 16 | Time runs out 9, Set aside 4, Say it did not move 3 |
| Neither, or a statement with something added (I cannot settle it) | 4 | Make 2, Unmake 2 |

So 112 of 197 moments are plainly one of the two; 65 more are one of the two with something added or taken away; 20 are neither or doubtful. **Counted over the eleven kinds:** three are requests (one of them given in advance), three are statements (one of them with no move behind it), three are neither, two are doubtful.

A move is a deed and not a sentence. I have counted a plain move as fitting the frame because "this has been paid" tells it whole; that is a choice of reading, and it is mine.

**The eight candidates the assignment named, one by one** (each inferred, from the uses cited):

1. **Money held by someone in the middle** (1.4, 1.5, 2.10, 4.9, 5.4). One of the two, with something added. Going in is a move, and "paid to Carla" is true. What is added is a claim: it is not Carla's. The holding itself is a state between two moves, not a moment. The addition cannot be dropped: in 2.7 nothing in the money shows whether Carla held Ben's 1,000 or passed on her own. Not a third kind: 75 to 88.
2. **Money locked so that nobody can move it yet** (6.2, 7.9, 7.12, the machine variant of 1.6). **A third kind**, where the money stays with its holder: nothing moved, nobody asked for a move, and yet money itself was touched, because what can be done with it changed. Where a lock is built by paying the money to a rule that then holds it, it can be told as a move into the middle whose middle is not a person. Third kind: 55 to 72.
3. **An authorisation that reserves money without moving it** (7.8). The same third kind as 2, with a promise to the shop attached. A defender of the frame would say it is two promises (the bank's to the shop, the cardholder's to the bank) and touches nothing; against that, the cardholder's money is really out of her reach from that second. Third kind: 55 to 70.
4. **A reversal.** Three different things hide under the word. A refund (2.5) is a new move back with a link: one of the two, 85 to 95. A chargeback (7.8) is a taking back: a statement plus by whose power, 70 to 85. A bounce (6.5, and a cheque that fails) is "it did not move after all", and that is not either sentence: it is the second sentence turned round. Neither: 60 to 75. Behind all three is something the frame's second sentence does not say: whether "this has been paid" is final or can still be undone.
5. **Money made and unmade** (1.8, 1.9). I cannot settle it. Told one way, issuing is "Ben handed Ana a voucher" and redeeming is "Carla handed it back": two statements of a move, with the addition that the thing moved is the mover's own promise. Told the other way, something that was not there now is, and later is not; no holder lost it and no holder gained it at the end. In mutual credit there is nothing at all behind the statement: the statement that Ana paid is the whole payment. Third kind: 40 to 60.
6. **A conversion** (7.1). One of the two, twice: two moves in two monies, each against the other, with a rate between them. What is added is the tie between the two legs. Not a third kind: 80 to 92.
7. **A netting** (7.6, 7.2, 7.8, 2.11). One of the two with something *removed*. The debts are truly settled and "this has been paid" is truly said, but nothing left any holder. This is the clearest case that "paid" and "moved" are not the same word: paid is about the debt, moved is about the money. Not a third kind, but it bends the second sentence: 70 to 85.
8. **A default** (1.11, 4.2, 4.8, 7.3, 7.12). Neither, and by its nature: the moment is that nobody did anything. It touches no money. It changes claims (the guarantor now owes, the fine grows, the pledge dies), and it usually needs someone to say it ("he has not paid") before anything follows. Neither: 85 to 95.

**Not on the assignment's list, and found by the map:**

- **Permit** (2.3, 3.2, 3.8, 6.1). A power to ask later. I have counted it as a request given in advance; it could as well be called a third kind of word, since on the day it is given it asks for nothing. Request with something added, not a third kind: 55 to 70.
- **Taken** (2.6, 4.2, 4.8, 7.8, 7.9). Money that moved without its bearer acting, sometimes without ever being in her hands. "This has been paid" fits; "by whom" does not have one answer.
- **Statements about money at rest.** The frame's two sentences are both about money in motion. Three things said in the map are about money that is not moving: "it is here" (the escrow keeper to the seller in 1.4; the count of holdings for alms in 5.6), "it is here for you" (every holding in the middle) and "it is here and cannot be moved" (Set aside). Only the last touches money; all three are sentences about money that are neither of the frame's two. (Inferred; that this is the one pattern behind the neithers other than time, 55 to 70.)

**In short** (inferred from all the above; 65 to 80): the frame holds for money in motion, on three conditions that the map keeps running into. The statement must be able to carry something more (for whom it is held, what it returns or passes on, by whose power). "Paid" must be allowed to be true when nothing moved. And a request must be allowed to be made long before, by someone else, or by a clock. It does not hold for money at rest that is fenced off, for the passing of a due date, or for "it did not move"; and whether it holds for money that is made and unmade is the question I could not close.

## The single click

"can I with a single click reward all the people that contributed to the service or good via MOR"

Described as a use, with the map's own moments only. Nothing here is proposed; it is the same exercise as for the 63 uses. Ana likes a record made by Ben, Carla and Dan, sold through Eve's shop. (Traced from 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 3.1, 3.5 and 6.1; inferred that these are the right uses to compose it from, 70 to 85.)

- The click is one **Order**, or one **Permit** given earlier and fired now. It is Ana's only moment. There may be an **Ask** before it (a price) or none (a tip, 3.3; pay what you want, 3.7).
- What follows is one of two shapes, or a mix. **Fanned at the source:** several plain **Moves**, one from Ana to each person (the end of 2.8). **Passed down:** one **Move** to the first receiver (or **into the middle**, if that receiver only holds it), then **Moves onward** from hand to hand (2.8, 2.9), each link keeping back its part without that part ever moving (2.7).
- Who "all the people" are, and their shares, is a rule and a count. It touches no money (2.8 c, 3.5 c).
- If the money pauses at any link, that link owes the next: a claim, waiting on a later **Move onward**. In practice links pay for many sales at once (2.9, 3.1), and then the tie between Ana's click and Dan's money is an account, not a path a coin took.
- If a link is itself owed something by the next, part may be settled by **Cancel against** (2.11).
- Each receiver, or each link, may **Say it moved**. Ana cannot see past the first move unless they do (4.5, moment 3).
- If one of the people cannot be reached, either the money waits **in the middle** for them, or **Time runs out** and a rule says whose it is (5.5).

So the click is one request, then between one and many statements that money moved, each of the onward ones carrying a link to what came in; plus rules and counts that touch nothing. Whether it is "one click" for Ana does not depend on how many moves follow.

## What this cannot tell

- The map is one model's description of practice, drawn from general knowledge. Nobody who runs a savings club, a card network or a probate office checked it. Nine uses are marked less sure.
- Each use is one story. A different ordinary story of the same use would shift marks in the table (the notes under 1.6, 1.10, 2.8, 3.8 and 6.4 show how), and every count would shift with them. The counts say how these 63 stories are built; they are not frequencies in the world.
- The list of uses is a list of names. Uses with no name in English, or none I know, are missing.
- The eleven kinds are a reading. The table is counted; the choice of columns is not.
- "Touches money itself" is the hardest phrase in the question and the map does not define it better than the three things do. Where money is itself someone's promise (1.3, 1.8, 1.9), the line between a move and a change in a claim depends on which promise one chooses to call money.
- Uses with no money in them at all (barter, labour exchanged for labour, a favour) are outside the map.
- **What I saw of MOR's Money, against the instruction to see none.** The ledger entry "What Money is" contains, between his two quoted sentences, the MOR lead's summary of the outline of Money draft 9: a sorting of its contents into what is likely grammar, what is now in Agreements, what is built on top and what belongs to another layer, with a handful of topic names. I read that passage once while reading the entry for his words, before I could stop. I opened no draft of Money, no code, no test and no other summary, and did not open Agreements draft 25. The map's moments and names were chosen from the uses, but I cannot show that the passage left no trace.
