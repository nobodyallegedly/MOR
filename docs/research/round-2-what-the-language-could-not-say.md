## 3. In plain words

### What the four turned out to be

1. **Counting across versions.** Two different troubles, seven entries each. On day three Stall forked its own line in seven Terms; nothing it built counted until it resolved them, after the close. In the rerun, seven makers wrote a third version that names the *first* version as the one it replaces. I checked the hosts' ground for refusing the seven: their deals *do* have a third version, lodged before the close, that names a rail and a place; whether it was in force is **unchecked**, because it turns on straight split draft 7, which no verifier held. The eighth refused entry stands on a different ground. The money went where draft 25's reading would send it, for a reason the count did not give.
2. **"Exactly these acts".** One list of acts gives three hashes by three honest readings of "ids end to end"; four keepers read one feed at four moments. The core has no single byte form for "this set of acts", and its own text hands "I hold these" to a cMIP (Envelopes rule 28).
3. **"Unchecked".** Every Terms that named an in-force cMIP was "unchecked" for the kit; every count that paid money was a hand applying cMIPs from their texts. The texts say what a verifier answers when it lacks a cMIP; none says what a hand-reading is. The count's 18 payouts and the bank's 14 credits agree exactly once the four 0-sat payouts are set aside: there is no "I tried to pay you and could not" in the data.
4. **The thief's rotation.** The bytes show a valid rotation by a holder of the chain key, which counts; old-key acts after it are void; a new identity with no link to the old; two texts by others speaking for it; no witness act. One window (clusters) played thief, victim and both vouchers. Read as staged, what changes is what the vouching and the "recovery" tell us about people; what the bytes say does not change.
