### What D3 does to OI-033

**In his words:** "Agreed. Only an agreement can keep you into something, and agreements have their own way out. But default should always offer a way out"

**What it leaves unsaid.** None of this is in his words, in the lean or in the line:

- **What the exit is, as an act.** Draft 25, G4.9, says no act ends Terms, or an agreement, without replacing them. Whether leaving is an update one signer makes alone, or an act of a new kind, is not said.
- **What leaving does to the others**: whether the agreement goes on without the one who left, and who is then "everyone who signed the original".
- **What it does to the leaver's own part**: a share, a role filled, rights earned. The lean says leaving never erases "what is owed"; it does not say what is kept.
- **Who "a signer" is.** A party bound without signing in its own hand (through an agent, through an agreement's signature, or by an update it did not sign) is not spoken of (OI-079, OI-028).
- **A receiver who resigns.** The MOR lead's set-out names it as part of the problem ("a receiver cannot resign"). Neither his words nor the line come back to it.
- **How Terms switch the exit off**, and what "said plainly before anyone signs" asks of the Terms or of a client.
- **"Agreements have their own way out."** Whether Terms that keep someone in must offer a way out, or the sentence only observes that they can, is not said. The line reads "those Terms have their own way out".
- **"Always."** His sentence says the default should "always offer a way out"; choice (c) lets Terms switch it off. Read together they agree (the default always has it; Terms may say otherwise); read alone, "always" says more.
- **A lock, a fork and a delegation.** Whether the default exit holds under an update condition that answers valid for nothing (OI-027), in an agreement held by a fork (OI-064), and for a delegator or an agent (OI-074, OI-078), is not said.

## The full list

**How to read it.** The numbers are version 1's; OI-235 to OI-240 are new and sit at the end of their cluster. Each item ends with a line "Now", which is one of: **closed** (his words answer it; the decision and the words are given), **changed** (a decision moves it without answering it: a new home, a new owner, a narrower or a wider question), **still open**, **can't tell** (with what is missing), or **closed before 17 November** (with the section of `history.md` that says so).

- An item that is **not** marked [thin] has one line that repeats version 1's coding in short. Its quotes and its source are in version 1, unchanged.
- An item marked **[thin]** was known to version 1 from one line of section 8.4. It has what it asks in full, and the six lines again, with the section of `history.md` read for it.
- **"Near it"** in a "Now" line marks a decision that comes close and does not reach the item. It is a note; the item is counted as still open.

**Four readings of mine, so that the coding can be redone if they are wrong.**

- *His words, and the choice they answer.* Most of his answers are "Agreed" to a choice the MOR lead set out. I read the answer together with the choice it answers, both quoted from the exchange, and count that as his words. I do not count the arrived-at line. Where only that line reaches an item, the item is "changed" and says "rests on the MOR lead's reading".
