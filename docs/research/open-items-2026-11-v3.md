### How the items were coded

**This whole coding is inferred.** No source labels an item as behaviour or as record. Each of the 231 items not closed has one code, with its ground, in the full list.

- **B, behaviour**: the item asks the text to settle how people should treat each other in a deal: what someone owes, stays answerable for, must be told, must be paid, may keep, or is protected from. Test: could two groups of honest people reasonably want opposite answers for their own deals?
- **R, record**: the item asks what the acts say and how a verifier reads them. Test: two honest verifiers holding the same acts must give the same answer, whatever the parties wanted.
- **N, neither**: bytes, tools, wording, the record of decisions, another layer's gap.
- **B and R**: the item asks both, and is split. It is counted apart, in neither B nor R.

Four readings of mine, so that the coding can be redone if they are wrong.

- *Both tests can pass.* Any default passes the first test, since people could want another default. So the definition decides, not the test alone: an item is B only where what it asks is a duty, an entitlement or a protection between people. Who must sign, what replaces what and which answer word are R even where a default is at stake.
- *A rule that moved.* An item whose rule D6, D8 or D9 sent to another layer is coded by what it asks, so most of cluster 6 is R. An item whose cMIP check is L is N (another layer's gap), unless it asks a behaviour question (one does: OI-158).
- *A limit is not a rule.* A stated cost that says what no record can do (make someone honest, make someone pay) is N. One of them is about being paid, and I could not place it (OI-115).
- *Seeing and telling.* "Must be told" and "is protected from" are in the definition, so the items about who may read a report beyond its leg are B. 9 of the 20 B items are of that kind.
