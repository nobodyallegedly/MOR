## How it was done

Five readers (subagents), each given one range of one text, the list of vector cases and the Rust core, listed every sentence that decides a verdict ("MUST", "counts only", "is invalid", "is void", "is unknown", "is no purchase", "settles nothing" and the like), skipping commentary. Each row says: the line in the text, the sentence, the verdict it decides, whether a vector case exercises it, what the Rust core does (the function, and "follows", "differs" or "not built"), and the kind:
- **vector**: the core follows the text and no case shows it; "+facts" or "+question" where a case needs a fact or a question the vector format does not have yet;
- **question**: the core does something else, or the text is unclear on a point a verdict turns on;
- **no vector**: client conformance, a stated cost, or a fact outside the core.

I then built the most important "vector" rows as cases, money, exits and authority first, and checked the questions I could. Line numbers are those of the texts on this branch.

## Questions confirmed by running the core

*S7g: all three fixed in the core, the text being clear, each with a vector showing the case and one showing the case that stays as it was (`docs/s7g-report.md`).*

1. **A rotation built on a pending rotation answers invalid** (Identity 172, everyday check 2, and rule 28 at 328: "acts under its new key are pending too"; the text says pending, "never valid and not invalid"). Ran: a genesis at a home, rotation R1 not yet receipted, rotation R2 on it: R1 pending, R2 **invalid**. Once R1's receipt arrives, R2 can count: an invalid answer that changes with what is held, against "validity is fixed by the bytes". In the core: `chain.rs`, `status_cx`.
2. **Terms nobody signed "claim" a work** (Agreements 466 to 470: "a publication carrying a work some agreement claims"; rule 1: an agreement exists once every party signed). Ran: a stranger publishes terms, signed by nobody, staking Ana's film; a Money-only payment to Ana for her own film is then **no purchase, owed back**. In the core: `view.rs`, `claimed`, which reads any held terms. Anyone could turn every Money-only sale of another's work into a refund owed.
3. **One payment counted toward two debts** (Money 212, rule 12: "Receipts or claims of one payment that name different purposes … MAY share it only if they name the same batch … Otherwise a verifier holding both counts neither"). Ran: two claims of one payment (the rail's answer: one payment), each fulfilling a different debt of 60, no batch: each debt shows **60 paid**. In the core: `view.rs`, `paid_toward_if`, which reads only the records naming the debt asked about.

### Questions

3. **Rotation on a pending rotation.** The text says an act bound to a rotation that does not count yet is pending, and invalid only once it can no longer count (172, 328); "Invalid is only for a rule the verifier can apply and sees fail" (444). The core (`status_cx` chain.rs:2072, `beyond` chain.rs:1911) calls R2, built on a still-pending R1, `invalid`, and every act bound to R2 too, and both turn `valid` when the receipts arrive. An owner who rotates twice before the first receipt sees later acts called invalid, and a reader trusting that verdict goes dark.
4. **Finality through an unrelated next rotation.** The text: final only when the n+1 rotation counts "under the home rule the homeless rotation declared" (633). The core (chain.rs:1332) accepts any next act that counts, including a homeless or escape rotation counting through other new homes. A provisional homeless rotation (old homes gone by absence statements) then becomes immune to a late objection from a living old home, which is the exit rule 49 was meant to keep closed to a thief.
5. **Homeless rotations of a self-hosted identity.** The text: an escape still needs step 5 (receipts from the new homes, 613), and a self-hosted home is never gone (591), so an unendorsed homeless rotation should not count by step 4. The core counts any homeless rotation the self-host serves as a normal rotation (`Operator::Own` vote, chain.rs:1421), with no new-home receipt and without an endorsement. It changes who wins a self-hosted contest and what "final" means there. Whether "serves" covers a homeless rotation is what to settle.
6. **Signing-key length.** The text: a key of a founding scheme with the wrong length makes the act invalid (731). `signing_key` (identity.rs:297) checks no length, so a rotation installing a 31-byte Schnorr key is valid and counts. The owner is then locked out of everyday acts by a rotation the text calls invalid, and a second implementation that checks lengths would resolve a different chain.
7. **Unknown or pending acknowledgements.** The text: an answer resting on what the verifier cannot tell is unknown, "never valid, void or invalid" (442), and "disputed … waits on void as well" (446). `acknowledged` (chain.rs:1650) counts only `Valid`, so an act whose only acknowledgement has unknown or pending status is `void`, and becomes `disputed` when the acknowledger's chain arrives. That is the void/disputed flip the text forbids. Payments and other relied-on acts rescued by rule 34 are affected.
8. **What "cannot tell" covers.** The text names "cannot tell" whenever whether a rotation counts cannot be told (391). The core gives it only for an objection of unknown status. A receipt whose operator's chain is not held is treated as absent (chain.rs:1492): the stop is `pending` and the rotation `Pending`. Unknown absence statements, unknown cosignatures and pending objections are also treated as absent. Two verifiers holding different operator chains name different reasons. Say which is meant.
9. **A contesting receipt is not a contested position.** Receipt check 6 says such a receipt "blocks nothing" and is shown as a dispute (296). The list at 69 gives only rules 40, 44 and 4 as the causes of a contested position. The core makes it the stop reason (chain.rs:1438, 1378): `contested` where the text gives `pending`. Acts bound to the rotation are pending either way, so only the "chain" answer differs.
10. **Rule 40's "none meets the rule".** With three homes and threshold 2, one home for R, one for R′ and one silent, the core says `pending`. Rule 40 and the definition at 69 say "contested" when competing rotations each miss the rule. The text does not say whether a silent home that could still decide makes it pending, and the order at 393 puts contested before pending, so a verdict turns on it.
11. **A malformed "mor" successor.** The text (809): it names no MOR identity, and no client follows it. The core reports it as another protocol's succession (`Elsewhere`, chain.rs:922), which a client would hand to a cMIP. It should probably be "none", or "declared, never followed".
12. **Dishonesty with no rotation of the home.** Rule 45: proven dishonest when the conflict "survives the home's own rotations". The core needs each receipt kept by an operator rotation or cosigned (chain.rs:1449), so an unaudited home that never rotates is never proven dishonest and stays only "contested". Read literally ("survives" no rotations), every unaudited conflict would be proof at once. The text should say which.
13. **Repeated auditors.** Check 5 compares the threshold with "the number of auditors". The core counts distinct auditors, so `[A, A]` with threshold 2 makes the genesis invalid. Cosignatures are counted per auditor too, so the core's reading is the only satisfiable one, but the text does not say so.
14. **"Exactly that entry".** For objections, absence statements and endorsements (786), the core accepts an act whose `objects` contain the entry among others. If "exactly" means a single entry, such an objection should not void anything.
15. **Endorsement binding.** The text: bound to the act just before the homeless rotation, or (after a chain signature) the act that set the key (623). The core accepts any genesis or rotation of the identity carrying the same key (chain.rs:1779). This matters only if a rotation re-installs an earlier key. Low priority.
16. **Declarations read as "ever declared".** Identity says the latest entry counts and null removes it (243). Text rule 14's check (chain.rs:2002) treats an identity as a collective if any past state declared a founding agreement. Whether Agreements means "once a collective, always": I cannot tell from this text. Flag it for the Agreements reviewer.

### The question rows

- **Row 1 (lines 116, 124): which rail Module counts.** The text: a receipt counts only if its field 0 names the rail Module of the very pointer or vault entry it was paid to, and otherwise counts for nothing. The core: `payment_counts_rec` (view.rs:8054) and `paid_toward_if` (view.rs:7947) read only `rail_valid`'s "paid to pointer P", never whether P's rails name field 0's Module. A collective's receipts are checked against *any* of its pointers (`rail_not_accepted`, view.rs:4467), and a person's not at all. If the caller's rail answer is meant to carry this check, the text should say that the verifier relies on it. As things stand, a vector with a receipt naming Module X, said to be paid to a pointer naming only Y, gets "counts" from the core and "counts for nothing" from the text.
- **Row 2 (line 120): the payment cMIP.** The text makes "the rail Module implements the payment cMIP the agreement names" a condition of counting. The core leaves it entirely to the caller (`rail_valid`'s doc, view.rs:106). Same question as row 1: whose check is it, and should a vector be able to show it?
- **Row 3 (line 122): a wrong cross-reference.** The new sentence says the grantee's pointer "counts where rule 12 counts it". In this draft rule 12 is "One payment, one count", and the pointer that counts is rule 1, so this looks like the old numbering. The code does what rule 1 says (`pointer_counts`, view.rs:8069). A reader following the reference literally finds nothing to apply.
- **Row 6 (line 126): a refund to the refund rail.** The text: "the others [conditions] apply to it as to any payment", which includes rule 29's "as the chain stood for it" and the payment cMIP. The core (`repaid`, view.rs:10160) checks only that the money reached the named refund rail and is within the payer's vault limit in force now. A vault lowered by a later rotation therefore changes an earlier refund's standing, and no lock-change protection is read. This matters for refunds owed back on a collective's closing.
- **Row 26 (line 212): one proof, two purposes.** The text: records of one payment naming different purposes count for neither unless they share a batch (the freeze scenario says "one proof claimed against two obligations counts for neither"). The core: `paid_toward_if` (view.rs:7947) looks only at the records naming the obligation asked about, so two claims (or two receipts) of one payment naming obligations A and B each count in full toward their own, and the money counts twice. `claim_overruled` (view.rs:8749) reads only receipts, and no code reads `batch` here. This is a money verdict and a double count, the most important row.
- **Row 29 (lines 195, 197, 372): previous hops.** Disagreement on the previous hop, and the whole route of rules 24 to 28 (row 51), are not built: field 6 of a receipt and field 10 of a claim are decoded and never read. Either they are left for later, and the step plan should say so, or both implementations will read them freely.
- **Row 37 (line 169): grant-key receipts in a refund.** The text: a receipt under a revoked grant, shown disputed, "signs no receipt"; and one past its grant's reach or limits counts for nothing (line 167's italic, Agreements rule 80). `paid_toward` and `receipts_for` use `money_counts` (view.rs:926), which reads backing. `repaid`'s receipt arm (view.rs:10177) uses `self.valid(&r.id)`, which reads reach only (view.rs:895), so a payer's grant-key receipt signed after its grant's revocation still shows a refund repaid.
- **Row 43 (line 316): "pending".** The text says that until the rail answers, such a payment is "pending (rule 8)". Rule 8 (line 179) says that whether it is a purchase is "unknown". The core answers `Unknown`, or `Unrecorded` where another record of the payment names another claim (view.rs:9758). The vow cases use "pending" for a different state (a sale awaiting confirmation). One word should be chosen, so that two clients answer alike.
- **Row 45 (line 318): a tip naming a claim.** The text: a payment to a pointer or toward an obligation "is no purchase and names nothing". `purchase_read` returns "not a payment for a work" only when field 9 is absent; with field 9 present it judges the claim without asking what field 5 names. Cannot tell the final answer without building the case (it depends on `sellers` and `recorded`), but nothing in the path says "no purchase" for a tip.
- **Row 50 (line 350): payout fees.** Cannot tell: `Unpaid`'s doc (view.rs:6533) says the plan's maximum fee "is not readable, its format open", yet formats.rs:4011 decodes a rail fee within the plan's maximum. Whether a payout short by an allowed fee counts as discharged needs reading the split-check code. I did not find where it is decided.
- **Row 51 (lines 372-386): routes.** Not built (see row 29). Rule 27 decides an open obligation of a conversion hop, which is money, so it matters once conversion services exist.

### Questions

**Row 3, line 469 (who must name a claim).** The text says a payment for a publication carrying a work that "some agreement claims" must name a claim. An agreement is terms plus the signatures its rules require. `claimed` (v:9040) counts any held terms act that stakes the work, even one nobody signed. So anyone can publish unsigned terms staking someone else's work and turn every Money-only payment for it into "no purchase, owed back". It decides money; the text should say whether a draft "claims".

**Row 4, line 478 (a line carrying none of the claims).** `purchase_read` accepts as the line any held fork, closing or release (v:9776-9782), without checking that it ends a holder of this work's stake or releases this work. On a request rail with no collective seller, the payment is then a purchase (v:9826). The text says such a line carries none of the agreement's claims, so no purchase.

**Row 14, line 370 (wrong payout receipt).** The text says a seller's payout receipt is wrong "once the seller has signed" the transfer. `wrong_payout_receipt` (owning.rs:404) also needs the buyer's signature act. It never checks the over-sale, so an over-sale, which "confers nothing" (391), still turns the seller's receipts into debts owed to that buyer in `service_account` (v:11619).

**Row 15, line 366 (splits follow transfers).** "A split service MUST follow" the transfers, and must pay the stakes "as currently held" (921). `split_unjudged` (v:10988) judges payouts against field 7's holders only. A split that pays the buyer after a completed transfer shows as a mismatch, and one that keeps paying the seller does not.

**Row 24, line 420 (co-owners' offer not signed by every party).** In a deal, `offer` puts a missing party signature among `problems` (selling.rs:173), so the offer does not count and any payment under it is "no purchase" (v:9728). Lines 599 and 611 suggest an offer not signed under the claim's agreement is a valid act, shown as outside it. Which applies when one co-owner alone signs the offer decides whether the buyer is owed a refund.

**Row 27, lines 429-436 (deal complete, refund when nothing is delivered).** No verdict reads the delivery of the content key, or a receipt naming the offer for a public-key publication. A payment that receives neither creates no refund obligation in the core. Key delivery is private, but the receipt half could be shown.

**Row 30, lines 440 and 444 (refunds owed and closing).** Apart from broken-stretch payments, the core neither lists refunds owed (no purchase, past a deadline, over-sale) nor lets them block a closing. `open_debts` reads only Money obligations (v:8972), and `refund_ended` is read only in `owed_back` (v:10045). Line 444 implies that a refund not yet ended does block a closing.

**Row 39, lines 84 and 731 (pointer check).** `pointer_check` (v:10566) implements the test, with "one service carries every address and every vault entry". But no verdict calls it: a payment to an owner's own pointer that bypasses the service still counts in `payment_counts` and `purchase`. The text says that pointer "counts, for Agreements, only if…". If that is meant to decide whether such a payment counts, the core does not apply it. If it is only for showing (741), the text should say so.

**Row 41, line 664 (backup only after a failed attempt).** `designated_grantees` (v:8117) lists field 29's grantees unconditionally, so a payment to a backup's pointer counts with no failure stated. The vector named "after the first attempt failed" states no failure and passes the same way. Only the split check reads `attempts_failed` (v:10794). Without that fact it then flags a backup's split as breaking the plan, where line 777 (italic) says unknown.

**Row 43, lines 675 and 676 (cannot tell).** A grant key may sign a layer's incoming share only "where the rail pays an account an owner holds". `split_key_problem` (v:5145) accepts it on any `rail_valid` answer and does not check where the rail paid.

**Row 44, line 684 (inner deal signs by its version in force).** `line_signed` (parties.rs:56) accepts the parties of any version of the inner line, up to the one in force: "carrying what it signed", as the build's mechanic. With v1 = {A, B} and v2 = {A, C} in force, A and B signing the outer deal is enough for the core; the text asks for A and C.

**Row 45, line 691 (unknown beyond an unheld layer).** A party whose terms the verifier does not hold is read as an identity with no signature act, so the agreement "does not exist". The loop walk skips versions it does not hold. The text says such a verifier answers unknown.

**Row 48, line 816 (non-summing split).** A split whose payouts do not sum exactly is "invalid", so no rule counts it. The core records `sums: Some(false)` but still lets the split match its receipt (`split_names`, v:11517). The receipt is then not an "unsplit" open obligation (930/952).

**Row 54, line 875 (unfilled role share).** The default, "to the stakes, in proportion", is not checked. A split paying an unfilled role elsewhere is not shown as a deviation.

**Row 56, line 905 (maximum fee).** `max_rail_fee` (plan key 6) is decoded (open_formats.rs:622) and never read in judging. `service_account` (v:11627) counts any shortfall as unpaid, even within the cap, or with no cap where the receiver bears the fee. That contradicts "where it states none, there is no cap" (903) and the client flag at 907.

**Row 35, lines 565 and 578 (free sale confirmation).** Line 565 asks the buyer's act to acknowledge both the act about the vow and its own signature on the offer. Line 578 says "any valid act of the buyer's acknowledging such an act". The core follows 565 (vows.rs:530). The two sentences give different states for an act that acknowledges only the vow's act.

**Row 62, line 43 (invalid, not unknown).** An Agreements act out of format (two cMIPs for one task, a plan paying a non-owner in the rest) makes `terms()` return `Err(Check)`. `vectors.rs` maps every Err of `agreement` to "unknown". A vector for rows 49 and 61 would then publish "unknown" where the text says invalid.

**Row 63, line 296 (claim priority).** Priority between conflicting claims "where they meet", by an anchor or a keeper of an agreement both belong to, is not built. `work_owners` says only `contested`.

**Row 65, line 1036 and line 112 (standing).** Standing includes a holder of a stake the act affects, a keeper, an arbitrator or a verifier. `arbitrated` (v:9487) and `judge_request` (owning.rs:440) accept only a party who signed the version. So a stake holder who is no party (a buyer by transfer) cannot start a judge's period.

### Questions

- **Row 9 (1148), "Obligations before that point settle under the parent."** The text does not say what a verifier decides from this sentence: which version's terms judge a debt, or a payment toward one, that was made before a clone completed. I found no code that names it. So I cannot tell whether the core does it, and no vector can be written until the verdict it decides is stated.
- **Row 15 (1175), what counts as "relied on".** The text says "once any party's or keeper's later act relies on a complete version", and lists "a later version cloning it". Line 1204 says a draft "is nobody's version". `deal_version_relied` (view.rs:1071-1104) counts any valid act whose parent or naming points to the version: a terms act with that parent (a draft signed by one party included), a split, any receipt or claim naming it, whoever signed it. It does not check that the relying act came before the disowning rotation. This matters because a version's completeness decides which version is in force for purchases and splits. As built, one party alone can keep a version complete against the other's rotation, by signing a draft clone of it.
- **Row 38 (1310), outcome 2.** The text says that, with no stake successor named, "outcome 1 or 2 redistributes or transfers" the absent holder's stake, and this is one of "the only advance permissions that move a stake without its holder". `moved_by_abandonment` (view.rs:1435) reads outcome 1 only. Its doc comment says "Outcome 2 is not read", and `docs/f270-build.md:222` notes it. A clone carrying out an outcome-2 transfer stays a draft until the vanished holder signs. That is the case the rule exists for, and it touches money and exits.
- **Row 50 (1376), the roster for a sale or release.** The text counts the owners' consent "where the collective's line placed them …, the roster at that line (rule 94)". `owners_consent` (view/owning.rs:597-640) reads field 7 of the collective's *current* agreement (`self.current`), with no stake transfers, and accepts a signature placed anywhere. Its doc comment calls this "a reading of the build: the act has no line of its own to read a roster at". So whether a past release or sale counts can change with a later clone or transfer. A buyer of part of the stake in itself is never asked.
- **Row 67 (1663), "or a version it descends from by complete clones".** `count_areas` (view.rs:4789-4794) counts a holder only if it has a signature act naming the act *and* has signed some version of the agreement's lineage. The alternative the text gives, a signature on a version the act descends from, has no code path I could find. Cannot tell: it may concern only acts that are themselves versions, which are judged by marks instead. If so, the sentence needs no code, but the text should say which acts it means.
- **Row 78 (1837 with 1138), "and that rule is how its holders consent".** The text reads as if the holders' rule is one way to consent: line 1138 offers either each holder's signature "or" holders meeting the rule. `owners_powers` (tiers.rs:300-313) puts `[5, stake]` in the required powers whenever a stake with a holders' rule by share is paid less or issued. `clone_static` (view.rs:1162) then makes invalid any clone whose mark omits it, even one every holder signed. It also asks `[5, stake]` where every holder of the stake is a member. A clone signed by everyone could be invalid only for its mark, so it matters which reading the text means.

### Questions

- **Row 5 (line 2150), a member key after the holder's rotation.** The text says a rotation disowning the signature that named a member key leaves the key valid for the collective, as any member signature the collective's line placed. `member_key_problem` (view.rs:849) and `valid` (view.rs:902) require that signature to be `Status::Valid` under Identity, so the key stops counting. This matters for money: a year of receipts signed with a Money holder's member key would stop counting when the holder rotates.
- **Row 102 (line 3434), a grantee's acceptance after its rotation.** The text places the acceptance as a member's signature and keeps it once an act of the collective acknowledges it. `accepted` (view.rs:4970-4976) reads only Identity's verdict (`Status::Valid`), so a disowned acceptance, or one a keeper rescued as disputed, ends the grant. The grantee could undo every act of its key by rotating, which the text forbids.
- **Row 28 (line 2321), a succession clone placing the successor elsewhere.** The text now settles it: "invalid (a false mark)". `plan_at` (view.rs:4395-4401) still returns `Unsettled` ("where they go is not written, Flaw B14"), and a unit test (`what_b14_leaves_open_is_refused`) asserts that. Two verifiers would answer unknown where the text says invalid.
- **Row 29 (line 2316), what "in its place" means in some cases.** The text does not say what happens with a successor who is already a party, several successors to a key held by one, or a party holding a custodian or recovery role. `plan_open` (view.rs:5845) refuses these as `Unsettled`. Either the text needs a sentence on these cases, or "the party out and its seat successors in … in its place" already decides them. This is for Nobody, allegedly, to say.
- **Row 25 (line 2305), a living party's own trigger.** The text lets a party trigger its own plan while alive. `plan_at` (view.rs:4366-4377) accepts only a declaration removing the party's voice as the trigger, and no act format writes a party's own trigger. So an automatic seat can never pass while the party is alive, and nothing in the formats says how it would.
- **Row 19 (line 2235), adopting by paying.** The text gives three ways to adopt: acknowledging, citing, paying on it. `acked` (view.rs:5296-5313) reads only acknowledgements and the citing act's history. Whether a payment by the collective's own key whose `fulfils` names the act also cites it depends on Money's `objects`. I cannot tell from the code whether "pays on it" is ever read, and a counterparty who was paid could still find the act void.
- **Row 80 (lines 2605 and 4004), a payer who gave no address.** Field 4 may leave open money owed back only to a payer "who gave no address", meaning no payee pointer, vault, or refund rail on a claim of theirs. `closing_inner` (view.rs:7567-7570) checks only that a notice to the payer is done and listed in `notices_lapsed`. The doc comment (view.rs:221-228) folds "no address given" into the caller's statement. So a collective could notice a payer who does have a pointer, let the deadline pass, and close. The core can check pointers and vaults itself.
- **Row 109 (line 3502), liveness in a collective.** `liveness` (view/owning.rs:465) does not check that the collective placed the act, though its comment says it does. No core verdict reads it today (the reference absence-proof module does not call it). It matters only once an absence-proof cMIP relies on the core's answer.

### Questions

1. **Who is a collective, for Text rule 14 (S7f's Identity question 16).** Plain words: a text act may carry a pointer to its signer's own chain only if the signer is a collective. The core counts an identity as a collective if any act of its chain ever declared a founding agreement. So a collective that removed its declaration stays one, and a person who declares a founding agreement in a rotation, even terms nobody signed, becomes one for Text. Precisely: Text rule 14 (line 61) says "where its signer is a collective" and does not say what makes one. Its latitude ("a verifier accepts, as predecessor, any of the collective's own records or links of its identity chain") is about the predecessor, not about who is a collective. So **rule 14 does not settle it.** Agreements rule 56 (Agreements 1526) and "Collectives in the identity chain" (3806): "A collective's genesis declares its founding agreement". Identity, "Which value counts" 1 (243): the latest declaration counts, and null means the kind is not declared. Code: `text_pointer`, chain.rs:2058 (`res.states.iter().any(..)`). The Agreements view itself reads the genesis (`collective_of` view.rs:6801, `founding_of` view.rs:6818). Ran: (i) a collective whose rotation sets kind 0 to null, its text naming that rotation: `valid`; (ii) an individual whose genesis declares nothing and whose rotation declares a founding agreement, its text naming the rotation, or its genesis: `valid`. Options: (a) **the genesis decides** (rule 56): a collective is an identity whose genesis declares a founding agreement, for good; a later null does not unmake it, and a rotation does not make one. (b) **the declaration in force**: a collective while the latest kind 0 at the act's binding is not null (Identity's rule for values). (c) **ever declared** (the core today). Lean: (a). It matches rule 56 and the Agreements view's own reading, and it keeps rule 14's commentary true: "an individual still chains its own texts by a cMIP's companion act". Cost: (a) is one line in `text_pointer`; case (ii) becomes invalid. (b) makes a collective's pointers fail after a null, a state Agreements never describes. (c) lets anyone become "a collective" for Text by declaring terms nobody signed, and then attach texts to its own chain.

### Questions

1. **A specification published through a grant key (row 10, line 75).** In plain words: a collective cannot sign with an identity key of its own hand; its acts are signed by grantees' grant keys. Rule 3 says a specification counts as its creator's "only when published in an act signed by the creator named inside it". The core answers "unknown" for any publication signed by a grant key (`specification_claim`, chain.rs:972, from Identity's `scoped`), even one Agreements calls backed (probe: the case "an act of a grant key within its reach, the grant accepted: backed" asked as a specification claim gives unknown). So no collective can ever be shown as a creator, and fee terms naming it read an unknown claim. Options: (a) a grant-key publication Agreements backs (rule 80) claims the specification for the grantor; unknown at an Identity-only verifier; (b) only the creator's own signing key claims it: a collective publishes through a member, who is then the creator; (c) as the core does now, unknown everywhere. Lean (a): it is what "signed by" means elsewhere for a collective. Cost of (a): `specification_claim` needs the Agreements view, or a second function there; of (b): a text line in Development; of (c): collectives cannot author.
2. **"`amount` carried as given" (row 20, line 325).** In plain words: a fee of a fixed amount per payment is written `[1, amount]`; the decoding step says the amount is "carried as given". The core and the Python check accept any item there (a text, a bare number), so `[1, "ten"]` is a specification. Read the other way, `amount` is Money's `[unit, value]` and anything else is "a fee … given against its rule" (401), so no specification. A spec hash's existence turns on it, so two implementations could differ. Options: (a) unchecked, as now; (b) checked as Money's `amount`. Lean (a), since step 4 lists what it checks and sets `amount` apart; the text should say "any item". Cost: (a) a word; (b) one check in `from_value` and in `check.py`, and fee amounts in a unit a verifier may never read.
