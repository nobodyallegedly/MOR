## In plain words

**Two versions of one deal (QF1, QF2, QF3).**
- Once a fork is settled, a new version on the dropped branch that **cites the settlement** plainly came after it and changes nothing. One that **cites nothing** may have come before the settlement, which then did not see it: the deal is **tangled** again and goes back to its last agreed version, until the parties settle cleanly. Nobody can do this alone: such a version needs every party's signature. This finishes Fable's item 4, which had stopped at QF1.
- A settling version now **names every tip it drops**, not just one (terms field 26 is a list), and the parties of all the branches sign it. So a tangled deal can always be settled: everyone sees every option, everyone signs the choice.
- To make this exact, the build reads a settlement as **clean** when every complete version of the deal is either something it saw (its own line, the tips it names, what it cites) or something made after it. The first clean settlement holds. This is a reading for you to confirm (below).
- The deal's reference version now **says which of its judges settles forks** (new terms field 27, one of its arbitrators or verifiers). Where it says none, nobody settles, and the deal waits on its reference.

**Splits (QF4).** A deal's split with **no number**, or with **a number another split also carries**, is a deviation that breaks the plan, like a reset of the tally chain. A missing number in the sequence (a gap) stays an alarm only, since that split may just not have reached this holder yet.

**A broken collective (QF5, QF6).**
- **Money owed back is repaid like any debt**: by a payment that names the payment it repays, shown either by the payer's receipt or by the collective's own claim. Once repaid in full it no longer blocks a closing.
- **An old stepping down is spent** once the member holds that area again, exactly as an old resignation is spent once the member comes back.

**A relay's delivery record (F184).** A relay's signed record that it served a file now counts as evidence for the relay's share **only when the payer's claim for that payment acknowledges it**, and only for the file the payment was for. A relay cannot pay itself by saying it delivered.

**Wording only.** Production's definition of a Module no longer lists "a unit" (a unit is a specification of its own). The core lists anchoring as the Envelope's task, and its table "Outside the core" gives anchoring to anchoring cMIPs instead of "verifying modules".

**The WebAssembly changed.** The display client must be released again.

## Questions for Nobody, allegedly

Writing the rules in exposed these. None is decided here: the build reads each one as stated under "As built", for you to keep or change. They are also in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft". One at a time, as you prefer.

1. **QG1. A refund to a bare key, or to nobody** (QF5). A payer who stayed anonymous but committed a one-time key is refunded "where the key's claim says" (Finance rule 10a), by a payment that no identity receives. Neither a receipt by the payer nor a claim naming the payer can show that repayment as the formats stand. A payer who committed no key can never be refunded at all (rule 10a), so the money stays owed for good, and a collective owing it can never close. *As built:* both stay owed. How should a refund to a bare key be shown as repaid, and can money owed back to nobody ever stop blocking a closing?
2. **QG2. Which of the payer's pointers a repayment must follow** (QF5). For a debt, Finance rule 14 picks the creditor's pointer from the creditor's own acts. Money owed back has none, except perhaps the payer's claim, which may name where a refund goes. *As built:* the payer's own receipt counts wherever the money went; the collective's claim counts where the rail shows the money reached a pointer or vault the payer published, any version. Should the repayment follow the refund rail the payer's claim names, where it names one?
3. **QG3. How a verifier knows a delivery record** (F184). The core reads no cMIP, so the verifier tells it which specification is the relay transport cMIP, as it already tells it which rails are push rails. *As built:* a verifier that is told applies the acknowledgement rule; one that is not reads a delivery record as any third party's act and counts it, as before F184. Should Law say instead that any cMIP act offered as a relay's evidence needs the payer's acknowledgement, so no verifier needs telling?
4. **QG4. The chain of judgment for the judge of forks** (the second half of QF2, not answered by F190). *As built:* only the judge field 27 names settles; if it stays silent, the deal stays on its reference.
5. **QG5. Production's other kinds called Modules.** Removing "a unit" from the Module definition, the same list still names "a media type" and "a signature scheme", which have kinds of their own in the specification format; and the core's table "Outside the core" still gives "time sources" to verifying modules, though the time reference is a task filled by a cMIP. Left as written. Align them too?

## Readings taken, to confirm

Nine, in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft":
- **"Citing the settlement"** is an entry `[settlement, settlement]` in the version's `objects`, after its parent and any tips it settles; a version after one that cites it, or one naming it in field 26, counts as citing it too.
- **"Clean"** and the first clean settlement holding, as above. **The one that matters most:** a settlement left unclean (by a version beyond its tips citing nothing, or by a rival settlement) holds nothing, so a later clean settlement naming every tip settles the deal whichever line it keeps, even one dropping that earlier settlement's line. Without this, two rival settlements (DQ3) could never be resolved, which QF3 exists to allow.
- **"Every tip"**: the tips a settling version drops lie apart from its own parent's line; field 26 is ascending, with no tip twice.
- **The judge's settlement** (type 23) also names every tip it drops (a list, "expand to multiple tips as needed"), and counts only where clean.
- **Field 27** is for deals only and must name one of field 13; terms naming anyone else are invalid.
- **QF4** applies to deals only; a repeated number is a deviation on every split carrying it.
- **QF5**: "naming what it repays" is field 5 of the receipt or claim naming the payment owed back; repaid in that payment's unit, summed as a debt is, the rail's proof checked.
- **QF6**: "holds the area again" means named its holder by a constitutional clone they signed after the line that registered their stepping down, as for a return (B10).
- **F184**: "the payer's claim" is the payment itself if it is a claim, otherwise a claim of the same payment by the payer its receipt names; "the object the payment was for" is the locked hash of the publication the payment fulfils.

## What changed in the code

**Core library** (`core/src/law/`):
- `formats.rs`: terms field 26 a list (ascending, none twice) and field 27 (the judge of forks, one of field 13, deals only); a deal's version may cite acts it was made after (`[act, act]` in `objects`) before a collective's chain citations, read by `deal_citations`; the fork settlement's field 2 a list.
- `view.rs`:
  - `deal_state` rewritten around **clean** settlements, with `fork_histories` (what each version shows it was made after); QF1 no longer refused but read as tangled; a tangled deal settled by a version naming every tip.
  - `arbitrated` reads the judge from field 27 (QF2) and takes every tip discarded.
  - `split`: `numbering` (QF4, `NumberBreak`); the delivery record's acknowledgement (`is_delivery_record`, `delivery_acknowledged`) and a new view input, `delivery_records`.
  - `owed_back` subtracts what was repaid (`repaid`, QF5).
  - `voices`, `area_returns`, `stepping_down_spent` and the rollback's check: an old stepping down spent (QF6).

**Bindings** (`wasm/src/lib.rs`): terms read with `settles` a list and `forkJudge`; `forkSettlementPayload` takes a list of tips discarded; `lawSplit` gives `numbering`; the specifications given to every reading take `deliveryRecords`.

**Repo client** (`clients/repo/`): `dealPayload` takes several tips to settle, arbitrators and the judge of forks; a settling version cites every tip; the seller's alarm points to settling by naming every tip, and to the judge of forks.

**Desk** (`clients/desk/`): a split with no number, or a repeated one, is said to break the plan (QF4).

**Collective client** (`clients/collective/`): its rollback asks Law whether a stepping down is spent, as it does for resignations (it already dropped one once the member was named holder again).
