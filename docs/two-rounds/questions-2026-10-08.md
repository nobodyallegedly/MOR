## Part A. Two complete versions of one deal (F186, from first principles)

**Background, the same both rounds.** A deal is an agreement between named people, with no keys of its own. A new version of a deal comes into force only when every party has signed it. Each version names the version it replaces. By mistake (two devices out of step, a careless app) or by one party's bad faith, every party may sign two different new versions that both replace the same version. Then the deal has two branches. MOR has no clock, so nobody can prove which was signed first.

**A1.** While a deal has two branches, which version should hold?
- (a) the version before the split, until the split is settled;
- (b) one of the two branches, chosen by a rule (say which rule);
- (c) something else.

**A2.** How should the split be settled?
- (a) as soon as everyone signs a further version on one branch, whether or not they knew of the other branch;
- (b) only by a further version that shows, in what it names, that its signers saw both branches;
- (c) something else.

**A3.** Once settled, can the other branch ever come back into force (for example if it later gets more versions than the winner)?
- (a) no, settled is final;
- (b) yes;
- (c) something else.

**A4.** What happens to things done under a branch that is not in force (payments, splits, sales made while the branches existed)?
- (a) they count for nothing in Law, and money already received still counts in Finance;
- (b) they count, as done under the version they named;
- (c) something else.

## Part B. A broken collective (F185's open questions, RB1 to RB6)

**Background, the same both rounds.** A collective is broken when its keys moved by a rotation whose new agreement fails Law. From that act on, the broken stretch, everything signed in the collective's name counts for nothing, until a rollback restores the agreement in force just before the broken act, with that agreement's constitutional powers.

**B1. Grants.** Before it broke, the collective had hired someone with a grant key (a split service, a publisher). During the broken stretch the grantee keeps signing with that key.
- Does what the grantee signs during the stretch count, or count for nothing like the collective's own acts?
- After the rollback, does the grant still work, or must the members grant it again?

**B2. What others relied on.** During the stretch the collective signed a debt, made a sale or gave a receipt. Those count for nothing. After the rollback:
- (a) may the collective adopt such an act by citing it from an act that counts, so the other party need not sign again;
- (b) or must everything still wanted be signed anew?

**B3. Absence during the stretch.** A member dies or vanishes during the broken stretch, and the others declare them absent under the absence clause of the agreement in force before the broken act. May the rollback register that declaration, so the vanished member does not block the way back?

**B4. Other ways of breaking.** Today a collective has a way back only if the failed rotation declared an agreement that fails Law's checks. If a rotation declares no agreement at all, or a malformed one, should that also be a broken act with a rollback, or a collective broken for good?

**B5. The last voice.** Some collectives give constitutional power to some members only (say, the two founders), while others keep voices in their areas. When the last of those named members leaves, the constitution freezes while other members still act in their areas. Should the "last voice" warning be given then too?

**B6. Ending a broken collective.** Can a broken collective fork or close without rolling back first?
