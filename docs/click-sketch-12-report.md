## In plain words

**The money reaches everyone, and the reports now fit together with nothing added.** At every leg the service reports what it received, passed on, kept and still owes, and each person paid confirms. The two halves are tied by the payment both name. Across three legs run by three different cMIPs, a reader holding every report follows the click to every person, sees a confirmation that disagrees, and sees money still owed as owed, not as a fee (runs 1, 9, 10). The three flaws of the first test are gone.

**But draft 12 changed the question from "can it be followed?" to "who can follow it?", and the answer is: almost nobody, by design, and the payer least of all.**

- **The payer sees nothing after paying**, not even the first leg (run 2). They signed nothing in the sequence, so no report goes to them. Before paying they can see which legs call for reports, but never whether those calls were answered (run 3). The "I" in "can I with a single click" is the one seat with no sight at all.
- **Each person sees their own leg.** The film's owner sees the film's service pay the director, the editor, the song and the library, and stops there (run 4). A band member reading up from what she received sees the band's service pay her, and not where its money came from (run 5).
- **A partner sees the whole climb only where every leg agreed** to send it their reports (run 12). One leg declining hides everyone beyond it (run 11).

So the grammar now protects the people at every leg from being watched, at the price the stated cost names: from any one seat, the click is a single leg.

## 2. Where draft 12 snaps

**2a. The payer, the one in the wish, has no seat.** Draft 12's stated cost ("One leg, and no further") speaks of a payer who wants to know about "the far end". The run shows it's sharper than that: a payer who signed nothing sees no leg at all, not even the first service's receipt of their own money. That's the core of the wish, so it's for Nobody, allegedly (section 4).

**2b. Whether signing reaches through, P17.** Rule 1.2 says a work signs its deal "by all its owners". Read one level (the work's own signer parties), each contributor sees one leg (run 5). Read through every agreement above (the band signs the song, the song signs as part of the film), a band member is a signer of the film's deal and sees everything above her (run 6). That second reading would also make a film's deal with its service need a signature from every member of every band of every song the film uses. Draft 12's "every one of its signers" reads as one level, and the sketch keeps that. Draft 12 should say so, since it decides both who signed what and who sees what.

**2c. A holder's own part is called "kept (a fee)".** When a band member receives for the band (the answer to 3g, run 10), she keeps her own 40%. K8's row has only "what was kept (a fee)", so her own share reads as a fee she took from the others. The row needs one more line: a holder's own part, or her part shown as passed on to herself.

**2d. The "data cMIP" needs nothing new from the grammar.** The partner's view in runs 11 and 12 was built with the block alone: each leg's call names the partner's place among where its reports are expected. The partner then reads one leg at a time and stitches the legs together. Everyone was seen only because they signed a call naming the partner, which is exactly rule 11.6. Not a snap, a finding: what a data cMIP adds is only *who* may be named as a reader (a partner, "whoever paid") and how a reader stitches legs. Whether that's enough, or 11.6 wants a cMIP named by fingerprint as well, is for the MOR lead.

## 4. For Nobody, allegedly, and the MOR lead

Two smaller points for the MOR lead's next draft: say that rule 1.2's signing is one level (2b), and add a holder's own part to K8's row (2c).

One question for Nobody, allegedly, since it is the wish itself:

**Should the payer be able to see the first leg?** As written, the person who clicks sees nothing of where their money went, unless they are also a partner every leg agreed to. Two ways it could be read:
- (a) as written: the payer trusts the work they paid, and each leg's people see their own leg;
- (b) a leg's call may name "whoever paid" among the places its reports are expected. Then a payer sees the first leg, one leg only, and only where the work's own people chose it.

The Dubsar Suite lead's lean, not a decision: (b). It needs no new block (the call's "where" already does it, 2d). It stays one leg, and it gives the wish's "I" something to see.
