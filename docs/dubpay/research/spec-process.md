# Spec-process research for DubPay: how decentralized payment specifications are made, tested and kept

Author: Nobody, allegedly

Date: 2026-11-20

Scope: the process, not the payments. Five processes are compared: Nostr NIPs, Lightning BOLTs and bLIPs, Cashu NUTs, Interledger RFCs, and Bitcoin BIPs.

How to read the evidence labels. A plain link means the researchers read that page or repository file themselves. "(search summary only)" means the claim comes from a search-engine summary of the linked page, which could not be opened. "Inference:" marks a reading of the evidence, by the researchers or by this report, that no source states. Wording from discussion threads and from the Bitcoin documents came through a page-summarising tool, so it is paraphrased here. Four links are withheld from this copy because their addresses contain a personal name; each is marked where it would have stood.

## 1. Short summary

In all five projects anyone may propose a change. They differ in who says yes.

- **Nostr:** a small unlisted group merges, and the repository owner can overrule them ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md#is-this-repository-a-centralizing-factor)).
- **Lightning:** a public meeting approves, and a change needs two independent programs that work together ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)).
- **Cashu:** the same two-program rule was written down only in August 2026 ([PR #398](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/pull/398)).
- **Bitcoin:** editors check form only and decide nothing ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)).
- **Interledger:** a ladder of stages exists that no text has ever climbed ([process file](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)).

Almost nothing is ever declared finished. Only Bitcoin freezes a text and then gives each correction a version number and a dated change list ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)).

No project has one shared test that every program must pass. What works is "test vectors": worked examples with known right answers. Cashu's programs also test themselves against each other every night ([drift check](https://github.com/cashubtc/cashu-ts/blob/main/.github/workflows/nightly-mint-drift.yml)).

The weakest point everywhere is reporting a flaw in the text itself. Lightning's policy sends reporters to the software teams ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/SECURITY.md)).

For a small team, the habits to consider copying are frozen, versioned texts, a two-program gate, retired texts kept with a pointer to their successor, and a private address for flaws. The habits to avoid are editing live texts in place, labels nobody applies, and unwritten rules about who decides.

## 3. The detail behind the table

### 3.1 Nostr NIPs

**Who proposes and decides.** Anyone can open a pull request (a proposed change). The README lists five acceptance criteria. The first asks for full implementation in at least two clients and one relay, and the last reads "Other rules will be made up when necessary" ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md#criteria-for-acceptance-in-this-repository)). The README also says the people with commit access exist "mostly for practical reasons" and that the repository owner can revoke access and rewrite history ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md#is-this-repository-a-centralizing-factor)). The repository has no contributing, security or governance file ([repository tree](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips)). By the researchers' count, 25 of the 124 commits made in 2026 carry no pull-request reference, and 22 of those are by the founder ([commit history](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commits/master)). Inference: the working rule is rough agreement among those with commit access, with the owner as tie-breaker. Nothing written says so.

**Draft to final.** There is no promotion step. By the researchers' count, 86 of the 94 files with a status line say "draft" ([repository tree](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips)), including NIP-01, the one text every program is expected to follow ([01.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md)). The README does not define any status word ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md)). The label that matters is "unrecommended". Eight NIPs received it in a single direct commit on 28 May 2026 ([commit 861675b](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/861675be8c7fc2578ee503a029a77f8e3697c3f3)). Retiring NIP-26 by the reviewed route took about 14 months ([PR #1051](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/1051)).

**Versions and retirement.** Texts have no version numbers and are edited in place. A hand-kept list of breaking changes was started in February 2024 ([commit 62c48ef](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/62c48ef)). One volunteer wrote almost all of its updates ([history](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commits/93d9a12/BREAKING.md)). It was deleted in September 2025 with no reason recorded ([PR #2058](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2058)). The old encrypted-message text, NIP-04, was marked deprecated in favour of NIP-44 on the day NIP-44 merged ([commit 822b70a](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/822b70a)). The same day NIP-44 gained a note saying it was not a drop-in replacement ([commit 2b78cc9](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/2b78cc9)). The real replacement arrived four months later ([commit df30012](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/df30012)). A January 2026 newsletter still describes NIP-04 as in use ([Nostr Compass #4](https://buttondown.com/nostrcompass/archive/nostr-compass-4/), search summary only). Inference: nothing is ever switched off centrally, so users are not stranded by force, but the old scheme never dies.

**Tests.** Only four NIP files mention test vectors, and NIP-01 has none ([repository tree](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips), [01.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md)). NIP-44 is the strong case. It publishes a checksum (a fingerprint) of an external vector file, so the text pins the exact examples without carrying them ([44.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/44.md)). There is no shared test suite. A relay tester exists as one contributor's personal project (a project named relay-tester; link withheld: its address contains a personal name).

**Reference implementation.** None. The README says NIPs document what may be implemented and that nothing forces any software to implement any of them ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md)). Inference: popular libraries written by the people with commit access act as informal references.

**Security.** There is no security file ([404](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/SECURITY.md)). The best-handled case was NIP-44. A volunteer cryptographer wrote it, and an outside audit was arranged inside the public discussion thread ([PR #746](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/746)). The audit notice was added to the text on the day of merge ([commit 9be5126](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/9be5126)). NIP-44 also lists its own known limits in the text ([44.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/44.md)). Inference: none of this was required by the process. It happened because of who the author was.

**Failures.** In July 2026 a merged change gave event kind 10011 a second meaning ([PR #2413](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2413)). The fix merged about ten weeks later ([PR #2417](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2417)). The remote-signing text, NIP-46, was rewritten repeatedly from 2023 to 2026 ([history of 46.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commits/master/46.md)). On 2 November 2026 the third-highest contributor said he had moved his work to another site and deprecated the repository in his client ([PR #1621](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/1621), as summarised by a fetch tool). The payment texts depend on other projects' specifications. NIP-57 builds on LNURL and states in its own words that a zap receipt "is not a proof of payment" ([57.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/57.md)). NIP-61 builds on Cashu texts and warns that tokens can be lost if the two sides disagree ([61.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/61.md)).

### 3.2 Lightning BOLTs and bLIPs

Newsletter links below point to the public Bitcoin Optech pages. The researchers read the same text in Optech's source repository, not on the public pages.

**Who proposes and decides.** Anyone may open an issue or pull request. Typo-level changes need two acknowledgements and no objections. Everything else is approved at the recurring public spec meeting. A protocol change needs two independent implementations that interoperate ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). The meeting ran every two weeks until September 2025 and about every four weeks since ([meeting issues](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/issues?q=is%3Aissue+meeting+in%3Atitle+sort%3Acreated-desc)). The record of the October 2026 meeting is a single summary comment with no attendee list ([issue #1365](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/issues/1365)). bLIPs are a lighter track for optional features. Each needs a "champion", editors do not judge merit, and anything meant to be universal must be a BOLT ([bLIP-1](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/blip-0001.md)). A second bLIP divides the shared number spaces so the two tracks cannot collide ([bLIP-2](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/blip-0002.md)).

**Draft to final.** There is no "final" status. BOLT 0 still describes itself as version 0 ([BOLT 0](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/00-introduction.md)). A feature is introduced with an odd number, which other programs may ignore. It is later given an even number, which they must understand. It can then be marked "assumed" and its rules removed from the text ([BOLT 9](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/09-features.md)). Draft features use a temporary number until merge ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Times from proposal to merge are long. Anchor outputs took about ten months ([PR #688](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/pull/688)). Offers took about four years as a pull request ([PR #798](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/pull/798)). Splicing was first proposed in 2018 ([Optech 2018-10-16](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2018/10/16/#proposal-for-lightning-network-payment-channel-splicing)) and merged in March 2026 ([PR #1160](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/pull/1160)). Inference: these durations measure implementation capacity more than argument, since each merge followed shortly after a statement that two implementations worked together.

**Versions and retirement.** Compatibility is carried feature by feature. The main retirement change removed two features and marked four as assumed. Its evidence was one developer's count of the announcements his own node could see ([PR #1092](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/pull/1092)). Rules for assumed features exist only in earlier revisions of the text ([BOLT 9](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/09-features.md)). Old channel types are retired by each implementation on its own schedule ([Optech 2024-12-06](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2024/12/06/#eclair-v0-11-0)).

**Tests.** Test vectors are required for new low-level constructions, preferably as machine-readable files ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Vectors are sometimes wrong. The taproot channel vectors were corrected five months after merge ([Optech 2026-10-23](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/10/23/#bolts-1352)). The shared protocol test tool supports one implementation only (a project named lnprototest; link withheld: its address contains a personal name). Inference: the two-implementation gate is satisfied by the implementers saying so in the discussion thread, not by an independent test.

**Reference implementation.** None. Four implementations are listed as peers ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/SECURITY.md)). Features often ship in one of them years before the text. Splicing reached wallet users in July 2023, almost three years before merge ([Optech 2023-07-19](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2023/07/19/#phoenix-wallet-adds-splicing-support)). The first implementer then had to move to the official version ([Optech 2026-05-08](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/05/08/#eclair-2887)).

**Security.** The specification had no security file until 9 September 2025 ([file history](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/commits/master/SECURITY.md)). The file tells reporters to contact the implementation teams and gives nothing for flaws in the text itself ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/SECURITY.md)). In 2019 three implementations shared the same flaw. Fixes shipped quietly, the teams then urged upgrades ([Optech 2019-09-04](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2019/09/04/#upgrade-ln-implementations)), and full disclosure followed ([Optech 2019-10-02](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2019/10/02/)). Inference: the hole was a check the text did not require, so the two-implementation rule could not catch it. In November 2022 a privately reported bug was held for the next regular release and was rediscovered and triggered before that release ([Optech 2022-11-09](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2022/11/09/#block-parsing-bug-affecting-multiple-software)).

**Failures.** bLIPs were proposed in 2021. The main objection at the time was that the real bottleneck is reviewer time, which a second track does not add ([Optech 2021-07-07](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2021/07/07/)). By November 2026 the index holds 14 bLIPs, all marked "Active" ([bLIP index](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/README.md)). 28 of the 67 pull requests ever filed are still open ([bLIP pull requests](https://github.com/lightning/blips/pulls?q=is%3Apr)). LNURL never joined either track and keeps its own, looser rule ([LUDs](https://github.com/lnurl/luds)). bLIP-1 still requires posting to a mailing list ([bLIP-1](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/blip-0001.md)) that the BOLT process file now treats as an archive ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)).

### 3.3 Cashu NUTs

**Who proposes and decides.** Anyone may open a pull request. The repository began as a single-author project in January 2023 ([first commit](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/e0602b5)). A written review rule was added on 12 August 2026 ([commit 973ab09](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/973ab09)). It asks for two approving reviews, then at least two implementation changes in different projects marked ready, and then the text is merged first ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md)). The first draft of that rule asked for five approvals from a named list. The names were removed before merge ([PR #398](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/pull/398), as summarised by a fetch tool). The "core maintainers" are not named anywhere in the repository ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md)). New texts use the placeholder "NUT-XX" until maintainers assign a number at merge ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md)).

**Draft to final.** There is no status ladder. A text is either an open proposal or merged and live. The only badge is "mandatory" (NUT-00 to NUT-06) or "optional", and a hand-kept table shows which wallets and mints support each optional text ([README](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/README.md)). Merged texts are edited in place, including breaking changes. In May 2026 one signature format was replaced and the old one stopped being accepted ([commit b969c4a](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/b969c4a)). Inference: a reader cannot tell from a text how settled it is without reading the change history.

**Versions and retirement.** Versions live inside the formats. Version 1 added a version prefix to every web address of the interface and a version byte to keyset identifiers ([PR #55](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/pull/55)). Tokens carry a version letter ([commit 6c2b244](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/6c2b244)). Old forms are marked "deprecated" and stay fully documented ([NUT-00](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/00.md)). The main implementation kept the old interface working alongside the new one from February 2024 ([Nutshell 0.15.0](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/releases/tag/0.15.0)). It removed the mint side in August 2026 ([Nutshell commit](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/commit/0e896c00c5bc7c67531f74c66cd600f7057de35c)). One library publishes a migration guide for each major version ([cashu-ts](https://github.com/cashubtc/cashu-ts)). Inference: the working rule is "old readers stay, old writers stop". No text states it, and no text says how long a deprecated form must be supported.

**Tests.** The specification repository holds test vectors as text files and nothing that runs ([tests folder](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/tree/8f83a97/tests)). A rule written in October 2026 says vectors are needed for cryptographic steps, encodings and deterministic derivations ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/AGENTS.md)). There is no shared test suite. Each implementation instead tests itself against the others' released builds ([CDK workflow](https://github.com/cashubtc/cdk/blob/main/.github/workflows/nutshell_itest.yml)). Since July 2026 one library runs a nightly check against the newest mint builds and files an issue when something drifts ([drift check](https://github.com/cashubtc/cashu-ts/blob/main/.github/workflows/nightly-mint-drift.yml)). These runs use a fake payment backend, not real payments ([workflow](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/blob/main/.github/workflows/wallet-compatibility.yml)). Inference: the 2026 run of edits pinning down behaviour the text had left open arrived after cross-testing began ([example](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/38d243a)). Testing against another implementation found what reading prose had not.

**Reference implementation.** Three are named as the gate: cashu-ts, CDK and Nutshell ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Early on the text followed the code. One proof scheme shipped in Nutshell six weeks before its text merged ([Nutshell commit](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/commit/6282e0a22a4d0db8c202ab140ebd74d96b9a4c3c), [PR #51](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/pull/51)).

**Security.** Reports go by private email. The policy says to keep public descriptions high-level until a fix is released ([Nutshell SECURITY.md](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/blob/main/SECURITY.md)). The three projects list different addresses ([spec](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/SECURITY.md), [CDK](https://github.com/cashubtc/cdk/blob/main/SECURITY.md)). Neither main mint has ever published an advisory ([Nutshell](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/security/advisories), [CDK](https://github.com/cashubtc/cdk/security/advisories)). The one detailed public disclosure was written by the reporter, not the project. Its details are known here from search summaries only ([Delving Bitcoin post](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/public-disclosure-denial-of-service-using-htlc-in-cashu/2090)). A change to a core cryptographic function shipped in February 2024 without a mention in the release notes ([Nutshell 0.15.0](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/releases/tag/0.15.0)). The written definition entered the specification a month later, from an outside contributor ([commit ed9aa25](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/ed9aa25)).

**Failures.** Of the seven wallets and five mints listed in November 2023 ([2023 README](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/74f26b81b6617db710fa1081eebc0c7203711213/README.md)), two wallets and one mint remain under the same name ([current README](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/README.md)). One implementation published its own "NUT-17" and "NUT-18" ([Moksha](https://github.com/ngutech21/moksha)). Those numbers later went to different texts ([commit 06c95ee](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/06c95ee)). On-chain payments were closed unmerged three times ([closed list](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/pulls?q=is%3Apr+is%3Aclosed+is%3Aunmerged+sort%3Acomments-desc)) before merging as NUT-30 ([commit 75baf4d](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/75baf4d)).

### 3.4 Interledger RFCs

**Who proposes and decides.** Anyone may propose by pull request. The process file says "maintainers", "editors" and a "core team" decide by "broad consensus", and it names none of them ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). The file says the work runs under a W3C Community Group ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). That group was closed in 2026 ([W3C activity page](https://www.w3.org/groups/cg/interledger/activity/2026/), search summary only). Two attempts at formal standardisation through the IETF expired (the IETF datatracker histories of the Interledger draft and the crypto-conditions draft, both search summary only; links withheld: their addresses contain a personal name). Inference: the deciders have been whoever was employed to work on it, first Ripple staff, then Coil-era contributors, then Interledger Foundation staff. The yearly commit counts fit that reading: 139 in 2019, 10 in 2020, 1 in 2021 and none in 2022 ([commit history](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/commits/main/)).

**Draft to final.** The written ladder is Proposal, Working Draft, Candidate Specification. A Candidate Specification is frozen, and only small corrections are allowed afterwards ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). No RFC has ever carried the Candidate status. Every living text, including the core protocol, is a working draft ([repository](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs)). The label "FINAL" appears only on deprecated texts and on one whose specification moved to its own site ([deprecated folder](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/tree/main/deprecated)). Inference: "FINAL" here means frozen because abandoned, not ratified.

**Versions and retirement.** A redesign gets a new RFC number. The old text moves to a `deprecated` folder with a pointer to its successor, and 21 numbered RFCs now sit there against 17 living ones ([deprecated folder](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/tree/main/deprecated)). The big break, to version 4 of the core protocol in 2018, has no migration section ([RFC 0027](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/0027-interledger-protocol-4/0027-interledger-protocol-4.md)). Inference: the break was affordable because nearly all implementers then worked together. Once outside users existed, known defects in the transport protocol were parked in a tracking issue for a "version 2" that was never produced ([issue #554](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/issues/554)). Retirement by housekeeping has gone wrong. A text that the architecture still depended on was deprecated in November 2023 and restored four months later, after an outside reader noticed ([PR #592](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/pull/592)).

**Tests.** The repository holds one vector file, for one protocol's encoding. Its table of which implementations run the vectors still shows one row as "TBD" ([vectors README](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/0029-stream/test-vectors/README.md)). The README still claims an automatic check of the wire definitions ([README](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/README.md)). That check's configuration was deleted in November 2023 ([commit history](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/commits/main/)). The newer Open Payments specification takes a different route: machine-readable interface files under one version number ([open-payments-specifications](https://github.com/interledger/open-payments-specifications)).

**Reference implementation.** The README names Interledger.js as the main reference ([README](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/README.md)). The Java implementation was archived in 2022 ([Quilt](https://github.com/hyperledger-archives/quilt)). The Rust one was archived on 18 November 2026 ([interledger-rs](https://github.com/interledger/interledger-rs)). Current effort is on Rafiki, which says it is intended for financial service providers only ([Rafiki](https://github.com/interledger/rafiki)). Inference: authority runs from code to text. The March 2026 clean-up removed features from the texts ([PR #602](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/pull/602)).

**Security.** The specification repository has no security file ([repository](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs)). Rafiki's security page shows no policy and no advisories ([Rafiki security tab](https://github.com/interledger/rafiki/security)). One known risk is written up honestly as a text of its own ([RFC 0018](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/0018-connector-risk-mitigations/0018-connector-risk-mitigations.md)). The Foundation reports commissioning yearly audits of Rafiki ([security profile](https://interledger.org/policy-and-advocacy/security-profile-interledger-open-source-technologies), search summary only).

**Failures.** An automatic "stale" bot labels quiet issues and closes them after a further seven days ([configuration](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/.github/stale.yml)). It twice closed the list of known transport-protocol defects as "completed" without the fixes being made ([issue #554](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/issues/554)). The process file still links to folders that do not exist ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). No fork of the specification was found. Inference: the pattern is attrition, not schism. People left instead of forking.

### 3.5 Bitcoin BIPs

Wording from the BIP documents came through a summarising tool, so it is paraphrased throughout this section.

**Who proposes and decides.** Anyone may submit a BIP. Editors only check form and scope, assign a number and merge, and they are told not to judge whether a proposal is likely to be adopted ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). The README describes the repository as a publication medium and archive ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/README.mediawiki)). Ideas must first be discussed in a dedicated mailing-list thread ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). BIP 3 replaced the 2016 process on 14 January 2026 (the date is from a search summary of [the list thread](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/Sh4I6ogv_sE)). A process document changes when it has been open for discussion for at least a month and nobody maintains an unaddressed, substantiated objection ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). For years there was one editor. Five more were added in April 2024 after public discussion ([Optech #299](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoinops/bitcoinops.github.io/master/_posts/en/newsletters/2024-04-24-newsletter.md)).

**Draft to final.** BIP 3 has four statuses: Draft, Complete, Deployed, Closed. A technical BIP must come with a working reference implementation and comprehensive test vectors before it is Complete. Deployed needs evidence of active use. A Draft that stalls for a year may be closed ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). The older public comments system was abolished because almost nobody used it ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). Inference: "Deployed" records use, not a recommendation. BIP 70 is still listed as Deployed with no warning ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/README.mediawiki)), although the main implementation removed it in 2020 ([release notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/master/doc/release-notes/release-notes-0.20.0.md)).

**Versions and retirement.** Once Deployed, a BIP is final. A breaking change is a new BIP that points back with a "Replaces" header. Smaller changes after Complete are logged with version, date and description ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). The data formats carry their own versions. One address format says exactly how a version-0 reader treats later versions and reserves one value that must fail ([BIP 352](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0352.mediawiki)). The seed-phrase text has no version field and lists that among its own shortcomings ([BIP 39](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)). Retirement in practice was done by the main implementation over successive releases: off by default first ([0.19 notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/master/doc/release-notes/release-notes-0.19.0.1.md)), then removed ([0.20 notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/master/doc/release-notes/release-notes-0.20.0.md)).

**Tests.** Vectors live in or beside the BIP and are versioned with it. Several were added after real bugs. The key-derivation text gained vectors in 2013, 2017 and 2020, each linked to the bug that motivated it ([BIP 32](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0032.mediawiki)). The better sets include invalid cases with the reason for rejection ([BIP 173](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0173.mediawiki)). They also split cases by role, so sender and receiver can be tested separately ([BIP 352](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0352.mediawiki)). There is no conformance programme. A volunteer project feeds the same inputs to several implementations and compares the results ([bitcoinfuzz status](https://delvingbitcoin.org/t/the-state-of-bitcoinfuzz/1946), search summary only).

**Reference implementation.** Required. For application-level BIPs it is usually small standalone code. One describes its own reference code as slow, naive and for demonstration only ([BIP 340](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0340.mediawiki)). The warning case is the 2013 chain split, where an internal limit of one program's database had become an accidental rule of the network ([BIP 50](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0050.mediawiki)).

**Security.** Inference: the BIP process itself has no rule for reporting a flaw in a text. In practice the author publishes an analysis and a successor BIP. An address checksum flaw was fixed by a new BIP with one changed constant ([BIP 350](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0350.mediawiki)). The old text was left frozen and given a dated "Disclosures" section ([BIP 173](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0173.mediawiki)). The 2013 incident was written up as a post-mortem with action items, inside the same register ([BIP 50](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0050.mediawiki)). The main implementation has had a written, severity-based disclosure policy since July 2024 ([announcement](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/Q2ZGit2wF7w), search summary only).

**Failures.** In 2023 a proposal's author complained that its pull request had sat open for nine months ([list thread](https://mirror.b10c.me/lists/bitcoindev/ZTawwRqGN4XUUu8C@camus/t/), search summary only). On 10 August 2026 one editor was removed, a day after the motion ([PR #2248](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/pull/2248)). BIP 3 names the editors but does not say how editors are appointed or removed ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)). The removed editor's objection on exactly that point appears as a commit in another repository, seen only as a cross-reference ([PR #2248](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/pull/2248)). The competing block-size BIPs of 2015 to 2017 all sit in the register as Closed ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/README.mediawiki)). Inference: the register records outcomes after the fact and did nothing to produce them. BIP 110 was assigned a number in December 2025 and marked Closed in August 2026 after a chain split ([BIP 110](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0110.mediawiki)).

## What a small team writing payment texts on a protocol that moves nothing should copy, and what it should avoid

Every point below is a proposal to consider, not a decision. Each rests on the linked source. Where a point goes beyond what the source says, it is marked as an inference. None involves handling keys or funds.

### Copy

1. **Consider freezing a text once it is in use, and giving every later correction a version number, a date and a one-line description.** Bitcoin's process does this, and its working texts carry live change lists ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md), [BIP 174](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0174.mediawiki)).
2. **Consider giving a breaking change a new document, and keeping the old one in place with a pointer to its successor.** Interledger's `deprecated` folder keeps every retired text readable ([deprecated folder](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/tree/main/deprecated)). Bitcoin uses a "Replaces" header ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)).
3. **Consider adding a dated note to a frozen text when a flaw is found, in place of a silent edit.** One Bitcoin text carries a "Disclosures" section added in 2024 ([BIP 173](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0173.mediawiki)). Another carries a "Shortcomings" section ([BIP 39](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)).
4. **Consider writing down, from the first version, what an old reader does when it meets a newer format.** One Bitcoin text says how a version-0 reader treats later versions and reserves a value that must fail ([BIP 352](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0352.mediawiki)). Lightning's odd and even numbering does the same job feature by feature ([BOLT 0](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/00-introduction.md)).
5. **Consider a gate of two independent programs that agree before a text is merged, and consider recording which two.** Cashu and Lightning both state the gate ([Cashu CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md), [Lightning CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Inference: the gate proves that implementers agree, not that the text is right. See the 2019 Lightning case in section 3.2.
6. **Consider a written rule for when test vectors are required.** Cashu's rule covers cryptographic steps, encodings and deterministic derivations ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/AGENTS.md)).
7. **Consider shaping the vectors like the best Bitcoin sets.** That means invalid cases with the reason for rejection ([BIP 173](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0173.mediawiki)), cases split by role, and machine-readable files beside the prose ([BIP 352](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0352.mediawiki)).
8. **Consider pinning an external vector file by its fingerprint in the text.** NIP-44 publishes a checksum so the text fixes the exact examples ([44.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/44.md)).
9. **Consider running the vectors and cross-checks automatically and on a schedule, with pretend money only.** One Cashu library's nightly check opens an issue the day something drifts ([drift check](https://github.com/cashubtc/cashu-ts/blob/main/.github/workflows/nightly-mint-drift.yml)). Cashu's compatibility runs use a fake payment backend ([workflow](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/blob/main/.github/workflows/wallet-compatibility.yml)).
10. **Consider stating requirements for the writer and for the reader separately, including what a reader does when the writer breaks a rule.** Lightning's writing rules require this ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md)).
11. **Consider one named, private place to report a flaw in a text, and a rule for what is published afterwards.** Cashu's policy gives a private address and asks for vague public wording until a fix ships ([Nutshell SECURITY.md](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/blob/main/SECURITY.md)). Inference: it stops short of promising publication after the fix. That second half is the part to add.
12. **Consider publishing post-mortems in the same place as the texts.** Bitcoin's 2013 incident report, with its action items, is itself a numbered BIP ([BIP 50](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0050.mediawiki)).
13. **Consider letting only one named role assign numbers, at merge, with a placeholder until then.** Cashu uses "NUT-XX" and forbids reserving numbers ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Bitcoin counts a number as assigned only once an editor announces it publicly ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)).
14. **Consider one registry for every shared number space.** Lightning's bLIP-2 divides the ranges and records each reservation in a table ([bLIP-2](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/blip-0002.md)). Cashu merged its error codes into one file ([error_codes.md](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/error_codes.md)).
15. **Consider saying in each text what it does not prove.** This matters most on a protocol that moves no money. NIP-57 says plainly that its receipt is not a proof of payment ([57.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/57.md)). NIP-44 lists its own limits ([44.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/44.md)).
16. **Consider keeping any reference program small, readable and separate from production software, working from published example values only.** One Bitcoin text calls its reference code naive and for demonstration only ([BIP 340](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0340.mediawiki)). Inference: this keeps one program's accidents from becoming the specification ([BIP 50](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0050.mediawiki)).
17. **Consider versioning the process document itself and writing down how it changes.** BIP 3 carries its own change list and a rule of one month open with no unaddressed objection ([BIP 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0003.md)).
18. **Consider a migration guide whenever something old stops being accepted.** One Cashu library publishes one for each major version ([cashu-ts migration guide](https://github.com/cashubtc/cashu-ts/blob/main/migration-5.0.0.md)).

### Avoid

1. **Consider avoiding edits in place to a text that programs already follow.** Nostr's remote-signing text was rewritten repeatedly under one name ([history of 46.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commits/master/46.md)). One developer reports apps stuck on different revisions of the Nostr wallet texts ([post](https://damus.io/nevent1qqsv8vc9uwv0z3ykjdh7vsv408enqxgrmmvzkk5aa65xae2pxf47fsqcxj26h), search summary only, single source). Cashu also made breaking changes in place ([commit b969c4a](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/commit/b969c4a)).
2. **Consider avoiding status labels that nobody applies.** Interledger's "Candidate Specification" has never been used ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). Nostr's core text is still "draft" ([01.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/01.md)). Every bLIP is "Active" ([bLIP index](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/README.md)). Inference: an unused ladder misleads more than no ladder.
3. **Consider not marking a text deprecated before its replacement exists.** NIP-04 was deprecated in favour of a text that said, the same day, that it was not a replacement ([commit 822b70a](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/822b70a), [commit 2b78cc9](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/commit/2b78cc9)).
4. **Consider not leaving the record of breaking changes to one volunteer's hand-kept file.** Nostr's file lapsed and was then deleted without a recorded reason ([PR #2058](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2058)).
5. **Consider not leaving "who decides" unwritten.** Nostr refers to a list of people with commit access without giving it ([README](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/README.md#is-this-repository-a-centralizing-factor)). Cashu's "core maintainers" and Interledger's "maintainers" are unnamed ([Cashu](https://github.com/cashubtc/nuts/blob/8f83a97/CONTRIBUTING.md), [Interledger](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)).
6. **Consider writing the rule for appointing and removing the people who run the register before it is needed.** Bitcoin had none, and the 2026 removal was contested for that reason ([PR #2248](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/pull/2248)).
7. **Consider not letting one person hold the numbering role alone.** Bitcoin had a single editor until five more were added in 2024 ([Optech #299](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoinops/bitcoinops.github.io/master/_posts/en/newsletters/2024-04-24-newsletter.md)). The reported effect was a jump from about 2 merges in a quarter to about 162 in the following nine months ([one-year review](https://groups.google.com/g/bitcoindev/c/erO5zP3FgS4), search summary only).
8. **Consider not sharing a number space without an allocator.** Nostr gave one event kind two meanings for about ten weeks ([PR #2413](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2413), [PR #2417](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/2417)). A Cashu implementation claimed two NUT numbers that later went to other texts ([Moksha](https://github.com/ngutech21/moksha)).
9. **Consider not letting an automatic tool close open design defects.** Interledger's bot closed the list of known transport-protocol flaws as "completed" twice ([issue #554](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/issues/554)).
10. **Consider not pointing flaw reports only at the software teams.** Lightning's security file does this ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/SECURITY.md)). Inference: a flaw in the text then has no owner, and the finder ends up coordinating several teams.
11. **Consider not fixing quietly and never explaining.** Cashu's 2024 change to a core function went unmentioned in its release notes ([Nutshell 0.15.0](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/releases/tag/0.15.0)). Its main mints have no published advisories ([Nutshell advisories](https://github.com/cashubtc/nutshell/security/advisories)).
12. **Consider not holding a known fix for the next routine release.** In 2022 a privately reported bug was rediscovered and triggered while its fix waited ([Optech 2022-11-09](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2022/11/09/#block-parsing-bug-affecting-multiple-software)).
13. **Consider not keeping vectors where nothing runs them, or in one implementer's own repository.** Interledger's "TBD" row was never filled in ([vectors README](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/0029-stream/test-vectors/README.md)). Its README still claims a check that no longer exists ([README](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/README.md)). The Bitcoin seed-phrase vectors live in the reference implementer's repository ([BIP 39](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0039.mediawiki)).
14. **Consider keeping the process document under the same upkeep as the texts.** Interledger's cites a closed group and missing folders ([CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)). bLIP-1 requires a mailing list that is now an archive ([bLIP-1](https://github.com/lightning/blips/blob/master/blip-0001.md)).
15. **Consider keeping "published", "in use" and "recommended for new work" visibly apart.** Bitcoin's BIP 70 is still "Deployed" with no warning years after removal ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/README.mediawiki), [release notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/master/doc/release-notes/release-notes-0.20.0.md)).
16. **Consider not retiring a text without checking what still depends on it, and recording why at that moment.** Interledger deprecated a live dependency by mistake ([PR #592](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/pull/592)).
17. **Consider not deleting retired rules so that the change history is the only record.** Lightning's "assumed" features are defined only in earlier revisions ([BOLT 9](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/blob/master/09-features.md)).
18. **Consider not depending on another project's text without naming the exact version relied on.** NIP-57 adds fields to another protocol's response ([57.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/57.md)). NIP-61 warns that tokens can be lost if the two sides disagree ([61.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/61.md)). Inference: the researchers found no pinned version of either outside text.
19. **Consider the cost of shipping ahead of the text.** Lightning's taproot channels ran on temporary numbers that had to change at merge ([PR #995](https://github.com/lightning/bolts/pull/995)). The first splicing implementer had to move to the official version ([Optech 2026-05-08](https://bitcoinops.org/en/newsletters/2026/05/08/#eclair-2887)).
20. **Consider not resting the whole process on one sponsor.** Inference from the Interledger record: implementations and the standards venue each went quiet when the sponsor's attention moved ([commit history](https://github.com/interledger/rfcs/commits/main/), [interledger-rs issue #652](https://github.com/interledger/interledger-rs/issues/652)).

## What could not be verified

Many hosts were unreachable during the research, and several claims rest on search summaries or could not be checked at all.

- **Quotations.** Discussion threads for Nostr and Cashu, and all Bitcoin documents, were read through a summarising tool. Their wording should be checked against the linked pages before being quoted anywhere.
- **Bitcoin Optech.** The newsletters were read in Optech's source repository, not on the public pages linked here. One anchor (the 2022 summit notes) was assumed, not confirmed.
- **Who holds merge rights.** No list of the people who can merge was found for Nostr, Cashu, Interledger or the two Lightning repositories. The names of the current bLIP editors were not captured.
- **Nostr.** The NIP-44 audit report could not be opened, so its finding count and funder are unverified. The 2025 academic disclosure and its handling were seen as search summaries only. The reasons for deleting the breaking-changes file and for the May 2026 mass strike-through are not recorded anywhere that was found. Reports of incompatible wallet apps rest on one undated post.
- **Lightning.** The 2018 "unanimous approval" wording, the summit write-ups and the 2021 bLIP thread were seen as search summaries only. Whether meeting minutes exist beyond issue comments was not established. Claims that one team diverges from the specification were found only in forum posts and are not relied on.
- **Cashu.** The 2025 vulnerability details, a 2026 academic paper and the funding information all rest on search summaries. No project-written post-mortem and no outside audit were found. Why the 2024 cryptographic change was made is not explained in any source read. The researchers' recollection of the reason is unverified.
- **Interledger.** The W3C group's closure, the IETF draft histories, the Foundation's audits and a planned bug bounty all rest on search summaries. Whether any RFC was ever published as a W3C report is unknown.
- **Bitcoin.** The activation thread for BIP 3, the Core disclosure policy and its October 2026 revision, and the block-size-war dates rest on search summaries or press. Whether the rival BIP repository seen in a cross-reference is in use is unverified.
- **Stranded users.** For every process, no source was found that counts users or funds stranded by a retirement, and none that shows nobody was. The same holds for organised interop events for Nostr and Cashu, where none were found.
