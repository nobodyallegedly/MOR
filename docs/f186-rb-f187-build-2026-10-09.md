## In plain words

**Two versions of one deal (F186).** Sometimes every party signs two different new versions of the same deal, for example from two devices out of step. The deal then has two branches. Until this is settled:
- The version before the split is the reference: the last version everyone agrees on.
- A new act may follow either branch, and it counts. Someone who buys under either branch has bought: the buyer is protected from the parties' mistake.
- A branch that grows settles nothing. This replaces the rule of 8 October, "the longer branch wins".

The split is settled only by a new version that names both branches: its parent on one branch, and the latest version of the other branch, in a new field. Every party signs it, so everyone saw the choice. Once settled, it is final: the other branch never comes back.

The seller's client must raise an alarm when a payment names a version that does not come from the version the seller holds. It shows both branches and says how to settle the fork. The desk, where an identity receives payments, now does this.

**A broken collective is quarantined (RB1 to RB6).**
- **Grants (RB1).** What a grantee (a split service, a publisher) signs while the collective is broken counts for nothing. After the rollback the grants work again: the rollback restores everything as it was just before the break.
- **What others relied on (RB2).** It is signed again after the rollback, never adopted. A payment the collective received while broken is owed back to the payer, unless the sale is signed again after the rollback. Until then it is shown as an open debt. The network shows it; it cannot force anyone to pay it back.
- **Absence (RB3).** A member may vanish while the collective is broken. The others can declare them absent under the old agreement's rule, and the rollback registers that declaration, so the vanished member does not block the way back. A client must always show a declaration to the member it names, with the way to contest it.
- **Technical breaks (RB4).** A rotation that drops or garbles the collective's agreement is a break too, with the same way back.
- **The last constitutional voice (RB5).** When only some members can change the constitution, the last of them is warned before leaving: "You are about to break the collective".
- **No ending while broken (RB6).** A broken collective cannot fork or close until it is fixed.

**Fable's review (F187), all nine items fixed:**
1. The rollback no longer gives a share of the new safety key to someone who had already left.
2. A rollback that registers an act the relays lost is refused before anything is sent. The client says "done" only when Law, reading the relays, finds the collective working.
3. The last voice is warned even when the others resigned from other devices.
4. Leaving names the agreement Law finds in force. When Law cannot be read, the review says so.

## Questions for Nobody, allegedly

One at a time, as you prefer. They are also in Law draft 10, "Open in this draft".

**About two versions of one deal (F186)**
1. **DQ1.** One branch splits again before the fork is settled. How is that counted and settled? *As built:* the verifier refuses to name a version in force.
2. **DQ2.** Three complete versions of one version. *As built:* refused.
3. **DQ3.** Two settling versions, for example one on each branch naming the other. *As built:* refused.
4. **DQ4.** A settling version made from an older version of its own branch, so that branch splits again. *As built:* refused.
5. **DQ5.** A party added on the other branch only is not asked to sign the version that discards it: the settling version needs the signatures its own parent asks for. *As built:* so.
6. **DQ6.** While a deal is forked, a split service keeps one tally per branch (rule 15a, F171). Should one tally run across both branches? *As built:* one per branch.
7. **DQ7.** The alarm's words, "does not descend from the version they hold", also cover a payment naming an *older* version of the same line, with no fork. *As built, literally:* the alarm is raised, and says "older version, not a fork". Intended?
8. **DQ8.** A deal's own concurrency rule and its keepers play no part in settling its fork. *As built:* so (the concurrency rule's format is still open anyway).

**About a broken collective (RB1 to RB6)**
1. **BQ1.** After the rollback, a grantee may sign without citing anything new. Such an act cannot be told from one made while broken, so it counts for nothing until it cites the rollback or a later decision. The same holds for an act made just before the break that no act of the collective took in. Intended?
2. **BQ2.** "Every payment the collective received": Law judges payments that name a claim or buy a work or offer, where the collective is the seller or the payee. What of a debt paid to the collective, or a payment naming nothing, during the stretch? Owed back too?
3. **BQ3.** A payment shown only by the payer's own claim, with no receipt from the collective, cannot be placed in the stretch. *As built:* not owed back.
4. **BQ4.** The contest act (Law type 14) has no format yet, so no client can sign a contest. The client shows the declaration and the way to contest, and says it cannot sign one yet. When should the format be written?
5. **BQ5.** "You are about to break the collective" uses "break", which in Law means a broken collective (rule 37d). When the last constitutional voice leaves, the constitution freezes, but Law does not call the collective broken. *As built:* your words first, then plain words saying the constitution freezes. Keep, or reword?
6. **BQ6.** A stepping down registered by the rollback holds only if the rollback's new version leaves that member out of the area. If it names them again and they sign it, they hold the area again (an existing rule, B10). *As built:* the collective client leaves them out. Should Law say so as a rule?

**Readings taken, to confirm**
- Field 26: its number, one hash, in a deal's clone only.
- A settling version may name any version of the other branch; its own parent must be its branch's latest version.
- While forked, the reference is what a verifier reports in force.
- A settling version naming a version the verifier does not hold is refused until it holds it.
- "Signed anew" is a new receipt of the collective for the same payment; an acknowledgement is adoption, and is not.
- The rollback's fourth element names declarations and their signatures; no new element.
- A collective whose very first agreement fails has nothing to roll back to.
- A published, not yet registered resignation counts as gone for the warnings only.

## Limits, stated

- **No split service for deals is built** anywhere in the repository, so the split service's half of F186's client conformance exists only as the core reading and binding (`fork_alarm`, `lawForkAlarm`) a service would call. The seller's half is built in the desk.
- **The desk's "version it holds"** is the latest version of the deal the identity signed, as its relays show. The desk keeps no deals of its own. Where the identity's own signatures are on both branches, it shows the fork itself.
- **The collective client cannot sign a contest** (BQ4).
- **The F187 client tests reproduce the Mac's faults** with a relay that loses acts, and with a resignation published from "another device" in the same program. The Mac's separate devices are not tested.
