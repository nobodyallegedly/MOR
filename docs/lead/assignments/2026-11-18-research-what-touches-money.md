## The question

**What touches money, and how?**

Cast the net wide: not only finance. Everything people, groups and machines do that involves money at some moment. At least:
- **Finance:** loans and repayment, instalments, deposits, escrow, insurance pools, bonds and stakes posted in advance, dividends, mutual credit, vouchers issued, passed on and redeemed.
- **Trade and work:** buying a good or a service, a subscription, an invoice paid in parts, a refund, a wage, a commission, a split among those who made something (the shower's click), a payment passed on down a chain of receivers.
- **Media and attention:** buying a work, paying per play or per use, a tip, advertising, a royalty, a licence.
- **Law and collective life:** a membership fee, a fine or a penalty, a deposit lost to a dispute, a tax or a levy, a grant, a prize, a budget a group votes.
- **Gifts and chance:** a donation, a gift, an inheritance, a bet, a lottery.
- **Machines:** an agent paying on someone's behalf, one machine paying another, a relay or a service paid for what it carries.
Add any family the sources show that this list misses.

For each, in plain words:
- **What it does with money:** asks for it, holds it, promises it, delays it, splits it, pools it, passes it on, returns it, converts it, counts it, earmarks it, forfeits it.
- **The moments it touches money itself:** each point where money is asked for, moves, or is said to have moved. Who acts at each moment, and what is said.
- **What it does without touching money:** what is only a promise, a rule, a measure or an account between people.

Then step back:
- **The ways of touching money**, across every family: the smallest list of distinct moments. Which families share them, and which add one of their own?
- **What only looks like touching money:** promises, accounts and measures that never move anything until a later moment does.
- **Where the families surprised you:** a use whose moments differ from what its name suggests.

**Prior art, to check the map against, not to build it:** the functions of money (medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment) and their critics; Zelizer on earmarking and the social meanings of money; anthropology of money and debt (Graeber, and his critics); ISO 20022's purpose codes, a working list of why payments are made; and any taxonomy of payment types used by central banks or payment networks. For each: what its list contains that the map lacks, and the reverse.
