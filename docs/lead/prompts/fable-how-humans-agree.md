A research question, philosophical first. Nothing to build and no system to design: we want to understand how humans agree before deciding anything about how a machine should help them.

**What we are trying to reach.** The lowest common denominators of agreeing: the smallest set of elements from which every human agreement can be written. A sale, a promise, shared ownership of a thing, partners splitting what it earns, a group that runs itself, a member joining or leaving it, an inheritance, a marriage, a treaty. We suspect that many traditions, including modern codes of obligations, define a great deal of size without defining much that is new. We want to know what agreeing actually is, underneath.

**Questions.**

1. What are the irreducible elements of an agreement? For each, why it cannot be removed, and what goes wrong in the traditions that left it out.
2. How have humans kept it simple? Look widely: Sumerian and Babylonian tablets (parties, the thing, the terms, witnesses, a date, a seal), Roman law, customary and oral law, the handshake, guilds and merchants' law, hawala, the commons as Elinor Ostrom described them, and a modern civil code such as the Swiss Code of Obligations. What stayed constant across them, what each added, and why.
3. Which complex arrangements are compositions of the simple elements, and which needed a genuinely new element? Show the compositions: a sale, a split of proceeds, joint ownership, a partnership, a group governing itself, succession.
4. How do agreements change, break and end? What is the minimum each tradition kept for changing an agreement, settling a dispute, leaving one, and ending one? Is leaving an element of its own, or a composition?
5. What do people trust, and why: the witness, the record, the seal, reputation, a third party, the group itself?
6. Where do legal systems grow without adding anything new? What is size that is not substance?

**How to answer.** Say where you are sure, where scholarship disagrees, and where you are speculating; cite sources where you can. Do not propose any technology or system design; that comes later, in a separate conversation. End with the shortest list of elements you would defend, and the three compositions you found most surprising.

**Form.** A written essay in plain prose, in sections, as long as it needs to be and no longer.
