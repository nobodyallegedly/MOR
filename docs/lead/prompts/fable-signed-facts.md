**The idea, as discussed with Nobody, allegedly (not decided):**
- The core defines **a summary of the facts a signer is bound by**, computed from the terms by the core's own rule, the same in every client: for example, who can end this agreement and whether you can alone; who can declare you absent, on what silence, by whose authority; what you owe and to whom; where your money goes if a promise fails; what you can always do whatever the terms (sell your share, resign).
- Facts a cMIP decides come from a **declaration in the core's vocabulary** that the cMIP's task produces, as you proposed for task 15.
- The **signature that binds a person to terms** (signing a version as a party, buying in, accepting a role) carries the **hash of the summary as data**, never as prose, so each client renders it in any language and the hash stays one.
- A verifier recomputes the summary from the terms and compares: a missing or wrong hash shows "signed without being shown", for everyone. The signer Module may compute and show the facts on its own screen before signing, as hardware wallets show an address.

**Limits already seen:** it proves the facts were computed and committed, not that a person read or understood them; a malicious client or signer can still commit the right hash and show something else, though the facts cannot be bent, only hidden, and any other client can recompute them; a cMIP may lie in its declaration, which shows afterwards.

**Costs already seen:** new grammar in a core that the stranger would halve (perhaps replacing many client-conformance passages); every task producing a declaration; vectors, a rehearsal and every client and signer rebuilt; a larger signer; the list of facts frozen with MOR 1; false comfort if the summary is taken for everything that matters.
