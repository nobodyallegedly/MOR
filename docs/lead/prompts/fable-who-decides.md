A question about the whole of Agreements, in the MOR repository (`nobodyallegedly/MOR`), on main: `spec/MIR-agreements-draft-17.md` whole, with the MICS (`spec/02-MOR-MICS-v30.md`) for the layers and the freeze test suite (`spec/03-MOR-freeze-test-suite-v30.md`) for its scenarios; `docs/state-of-mor.md`'s line for 3 November on `claude/lead-records`. Not a review of single rules: a question of shape, before the freeze.

**The trigger.** Agreements is about 60% of the six MIRs: about 90,000 of 148,000 words, longer than the other five and the MICS together. Without its italic notes and its reasoning and scenarios it is still about 65,000 words: Part one 29%, Part two (collectives) 22%, Part three (rare cases) 9%, Part four (formats) 31%, words used 8%. Collectives, with their rare cases and formats, are about a third of it.

**Nobody, allegedly's words, 3 November 2026:** "We probably gave the core too much to do." "We gave very specific tasks the MIR has to sustain, instead of asking ourselves what is the grammar of agreeing that can be used to build every single one of these ideas." "We wanted agreements to rule vows and publications, in other words selling, monetary splits, in other words redistribution, control of a collective identity, in other words management." The other five MIRs he is mostly fine with.

**Where a conversation went** (a separate, philosophical one on how humans agree, MOR not named): almost every clause of any agreement answers one of six questions: who is bound; what each side owes; when, and under what conditions; how we will know it was done; what happens if it is not; why I should believe you. Complexity enters mostly in verification and enforcement, and in trying to pre-answer "when" and "what if" for many futures, because words are incomplete (the incomplete-contracts literature).

**The MOR lead's suggestion, not decided:** what matters for incompleteness is who decides what was not written down (in that literature, ownership as the residual right to decide). If so, Agreements might need to hold only three things: what we wrote; who decides what we did not; how to stop being bound. **Nobody, allegedly's reading of it:** "What we did not write down… to me translates to cMIP." The MOR lead's addition: a cMIP may hold how the unwritten is decided, never the way out (leaving, carrying one's identity and what is one's own, succession, forking, closing), which is a guarantee no module may break.

**Please judge, plainly:**
1. Is this the right shape? Test it against Agreements as written: for each part and its larger rules (making an agreement, claims and ownership, publications and vows, splits, time and judges, keepers, changing an agreement, collectives, tiers and clones, members joining and leaving, grants, succession, abandonment, how a collective ends, a broken collective, made before and made after, the formats), say which it is: the grammar every act is written in; a guarantee no module may break; or a way of deciding the unwritten that a cMIP could hold. Use the project's admission test.
2. The scars. Much of the size answers real attacks and failures (a stolen key, a race between two payments, a member who vanishes, a thief's acts before a rotation, a collective whose rule can no longer be met). For each one you find, say whether the smaller shape still answers it in the core, would hand it to a cMIP safely, or would lose it, and what that costs whom.
3. What a smaller Agreements would be: its parts and a rough size, what moves to cMIPs (and whether a default cMIP would have to ship with the release so a collective works on day one), and what changes in Identity, Money and the MICS as a consequence.
4. The cost of doing it now: texts, core, vectors, the C verifier, the apps; the one-way doors (formats, signed messages) it would open or close; and whether any of it could wait for MOR 2 without harming the way out.
5. Where you think the suggestion is wrong.
