A first reading of MOR by someone who has never seen it, in the MOR repository (`nobodyallegedly/MOR`), on main.

**Read only the texts, and form your own view before anything else.** Start with `spec/README.md`, which names the current drafts, then the MICS (`spec/02-MOR-MICS-v37.md`), the six MIRs (Identity 16, Text 11, Envelopes 12, Money 14, Agreements 24, Development 12) and the freeze test suite v37; `core/` and `core/vectors/` if you want to see what a rule means in code. **Do not open `docs/` (findings, reviews, the lead's notes, reports) until your own view is written:** they hold the history and its reasoning, and this read is asked precisely without them. Afterwards you may look at them to say where your view differs, in a separate last section.

**Why.** MOR's core is meant to freeze and then serve for a long time, built on by people who never met its authors. Every review so far was written by readers who knew its history. This one is not.

**Please say, plainly:**
1. **What MOR is, in your own words,** from the texts alone, in a paragraph a stranger would understand. Where the texts made that hard, say where.
2. **Can it be understood without its history?** Name the places where a careful stranger gets lost, cannot tell why a rule exists, or would read a rule two ways.
3. **Does the core stay within its own admission test** (the shared language every act is written in, or a guarantee no module may break)? Name what you would take out, what is missing, and what is said more than once.
4. **The way out:** leaving, carrying one's identity and what is one's own, succession, forking, closing, the escape. Does it hold, from the texts?
5. **Who the rules serve.** Picture a person who reads rules literally, does not negotiate, does not see the trap nobody names, and needs to leave without a confrontation. Where do the texts protect that person, and where do they quietly favour whoever is stronger, better advised or more willing to play the game?
6. **The size.** Is the core as small as what it must do? If you had to say it in half the words, what would go?
7. **Your view on freezing it:** ready, ready after named changes, or not ready, and why.

Plain words first, then precisely, quoting the sentence you judge. Do not change texts, code or vectors; decide nothing.
