## The principle

- **Protect the unknown party.** An Agreements principle ("It’s an agreements principle"). A participant who joins through a role after the Terms were made (a filling) has no say in the agreement's updates, and no update reaches her: "There is no valid update for her anymore. Only a valid agreement". What she signed binds her and binds the seller to her, for as long as its words say on the clock the Terms name. Ledger: "Protect the unknown parties", "Whose place counts; protect the unknown party, as a principle", "For the unknown party there is no update".

## What stays as it is, now settled

- Named signers and buyer by assent stay cMIPs; they do not become the grammar's defaults.
- A reader lacking a cMIP answers only "unchecked"; a hand reading is nothing in MOR; whether a client accepts other methods is the client developer's choice ("What they should find is a closed door").
- A place (keeper) named only by a rule the parties never signed does not order their Terms; a reader may add "per that keeper, which I accept", labelled as the reader's.
- Nothing is added to Identity: a key change that waits is a home's service; the two keys are kept apart; rule 34 stays (a mention does not rescue a post; relying does).
- A sealed container gets no name in the core; a relay names it by its hash for its receipts.
- "Before the close" was the simulation's numbered windows.

## Words

- An uploader **lodges** an act at a relay; the relay **relays** it; a client **fetches** it. The layers are split so relays can specialize; a keeper is what an agreements relay sets itself up to be.
- Draft 26 opens with an introduction: not to be used bare; "the basic sockets to draft rule books, not one unified way of agreeing, more like a language used to decide how to agree"; the keeper introduced as where the agreement is kept; knots discussed where Rely is.

## For Dubsar's groupings

- Every grouping that turned on the defaults question: the cMIPs stay cMIPs.
- Every count that rests on a place: read it as the keeper, and check whether the Terms named it.
- Every use of "in force" by a hand reading: unchecked, as before; the answer is "a client that supports the cMIP".
- Anything built on G10.6(b), the update condition reaching a buyer: it goes.
- Subscription-shaped cMIPs (a term sold by the month): the term ends by the Terms' clock, not by an update.
