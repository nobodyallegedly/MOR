## C. Real costs of the model, for him, one at a time

1. **"Nothing later" (questions 7, and 4 in part).** With the keeper as a where and no ordered record (his 08:34), no reader can prove that nothing came after: every "not ended" is "as shown", and may change when an act turns up. This is a cost of 08:34 more than of no versions.
2. **Closing an agreement whose keeper is dead (questions 1 and 4 together).** If the ending condition must be met by acts at the old where ("act exists @"), a dead keeper means it can never be met, and the pointer only shows lineage. If the pointer's own signatures meet it from the new chain, the old where is passed over for that one act.
3. **An agreement that is a party, and ends (questions 13 and 14).** It can sign nothing more, so what names it as a party cannot end by default; and in the dual framework the outer agreement binds its founders, never its later members.
4. **Acts near a clock ending (question 16).** Knots cannot show that an act came before a time; only an anchoring act of its signer's dates it. The lead's lean: without one, not shown.
