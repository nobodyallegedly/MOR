## Where Fable's is smaller, and the MOR lead concedes

- **G, the refund (Fable R16).** No new grammar: a role share "refund", filled by the payer on a keeper's record of its contest, signed at the vow's point, with one timing clause in rule 22 (evidence acknowledged in any claim of the payment before the split) and rule 34 scoped to deals. The MOR lead's "held share on a condition" adds a mechanism this does not need.
- **F, the closing (Fable R10).** A misreading: an undecided condition makes the closing unknown, and unknown is never complete. One sentence to make it plain, not a new rule.
- **V and S (Fable R38, R39).** Misreadings: rule 60 binds field 10, rule 4 sets an unlisted rail aside before any proof is read. Both are code defects, not text changes.
- **AN (Fable R11).** A holders' rule cannot bind release on leaving (rules 94, 98.2); a misreading, not wording.

## Where the two differ for real

1. **E, leaving a split service (Fable R5).** Both say change; the mechanisms differ. The MOR lead: a publication may name in advance a rule letting the owners' side replace a partner or service. Fable: the owners' side may always, alone, end the publication or replace its service; every other version still needs every party. Fable's protects every owner without anyone having thought ahead; it removes a partner's veto by default. **His decision.**
2. **J, the default control cMIP (Fable R35).** The MOR lead said pin it by hash. Fable: it cannot be pinned (its hash covers its creator, who uploads after the freeze), so drop the default from the conformance sentence: a client holds the model the terms name, or shows unknown. The MOR lead accepts the "cannot pin" and flags what Fable's sentence leaves open: a collective whose terms name no model (field 35 absent) would then have none, which is against the trim's test Nobody, allegedly, set ("a safe default in the core, explicit choices above it"). **His decision**, with a middle way to put to him: the core names the default by what it decides (a short list of its rules in the core), not by a file.
3. **R1's reach.** Fable goes one step further than C: every act bound to an abandoned rotation becomes disputed rather than invalid (Identity everyday check 2), not only completed sales and endings. The MOR lead agrees in principle; it changes Identity, so it goes with C.
4. **AB, a collective as a confirmed successor (Fable R7).** The MOR lead: say it never is. Fable: let a collective confirm by a chain signature, or say it never is; his choice. Small.
5. **Q (Fable R24)** stated cost rather than change; **P (Fable R23)** a half-clause that may wait; **AG (Fable R33)** a cMIP's; **AM** not in Fable. The MOR lead takes Fable's marks.

## What goes to Nobody, allegedly, one at a time

1. **C / R1**, the escape: both lean to the buyer, as Identity already does for a stolen chain key.
2. **E / R5**, leaving a split service: Fable's always, or the MOR lead's by advance rule.
3. **J / R35**, the default control model: dropped, or named by what it decides.
4. **K / R34**, the claim sentence: both agree; it reverses the 23 October design note, so his sign-off.
5. **AB / R7**: a collective confirms by chain signature, or never.

A, B, D and H are way-out changes both reached with the same sentence; they can be signed off together.
