# The path to MOR 2, draft 1

*GitHub session, 13 October 2026. Branch `claude/mor-path-to-mor-2-draft-ezta89`, not merged. A document only: `spec/`, `cmips/`, `modules/`, the code and the site unchanged. Roadmap: "Toward universities", "With the first wave, the path to MOR 2".*

## In plain words

The short document that goes out with the first wave is drafted: `docs/companions/MOR-the-path-to-mor-2-draft-1.md`, about 1,700 words, eight to ten minutes to read. It is marked as a draft for you to redraft in your own hand, like the other documents that are neither core nor technical.

It says, in this order:

1. **In short.** The one sentence from the doors ("MOR is a way for people to share, pay and agree…"); MOR 1 frozen, "an experiment, not even a prototype", in your words; its rules do not change; what breaks it is recorded and becomes the ground for MOR 2. Then the call, two invitations: **break the core; build above it.**
2. **MOR 1 is frozen.** The MICS, the six MIRs, the freeze suite, the six hashes as the one root. No process for changing a MIR. A wrong rule stays wrong in MOR 1, with the finding beside it; code that misreads a rule is a bug in code, and code can be fixed. Why freeze an experiment: a moving target cannot be broken properly. How it was made, plainly (an author without a coding background, AI drafting and code, AI review, much not yet read by a human expert), as the paper says it.
3. **Break the core.** Where to start (the MICS's own three: identity continuity, ordering without a clock, whether any real use needs what the core cannot say). **How to report** (F287): to the public reporting identity on MOR, each report a signed act, kept and answered in the open; GitHub issues as a mirror for those not on MOR yet.
4. **How MOR 2 is proposed.** MIPs (the name reserved by F269, its lineage BIP and NIP), drafted layer by layer like the MIRs, Identity, Text, Envelopes, Money, Agreements, Development; a new layer gets its own MIP (F286). Judged against what MOR 1's breakage taught. What MOR 1 hands on (from the MICS's "MOR 2" section and Development rules 21 and 27): extensions in wide use as candidate tasks, succession and cloning to move across, a new tag prefix. Nobody owns MOR 2: nobody appointed, anyone's to build, people move by choice.
5. **Build now.** cMIPs, plugging in as a task, an extension or an xtype (F278, Development rules 18 and 19); Modules under a MIR or a cMIP; named by hash, signed by their maker, frozen at publication, fees owed only where an agreement signs them in. Competing on adoption, no registry (rule 17). The listing on dubsar.org: "one listing among any; anyone can run another" (F288).
6. **What we keep about you.** Nothing collected, no follow-up, the way back always open: the site's box, MOR, the contact address, GitHub.
7. **Where to read next.** The MICS, the freeze suite, the findings log, the paper, the case studies and films, with "none of them is what MOR is" (your fear that one use becomes what MOR is).

No flaw in a MIR found. Nothing in it is a new rule; where it states a rule, it is the MICS's or Development draft 8's wording, simplified.

## What it leaves for you

**Your own hand.** The voice, the order and the opening are yours to rewrite. It is written in the companions' voice, plain words first, the precise wording under each, with the decision numbers in brackets for a reader who wants to check.

**Three blanks, in square brackets, filled only by later steps:**

- the reporting identity, to be made at step 17 with the real identities;
- the listing's address on dubsar.org, once it is built (F288 says before the first wave);
- the six hashes are not quoted; the document points to `spec/`. Whether to print them in the text once frozen is yours.

**Questions it does not answer, because nothing decided says:**

1. **Who answers reports.** F287 says each report is "kept and answered in the open", but not by whom. Until the collective exists, the reporting identity's keys are someone's: yours, the machine agent's, or another. The draft says "reports are answered" and names nobody.
2. **An attack that hurts real users today.** "In the open" suits a frozen rule, which nobody can fix anyway. It suits less well a bug in a client, a relay or the core library that could let someone steal keys from people using MOR 1 now; projects usually allow a quiet first report for those. The draft does not offer one, because F287 does not. If you want one, it is a decision (and a stated cost either way).
3. **"Judged against what MOR 1's breakage taught": judged by whom?** Nobody owns MOR 2, so the draft frames this as the question a MIP has to answer ("which recorded break does this answer, and what does it cost?"), not as a jury. If you meant more, say so.
4. **GitHub reports into the open record.** The draft says what is reported on GitHub is "carried into the same open record". F287 says GitHub mirrors; whether someone re-publishes GitHub reports on MOR, and as whom, is not decided.
5. **The paragraph on how MOR was made.** Taken from the paper's section 2, which you approved. It is short here; keep, cut or rewrite.
