## Purpose

This is the end-game checklist for freezing the MOR core. It is for the founding team, not for readers of the protocol.

**Freeze rule:** the MICS is frozen whole, its six MIRs together, at the first release. Before that, each scenario below is either **run**, exercised against the founding components and passed, or **reasoned**, walked through on paper against the texts without running code. Every scenario the founding components can run is run. The freeze publishes, with the six texts and their hashes, a **freeze report** marking each scenario and each core component run or reasoned. The cMIPs and Modules used may be clunky; only the core has to hold. A failure found before the freeze that needs a core change is a finding, fixed before the freeze.

*A reasoned rule is frozen on argument, not evidence. The report says so, so nobody mistakes one for the other, and the next reviewer knows where to press first.*

**Conformance versus validity.** Some pass criteria name rules only the signer's own client can honour (what you sign is what you saw; showing what a rule computes; never defaulting to a device-bound policy; keepers never holding content keys). They are tested by inspecting the founding clients, not by verifiers, and are marked *(conformance)*.

**What an answer is.** Money rule 12 judges an answer as a whole, counting the pairs it depends on. For the freeze, each question the test vectors ask of a verdict is one answer; a list among them (a vow's sales) is one answer, judged as Money rule 12 says for a list.

**The model in v38's steps.** The words below are used in the steps v38 rewrote as the ledger entries beside them have them; nothing here is a rule beyond them.

- An agreement has no versions that replace one another. It lives until its **ending condition** is met: signatures, or any other condition the bytes can check, a clock among them. Any change is that end and a new agreement; ended with no new agreement, it is **dissolved** (Agreements, “No versions: the chain guards the end and forces knots”; “Nothing is updated: new acts”; “The ending condition”).
- A new agreement's first act may carry the **closing pointer**: it names an old agreement, says that it is no longer valid, and that it once existed. It closes the old agreement only where its signatures meet the old one's ending condition, wherever the new one is kept, the old keeper dead or not; one act may carry several, each meeting its own (Agreements, “The closing pointer: no longer valid, and it once existed”; “A keeper is a relay: lodge, relay, fetch”; the MOR lead's readings A1 and B20). In a step, a new agreement **pointing to** an old one carries the closing pointer to it; an agreement's **first act** is the act that publishes its terms, its signer's assent; the pointer's **signatures** are those of the parties who sign that first act. Without the pointer a new chain is born, and the old agreement's tip may come back when its keeper returns (Agreements, “Ending the old agreement is an act on its chain”).
- Two acts that both end one agreement, nothing ordering them, are a **fork**: neither takes effect, the agreement stays as it was, and its own rule, or a resolver it names, settles it (Agreements, “A fork is a fork”; the MOR lead's reading A2).
- An **unknown party** takes part through a spot. While an obligation toward one is open, the agreement is not dissolved, whatever its ending condition says; where a verifier cannot tell whether one is open, the dissolution is **not shown**, never a yes. Once the ending condition is met with a filling open, the spot takes no new identities, no new assent counts, and a payment naming the agreement is read under its words. The known parties are protected by their signatures, or the scheme of signatures they agreed to (Agreements, “No dissolving with open obligations to unknown parties”; “Protect the unknown party is an Agreements principle”; the MOR lead's readings B5 and B12).
- A collective that changes who it is is a new agreement, a new party; the old one runs on while it has obligations through standing agreements; each counterparty, asked only about its own dealings, **renews or exits**, and nothing carries it over (Agreements, “A change of who they are: renew or exit”).
- An act that names a clock is anchored to it; its place against a time is shown only by its own anchoring. An agreement that states a clock is **provisional until anchored**: until its anchoring is shown it is not shown as in force, with the reason "not yet anchored", and nothing counts under it. Only a condition in time that names no clock is refused. An ending by clock triggers: no further act is needed (Agreements, “An act that names a clock is anchored to it”; the MOR lead's reading B15).
- A **keeper** is a relay where the agreement's acts are: it accepts the lodge, relays the act and lets clients fetch it. An act counts for the agreement where it is there; the keeper keeps no order in the core. Where the bytes cannot confirm that an act was there, the answer is **unchecked**, every signed act that bears on it shown beside it as evidence. Where several keepers are named, every one must show an act, unless a cMIP in their socket lifts it (Agreements, “The keeper's ordered record is a service, not the core”; “A keeper is a relay: lodge, relay, fetch”; “Rely when a relay dies: a proof, not one for the bytes”; “Keepers: cross verification by default, a socket to lift it”). Order is what the acts' own knots show; where a step needs an order no knot shows, it names a keeper service, a stand-in.
- An answer of absence ("not ended", "missed", "no obligation open") is given **as far as the acts held show**, naming how many keepers it rests on; it is never a proof that nothing came after (Agreements, “"As far as I can see" is the honest answer”).
- A step's answer marked *open* is one the ledger and the MOR lead's readings do not settle; it is listed, with a lean, beside the suite (`docs/freeze-suite-v38-changes.md`). A cMIP a step needs in a socket is named `stand-in: <what it does>`: a stand-in, for DubLaw's launch set to fill (Core components, "Stand-ins").
- Where terms name no ending condition, it is every known party's signature (the MOR lead's answer 1). A new agreement whose closing pointer misses the old one's ending condition is in force among its signers, each bound by both, and the old agreement goes on; a client warns before such a pointer is signed (the MOR lead's answer 2).
- **The cMIPs, and the core beneath them.** In Agreements, what an agreement does is always a cMIP's answer; no agreement step is answered by the core alone, and Agreements is not to be used bare (Agreements, “Shares and how they move are the cMIPs'”; “A late version 3 is rejected”). What a share is, how it moves, who is paid, what is owed back, are the cMIPs'. So every agreement step ends with **Runs on**, naming the cMIPs it runs on, by socket where the step gives one, each answer that is an agreement's doing given as that cMIP's, and without the cMIP, unchecked; and with **Beneath, it passes if**, naming the grammar's guarantees the step tests, which no cMIP may break: who signed; what names what; a fork; the stated where; an anchoring; the unknown party's protection (the MOR lead's reading of 13:58 in “Shares and how they move are the cMIPs'”, to check). A step a cMIP cannot change carries neither, and is listed beside the suite.
- **A closing pointer while obligations stand** (the MOR lead's answer 24, to check): a closing pointer closes the old agreement to every new commitment; the acts that deliver its standing obligations are still made under it, by its last rules, by those its ending condition and powers name, until each is delivered; then nothing more is made under it.

A holder that is a collective is paid at the collective's pointer, which leads to the split service its split agreement names, which receipts in its own name and splits again by the collective's stake. A payout of the owners' line to anyone who is no holder of the stake in the version in force for the receipt is a mismatch; so is a payout to the publisher on the owners' line. A receipt the publisher's service signs as a holder's payout is invalid, signed by another than the hop's receiver, whatever rail's proof it carries: each holder receipts its own payout.
8a. **The service the publication names, and its backups.** A publication names its split service in field 3 and may name backups in order (field 11), with what counts as a failed attempt (field 12): a payment is paid at field 3's service, a payout of a later layer from a backup counting only where, for that payment, the attempt at field 3's failed as the split cMIP defines failure, and the next payment is tried at field 3's again; a payout to anyone the parties did not sign is a mismatch whatever is stated; a verifier not holding the split cMIP's answer reads a payment to the first service as received under the publication and one to a backup as unknown, never as a mismatch. A lone owner who self-publishes (one holder, every share hers, no partner) is her own split service, the owners' line hers. A work of two owners whose publication names one of them as its split service (an owner acting as treasury) is paid to that owner, who pays the other its share. A publication with no field 3 is out of format. An owner that is another ownership agreement is paid through it, its part divided by its own stake.
8b. **A payment split exactly, each cut at its flow address.** A fan buys the publisher's publication for 1,000 units, naming its price tag, whose payee is its split service S. S gives no payment request within the wait the publication's field 12 sets, so the attempt failed, and the fan's wallet pays the backup T, field 11's, the payment still naming the tag. T receipts it in its own name and splits it under the same plan: the publisher's 150 off the top, then the owners' line of 850, 510 to Ana and 340 to Ben, by the ownership agreement's stake, read by reference. Each receipts its payout. Each participant's check (Agreements rule 18) finds its whole cut at its own flow address; the three cuts sum to the 1,000 paid; a cut whose receipt the rail places elsewhere is shown so; before any split, the cut is the service's open obligation. The next payment is tried at S again.
9. *(Agreements, “An act that names a clock is anchored to it”)* **A subscription that states a clock** *(new in v38)*. The subscription's publication states a clock: its month is counted on a block height. Its first act is anchored to that clock. Before the anchoring is shown, the publication is provisional: a verifier shows it as not in force, with the reason "not yet anchored", never as invalid, and nothing counts under it, a listener's assent waiting with it; once the anchoring is shown, the publication is read from its anchor. A listener's assent, naming the month, is anchored to the clock too, and her month is counted from her assent's own anchoring (Agreements, “Ana's month ends by the clock the agreement names”). A second publication whose words sell "one month" and whose terms name no clock is refused in every client: a condition in time that names no clock, the only case refused (Agreements, “An act that names a clock is anchored to it”). A publication that names a clock and whose anchoring a verifier does not hold is not refused: it waits, not shown, "not yet anchored".
10. *(Agreements, “No dissolving with open obligations to unknown parties”)* **No dissolving while a listener's month is open** *(new in v38)*. The service and the creator groups, every known party of the subscription's publication, sign its ending, meeting its ending condition, no new publication following. A listener paid for a month that has not run out on the clock: her filling is open, an obligation toward an unknown party. The publication is not dissolved while it is: its spot takes no new identities, no new assent counts, and a payment naming it is read under its words (the MOR lead's reading B5); her keys for the month are still owed, and she keeps what she bought, nothing later reaching her (Agreements, “Protect the unknown party is an Agreements principle”). Once her month has run out on the clock, no obligation open, the publication is dissolved. A verifier that does not hold the cMIP that answers whether an obligation is open (`stand-in: open obligation check`), or that lacks a listener's filling, cannot tell: it does not show the publication dissolved, and says why, never a yes. An answer that no obligation is open is given as far as the acts held show, naming the keepers it rests on (Agreements, “"As far as I can see" is the honest answer”). A new publication pointing to the old one, made while her month is open, reaches nothing of hers: she is bound by the publication she bought under.

**Passes if:** both sharing models run side by side and each creator can see which applies to them, every monthly split balances, the lapse is verifiable against the time reference, access stops without any core change, and the failed month is visible and owed, including what only the payers' claims revealed; money passes layer by layer, pool, publication, owners, each split service receipting in its own name and splitting under its own publication, partners first and the owners' line by the ownership agreement's stake, each receipt it has not split its open obligation, and no service signs in anyone else's name; and a publication ends only as its ending condition answers, an ending's place against its clock shown by its own anchoring, unknown to a reader without its cMIP, never in force by default, everything received before still split and paid (7.7 to 7.7d); a publication that states a clock is provisional until anchored, shown not in force with the reason "not yet anchored", and refused only where a condition in time names no clock (7.9); and no publication is dissolved while a listener's obligation is open, a verifier that cannot tell never showing it dissolved (7.10).

### 8. Specifications

1. The six MIRs are uploaded as specifications with no creator; anyone can upload them again and their spec hashes stay the same. A read-only client recomputes the six hashes from the texts it ships, and shows them; two clients built by two people recompute the same six. A frozen specification committed with a trailing space, or with two final line breaks, fails the repository's check; one final line break is no part of the text, and the hash is the same with it or without it. Two clients map an agreement's task numbers to the same tasks.
2. **No published rule in MOR 1.** A cMIP author uploads a split cMIP with a verification rule on the RV32IM profile, its binary carried as a locked object the specification names. *The profile is a specification of its own, uploaded after the freeze, named by hash and adopted by choice: a Module whose field 5 names the Development MIR, each rule naming it by its spec hash in its `rule-ref`; until then each founding cMIP and Module with a verification rule says in its text that the rule is a reference implementation, not a program uploaded under Development rule 22. This step is run once a profile is uploaded.* A fixed version is uploaded later, naming the first as its predecessor. The first stays on record, frozen once uploaded. A stranger uploads a "successor" naming the same predecessor; a client shows the creator mismatch.
3. An agreement names an extension for a kind of work no task covers; its specification declares, in field 10, the layers it acts on beyond Development. A client without the extension cannot sign; a client with it enforces the extra rule. Two clients read the same layers from field 10; a specification whose field 10 is out of order, or carries a number other than 0 to 4 (0 Identity, 1 Text, 2 Envelopes, 3 Money, 4 Agreements: 5, Development's own, and 7 among those refused), is no specification in both: a listing shows it so, and an act naming it is unknown in every client, never invalid unless a rule the verifier can apply shows it so; for a rail Module, see step 8.9. An extension that tries to allow a split not summing exactly is overridden by the core: the act is rejected.
4. A signature-scheme specification is uploaded. An identity rotates to a signing key of that scheme; a client without it shows the rotation as valid and the identity's later acts as unknown; a client with it verifies them. An identity born under that scheme, read by a client without it: a later rotation the client can verify, and every act under it, unknown, never invalid.
5. Two clients compute the same running summary for a three-act sequence from the published test vector.
6. A cMIP that checks proofs of delivery is uploaded with field 12 in its specification; two clients read it as a proof-of-delivery checker and find it by it. A publication selling a proven delivery names it in its access form; a verifier asks its checker for the proof's verdict, and checks the proof, never the cMIP's word. No agreement names it by a task number. A second publication selling a proven delivery names a cMIP whose specification lacks field 12: the verifier asks its checker just the same, field 12 serving discovery only.

7. Two specifications are uploaded, by two identities, each as a public bake naming the Development MIR's spec hash as its media spec; a third bake carries a specification's content under another media spec. A listing reading those relays finds the two specifications and takes the third for none; a second listing, any other, must find the same. A release names a specification in its manifest's "implements" by its spec hash, a bake beside it as a hint only (under a release manifest cMIP); a reader holding another bake of the same specification finds it; a hint whose bake is not of that specification is shown as a wrong hint, and changes nothing the entry names; a client making the release refuses to name the third's content as a specification.
8. Two tools upload the split cMIP's text, one writing field 2 and one not: a listing shows two specifications of one text, as versions, each with its own spec hash, with the fields in which they differ; a client adopting by text finds both; an act of a type the text defines, with no field 3 anywhere, is judged by the text, never by field 3.
9. Two rail Modules are uploaded with field 11 (the rail's kind) and field 13 (the set its proof binds); the payment cMIP reads the kind and the binding from the specification the receipt names, the same whatever build of the Module a client runs. A rail Module naming in field 5 a payment cMIP (one whose field 2 lists Money's task 6) without fields 11 and 13 is no specification, and a receipt naming it is unknown in every client, never invalid unless a rule the verifier can apply shows it so; the payment cMIP adopts a Module only from its specification.
10. The MICS document and the freeze suite are uploaded under kinds 6 and 7, with no creator; a MIP forking the Money MIR is uploaded under kind 8, with its creator and, in its predecessor field, the MIR it forks. A listing shows the MICS document and the suite as such, never as MIRs, and the MIP in the MIPs' part of its own, under the Money MIR, the MIR's fork count 1; a second MIP forking the first stands under it, the first's count 1. A MIP proposing a new layer names the MICS document in its predecessor field and is listed under it; one naming no predecessor is no specification.
11. **A request rail binds payee and purpose.** A request rail Module (field 11 is 0) is uploaded with field 13 holding the payee but not the purpose, and another with the purpose but not the payee: each is no specification, and a receipt naming it is unknown in every client, never invalid unless a rule the verifier can apply shows it so; uploaded with both, each is a specification.
12. **A disowned bake leaves the specification unclaimed.** A creator uploads a specification, and a thief with the creator's stolen signing key uploads two more in the creator's name; an adopter relies on one of the thief's (a valid act acknowledging it). The creator rotates, keeping the honest bake and disowning the thief's two: the honest one stays claimed; each of the thief's is unclaimed by the creator, the one relied on shown as disputed and unclaimed all the same; a stranger's bake of a specification naming the creator claims nothing for the creator; a bake the verifier does not hold is unknown.

**Passes if:** spec hashes are stable wherever uploaded, every uploading tool and listing agrees on what a specification's bake names as its media spec, every client computes the same summaries, verification under an uploaded profile waits for the profile, a client recomputes the six hashes from the texts it ships, every specification carries what its kind requires, and a malformed one is no specification, an act naming it unknown, a rail's kind and binding are read from its specification, and the MICS document, a suite and a MIP are listed under their own kinds, the extension adds a rule without relaxing any core rule, no client signs an agreement naming an extension it lacks, and a new scheme is adopted without any core change.

### 9. The way out, walked

*Each exit from a work, a collective or the protocol, written as the acts it needs, so the way out is shown and not only argued. Each step names who signs, and what a verifier checks to tell it counts.*

1. **An owner leaving a work.** A song's ownership agreement targets its claim; its stake gives Ana 400,000 and Ben 600,000. Two publications read it by reference: one through a label, a partner taking 150,000, and Ana and Ben's own, self-published, with no partner. Ana sells her whole share to Cy: a transfer (Agreements type 4) Ana signs, by a chain signature too, and Cy completes; it names the ownership agreement, its chain; Ben is notified, not asked (Agreements rule 10). Neither publication changes. A purchase under either, whose split follows the complete transfer (split key 8), pays Cy 400,000 of every million left on the owners' line; one whose split follows only Ana's signature still pays Ana; once the transfer is complete, both signatures placed, a split paying Ana for the share breaks the plan, and from the service's first split citing the transfer (split key 8), every such payout, receipted or not, is the split service's open obligation to Cy (rules 10 and 30). A payout receipt Ana signs for the share, bound to a rotation later than her transfer's chain signature, is a wrong receipt. *(Agreements rules 10, 12 and 15.)*
2. **A member leaving a collective.** A collective of three, whose key grammar gives each member a share of the chain key, any two rebuilding it, with an escrowed share the abandonment authority releases as its recovery path. Its third member resigns alone; any member draws a record registering the resignation, naming the collective and its previous decision: that record is the line. The two who stay sign a new agreement pointing to the collective's, without the third, under its constitutional change rule, and a rotation of the collective's chain key, rebuilt from the two shares, declares it, re-dealing the key to the two, both needed, and an escrowed share of the new key where the recovery path puts it, the way that survives the loss of either. The collective has changed who it is: each of its counterparties is asked to renew with it or exit, nothing carrying it over. The departed member keeps their share of the collective's stake, a departed holder. **Their power ends at the line**: an act they sign naming the collective, after the line, counts for nothing, from any device; a signature of theirs placed before the line still counts for the act it signs; **their own key continues to sign their own acts**: a post of their own, a purchase of their own, a publication they are a partner of, all stand, untouched. An act they sign naming the collective with nothing to back it is nobody's. *(Agreements, “A change of who they are: renew or exit”.)*
3. **Succession.** A collective's member dies. Their plan passes their seat to a colleague and their share to two children in equal parts. The abandonment authority's declaration, its outcome removing the dead member's voice (outcome 0), triggers it; any member draws the record registering the declaration, its line. The seat passes by a new agreement pointing to the collective's; the share by succession, its pay unchanged. The board has changed: each counterparty is asked to renew or exit. In a second collective, the dead member held the chain key alone, with an escrowed share: their plan names one successor for the key holding, never two; the succession clone carries a succession plan for that successor, so **a single chain-key holder still has a named successor and an escrowed share**; a founding naming a single holder with neither is refused. In a third, the seat passes to a member already a party, who then holds both seats, every threshold unchanged; before any party signs that plan, its client shows what each successor would hold after it *(conformance)*. In a fourth, a collective of one, nobody but the declared member remains to draw a record: the declaration takes effect at the recovery rotation the recovery path makes, the rotation that removes them; where the declaration's authority were a threshold needing others, that rotation would name their signature acts. *(Agreements, “A change of who they are: renew or exit”.)*
4. **Closing and fork.** A collective that cannot agree forks: each side founds its successor first, pointing to the original; a member draws the fork act naming the collective, its line, and the successors; the members on each side chain-sign it under the constitutional change rule, and each successor assigned a debt chain-signs it too; once complete the original is closed, and nothing done for it afterwards counts (as step 3.9, whose questions stay open). The original's shares in works pass to the successors in the shares the fork names. **A publication whose ownership agreement names the closed collective as owner**: a payment after the fork's line is read under its words (step 3.9aj); so for a publication whose ownership agreement is itself ended by a release. A second collective dissolves: a member draws the closing, chain-signed under the constitutional change rule, no obligation toward an unknown party open (Agreements, “No dissolving with open obligations to unknown parties”). *(Agreements, “A fork is a fork”; “No dissolving with open obligations to unknown parties”.)*
5. **Rotation and escape.** A collective rotates its chain key: the key is rebuilt from the members' shares, the rotation signed with it, revealing it and committing the next; its homes receipt it as any chain act (Identity rule 20). Its home turns hostile, refusing the rotation and objecting to a homeless one. **The collective escapes**: a homeless rotation signed with its chain key, rebuilt from k of its members' shares, is its own escape endorsement; the old home's objection voids nothing; a home reading Identity alone counts it, since the genesis declares no signing key; a person's homeless rotation under the same objection, with no endorsement, does not count, the person's chain ending before it (Identity rules 49 and 53, "The escape endorsement"). *Stated cost: what protects a collective from a thief is its key grammar, not its homes.* A collective's genesis is signed the same way, by a chain key revealed in its signature, its commitment naming the next (Identity rule 1). *(Identity rules 1, 7, 20, 49 and 53.)*
6. **A refund owed back.** A publication's parties end it by its ending condition, no new publication following; its split service has received a payment on a push rail, recorded after the service's own signature on that version: a payment racing the ending, read under the publication's words, here owed back (the MOR lead's reading B12). The service has already paid out the partner's share and part of the owners' line. **It is owed back by the split service from what it holds, and past that by each party in the share it received, on the owners' line each holder paid, their own payout**, each an open obligation, visible until repaid; an anonymous payer's refund is owed to the fresh identity the payment committed to, and paid where its claim names (Money rule 17). A tag withdrawn by its signer alone, a version with no price, stops sales from then on; past sales stand. A purchase the buyer contests, by a contest naming their claim, is shown beside it, changing nothing. *(Agreements, “The ending condition”; Money rules 17 and 18.)*
7. **A work cloned to MOR 2.** What crosses is the bake (its bytes), the claim and the ownership agreement, each in core format: a MOR 2 client reads who made the work and who owns what share. A successor changes the tag prefix, so nothing replays: each is made anew under MOR 2, saying what it said, who made the work and who owns what share, with every hash new, its work hash and the claim it targets among them (Development rule 27: every hash is new). The publications are distribution: their partners, split service and split plan are remade under MOR 2's own rules. **What MOR 1 checks is the source alone**: the bake, the claim and the ownership agreement each valid in core format, read without any publication; how MOR 2 checks what it makes anew is MOR 2's. *(MICS, "MOR 2".)*

**Passes if:** an owner leaves a work by the transfer alone, every publication paying the buyer from its split service's first split following the complete transfer, both signatures placed, and owing the buyer every payout to the seller from its first split citing it (9.1); a member's power for the collective ends at the line that registers their departure while their own acts stay theirs, and each counterparty renews or exits (9.2); a seat and a share pass by the plan, a single chain-key holder's successor and escrowed share are always named, and the client shows what each successor would hold (9.3); a payment under a publication over an owner a fork or closing ended is read under its words, and nothing is dissolved while an obligation toward an unknown party is open (9.4); a keyless collective rotates, and escapes a hostile home by its homeless rotation, its own escape endorsement, which no member signs (9.5); a payment racing an ending is read under the publication's words, here owed back by the split service, then by each party, on the owners' line each holder paid, their own payout (9.6); and a work's bake, claim and ownership agreement, the source of its crossing to MOR 2, are each valid in core format, read without its publications (9.7).

## Freeze procedure

1. Fix what the texts fix only at the freeze: each frozen text's header becomes the text's name alone (for instance "# MIR: Identity", as Development rule 26 writes it), with no draft number or approval line, since the header is part of the hashed text; the SHA-256 of the X-Wing draft's revision 11, written into Envelopes as `XWING`; and then the six MIRs' spec hashes, computed as Development rule 26 says, with the MICS document's and this suite's. *The RV32IM profile and its vectors are not among them: the profile is a specification of its own, uploaded after the freeze. The signature-scheme and unit specifications and the pinned Unicode version are written in the texts; how a home is queried is the relay transport cMIP's; the running-summary vectors over stand-in ids are made public with the conformance vectors, the three-act sequence's final once the hashes are.*
2. Build the founding cMIPs and Modules: a home relay, a basic relay, the genesis client (with the air-gapped chain key Module), a read-only client and a basic client; and, so every layer meets friction, a rail Module (Money), a split Module and a deal-assessment tool (Agreements). Clunky is acceptable.
3. Run every scenario the founding components can run; reason through the rest against the texts. Mark each run or reasoned, ticking each component as it passes.
4. Machine adversarial review: the texts, the code and the draft freeze report, reviewed by a model given the budget to break things and propose fixes.
5. Specialist questions: from the draft freeze report, each rule that is only reasoned becomes a targeted question to a specialist in its field, one person and one question at a time. Then human adversarial review: one reviewer attacks the whole, the running prototypes as well as the texts. Nobody outside decides the freeze.
6. Fix any failure requiring a core change, and rerun or re-reason the affected scenarios.
7. When every box is ticked, run or reasoned, the MICS is frozen: the six MIRs' texts, committed as canonical text, their hashes and the freeze report are made public together.

After the freeze, a flaw in the core is fixed only by a new protocol running alongside, with users migrating by choice.
