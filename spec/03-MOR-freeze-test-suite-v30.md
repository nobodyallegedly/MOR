# MOR Freeze Test Suite

*Version 30, freeze-ready, for Nobody, allegedly, to approve.*

*This suite tests the six freeze-ready MIRs: Identity draft 15 (its "Written for people" section included), Text draft 11, Envelopes draft 11, Money draft 11, Agreements draft 17 and Development draft 11, mapped by MICS v30. A rule is cited by its MIR's name and number ("Agreements rule 76"); a step is cited by scenario and number ("step 3.7ab"). Where a step and a MIR differ, the MIR decides.*

## Purpose

This is the end-game checklist for freezing the MOR core. It is for the founding team, not for readers of the protocol.

**Freeze rule:** the MICS is frozen whole, its six MIRs together, at the first release. Before that, each scenario below is either **run**, exercised against the founding components and passed, or **reasoned**, walked through on paper against the texts without running code. Every scenario the founding components can run is run. The freeze publishes, with the six texts and their hashes, a **freeze report** marking each scenario and each core component run or reasoned. The cMIPs and Modules used may be clunky; only the core has to hold. A failure found before the freeze that needs a core change is a finding, fixed before the freeze.

*A reasoned rule is frozen on argument, not evidence. The report says so, so nobody mistakes one for the other, and the next reviewer knows where to press first.*

**Conformance versus validity.** Some pass criteria name rules only the signer's own client can honour (what you sign is what you saw; showing what a rule computes; never defaulting to a device-bound policy; keepers never holding content keys). They are tested by inspecting the founding clients, not by verifiers, and are marked *(conformance)*.

**What an answer is.** Money rule 12 judges an answer as a whole, counting the pairs it depends on. For the freeze, each question the test vectors ask of a verdict is one answer; a list among them (a vow's sales) is one answer, judged as Money rule 12 says for a list.

7w. **A broken collective, and its way back.** A collective of four, its constitutional change rule every party whose voice remains, removes a member who resigned, but the record registering the resignation is lost on its way to the relays, so Agreements still counts four voices; the rotation declares a clone whose mark names only the three who stay. The mark is false (Agreements rule 46): the collective is broken from that rotation, the broken act. A release its members sign during the broken stretch counts for nothing, and every client says the collective is broken, with Agreements' reason. **A resignation during the broken stretch:** a second member resigns, naming the agreement in force just before the broken act (here the founding agreement); a record drawn in the stretch registering it is no line. **The rollback:** a later rotation declares a clone of the founding agreement without the two who resigned, naming the broken act and registering both resignations, its mark naming the constitutional change rule with the two members who remain, who both sign: the collective works again under that clone, its identity chain carrying on, the broken act and the release still shown and still counting for nothing; a release signed after it counts. A rollback marked with the clone rule, or whose parent is the broken clone, or naming a later rotation as the broken act, or registering an abandonment declaration, puts nothing in force, and the collective stays broken. A rollback that registers neither resignation needs all four signatures. Where every voice has resigned, nothing meets the rule, and no rollback is possible. **The last voice:** in a collective of two, after one member has left, the client of the other, before their resignation is signed, says that they are the last voice that remains, that the collective's works will be frozen, and that nobody will be able to change, release or move them; they may still sign it, and the works then freeze as they stand, a stated cost (Agreements rule 70). During a broken stretch, the same warning is given to the last voice that remains for the rollback. **Quarantine**: a grantee of the collective, under a grant made before the break, signs an act during the broken stretch citing only its grant: it counts for nothing, and stays void after the rollback even acknowledged by the collective (signed anew, never adopted); an act the grantee made before the break, cited by an act of the collective before it, stands; a grant made during the stretch backs nothing; after the rollback, the grantee's act citing it counts. **What the collective keeps is judged by the publication a payment was made under**: a payment receipted for the collective during the stretch under a publication it signed before the break is kept; one under a publication it signed during the stretch (or paid to the collective itself under its split agreement of the stretch), receipted for it or shown only by the payer's claim, is owed back to its payer, shown as an open obligation; an acknowledgement of that receipt after the rollback does not change that; a receipt drawn for the collective for the same payment after the rollback signs the sale anew, and it is no longer owed back. **After the rollback**, the grantee's act citing nothing newer than the time before the break counts for nothing, one made just before the break that no act of the collective took in included; citing the rollback, it counts. **A declaration of absence during the stretch**: a member vanishes; the absence authority of the agreement in force just before the broken act declares them absent; the rollback registers the declaration, and, where the clause asks for two of the other members, the other's signature act on it, which the rollback names and places; without it, or with only an acknowledgement made during the stretch, the rollback puts nothing in force; with it, the vanished member no longer counts and the rollback completes. The member's client shows them the declaration, with the way to contest it, and **the member signs a contest**: it names the declaration, is shown beside it, and voids nothing; a "contest" by anyone else shows nothing. **A declaration sealed to the other members only, never to the member it names, counts for nothing**: a record registering it removes no voice; sealed to that member too, it counts. **A technical break**: a rotation removing the collective's Agreements declaration, or carrying one in neither form, is a broken act; a rollback brings the collective back. **No ending before the fix**: a closing every member signs during the stretch counts for nothing, then and after the rollback; a client refuses to prepare one; **a closing every member signs on the last good link before the broken act counts for nothing while the collective is broken**; and after the rollback, a closing every member signs counts for nothing while a payment under a publication of the stretch is owed back. **Repaid**: a payment back to the payer naming the payment owed back, its rail's proof unchecked, repays nothing; part of it, by the payer's own receipt, leaves it owed; the rest, by a claim drawn for the collective paid to the payer's pointer, repays it: it is no longer owed, and the closing is no longer blocked by it. **The debt rule**: the payer's own claim on the payment names a refund rail: a repayment to their pointer repays nothing, one to that rail repays it (up to the payer's vault limit for its unit, above it only once it reaches the vault: Money rule 17); the payer's own claim holds an older pointer: a repayment to a newer one repays nothing, one to the older repays it; with no act of theirs on the payment, their pointer in force. **A bare key**: an anonymous payer committed a one-time key and signed a claim with it naming a refund rail; a claim drawn for the collective, its rail's proof showing the money reached that rail, repays it. **Owed to nobody**: a payment that committed no key, owed back: a closing not naming it is blocked; one naming it in field 4 is not, and the money stays shown as owed. **A payer with no address**: a closing naming the payment with no notice is blocked; with a notice of the collective sealed to the payer and every member, its deadline not yet passed, blocked; passed with no address given, as the time reference shows, not blocked, a holder named beside it shown; a notice sealed to someone else only, blocked. **The last constitutional voice**: in a collective whose constitutional change rule names two of three members, after one of the two has left, the client of the other says, before their resignation is signed, "You are about to break the collective's constitutional layer. Once you leave, nobody will be able to change its rules again. The other members keep acting in their areas.": the constitution freezes as it stands while the third keeps a voice in the area. **A returned member**: a member resigns, a record registers it, and a constitutional clone naming them, which they sign, brings them back; a record registering the old resignation again takes nothing from them, and neither does a rollback registering it (Agreements refuse it before the rotation); a resignation they sign after their return counts; the client closes their departed entry, and its rollback never names them as leaving. **An old stepping down**: a holder steps down from an area, a record registers it, and a constitutional clone naming them its holder again, which they sign, gives it back; a record registering the old stepping down again takes nothing from them; a stepping down they sign after the return counts. **Never registered**: a member resigns, or steps down from an area, and it is never registered; a later constitutional clone naming them, as member or holder, which they sign, spends it: registered years later, it takes nothing; a resignation signed after that clone counts. A departing member's signature placed on the clone the rollback puts in force still counts there, and the rollback still registers their resignation. **A collective whose first agreement fails** has nothing to roll back to, and its client says it is founded again. **The rollback, in detail:** a rollback declaring the broken clone itself puts nothing in force; a member whose departure a line registered before the broken act is not a member after the rollback, is written to the departed entry, and is dealt no share of the new chain key; a resignation of the stretch that the relays lost is refused by Agreements before the rollback's rotation is sent, and the client never says "done" unless Agreements, reading the relays, finds the rollback's clone in force; each voice resigning from its own device, the last is warned, its client reading the others' uploaded resignations; leaving while Agreements cannot be read says that no last-voice warning can be given; leaving when this device's copy is a version behind names the version Agreements find in force; a member's signature placed before the rollback counts there; an acknowledgement made in the stretch places no signature on a declaration; stepping down during the stretch draws no record and is registered by the rollback, whose clone leaves that member out of the area.
7x. *(Agreements rule 48)* **A departed holder, paid less.** A collective of three holds its income in its own stake, 400,000, 300,000 and 300,000, which its split agreement's owners' line divides; the third member, Cy, leaves and keeps 300,000 as a departed holder. 7y. *(Agreements rules 48, 87 and 89)* **A departed holder's stake moves by succession, its pay never cut.** In 3.7x's collective, Cy names a stake successor. A declaration of absence against Cy stating outcome 1 is refused; one removing Cy's voice is valid. 7z. **What the line placed stays placed.** A member resigns; the collective registers it and lives a year under that line; the member then rotates and disowns the resignation: the line stands, every record after it stands, the resignation is shown as disputed, and the member comes back only by a constitutional clone naming them. The same with a declaration of absence registered by a line whose authority rotates and disowns it, and with the declared member disowning the signature field 1 names: the record stays a line, the declaration shown disputed. *Stated cost: a thief holding a member's signing key can resign the member; once the collective's line registers it, the member's rotation does not undo the departure for the collective.*

7aa. **A hole in the history voids nothing.** A verifier missing one act a line's history names: whether an act is before that line is unknown to it, nothing is void on it, and no voice is taken away; it answers unknown, and judges again once it holds the act. An action citing a decision the verifier does not hold is unknown; held, and no decision of the collective, it counts for nothing.
7ab. **A disowned vote stays placed.** A holder votes on an owners' matter; the record names the signature; the holder rotates and disowns it: the change stays in force, the vote shown disputed.
7ac. **An issuance.** A collective's stake in itself carries a sixty per cent holders' rule; owners holding sixty per cent decide an issuance: every existing share is scaled alike, and each holder is offered its proportion on the same terms by an issuance offer, public or sealed to that holder, naming its period on the collective's time reference. One holder lets its period lapse; one takes up its proportion and pays, its take-up counting where the time reference places it within the period, placed by the collective's line or not; the rest goes to the new holders the offer names. An offer that did not reach a holder starts no period for that holder, and the issuance waits. An issuance every holder signs needs no offer. A standing waiver of pre-emption in the stake's holders' rule, set by every holder, lets an issuance the holders' rule decides go without offers, and binds a buyer who bought under it; set or removed by fewer than every holder, the version carrying it is a draft. One giving a holder that took up less than its proportion, or scaling one share unlike the others, is a draft; a share added any other way needs every holder. *Stated cost: a holder that lets its period pass, or has no money for its proportion, is diluted.*
7ad. **Who holds what at the vote.** A seller votes on an owners' matter, sells part of its stake, and the buyer votes on the same version: one of them counts, by the collective's line placing the transfer (an act for the collective acknowledging it, or a record naming it among transfers registered). A transfer the collective acknowledged but the verifier does not hold leaves the vote unknown. *Stated cost: a buyer has no vote until the collective places the transfer.*
7ae. **Owners choose the operators.** A collective's constitutional change rule is counted among its stake's holders by share. The owners vote the operators out by a constitutional clone; the operators do not rotate: the clone stands complete and unexecuted, visible to every reader of the chain, and the collective runs on under the old members. Where the constitution gave the owners the recovery path's custodian, the rotation is made. A rotation by the operators without the owners' signatures breaks the collective. The founding client warned where no share of the chain key and no custodian was under the owners' control *(conformance)*. *Stated cost: the owners' power over membership binds only through the chain key.*
7af. **Owners see the money.** A collective with departed holders pays a member from its reserve: the payout is done only once sealed to every holder of its stake in itself, or public; sealed to the members alone, it binds no one. Where one holder is a deal, the payout is public, and where one is a collective, it is sealed to each member of that collective; sealed to the deal's split service alone, to the parties of a version of the deal, or to the holder collective's own hash, it binds no one. A price tag a member draws for the collective is a Money act, done the same way.
7ag. **A departed holder vanishes.** The collective sells a work, which needs its departed holder's agreement, and the departed holder has vanished. 7ah. **Counting by share.** A constitutional change rule by share, sixty per cent, departed holders holding half the stake in itself: the members' sixty per cent is of what the voices that remain hold, and voices that remain holding less than it asks for meet it together. The release rule counted by share: a holder splitting its share among identities it controls gains nothing; a release rule written as a number of heads is invalid. An area's threshold still counts its holders by head, in MOR 1, as every layer's rule does.
7ai. **The collective's own reserve.** A collective's split agreement naming the collective itself as a partner, at 50,000, pays that part to the collective's vault, never split again, and never to its flow pointer; with no vault declared, the split agreement is invalid. 7aj. **A holders' rule.** Dana holds 10% of a collective's stake in itself under a 60% holders' rule she signed; owners holding 70% take 5% off the top for a studio, by a version of the collective's split agreement adding the studio as a partner, signed for the collective by every member (a protected clause), their consent named under `[5, stake]` with them; she votes no, and the version counts, every holder paid alike less. 7ak. **Proxies.** A holder names a proxy, standing over its stake, placed by the collective's line: the proxy's signature on an owners' matter counts as the holder's vote, and the holder's own signature on the same version beats it. 7am. **A members' collective, with no owners' additions.** A collective naming none of the owners' additions (no holders' rule, no rule by share, no issuance, no proxy) is founded, loses a member, forks and closes exactly as steps 3.7, 3.9 and 3.9f say: every addition optional, its default the rule without it.

9. *(Agreements rule 91, shape B)* **The fork of a collective.** A collective of three cannot agree: one member wants a new split service, the other two want a new arbitrator instead, each side refuses the other's change, and the judicial tier needs every member. A fork act carrying a field 2 or 3 is out of format, and no fork. A fork assigning the first debt to the second side and the second to both jointly, but not the third, does not take effect: the third, not public, is in the history it cites. The fork that also assigns the third to the first side does. Every member on a side signs it by a chain signature with their own chain key, its drafter included, and each successor owing a debt signs it the same way, by a chain signature with its own chain key (step 3.9ae); until all have, nothing closes. Once complete, the collective is closed in Agreements: a publication a member draws for it afterwards counts for nothing, one before the line still stands. A payment naming the price tag of a successor's publication of the work, its split service's receipt naming the work's ownership agreement at the version giving the successors their shares, is a purchase, its owners' line paid to the successors and the departed holder by those shares. A successor whose founding terms drop the departed holder makes the fork incomplete. A fork with a member on no side under the default constitutional rule, a stranger on a side, or naming an agreement not in force, closes nothing; with no members' stakes in the collective, the default counts each member alike; two complete fork acts on the same agreement are concurrent acts on its chain, and neither closes it until one names the other. **A pointer left outside**: a fork drawn on an old line leaves out the collective's payee pointer, while a buyer's payment to it, and the act for the collective taking it on, lie in its history: the payment still counts; so it does where the pointer lies inside the history and the act that took it on outside, a departure racing it. **Judged by its own history**: a fork leaving out a debt binding in its history does not take effect; a departure registered after it, by a record that never saw the debt, does not make it complete.

### 4. A democracy round

1. Eligible identities prove membership through a verifying module, without revealing who they are.
2. Each allocates or delegates a fixed pool of points.
3. ### 9. The way out, walked

*Each exit from a work, a collective or the protocol, written as the acts it needs, so the way out is shown and not only argued. Each step names who signs, and what a verifier checks to tell it counts.*

1. **An owner leaving a work.** A song's ownership agreement targets its claim; its stake gives Ana 400,000 and Ben 600,000. Two publications read it by reference: one through a label, a partner taking 150,000, and Ana and Ben's own, self-published, with no partner. Ana sells her whole share to Cy: a transfer (Agreements type 4) Ana signs, by a chain signature too, and Cy completes; it names the ownership agreement, its chain; Ben is notified, not asked (Agreements rule 10). Neither publication changes. A purchase under either, whose split follows the complete transfer (split key 8), pays Cy 400,000 of every million left on the owners' line; one whose split follows only Ana's signature still pays Ana; once the transfer is complete, both signatures placed, a split paying Ana for the share breaks the plan, and from the service's first split citing the transfer (split key 8), every such payout, receipted or not, is the split service's open obligation to Cy (rules 10 and 30). A payout receipt Ana signs for the share, bound to a rotation later than her transfer's chain signature, is a wrong receipt. *(Agreements rules 10, 12 and 15.)*
2. **A member leaving a collective.** A collective of three, whose key grammar gives each member a share of the chain key, any two rebuilding it, with an escrowed share the abandonment authority releases as its recovery path. Its third member resigns alone (Agreements rule 70); any member draws a record registering the resignation, naming the collective and its previous decision: that record is the line. The two who stay sign a constitutional clone without the third, and a rotation of the collective's chain key, rebuilt from the two shares, declares it, re-dealing the key to the two, both needed, and an escrowed share of the new key where the recovery path puts it, the way that survives the loss of either (rules 59 and 69). The departed member keeps their share of the collective's stake, a departed holder. **Their power ends at the line**: an act they sign naming the collective, after the line, counts for nothing, from any device; a signature of theirs placed before the line still counts for the act it signs; **their own key continues to sign their own acts**: a post of their own, a purchase of their own, a publication they are a partner of, all stand, untouched. An act they sign naming the collective with nothing to back it is nobody's. *(Agreements rules 58, 69 and 70; "Made before, made after", 3.)*
3. **Succession.** A collective's member dies. Their plan passes their seat to a colleague and their share to two children in equal parts (Agreements rule 83). The abandonment authority's declaration, its outcome removing the dead member's voice (outcome 0), triggers it; any member draws the record registering the declaration, its line. The seat passes as a constitutional clone; the share by succession, its pay unchanged. In a second collective, the dead member held the chain key alone, with an escrowed share: their plan names one successor for the key holding, never two; the succession clone carries a succession plan for that successor, so **a single chain-key holder still has a named successor and an escrowed share**; a founding naming a single holder with neither is refused (rule 59). In a third, the seat passes to a member already a party, who then holds both seats, every threshold unchanged; before any party signs that plan, its client shows what each successor would hold after it *(conformance)*. In a fourth, a collective of one, nobody but the declared member remains to draw a record: the declaration takes effect at the recovery rotation the recovery path makes (rule 59), the rotation that removes them; where the declaration's authority were a threshold needing others, that rotation would name their signature acts ("Made before, made after"; "Abandonment declaration (type 13)"). *(Agreements rules 59, 83, 87 and 89.)*
4. **Closing and fork.** A collective that cannot agree forks: each side founds its successor first; a member draws the fork act naming the collective, its line, and the successors; the members on each side chain-sign it under the constitutional change rule, and each successor assigned a debt chain-signs it too; once complete the original is closed, and nothing done for it afterwards counts (Agreements rule 91). The original's shares in works pass to the successors in the shares the fork names. **A publication whose ownership agreement names the closed collective as owner sells nothing from the fork's line**: a payment after it is owed back, as a payment racing an ending is; so does a publication whose ownership agreement is itself closed by a release (rule 12). A second collective that holds nothing and owes nothing closes: a member draws the closing, chain-signed under the constitutional change rule; "holds nothing" is read from the ownership agreements of the works that list it as an owner. *(Agreements rules 12 and 91.)*
5. **Rotation and escape.** A collective rotates its chain key: the key is rebuilt from the members' shares, the rotation signed with it, revealing it and committing the next; its homes receipt it as any chain act (Identity rule 20). Its home turns hostile, refusing the rotation and objecting to a homeless one. **The collective escapes**: a homeless rotation signed with its chain key, rebuilt from k of its members' shares, is its own escape endorsement; the old home's objection voids nothing; a home reading Identity alone counts it, since the genesis declares no signing key; a person's homeless rotation under the same objection, with no endorsement, does not count, the person's chain ending before it (Identity rules 49 and 53, "The escape endorsement"). *Stated cost: what protects a collective from a thief is its key grammar, not its homes.* A collective's genesis is signed the same way, by a chain key revealed in its signature, its commitment naming the next (Identity rule 1). *(Identity rules 1, 7, 20, 49 and 53.)*
6. **A refund owed back.** A publication's parties sign a later version selling nothing, ending it; its split service has received a payment on a push rail, recorded after the service's own signature on that version: a payment racing the ending, owed back (Agreements rules 11 and 12). The service has already paid out the partner's share and part of the owners' line. **It is owed back by the split service from what it holds, and past that by each party in the share it received, on the owners' line each holder paid, their own payout**, each an open obligation (rule 30), visible until repaid; an anonymous payer's refund is claimed by a signature with the key the payment committed to (Money rule 19). A tag withdrawn by its signer alone, a version with no price, stops sales from then on; past sales stand. A purchase the buyer contests, by a contest naming their claim, is shown beside it, changing nothing (rule 54). *(Agreements rules 11, 12, 30 and 54; Money rules 18 and 19.)*
7. **A work cloned to MOR 2.** What crosses is the bake (its bytes), the claim and the ownership agreement, each in core format: a MOR 2 client reads who made the work and who owns what share. A successor changes the tag prefix, so nothing replays: each is made anew under MOR 2, saying what it said, who made the work and who owns what share, with every hash new, its work hash and the claim it targets among them (Development rule 27: every hash is new). The publications are distribution: their partners, split service and split plan are remade under MOR 2's own rules. **What MOR 1 checks is the source alone**: the bake, the claim and the ownership agreement each valid in core format, read without any publication; how MOR 2 checks what it makes anew is MOR 2's. *(MICS, "MOR 2".)*

**Passes if:** an owner leaves a work by the transfer alone, every publication paying the buyer from its split service's first split following the complete transfer, both signatures placed, and owing the buyer every payout to the seller from its first split citing it (9.1); a member's power for the collective ends at the line that registers their departure while their own acts stay theirs (9.2); a seat and a share pass by the plan, a single chain-key holder's successor and escrowed share are always named, and the client shows what each successor would hold (9.3); a fork's or closing's line ends the publications over the closed owner (9.4); a keyless collective rotates, and escapes a hostile home by its homeless rotation, its own escape endorsement, which no member signs (9.5); a payment racing an ending is owed back by the split service, then by each party, on the owners' line each holder paid, their own payout (9.6); and a work's bake, claim and ownership agreement, the source of its crossing to MOR 2, are each valid in core format, read without its publications (9.7).

## Freeze procedure

1. Fix what the texts fix only at the freeze: each frozen text's header becomes the text's name alone (for instance "# MIR: Identity", as Development rule 26 writes it), with no draft number or approval line, since the header is part of the hashed text; the SHA-256 of the X-Wing draft's revision 11, written into Envelopes as `XWING`; and then the six MIRs' spec hashes, computed as Development rule 26 says, with the MICS document's and this suite's. *The RV32IM profile and its vectors are not among them: the profile is a specification of its own, uploaded after the freeze. The signature-scheme and unit specifications and the pinned Unicode version are written in the texts; how a home is queried is the relay transport cMIP's; the running-summary vectors over stand-in ids are made public with the conformance vectors, the three-act sequence's final once the hashes are.*
2. Build the founding cMIPs and Modules: a home relay, a basic relay, the genesis client (with the air-gapped chain key Module), a read-only client and a basic client; and, so every layer meets friction, a rail Module (Money), a split Module and a deal-assessment tool (Agreements). Clunky is acceptable.
3. Run every scenario the founding components can run; reason through the rest against the texts. Mark each run or reasoned, ticking each component as it passes.
4. Machine adversarial review: the texts, the code and the draft freeze report, reviewed by a model given the budget to break things and propose fixes.
5. Specialist questions: from the draft freeze report, each rule that is only reasoned becomes a targeted question to a specialist in its field, one person and one question at a time. Then human adversarial review: one reviewer attacks the whole, the running prototypes as well as the texts. Nobody outside decides the freeze.
6. Fix any failure requiring a core change, and rerun or re-reason the affected scenarios.
7. When every box is ticked, run or reasoned, the MICS is frozen: the six MIRs' texts, committed as canonical text, their hashes and the freeze report are made public together.

After the freeze, a flaw in the core is fixed only by a new protocol running alongside, with users migrating by choice.
