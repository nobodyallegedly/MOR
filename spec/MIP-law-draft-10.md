## Purpose

Law is the language of verifiable agreements between identities. It defines:

- how an agreement is proposed, signed, recorded and changed (by cloning, never by editing);
- keepers, the notaries that record deals as they happen, so a rotation cannot erase what someone relied on;
- stakes in works and publications, how they are bound, transferred and released;
- splits: how money that reached the owners' payee pointer is divided, exactly, including module fees and shares assigned to roles;
- offers accepted by payment, conditions, and time;
- grants and collectives: acting for others, within limits;
- abandonment, and what happens when someone disappears from a deal;
- non-performance: the core never enforces, but it makes default impossible to hide.

Finance ends when money reaches a payee pointer. Law says who owns what, how it is shared, and who failed when something breaks.

## Dependencies

Identity, Envelope, Text and Finance. Law is optional per client, but all or nothing: a client implements it fully, or shows its acts as unknown (core, client conformance). A client that acts on agreements implements Law (F11). No rule of a lower layer depends on Law: where Law needs a lower layer to carry something, it is a field there (the publication's `for`, Envelope).

## Definitions

- **Terms.** An act proposing an agreement: its parties, text, cMIPs, keepers and rules.
- **Signature.** An act by one party naming terms (or a clone, a transfer, a release) as agreed. Under one signer per act, every agreement is terms plus one signature act per party.
- **Agreement.** Terms together with the signatures its rules require. Its id is the id of its terms act. An agreement commits identities, not keys: a later rotation changes the pen, never the promise. A party is bound only through its own signature act.
- **Agreement chain.** The acts on one agreement (signatures, clones, records, transfers, payouts, contests), each naming the chain and the act it follows in `objects` (Envelope).
- **Position.** An agreement that confers income without ownership.
- **Keeper.** A relay, operated by an identity, named by an agreement to record the acts around a deal as they arrive, and to sign what it recorded: a notary stamping sealed envelopes. A keeper records act ids and signers, never content. A record counts only alongside the act it names.
- **Arbitrator or verifier.** A third party named by an agreement to judge content (escrow and milestones, arbitration, regulated checks). Unlike a keeper, it receives the keys of the acts it must judge, by ordinary key delivery.
- **Stake.** A share in a work or a publication, defined by an agreement, identified by the agreement and its index in the stakes list. Shares in each always add up to 100%. The current holder of a stake is whoever the transfer chain on the agreement names. A stake whose object is the collective whose agreement defines it is a share of all that collective's income, whoever earns it and whenever (F121, Q8); it decides what every holder is paid, member or departed (F124, N5).
- **This collective.** In a collective's own terms, null written as a stake's object or holder, or as a grant's grantor, names the collective whose agreement it is, as Identity lets a genesis name itself as its home's operator by null (F124, S1). Founding terms can so name a collective that does not exist yet.
- **Work claim.** An act binding a work's hash to its creators.
- **Split plan.** The part of an agreement that says how incoming payments are divided: fixed shares on stakes, role shares, fees, a maximum fee per payout, and what happens to unfilled roles.
- **Split service.** An identity that receives payments on behalf of a work's owners, divides each one according to their agreement, and pays every party out. It can be a company, a collective, a friend running software, or the owners themselves; a sole creator is simply their own split service. Where an agreement names one, the payee pointer of the identity its publications are made for counts, for Law, only if every address in it also appears in that service's own signed pointer in force, and every entry of its vault in force in the service's own signed vault (rule 18, F121, F123, F124 P2).
- **Payer-side splitting.** The other split model (F124, P2): where an agreement names no split service, the payer's wallet, reading Law, pays each holder's own pointer by the stakes of the work's claim, one flow with the holders' identities as destinations (F64).
- **Split.** A split service's signed act dividing one incoming payment into payouts, delivered to every holder it pays and naming each fee and who received it (type 8, rule 20, F121 Q9).
- **Payout.** One receiver's part of a split, paid as a simple Finance payment with its own receipt.
- **Role share.** A share assigned to a role rather than a named party, filled when the payment happens: for example the reposter who led to the sale, the relay that served the file, the rail Modules the payment ran through. Its evidence is an act signed by a party other than the split service and the payee, save for a rail Module's role share, whose evidence is the receipt or claim naming that rail Module where the payee's own pointer or vault names it (rule 22, F119).
- **Standing offer.** Published terms that anyone accepts by paying.
- **Time reference.** A source of time an agreement names, such as a block height. Only it decides whether an act is before or after a point on its reference, and whether something is past its terms, for that agreement (Q35). *Before and after among acts done in a collective's name are judged on the collective's own sequence ("Made before, made after", F109); the time reference judges deadlines and absence.*
- **Collective.** Several identities acting together as one identity. A collective is a full identity (Identity MIP), with its own genesis, homes, signing key and safety key; its founding agreement says how its members hold and use those keys.
- **Key grammar.** The part of a collective's founding agreement that says how its keys are held and activated, and the way to rotate that needs less than every member.
- **Deal.** An agreement that founds no collective (its terms carry no key grammar). A deal exists when every party has signed it, and changes only with every party's signature (F107).
- **Tier.** In a collective's founding agreement and its clones, each field of the terms belongs to one of three tiers ("Tiers"). **Constitutional:** who decides: the parties (membership), the change rules, the key grammar, the areas, the constitution's words, the departed members entry. **Judicial:** who judges and by what: the protected clauses, changed only with every member's signature (rule 46a, F121). **Operational:** everything else. Deals have no tiers (F103, scope; F107).
- **Change rules.** The rules a collective's clone must meet, each covering part of the terms: the **constitutional change rule** (terms field 18; every party when none was agreed); the **judicial tier's rule**, every party whose voice remains, fixed by this MIP and never by the terms (rule 46a, F121); the **clone rule** (field 5), for operational matters outside every area; and the **power** over each area. In a deal, the signing rule and the clone rule are every party.
- **Area.** A part of a collective's operational tier that the constitution gives to some of its members, the area's **holders**: acts of the collective, by whole layer or by act type, and operational fields of its terms. Its holders alone decide there, and may grant within it (Q5); its holders, and the rule they act by, change only by a constitutional change, and a holder may step down from it at once (rule 37b, Q13). An area with no holder whose voice remains stands frozen; a clone may list none at all (Q21). An area reaching a whole layer is also called that layer's **lane**: lanes and areas are one construct (Q10). A lane also decides which cMIPs the collective adopts for that layer's tasks, and the Production lane which extensions it adopts (F106).
- **Voice.** A party's say in an agreement: counting toward its change rules and, in a collective, the areas it holds. A party loses its voice only by its own resignation (rule 37a), by an abandonment declaration (rule 53), or by a constitutional clone that removes it (rule 37); it loses its voice in one area by stepping down from it (rule 37b), or by a constitutional clone that replaces it there (Q13); it never loses its stake that way (Q6). In a collective, a resignation, a stepping down and a declaration take effect at the collective's line registering them ("Made before, made after", F109).
- **Record.** The collective's everyday line: the act by which it writes a complete clone of its agreement in its own sequence, naming the signature acts that complete it, so that the collective's later acts are judged under it, and registers its members' departures and rotations (type 17, rule 37c, F106, F109).
- **Line.** An act of a collective, in its own sequence, at which an event that changes who may act for it takes effect for its acts: a record (type 17) or a rotation of the collective, each naming the latest act of every other sequence the collective keeps. What was done in the collective's name counts as made before or after a line by its place in the collective's own sequence, that is, by whether the history the line cites holds it ("Made before, made after", F109, F127).
- **Decisions chain.** A collective's decisions, in order (F127): its genesis (founding), its rotations (members joining or leaving, every constitutional change), its records (its other clones, the release rule among them, and the departures they register), its grants and revocations (F128), and its fork or closing. Each cites the previous decision and the action head it saw: a record by its own sequence and the tips it names, a rotation by its kept tips (Identity), a grant or revocation by its own sequence and its citations in `objects`, as an action cites (reading, F128), a fork or closing by its chain act and tips. Records, rotations, forks and closings are the lines.
- **Actions chain.** Every other act in a collective's name (F127): debts, payments, publications, releases, sales, the acts signed with a grant key (F128). Each cites, in `objects`, on the collective's chain (the collective's identity as chain), the decision it acts under and the heads it joins, its own sequence's previous act counting as cited (rule 35b). Identity's own everyday acts carry no objects and are on neither chain; nor are negotiation messages, which are talk (W6, F128).
- **History.** What a decision or an action cites, transitively: the previous acts of its own sequence, the tips it names, what each act reached cites on the collective's chain, and the tips of each record reached (F127). An act is before a line when the line's history holds it.
- **Tie rule.** Where a decision ending powers (a fork, a closing, a departure, a grant's revocation, a departure emptying an area, F128) and an action using them cite neither each other, the ending wins: the action is void (F127). *A counterparty protects itself by waiting until the actions chain visibly moves past its act before delivering.*
- **Power.** The right to bring a change into force: the constitutional change rule, the judicial tier's rule, the clone rule, or an area's holders meeting its threshold.
- **Mark.** A clone's terms field 4: which of the parent's change rules it claims to meet, and the parties whose signatures meet it (F104).
- **Layer.** One of MOR's layers (core, "Structure"): Identity; Envelope and Text; Finance; Law; Production. An act whose `spec` is a MIP belongs to that MIP's layer. An act whose `spec` is a cMIP belongs to the layer of each task the collective's agreement in force (rule 37c) names that cMIP for (terms field 2), the task's layer being that of the MIP defining it (Production, table of tasks); an act whose `spec` is an extension the agreement names (field 15) belongs to Production (F106). An act whose `spec` is a specification the agreement in force names neither for a task nor as an extension belongs to no layer; in a collective with areas it counts for nothing, unless an area reaches it by a kind naming its type (rule 36a, Q16). Production defines no tasks and no act types of its own: specifications and releases are Envelope publications.
- **Extension.** A cMIP an agreement names outside the listed tasks (field 15), which adds rules and never relaxes the core's (Production). Its specification declares the layers it acts on (Production, specification field 10, Q18); its own acts belong to Production.
- **Grant.** A delegation of authority, with limits, from an identity or collective to another identity. A grant never reaches beyond the power of whoever issued it. A collective's grant is a decision naming the grantee's own identity, which signs to accept it, and hands the grantee a grant key (F128). In a deal, each payee's grant to the split service is written in the deal's terms (field 14), naming "this agreement" by null (F129, H4).
- **This agreement.** In a payee's grant to a deal's split service, null written as the agreement concerned (grant field 2) names the deal whose terms list that grant in field 14, with the versions it clones (F129, H4), as null in a collective's terms names "this collective" (S1). A grant can so name a deal that does not exist yet.
- **Payee.** In a deal naming a split service, a party whose own payee pointer receives the deal's incoming money, and who grants the service a grant key for it (field 14; F129, H4).
- **Grant key.** A key of an identity, a person or a collective (F128, extended), scoped to one grant's area and reach, named by the grant (field 9), made and held by the grantee: like a device of the collective limited to that scope (F128). What it signs is the grantor's own act (for a collective, a strand of its actions chain), citing the grant; a revocation removes it, a decision ending powers. Every grant key ends at a fork's line. *"In the end, it is an extra key that is tied to both the collective and the grantee's hashes" (Nobody, allegedly). Cost, stated: a stolen grant key acts within its scope until revoked.*
- **Negotiation message.** A Law act carrying text, by which two sides negotiate: each names the previous message of its thread and acknowledges the latest one received from the other side (type 18, rule 56, F118). Like every Law act it may carry acknowledgements (Envelope rule 4a, F110); a text act never can.
- **Contest.** A signed objection, by a party with standing, to another act. It changes nothing about any act's validity, status or selection; it makes disagreement visible. *Draft 3 called this a dispute; the word is now reserved for the status Identity gives an act after a rotation (F69).*
- **Standing.** Who may contest an act: a party to its agreement, a holder of a stake it affects, or a keeper, arbitrator or verifier the agreement names.
- **Protected clause.** In a collective, a clause that can move a party's stake or voice, or decide who wins a dispute: the abandonment clause, the succession plan, the concurrency rule, the keepers, the split service, the time reference, the arbitrators or verifiers, the condition and anchoring cMIPs (Q3), and the chain of judgment (F121). Together they are the judicial tier, which changes only with every member's signature, one version for everyone (rule 46a, F121); the split service is operational in kind, and protected as a stated exception, since it can move money. A deal needs none: no clone of it completes without every party (F107).
- **Judge.** A specification named for a judicial task (9 condition evaluation, 10 time reference, 11 anchoring), or an identity named to judge or testify: a keeper's operator, an arbitrator or verifier, the abandonment authority; and, for the chain of judgment, the split service (F121).
- **Chain of judgment.** For a judge the terms name, the ones that take over, in order, when it answers "unknown" or does not act within its period, named in the terms and signed by everyone, so that it passes with no new signature. Each link names its period, measured on the agreement's time reference (terms field 21, rule 34a, F121, Q7).
- **Departed holder.** An identity that left a collective, by resigning or by being removed, and kept a stake in it. Its stake is in the collective's stakes (field 7), which decide what it is paid; the collective's **departed members entry** records only that the identity is departed, without voice (terms field 22, rule 46b, F121, F124 N5).
- **Fork of a collective.** The end of a collective whose members split into sides that will not agree: each side first founds its successor; a fork act naming the successors, signed by the members on its sides with their own identities under the constitutional change rule, closes the original collective in Law and passes its ownership to the successors, each naming the original in "forked from", a back-link only (type 19, terms field 23, rule 47a, F121, F124 N1, N4). Not to be confused with a fork of records or of an agreement chain (rules 5, 37c, 47), which the concurrency rule settles.
- **Closing.** The end of a collective that holds nothing and owes nothing, every work sold or released, every debt paid or released by its creditor: a closing act, signed under the constitutional change rule, after whose line the collective's keys count for nothing in Law (type 20, rule 47a, F124 N9, F125 D5).
- **Creditor's release.** An act by which a creditor ends an obligation owed to it without full payment, for instance against stakes or a partial payment; only the creditor signs it (rule 47b, F125). Since F126 it is a Finance act (Finance type 4; Law type 21 retired): a collective creditor signs it by its Finance lane alone; a release traded for future terms is also a Law agreement, signed by the collective's Law lane, which the release names. *MOR has no court and cannot force a creditor to let go: a debt ends by payment or by its creditor's word.*
- **Done.** An act in a collective's name (signed with its key, or with a grant key of its) is done once it is sealed to every member of the agreement in force for it (or public) and on the chain, citing the collective's head as its signer knew it (rule 35a, F126, F128); before that, even signed, it is planning, and binds no one, its signer included. Where it is held is never a condition: relays are transport (F128). Records too, and forks and closings: one rule for every act in the collective's name (W3, F127; W5, F128). *"One thing is to act, one thing is to read" (Nobody, allegedly).*
- **Purchase.** A payment for a work under a Law claim, naming the claim it pays under: the claiming agreement, and the line at which the payer's client read it current (Finance receipt and claim field 9, rule 32, F126). On a request rail, the claim it names is the one the seller's request committed to; on a push rail, each holder the claim names settles it on its own chain (rule 32a, F128, W4). Where the seller is a collective, it becomes a **sale** once the collective's actions chain records it (rule 32a, F127, W2).
- **Request rail, push rail.** A request rail is one on which the payee's side commits to each payment before it is made (the payment cMIP's commitment, which an invoice carries); a push rail is one on which the payer pays an address with no such request. The rail Module's specification says which it is (payment cMIP; F128, W4).
- **Concurrency rule.** Terms field 10: how an agreement settles changes made concurrently, a fork of records (rule 47). Called the fork rule until F121 named the fork of a collective.
- **Release to the public domain.** An act ending a work's claim, at once or at a point it names on the time reference: nobody earns from the work as its owner any more; its history, who made it and who owned it, stays named; its content key is published, or delivered at that point by the identity the act names (type 5, rule 17, F121, F124 N11).

## Act formats

All acts are Envelope MIP acts, private by default, visible to the agreement's parties and keepers unless they choose otherwise. Terms (type 0) and signatures (type 1) have exact formats, in the fields a collective needs (draft 6), with the tiers' fields (draft 7); grants have exact formats for the fields that tie them to an area (fields 5 and 6, draft 7), and resignations and records exact formats (draft 7), and so do the abandonment declaration (draft 8, B12), the negotiation message (draft 10, F118), and, with F121's answers, the fork, the release and the split, and terms field 7; with F124, the closing; with F125, the creditor's release (since F126 a Finance act, type 21 retired); with F126, terms field 25 (withdrawn by F128, never reused); with F128, the grant key (grant field 9) and the revocation (type 10); the fields still marked open, and the other acts, are sketched, and exact formats follow once the structure is settled. *A client that meets terms using a field whose format is still open refuses them rather than accepting them unchecked (Envelope rule 11, fail closed).* Every Law act type may carry acknowledgements (`acks`, Envelope rules 4a and 7b, F110).

### Terms (type 0)

```cddl
terms-payload = {
  0 => [+ hash],            ; parties: identity or collective hashes
  1 => tstr,                ; the terms' words, as canonical text; in a collective, the constitution's and general words (each area's own: field 20)
  2 => { * uint => hash },  ; cMIPs, at most one per task (task number => cMIP hash); empty if it uses no task
  ? 15 => [+ hash],         ; extensions: cMIPs outside the listed tasks, which add rules and never relax the core's; in a collective, adopted in the Production lane (F106)
  ? 16 => [+ succession-plan] ; succession plans of parties, part of the signed terms
  ? 3 => keepers,           ; named keepers and the rule for what counts as recorded
  4 => rule / mark,         ; founding terms: signing rule, which signatures make the agreement exist: every party, in a deal (F107) and in a collective (Q11)
                            ;   a clone: its mark, which change rules of the parent brought it into force, and by whom (F104)
  5 => rule,                ; clone rule: in a collective, operational matters outside every area (the judicial tier needs every
                            ;   party whose voice remains, rule 46a, F121); every party in a deal (F107)
  ? 18 => rule,             ; constitutional change rule; absent: every party (F103) (only with field 12)
  ? 19 => [+ area],         ; operational areas, powers of members (only with field 12)
  ? 20 => { + uint => tstr }, ; each area's own words, as canonical text, by area id (only with field 19)
  ? 6 => [ hash, any ],     ; time reference: the task cMIP and its parameters; a naming for task 10 (Q25), the same specification
                            ;   as field 2's task 10 where both are present (Q31)
  ? 7 => stakes,            ; stakes defined, in works, publications, or the collective itself (Q8), written null (S1)
  ? 8 => split-plan,        ; how incoming payments are divided (format open)
  ? 9 => abandonment,       ; the abandonment clause
  ? 10 => concurrency-rule, ; how forks of records are settled, where parties may act independently (format open)
  ? 11 => hash,             ; parent: the agreement this one clones (with its latest act in objects)
  ? 12 => key-grammar,      ; present if this agreement founds a collective
  ? 13 => [+ hash],         ; arbitrators or verifiers named, who receive keys to judge content
  ? 14 => hash / [+ hash],  ; where incoming payments go to a split service: in a collective, its grant (one hash); in a deal,
                            ;   one grant per payee, each naming this agreement by null (F129, H4)
  ? 17 => refund-terms,     ; for standing offers: how long a refund stays claimable, on the time reference (format open)
  ? 21 => [+ chain],        ; the chain of judgment: who takes over from a judge (rule 34a, F121)
  ? 22 => [+ hash],         ; the departed members entry: who left the collective (rule 46b, F121, F124 N5; only with field 12)
  ? 23 => hash,             ; forked from: founding terms of a successor of a fork, the original collective, a back-link (rule 47a, F124 N4; only with field 12)
  ? 24 => rule              ; the release rule: which of a stake's holders sign its release (rule 17, F121); founding terms set it, a clone every owner signs changes it (N8)
}                           ; key 25 (the relays, F126) is withdrawn (F128) and never reused: terms carrying it are invalid

                                      ;   period, on the time reference, the one before it has to act once asked (Q7)

         / [ 2 ]                      ; the split service (field 14): next are grants naming the services that take over;
                                      ;   in a deal, refused meanwhile (question H6, F129)

key-grammar = {

  1 => holding,             ; how the safety key is held, usually stricter
  ? 3 => recovery           ; a way to rotate that needs less than every member, required when holding 1 names every member

  2 => uint,                ; how many of the holders decide together: at least 1, and at most the number of holders,

  ? 3 => [+ kind],          ; the acts of the collective it reaches
  ? 4 => [+ field-ref],     ; the operational fields of the terms it reaches
  5 => uint                 ; its id: permanent, unique among the agreement's areas, never reused (Q32)
}                           ; an area reaches at least one act kind or one field; it is identified by its id, never by its place in field 19

          / [ 1, task: uint ]             ; one entry of field 2: the cMIP for this task, where the task is operational

                                          ;   ascending by the power's deterministic encoding, no power twice;
                                          ;   each power's signers ascending by hash, none twice (B8)
power = [ 0 ]                             ; the parent's constitutional change rule (its field 18, else every party)
      / [ 1 ]                             ; the parent's clone rule (its field 5)
      / [ 2, area: uint ]                 ; the power over the parent's area of this id
      / [ 3, party: hash ]                ; the succession plan of this party, signed in advance by every voice (rule 48c, Q14);
                                          ;   its signers are the seat successors entering
      / [ 4 ]                             ; the judicial tier's rule: every party of the parent whose voice remains (rule 46a, F121)
```

*A rule's threshold is between 1 and the number of parties; named parties are parties. In a key grammar, members of a holding are parties. An area's holders are distinct parties; a clone that takes a party out of field 0 takes it out of every area's holders too, and founding terms never list an area without holders (Q21). A clone that takes a holder off an area need not lower its number: a number left above the holders that remain is met by all of them (rule 44d, Q26), and the refit sets it again; a clone that changes an area's number sets it within its holders. The signers of each power in a mark are distinct, listed ascending by hash, so that one meaning has one encoding (B8); a mark listing them otherwise is invalid. A threshold is written as the founders chose it; how it counts once voices are removed is rule 44d.*

**A clone** is terms with a parent (field 11). Its inside names, in `objects`, the parent's chain and the act of that chain it follows: `[[parent, act]]`. Founding terms name no chain. A clone's field 4 is a mark, founding terms' field 4 a rule; terms whose field 4 has the other form are invalid.

**Founding terms exist only when every party has signed them,** a deal's and a collective's alike (F107, Q11): founding terms whose field 4 is not `[0]` are invalid. *A person is never added to a collective without their approval: a founder who has not signed leaves the collective unfounded, and a member added later joins only by signing the clone that adds them (rule 45).*

**A deal** (terms without field 12) has every party as its clone rule too: any deal whose field 5 is not `[0]` is invalid, and so are terms of a deal carrying field 18, 19 or 20 (F107). *The fields are kept rather than dropped so that every agreement has one format, and a deal says on its face what it needs.* **In a deal, the absence authority is one identity** (Flaw B19): a party, a keeper's operator or a collective, named by the clause's first form, `[0, hash]`; terms of a deal whose abandonment clause names a threshold of the other parties, `[1, uint]`, are invalid, whatever its number. *A deal has no sequence of its own in which several signatures could be placed against its line. Parties who need to decide absence together form a collective, which orders its own acts on its own sequence (F109), and name it as the authority: "a collective made of collectives".*

**A judge never handles what it judges** (Q20), in every agreement, a deal's as a collective's (Q24). A judge is a specification named in field 2 for a judicial task (9 condition evaluation, 10 time reference, 11 anchoring), or named in field 6 as the time reference, which is a naming for task 10 (Q25). A judge is named for no other task, judicial or not, and is not named as an extension (field 15): terms that do either are invalid, founding terms and clones alike, whatever a clone's mark says. *"It removes a slight conflict of interest." An extension fills no task, but it adds rules to acts; one that is also a judge would handle what it judges, so every way a specification can be named is covered (Q25). In a deal every party signs every cMIP, so no lane can slip a judge in; the reason is the judge's, not the lanes' (Q24). It also keeps R4 away from the judges: no specification serves a judicial task and a lane's task at once, so no lane's holders ever have a say over a judge, and no change to a judge reaches a lane's acts.*

**The chain of judgment** (field 21, F121) follows judges the terms name: a judicial task the terms name a specification for, an identity they name as a keeper's operator, an arbitrator or verifier, or the abandonment authority (its first form), and the split service where field 14 names one. Each judge has one link at most, the links ascending by the judge's deterministic encoding; those that take over from a judge are distinct, and none is the judge itself. **A specification that takes over from a judicial task is a judge too, so it is named nowhere else in the terms**: for no task, as no extension, and in no other link (Q20, Q25). **Each link names a period above zero** (Q7): the time the one before it has, once asked, to act, measured on the agreement's time reference; after it the next may act. *A period is compulsory for every link, a specification's included, though a specification always answers.* **Terms whose chain follows a judge that can stay silent (an identity, or the split service) name a time reference** (field 6, or field 2's task 10), itself a judge, which may have a chain of its own. Terms breaking any of these are invalid. **In a deal, a chain following the split service is refused meanwhile** (question H6, F129): a service taking over would need a grant from every payee, which a link naming one grant cannot carry; refused, never guessed. How a chain passes is rule 34a. *The chain is signed with the terms, by everyone: in a deal by every party, in a collective as part of the judicial tier (rule 46a); so nobody signs again when a judge fails.* *"With compulsory time reference" (Nobody, allegedly, Q7).*

**The departed members entry** (field 22, F121, F124 N5) appears only in terms carrying a key grammar. It lists identities, distinct, none of them a party, each a holder of a share of the stake in the collective itself (field 7). Terms breaking any of these are invalid. It records only that each is departed, without voice (rule 46b); the stakes decide what every holder is paid. *"They are stakeholders. So the stakes still show them, but the organizational part files the ID as departed" (Nobody, allegedly).*

**Stakes** (field 7, Q8). Each stake names its object once; its holders are distinct, each share above zero, the shares summing to 1,000,000 (rule 15a). Terms breaking any of these are invalid. **Null names this collective** (F124, S1): a stake whose object is null is a share of all the collective's income, and a holder written null is the collective itself, holding that stake. Only terms carrying a key grammar write null; a stake in the collective held by the collective itself is invalid. Founding terms so carry the members' stakes in the collective, and the stakes of the works it owns, from its birth. *"Let's reuse the mechanic" (Nobody, allegedly): the collective's identity is its genesis act, which declares the founding agreement, so the founding terms could not name it by its hash. In its own terms, founding or clone, the collective is written null, never by its hash, so that one meaning has one encoding (as B8); a clone writing the collective's own hash is invalid where a verifier holds its genesis (reading 1, confirmed, F125).*

**Forked from** (field 23, F121, F124 N4) appears only in the founding terms of a collective (with field 12, without field 11): the original collective's identity. It is a back-link for readers and decides nothing: the fork act names its successors (rule 47a). It is no parent: the founding terms are signed by every founder, as any founding terms are (Q11). Terms breaking this are invalid. *A successor is founded before the fork act exists, so it cannot name the act; it names the collective it leaves (reading, to confirm: the collective, rather than its agreement, which may still change before the fork).*

**The release rule** (field 24, F121, F124 N8) is set in an agreement's founding terms: which of a stake's holders must sign its release to the public domain, a rule counted among them (rule 17); absent, every holder. **A clone every owner signs may change it**: a clone whose field 24 differs from its parent's is a draft until every holder of every stake the parent defines has signed it (rule 45), besides the power its mark names: **the ordinary clone rule**, field 24 lying in no area (rule 44c; F125, D4). *"Freedom of making mistakes and fixing them" (Nobody, allegedly). Reading (to confirm): the owners are the holders the parent's field 7 names, a holder written null being the collective, whose consent the mark already carries.*

**Field 25 is withdrawn** (F128). *F126 had a collective's terms name its relays, an act in its name done only once its outside was on one of them, "one is enough"; F127 made them constitutional, "because so much stands on it". Weighing W4 (Nobody, allegedly: "naming relays seems to be a solution, again… but I am sure it is also creating problems, just unsure which ones"), the cause was named: "done = on a named relay" made an act's validity depend on infrastructure (a vanished or hostile operator freezing a collective, even its decision to change relays; leverage over the collective; omission to readers checking one relay; offline verifiers), crossing MOR's line that an act is valid by its signatures and content wherever held. The relays were a witness of time; the two chains and the tie rule (F127) answer "before" without them.* Terms carrying field 25 are invalid, and the number is never reused. **Relays are transport**: where a collective's clients publish and look first is client conformance, or a cMIP's (the relay transport cMIP), never the core (rule 35a).

**An agreement has one time reference** (Q31). Where terms carry both field 6 and a cMIP for task 10 in field 2, the two name the same specification, field 6 adding its parameters; terms where they differ are invalid, founding terms and clones alike. *One check, no change of format: terms that use either place alone stay valid.*

**Areas** (field 19) appear only in terms that carry a key grammar (field 12) (F103, scope), and area words (field 20) only for an area that exists. No two areas reach the same act or the same field (Q5): two kinds overlap when some act could belong to both, as computed from the six MIP hashes and the terms' own fields 2 and 15 ("Layer"). So `[0, layer]` overlaps `[1, spec, type]` when `spec` is a MIP of that layer, a cMIP the terms name for a task of that layer, or, for Production, an extension they name; and a lane overlaps a field reference to a task of its own layer, since the lane already decides that task's cMIP (rule 44b). The one exception is a specification the terms name for tasks of two layers or more, never a judicial task (Q20): its acts belong to each of those layers, and are reached by the lane of each, which then decide them together (rule 36a, R4). A field reference names an operational field or task ("Tiers"). Genesis and rotations are never in an area's reach: the key grammar governs them; nor are records (type 17), judged by the clone they name. An area whose kind names a genesis or a rotation (Identity's chain acts) or a record is invalid, never read as reaching nothing (B7). Terms breaking any of these are invalid.

**An area is followed by its id** (Q32). The ids of an agreement's areas (area key 5) are distinct. An area of a clone is the parent's area with the same id, wherever it sits in field 19; grants (field 5), area words (field 20), steppings down (resignation field 1), marks (`[2, area]`) and a refit (rule 37b) name an area by its id. A clone may drop an area, and adds one only under an id that no earlier version of the agreement, back to its founding terms, gave to an area. Terms breaking either are invalid. *An area keeps its id through any redraw, so removing or reordering areas never moves a grant, words or a refit onto another area.*

*Which layer an act belongs to is computed from its own `spec` field, the six MIP hashes and the collective's own terms, never from a label the cMIP's author chose (F106): a collective that gives Finance to some members gives them the Finance MIP's own acts, its receipts and claims on every rail among them, and the acts of every Finance cMIP it adopted, by its own list. A new rail Module needs no clone at all: the collective's payee pointer, an act of its Finance lane, or its vault names it, and a receipt counts only on a rail Module so named (Finance rule 12a, F115). An act of a specification the terms name nowhere falls in no lane; in a collective with areas it counts for nothing, unless a `[1, spec, type]` kind of some area names it (Q16), since otherwise an unlisted specification would be a way around every lane. A specification named for tasks of two layers belongs to both; where the two lanes are held by different areas, each lane's holders must sign its acts, as for a clone across two areas (Q7), and naming it for the second layer needs both lanes' power (rule 44b), so neither lane's holders can bind the other's acts alone (R4).*

*Cost, stated (F106): a collective cannot give its releases to one member and its other publications to another by lane alone, since both are Envelope publications; a `[1, spec, type]` kind still names a single type.*

### Signature (type 1)

```cddl
signature-payload = {
  0 => hash                 ; the act signed: terms, a clone, an act of a collective an area reaches, a grant within an area,
                            ;   or an abandonment declaration whose authority is a threshold of the other parties (B15)
}
```

The inside names, in `objects`, the act signed as both chain and predecessor: `[[signed, signed]]`. *A signature follows the act it signs.* Signatures naming one act are parallel consents to it, never a fork among themselves: rule 5's forks are between acts that change an agreement. A signature counts only while valid under Identity's rules, save where a collective placed it before its line registering its signer's rotation (rule 5, C5).

### Resignation (type 16)

```cddl
resignation-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the agreement of the collective in which the member gives up their voice
  ? 1 => uint               ; stepping down: the area given up, by its id in that agreement (Q32); absent: the whole voice
}
```

Signed by a party to that agreement, with its own key. Like a signature, its inside names the agreement as both chain and predecessor, `[[agreement, agreement]]`, and it is never a fork. It takes effect for the collective's acts at the collective's line registering it (record field 3), never at its place in its signer's own sequences, which are not read ("Made before, made after", F109). Without field 1 it gives up the signer's voice in that agreement and in every agreement descending from it by clones (rule 37a). With field 1 it gives up only the signer's place among that area's holders, in that agreement and its descendants, until a constitutional clone refits the area (rule 37b); the signer must hold that area there. Only a party to an agreement founding a collective may resign or step down; a deal is left only as its terms provide. *Earlier passes had the resignation name the latest act of every other sequence its signer keeps (Flaw E); F109 removes that field: which device a member signed from no longer matters to the collective.*

### Abandonment declaration (type 13)

```cddl
declaration-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the agreement in which the party is declared absent
  1 => hash,                ; the clause's version: the version of that agreement whose abandonment clause (field 9) it applies,
                            ;   the last one, back from field 0 through its parents, that the party signed (rule 51); the judicial
                            ;   tier changing only with every member whose voice remains (rule 46a, F121), it carries the clause in force for every such party
  2 => hash,                ; the party declared absent: a party to the agreement of field 0
  3 => [+ uint]             ; the outcomes (rule 53), ascending, none twice, each one the clause allows
}
```

Signed by the authority the clause names (rule 49), with its own key (B12). Like a resignation, its inside names the agreement as both chain and predecessor, `[[agreement, agreement]]`, and it is never a fork. **A declaration counts only when it passes rule 51's checks:** it is valid under Identity; the party is a party to the agreement field 0 names; field 1 is the last version of that agreement, back from it, that the party signed by a valid signature act, and carries an abandonment clause; every outcome it states is one the clause in force allows, the abandonment clause of the agreement field 0 names; and it is signed by that clause's authority (reading 7, corrected: the clause in force, for every party, one whose voice was removed before a judicial change included): the identity the clause names, or, where the authority is a threshold of the other parties, one of them, its number counted where the declaration takes effect (rule 49, Q37). **Where the authority is a threshold of the other parties, one of them signs the declaration and the others add signature acts naming it** (type 1, field 0), as for terms; **it counts once the required number have signed** (B15): its signer and the signers of those signature acts, each among the other parties whose voice remains where it takes effect, meet the number counted there. In a collective a signature act on a declaration counts as the collective's own sequence places it ("Made before, made after", 2): toward a line, only where the collective placed it at that line or before, by an act of the collective acknowledging it before the line, or by the record registering the declaration acknowledging it (Envelope, `acks`), as a record names the signature acts that complete its clone (A2). A signature the collective places only after that line completes nothing there; a later record registering the declaration counts it. *Fixing the signatures at the line keeps a declaration from being completed late, by a signature arriving after its line, and changing what an earlier line did (A2's reason). Client conformance: the collective's client acknowledges the others' signatures in the record registering the declaration, or before.* **Where the declaration takes effect at a recovery rotation** (C7), the collective cannot act without the declared member before it, so **the recovery rotation places the others' signatures: its Law declaration names them**, as it names its clone's (Flaw M), and they count at that rotation (Flaw B18, "Collectives in the identity chain"); a signature act on the declaration that the rotation does not name, and that no act of the collective placed before it, completes nothing there. *Client conformance:* the members who make the recovery rotation name in it the signature acts on the declaration that they hold. In a deal the authority is one identity, never a threshold of the other parties (B19), so a deal's declaration is complete with its own signer. A declaration that fails any check is no declaration: a record naming it in field 3 counts for nothing (record, field 3). *The core does not check the period of absence (clause key 2): it is judged on the time reference, outside the core, and a party wrongly declared absent contests it (rule 52). Field 1 names a version rather than repeating the clause, so a verifier checks it against the version the party signed, and a declaration can never pick a friendlier clause. Since F121 that version carries the one clause in force for every member whose voice remains (rule 46a); the field and its check stay as they were.* *Where a party's voice was removed before a judicial change, the version it signed last may carry an older clause; the clause in force applies to it, as to every departed holder (reading 7, corrected by Nobody, allegedly).* In a collective it takes effect at the collective's line registering it (record field 3), or at the recovery rotation where the declared party is the one who would draw that line: the rotation that removes the declared party (C7, B16); in a deal, at its own place, as the deal's keepers record it (Q28; "Made before, made after").

### Negotiation message (type 18)

```cddl
negotiation-payload = {
  0 => tstr,                ; the message, as canonical text (Text MIP)
  ? 1 => hash               ; format: a text format cMIP, as a text act may name one (Text MIP)
}
```

Signed by one side of the negotiation, with its own key (F118). **A thread** is identified by its first message: the first message names no chain in `objects`; every later message names, in `objects`, the thread and the message it follows, `[[thread, previous]]`, the previous message being the thread's first message or another negotiation message of the same thread. **The two sides** of a thread are the signer of its first message and the first other identity to sign a message of it; a message signed by anyone else is no part of that thread's record. Each message's `acks` name the latest message of the other side that its signer has received, and nothing else; a side that has received nothing from the other side yet carries no `acks`. Its text is shown as a text act's is, plain text always available (Text MIP rules 3 to 5, rule 4a). *A text act carrying acknowledgements is invalid (Envelope rule 4a, F110), so negotiation lives on Law's own layer, as F110's reasoning assumed: a reaction or a text reply can never be a negotiation message (F118).* *Cost, stated (F118):* a client that does not implement Law shows a negotiation message as unknown, never as text; a negotiation needs a Law client, which it needs anyway to sign the deal it leads to.

### Record (type 17)

```cddl
record-payload = {
  ? 0 => hash,              ; the complete clone of the collective's agreement it writes on the record
  ? 2 => [+ hash],          ; the signature acts that complete that clone (A2); present exactly when field 0 is
  1 => [* kept-tip],        ; the latest act of every other sequence the collective keeps
  ? 3 => [+ hash]           ; what it registers (A1, C3, C5): resignations and steppings down by parties, abandonment
                            ;   declarations removing a party's voice, and parties' own rotations, each by its act's id
}                           ; at least one of fields 0 and 3

kept-tip = [ act: hash, position: uint, summary: hash ]   ; as in Identity: the act's id, its position, and the running summary including it
```

Signed by the collective, with its signing key, as an everyday act in its own sequence. Its inside names, in `objects`, the clone as both chain and predecessor, `[[clone, clone]]`, as a signature does; a record naming no clone names the agreement in force for it the same way, `[[agreement, agreement]]`. **A record is the collective's everyday line** ("Made before, made after"): field 1 names the collective's other sequences. **A record is a decision** (F127): its own sequence and the tips field 1 names are the action heads it saw, the previous decision among their history; it carries no other citation. **A record must be done** (W3, F127): one not sealed to every member of the agreement in force for it, nor public, is no line: it puts nothing in force, registers nothing, and places no signature (rule 35a). *One rule for every act in the collective's name, no exception by type. Example: a key holder draws a record registering a member's resignation, sealed to nobody: it is no line, and the member's voice remains until a record that is done registers it.*

**A record puts its clone in force with the signature acts it names, and only those** (A2). Each act field 2 names must be a valid signature act naming the clone (rule 5); the record places each at its own position, and the clone counts as complete at the record only if those signatures meet the powers its mark names, counted as rule 44d says at the record. A signature act naming the clone that field 2 does not name counts nothing toward it at this record, whenever it was made. *Earlier passes had the record count as acknowledging every signature act naming its clone (Flaw F). Building the ordering test found that this let a record be completed late, by a signature arriving after it, and then knock a later record out of force; and that it let a member who had already left sign a clone and be counted (F109). Naming the signatures closes both.* A record puts its clone in force only if that clone is complete, judged with the signature acts it names, and its parent is the agreement in force for the record act itself (rule 37c), or, where records on concurrent lines leave a fork for the record (rule 37c), the latest clone of one of its branches, the record coming after both lines (rule 47, B11): records follow the agreement chain one clone at a time, never skipping it. A record of a clone that changes the constitutional tier puts nothing in force: only a rotation declares such a clone (rule 37). **A record's two parts count apart** (Q36): a record whose clone it does not put in force still registers what its field 3 names, and is still a line. No area reaches a record, as none reaches a rotation: it is judged by the clone it names alone, whose own mark already carries the consent it needed, so the holders of a Law lane cannot hold up another area's change. *The key grammar's holders produce the collective's signature on it, as on every act of the collective; they could already withhold that signature from any area's acts, so the record gives them no new power.*

**Field 3 registers departures and rotations** (A1, C3, C5). Each act it names must be a resignation or stepping down (type 16) by a party of the agreement in force for the record, an abandonment declaration removing such a party's voice (rule 53), or a rotation (Identity) of such a party's own identity; a record naming any other act there counts for nothing. A declaration removes a voice when it counts (type 13), its outcomes include 0, its party is a party of the agreement in force for the record, and the agreement it names is that agreement or one it descends from by clones (B12); where its authority is a threshold of the other parties, that number is counted at this record, among the other parties whose voice remains there, with the resignations and steppings down this same record registers in effect, other declarations it registers not (rule 49, Q37; reading in "Open in this draft"), and met by its signer with the signers of the signature acts naming it that this record or an earlier act of the collective acknowledges (B15). Each takes effect for the collective's acts at this line ("Made before, made after"), whether or not the record puts its clone in force (Q36). The record is not before itself: a clone the same record writes is judged with the departures it registers already in effect, so a signature it places counts for its departed signer only where an act of the collective placed it before (C2). *Client conformance:* the collective's client registers a departure, and a member's rotation it learns of, in its next act, as it records a complete clone at once (rule 37c). The rotation that follows a departure (rule 37) still fences off the departed member's key share (F100). *A departure needs nobody's permission; the line is the collective's, drawn with the everyday key, and a collective that delays it only delays itself (F109).*

### Made before, made after

*MOR has no clock, and every identity, a collective as much as a member, may keep several sequences at once, one per device (Envelope, "Sequences"). Earlier passes of this draft judged a collective's events by the acts of the member concerned, in that member's own sequences; members keep as many as they like, forget some, and nobody else can see them all, so each patch opened another gap (Flaws E to L). Envelope's own principle settles it instead: every "before" in the core is judged inside one named chain, by reference to a specific act (Envelope, "Chains", rule 3). For anything a collective does, that chain is the collective's own (F109).*

*F127 names what the collective's sequences already were: two chains. Its decisions (genesis, rotations, records, fork, closing) are its lines, each citing the action head it saw by the tips it names. Its actions (every other act in its name) are the acts of its devices' sequences, each citing its previous act there; since F127 each also cites, in `objects`, the decision it acts under, and the heads of the other devices it knows, so that the next act joins the heads where several devices signed at once (rule 35b). A sequence is one device's strand of the actions chain; a join ties strands together between lines, where F109 tied them only at lines. "The chain's role is to make acts unmissable, not to confirm them."*

**For anything done in a collective's name, "before" and "after" are judged only on the collective's own sequences, never on a member's personal sequences** (F109). This holds for every event that changes who may act for the collective: a member's resignation or stepping down (rules 37a, 37b), a declaration removing a voice (rule 53), a member's own rotation (Identity), a clone put in force (rules 37, 37c) and the freeze of an area (rule 37b). Each takes effect for the collective's acts at a **line**: a record (type 17) or a rotation of the collective, which names the latest act of every sequence the collective keeps other than its own (record field 1; a rotation's kept tips, Identity).

1. **The collective's own acts.** An act of the collective counts as made before a line when the line's history holds it (F127): when it precedes the line in the line's own sequence, or lies in the ancestry of a tip the line names, proved by position and running summary as for a rotation's kept ancestry (Identity), or is reached from there through what those acts cite on the collective's chain, the tips of a record so reached included; for a rotation, its kept tips alone, by Identity's rule. Every other act of the collective counts as made after it, whenever it was really made: an act on a branch the line left out, by mistake or on purpose, an offline device's act its drafter had not heard of, and a fork made later from an act before the line. Lines are ordered among themselves the same way, by the collective's tips alone. **For the agreement in force at a record, only the records before it count** (B2); **for an action, the records its history holds, and those before them** (F127, rule 35b): a record it does not cite, a concurrent one included, decides nothing for it, save where it ends a power the action uses (the tie rule, point 7). *B2 had every record an act is not before count, a concurrent one included; since F127 an act within its signer's powers counts as the signer knew the collective's head.* *Read literally between two records drawn on two devices, each would count as after the other, each would judge the other's clone under the wrong parent, and A4's fork could never arise.*
2. **Others' acts are placed by the collective's.** An act of another identity done in the collective's name has no place of its own in the collective's sequence. A member's signature act is placed at the act of the collective it signs (C1); at a record naming it among the signature acts that complete its clone (record field 2, A2); at a rotation naming it among the signature acts that complete the clone it declares (rule 37, Flaw M); at a recovery rotation naming it among the signature acts on a declaration that takes effect there (Flaw B18); and at any act of the collective acknowledging it (Envelope, `acks`) (C2). It counts as made before a line when one of those acts does, and as made after it otherwise. An act signed with a grant key (F128) is the collective's own, on the grantee's strand of its actions chain, citing its grant, as every action cites (F127, rule 35b); it has no place by its key: it is before a line when the line's history holds it, that is, when an act of the collective before the line joined it, or a later act of the same strand that such an act joined; where a decision ending the grant's powers does not hold it, the tie rule voids it (point 7), save where the collective itself adopts it (rule 40, A6).
3. **A departure takes effect at the collective's line** (A1, C3). A resignation, a stepping down, or a declaration removing a voice takes effect for the collective at the line registering it: a record naming it in field 3. A member's voice remains for an act of the collective made before that line, and through any signature of theirs placed before it; for everything after it, it is gone (rules 37a, 37b, 44d). **Two lines neither of which is before the other are concurrent** (A4): where a departure is registered on both, an act counts as made before it only when it is before both; records on concurrent lines are a fork of the agreement chain (rule 37c). A declaration takes effect at the collective's line, or, where the declared member is the one who would draw it (for instance the only holder of the collective's signing key), at the rotation made by the recovery path (rule 36), a line by its kept tips: the rotation that removes the declared member, declaring the clone that takes that party out of the parties, so that clone is counted without the declared party (B16); where the declaration's authority is a threshold needing more than its own signer, that rotation names the others' signature acts and places them there (Flaw B18); the abandonment authority draws no line for the collective (C7). *A rotation names no declaration itself, only the signature acts completing one where its number needs them (B18); a recovery rotation declaring nothing new (Flaw B1) removes nobody, and leaves the declaration to the first record under the new key.* A rotation declaring a clone that removes a party, or takes a holder off an area, is that change's own line (rule 37).
4. **A member's own rotation is registered the same way** (C5). The collective registers a member's rotation (Identity) on its line, as it registers a departure (record field 3). A signature act of that member made with the key the rotation replaced, and placed before that line, stays valid for the collective, whatever the rotation kept or disowned, where it signs an act the collective's key holders also signed: an act of the collective, or a clone that a record or rotation of the collective puts in force, naming that signature among those that complete it (Q33, A2, Flaw M). Its status under Identity is unchanged, and every client shows that status. Such a signature placed after that line is judged by Identity alone (rule 5). *If the member's key was stolen, the thief's signatures placed before the line count too, but only where the collective's key holders also signed: a stolen member key can finish an act the collective signed, never start one; the collective then reorganizes around the theft.* *Cost, stated (C5): within a collective, Identity's theft protection is softened to this extent.* *Cost, stated (Q33): a signature on a clone that no act of the collective placed before the line registering its signer's rotation is judged by Identity alone, so one made from a device the rotation forgot no longer counts, as with C2; the collective's client avoids it by acknowledging members' signatures as they arrive.*
5. **The collective's keepers place its own acts a line left out** (A3, C4). An act of the collective that a line left out counts as made before that line when the keepers named by the collective's agreement in force for the line (Q34) recorded it before recording the line, as the keepers' own rule counts what is recorded: any one, a threshold, or all of them (rules 8, 11, 11a; B9). Keepers place only the collective's own acts: never a member's signature, a grantee's act, or one line against another, which only the collective's tips order. *Stated: a keeper that records a line late opens a window, the trust in keepers rule 11a already states.*
6. **Nothing else places.** An acknowledgement by any other identity (Envelope), a member's friend or a counterparty, carries no place in the collective's sequence: it keeps the act visible and changes nothing here (Flaw G). A member's personal sequences, and which device signed what, are never read for the collective.
7. **The tie rule** (F127). Where a decision ending powers (a fork, a closing, a departure, a grant's revocation, a departure emptying an area: F128, reading 4 adjusted) and an action using them cite neither each other, **the ending wins: the action is void.** An act of the collective a fork's or closing's history does not hold counts for nothing (rule 47a); a member's voice a line removes is gone for every act the line's history does not hold, an act judged under a version earlier than the one the departure names included (rule 44d); an act signed with a grant key that a revocation of its grant, or the line emptying its grant's area, does not hold is void (rules 37b, 40; G1, G2). *An action missing from the ending's history was made with powers that are ending; its counterparty deals again with a successor. Otherwise anyone holding a power could keep a fork or closing from settling by racing it. A counterparty protects itself by waiting until the actions chain visibly moves past its act before delivering.* *Stated limit:* two acts signed at the same moment without seeing each other cannot be ordered by hashes alone; with two chains this remains only where a decision ending powers meets an action using them, and the tie rule settles it. Where the decision ends no power (a record of an operational or judicial clone), each act is judged under the decisions it cites (rule 35b).

*Costs, stated (F109).* *A departure takes effect for the collective's acts when the collective draws its line, not when the member signs it: until then, the departed member's signature still counts on the collective's acts. The line is drawn by whoever produces the collective's signature, who could already withhold it from any act. An act of the collective on a branch its own line left out counts as made after it, unless a keeper placed it: the cost falls on the collective, whose line it was, and on whoever relied on the act; it is the power its key holders already have at a rotation, which can leave out a branch (Identity rules 15 to 17).* *A member who left can still complete an act the collective signed before its line, and it counts: "the act was drafted with her in it" (C1). A pending act stays completable by whoever held the area when it was made.* *A signature the collective never acknowledged before its line, on a clone recorded only after it, no longer counts for its departed signer (C2); a collective's client SHOULD acknowledge members' signatures as they arrive, which keeps Q23's outcome.* *Client conformance:* the collective's devices share their tips before a line is drawn, and a client warns before drawing one with a device not heard from, as before a rotation (Identity).

**In a deal, a declaration draws its own line** (Q28). A deal has no sequence of its own, and F109 does not reach it. An abandonment declaration with outcome 0 (rule 53) removes the party's voice in a deal from then on: every signature act of that party on the deal counts as made after it, except one that the keepers the deal names recorded before recording the declaration, as their rule counts it (rules 8, 11, 11a; B9), so a clone of the deal completed, and so recorded, before it is never undone. The other parties then change the deal without the declared party (rule 45b). *A deal's authority is one identity (Flaw B19): its signature alone makes the declaration, so the declaration is its own line, with nothing to complete after it.* *A signature on a deal's clone names the clone, an agreement, so the deal's keepers can place it; deals that need protection name a keeper.*

### Collectives in the identity chain

A collective's genesis declares its founding agreement in the declarations slot (Identity): `[LAW, 0, agreement]`, where kind 0 is **the agreement the collective lives under** and the value its id. A rotation declares, with the same kind, each complete clone that changes the constitutional tier, membership included (rule 37), its value naming the clone and the signature acts that complete it: `[LAW, 0, [clone, [+ hash]]]` (Flaw M); the recovery rotation of C7, where a declaration taking effect there needs other signatures than its signer's, adds a third element naming those signature acts: `[LAW, 0, [clone, [+ hash], [+ hash]]]` (Flaw B18); only such clones need the collective's keys to rotate (Q8). Every other complete clone is written by a record act in the collective's own sequence (rule 37c, F106).

**A rotation puts its clone in force with the signature acts its declaration names, and only those** (Flaw M), as a record does (A2): each must be a valid signature act naming the clone (rule 5); the rotation places each at its own position; and the clone is complete at the rotation only if it is complete (rule 45) judged with those signature acts alone, the powers its mark names counted as rule 44d says at the rotation. A signature act naming the clone that the declaration does not name counts nothing toward it there, whenever it was made. *So no signature arriving after the safety key's ceremony completes the clone late, and the records made since are never judged anew. Identity is unchanged: a declaration's value is the higher MIP's to define (Identity rule 8b). The genesis declares founding terms, which exist only when every founder has signed them (Q11), and names no signatures.*

**A recovery rotation places the signature acts on a declaration that its third element names, and only those** (Flaw B18), as it places its clone's (Flaw M). Each act the third element names must be a valid signature act (rule 5) naming an abandonment declaration (type 13) against a party that the declared clone takes out of the parties and that the collective's signing key cannot be produced without under the agreement in force at the rotation: the declaration that takes effect at this rotation (C7, B16). A rotation naming any other act there puts nothing in force, as one naming an act that is not a signature on its clone (Flaw M). The rotation places each at its own position, and the declaration's number is counted there, among the other parties whose voice remains at the rotation, met by its signer with the signers of the signature acts placed at or before it (rule 49, Q37, B15). *The declared member is the one who would draw the collective's line, so no act of the collective can place the others' signatures before the rotation; the rotation itself does, at the safety key's ceremony, so no signature arriving later completes the declaration there (A2's reason).*

For an act the collective's key signs, the agreement in force is the agreement named by the latest declaration of kind 0 at the chain act its key is bound to, or, where a valid record made since that declaration counts for it, the clone named by the latest such record (rule 37c): for a record, a record before it; for an action, a record its history holds, or one before such a record (F127, rule 35b); its areas are the ones that act is judged by. **A rotation that declares no new agreement changes keys, not rules** (Flaw B1): its declarations slot carries the Law declaration on unchanged, and the agreement in force carries forward, recorded clones included; the records made since the last declaration, under that rotation's binding and the earlier ones, keep counting. Only a rotation declaring a new value of kind 0 supersedes the records before it. *As draft 7 was written, the agreement in force fell back to the last one declared at a rotation: a collective that adopted a payment cMIP by a record, then rotated to fence off a departed member's share before the refit was signed, would silently have undone the Finance lane's adoption for every act under the new key.*

### Split plan and split (type 8)

```cddl
split-plan = {
  0 => [+ share-rule],      ; fixed shares and role shares
  1 => hash,                ; split cMIP, which declares the remainder rule
  ? 2 => [+ fee],           ; module fees, each consented by whoever bears it
  ? 3 => unfilled,          ; what happens to a role share nobody fills
  ? 4 => [+ uint],          ; indexes of shares taken off the top, before the rest is divided
  ? 5 => uint,              ; who bears payout rail fees: 0 each receiver (default), 1 the split service
  ? 6 => amount / uint      ; maximum fee per payout the service may deduct: absolute, or in millionths of the payout
}
share-rule = [ 0, stake: uint, part: uint ]         ; a stake, by its index in the terms; paid to its current holder
           / [ 1, role: tstr, part: uint ]          ; a role, filled at payment time
           / [ 2, receiver: hash, part: uint ]      ; a named receiver that holds no stake (a service, a position)
fee        = [ module: hash, part: uint, bearer: hash ]
unfilled   = [ 0 ] / [ 1 ] / [ 2, party: hash ]     ; to the owners pro rata / held open / to a named party

split-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the receipt or payer's claim that brought the money to the owners' payee pointer
  1 => [+ payout],          ; each payout, summing exactly to the amount received
  2 => hash,                ; the split cMIP used
  3 => hash                 ; the owners' agreement whose stakes it pays (F121)
}
payout = {
  0 => hash,                ; the receiver
  1 => uint,                ; the amount, in the smallest part of the receipt's unit
  ? 2 => uint,              ; the stake it pays, by its index in the agreement's field 7
  ? 3 => tstr,              ; the role it fills
  ? 4 => hash,              ; the evidence for that role: an act signed by someone other than the split service and the payee,
                            ;   or, for a rail Module's role share, the receipt or claim naming that rail Module, where the
                            ;   payee's own pointer or vault names it (rule 22, F119); only with key 3
  ? 5 => hash,              ; a fee: the module whose fee it pays, its receiver being key 0 (Q9); never with key 2 or 3
  ? 6 => uint               ; the rail fee deducted, within the plan's maximum
}
```

*Draft 9 sketched a payout as an array whose optional parts could not be told apart; a map says which is which (F121, Q9, made exact so the split's fees and stakes can be checked).* **Every payout matches its stake exactly** (F124, N10, rule 26): for each stake a split pays, each holder receives its share of what the split pays that stake, but for rounding of one smallest unit per payout, and nobody else receives on it; every fee is taken before the stakes divide, so it falls alike on every stake. Any deviation, either way, breaks the plan, and is shown so. *No holder is paid a whole smallest unit or more below its exact share, and none above it by as many units as the stake has holders or more, which leaves room for the leftovers of rounding going to one holder (rule 21; reading 7, confirmed, F125).* Each payout is then paid as a simple Finance payment, with its own receipt naming the split. A fee taken off the top, and an app share for the client that brought the sale (a role share), are both expressed in the plan. **A split is delivered to every holder it pays** (Q9): each receiver of a payout naming a stake is among its recipients (Envelope, `to`), unless the split is public (*reading, to confirm: a public split is delivered to everyone*); and **every fee it takes is a payout naming its module and its receiver**. *The core cannot know who owns a service, so it never hides a fee: every holder sees what was taken before their share, and by whom (Nobody, allegedly: "legible and poetically public").*

### Fork (type 19)

```cddl
fork-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the original's agreement in force at the fork
  1 => hash,                ; the original collective
  2 => hash,                ; the original's identity-chain act (genesis or a rotation) its latest acts are bound to
  3 => [* kept-tip],        ; the latest act of every sequence the original keeps under that chain act: the fork's line (N2),
                            ;   the action head it cites (F127)
  4 => [2* side],           ; the sides
  ? 5 => [+ fork-share],    ; the original's stakes, where not divided by default
  ? 6 => [+ [ obligation: hash, sides: [+ uint] ]]  ; every obligation in the history it cites: the side, or the sides jointly, owing it (N13, F127)
}
side       = [ successor: hash, members: [+ hash] ]   ; the collective the side founded first, by its identity (N4), and its members
fork-share = [ agreement: hash, index: uint, [+ share: uint] ]   ; a stake the original holds, and each side's share, in millionths
```

Signed by one member of the original collective, with their own identity, never the collective's key; its inside names, in `objects`, the original agreement as chain and predecessor, `[[agreement, agreement]]`. Every other member it lists adds a signature act naming it (type 1), as for terms, and so does each successor a debt is assigned to (N13). **A fork is complete** when: it is valid under Identity; its chain act counts in the original's identity chain and every kept tip it names holds there (as a rotation's kept ancestry, Identity); the agreement it names is the one in force at its line, the original's records before the line counted (rule 37c); every member it lists is a member whose voice remains at the line (rule 44d's voices, the departures registered before it counted), on one side, and signed it; **those members meet the constitutional change rule, counted as rule 44d says** (N1); **each side's successor is held, and its founding terms, as its genesis declares them, have exactly that side's members as parties, and keep, in its stake in itself (field 7) and its departed members entry (field 22), every departed holder of the original and every member whose voice remains on no side, each at exactly their share of all the original's income** (N1, N4; how the members divide the rest is theirs); and **each successor a debt is assigned to signed it**, its signature counting by its own rules; and **it hands out everything in the history it cites** (F127, F126 item 2 rewritten): field 6 names every obligation of the original in that history, published or not, paid or not, save one sealed neither to every member nor publicly, which is never the collective's (rule 35a), one signed with a grant key included (F128), and every obligation an earlier fork handed to the original, where the original's own signature act on that fork lies in that history (F125 reading 4, as adjusted in F126); **otherwise the fork does not take effect**; and **the fork is done** (W5, F128): sealed to every member of the agreement in force at its line, or public, as it cites the collective's head by its line; otherwise it does not take effect either. *"Information runs free": an ending is no exception. Client conformance: the members' clients also make a fork public, since creditors and buyers depend on it.* A verifier that does not hold every act that history names cannot tell what it must hand out: for it, the fork is not complete. **What lies outside that history is void** (the tie rule, "Made before, made after", 7): an obligation of the original the fork's history does not hold binds no one, and no successor owes it. *F127: "a fork cites the action head; everything in that history must be handed out, or the fork does not take effect." D1's "a debt the fork did not assign is owed by every successor jointly" is withdrawn, and with it tension T1 (a successor owing a debt it never signed for): a successor owes only what the fork handed it, by its own signature. W1 is answered: publishing has no place in the line, and needs none, since what the line cites is handed out whether published or not; a debt published late was either in the history, and handed out, or outside it, and void. Readings (to confirm): "everything" is every obligation, published or not, and paid or not, so that whether a fork took effect never changes with what happens after it; while the history is not all held, the fork is not complete for that verifier.* With no stakes written, a member who signs no side keeps each member's equal share, leftovers to the first (rule 15a; reading 3, confirmed, F125). **The line:** an act of the original counts as made before the fork when it is bound to an earlier link of the original's identity chain than the fork's chain act, or, bound to it, lies in the history the fork cites: a kept tip it names, their ancestry, and whatever acts there cite on the original's chain (F127), a grantee's act so joined included; every other act of the original counts as made after it. Field 4 names each successor once, never the original; field 5's shares sum to 1,000,000, one per side, one entry per stake; field 6 names each obligation once, its sides ascending, each a side the act lists. A fork act breaking any of these is no fork. *"Forks is a constitutional decision" (Nobody, allegedly). Signing with their own identities keeps the key grammar out of it: a key holder refusing to rotate could otherwise block the fork and keep the identity (Q3). "If they are in a position to fork, how they fork is up to them": each side founds its collective first, and the act names it, so no successor needs to claim descent.*  Field 23 of each successor names the original collective, not its agreement, since the agreement may still change before the fork; any two complete forks or closings of one collective are concurrent, and neither ends it (reading 4, confirmed, F125). *A complete fork is never undone: a debt published after it was either in the history it cites, and so handed out, or outside it, and void (F127; F125 D1's "a fork is never undone over a debt" kept, its joint liability withdrawn).*

### Closing (type 20)

```cddl
closing-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the collective's agreement in force at the closing
  1 => hash,                ; the collective
  2 => hash,                ; its identity-chain act its latest acts are bound to
  3 => [* kept-tip]         ; the latest act of every sequence it keeps under that chain act: the closing's line
}
```

Signed by one member of the collective, with their own identity; its inside names the agreement as chain and predecessor, `[[agreement, agreement]]`; the other members add signature acts naming it (F124, N9). **A closing is complete** when: it is valid under Identity; its line holds and the agreement it names is the one in force there, as for a fork; **it is done** (W5, F128): sealed to every member of that agreement, or public (client conformance: made public); its signer and the signers of signature acts naming it are members whose voice remains at the line and **meet the constitutional change rule**, counted as rule 44d says; **the collective holds nothing**: no stake of a latest version of an agreement a verifier holds names it as a holder, by its identity or as null in its own terms, other than in its own income, unless the work's release (rule 17) counted with the collective's consent placed before the line; and **the collective owes nothing** (F125, D5): every obligation that binds it and lies before its line, that is, in the history it cites (F127; one outside it is void, the tie rule), and every obligation it owes as a fork's successor (rule 47a), is fulfilled in full by receipts a verifier holds, or ended by its creditor's release (Finance type 4, rule 47b); an obligation it owes as a successor is placed by the original's line, not its own (reading 5, confirmed, F126). After its line, the collective's keys count for nothing in Law, as after a fork, and its open offers are withdrawn, as by a fork (reading 6, confirmed, F125). *"Let's close… work is either sold or made public domain. Clean" (Nobody, allegedly).* *A collective that cannot pay cannot close: it stays open, abandoned, its debts visible (D5). "Holds nothing" is judged on the latest versions of the agreements a verifier holds (reading 6, confirmed); a stake sold by a transfer (type 4, its format still open) is not seen, so a collective that sold one is shown as still holding it.*

### Creditor's release (retired type 21)

*Under F125 the creditor's release was Law type 21. F126 moves it to Finance (Finance type 4, "Creditor's release"): forgiving a sum only gives up money, "a simple money decision" (Nobody, allegedly), so a collective creditor signs it by its Finance lane alone (E2). The type number 21 is retired and never reused. A release given against future terms is also a deal, a Law agreement of its own (rule 47b).*

### Release (type 5)

```cddl
release-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the work released, by its hash
  1 => [+ [ agreement: hash, index: uint ]],  ; the stakes in it whose claim ends: the work's owners, past and present
  ? 2 => [+ hash],          ; the work claims binding it to its creators: its creators' history
  ? 3 => [+ [ publication: hash, key: bstr .size 32 ]],  ; the content key of each publication carrying it, published
  ? 4 => [ point: any, keeper: hash ]   ; a timed release (N11): the point on the time reference, and who delivers the keys then
}                           ; field 3 is present unless field 4 is
```

Signed by one direct owner of the work, with its own key; every other holder whose signature the release rule asks for adds a signature act naming it (type 1). Its inside names, in `objects`, each agreement whose stake it ends, as chain and predecessor. **A release is public** (its content key on its outside), so that the content keys it carries are published. **A release is complete** when it is valid under Identity and public, each stake it names is a stake of that agreement whose object is the work, and the release rule of each such agreement in force (field 24; absent, every holder) is met among that stake's holders: **every direct owner of the work signs, departed holders of a stake in the work included, unless the release rule says otherwise** (F124, N7). **A holder that is a collective signs by its own rules**: its signature (the release itself, or its signature act on it) counts only with its consent, and a release, or a collective's signature on one, is reached by the lanes of every layer a release touches, Envelope, Finance and Law, each lane that exists meeting its threshold (rule 36a, Q7). *Two levels of ownership: the collective owns the work; its members and departed holders own shares of its income, not the work. Releasing is a business decision of the collective, like setting a price to zero, which departed holders, passive, accept; it affects every holder alike.* **A timed release** (F124, N11) names a point on the time reference of each agreement whose stake it ends, which must name one, and the identity that delivers the content keys then (field 4): the claim ends at that point, as the time reference judges it, and is undetermined where it answers undetermined. *Cost, stated (N11): the keys' delivery rests on that identity; if it vanishes, the work is released in Law but its key may never come out; the owners may always publish the key earlier. That identity is named for the keys alone, not as a keeper in rule 7's sense, which never receives content keys as such (rule 12; reading 8, confirmed, F125).* *N12: every stake in a work lives in its one claiming agreement and its transfers on that agreement's chain, so a release signed by every holder there is complete; a claim the release does not name, by another identity, stays openly contested beside it (rule 15), and the release cannot end it.*

### Grant (type 9)

```cddl
grant-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; grantee
  1 => uint,                ; scope: 0 sign new deals, 1 manage named existing ones, 2 act for an identity (post, publish, spend)
  ? 2 => [+ hash] / null,   ; the agreements concerned, for scope 1; null: this agreement, the deal whose terms list the grant
                            ;   in field 14 (F129, H4); null only with scope 1, never with field 5, 7 or 8
  ? 3 => any,               ; limits: per-deal cap, total, spending, as the grant's cMIP defines
  ? 4 => hash,              ; the cMIP defining the limits
  ? 5 => uint,              ; for a collective: the area whose power issues it, by its id in the agreement in force at the grant's binding (Q32)
  ? 6 => [+ kind],          ; the acts it reaches; required with field 5, and within that area's reach
  ? 7 => hash,              ; a reinstatement: the ended grant it reinstates, fields 0 to 6 repeating that grant's (rule 37b, Flaw N); only with field 5
  ? 8 => null,              ; the grantor is this collective, the one whose founding terms name the grant (S1); never with field 5 or 7
  9 => signing-key          ; the grant key (F128), in Identity's form [ scheme, key ]: made and held by the grantee, scoped to this grant
}

revocation-payload = {
  0 => hash                 ; the grant whose key it removes (F128)
}
```

**A grant of this collective by its founding terms** (field 8, F124 S1) is written before the collective exists, so founding terms can name it (field 14, field 21): its grantor is the collective whose founding terms name it, and **it is carried by the founding terms, which every founder signs** (F125, D6): signed by a founder, the core not judging which, it counts through them; the collective's key takes over from then on, for every later grant and revocation; the grantee accepts it by its own signature act (F128). It names no area, and reinstates nothing. A publication a grantee makes with its grant key is the grantor's own, signed by it, so payment reaches the grantor whatever client pays (F128); it needs no `for` field. A grant with field 5 is signed by the collective and completed by signature acts of the area's holders (rule 38a). Each kind in field 6 must lie within the area's reach: every act it could reach, computed as for overlapping kinds from the agreement in force ("Areas"), is an act the area reaches; otherwise the grant is invalid. A reinstatement (field 7) is the exception: its fields 0 to 6 must repeat those of the grant it names, which must be a grant within the area of the same id, and it backs acts only within the refitted area's reach (rule 37b); one whose fields differ is invalid. **The grant key** (field 9, F128). The grant names the public part of a signing key the grantee made and keeps (reading: the grantee makes it, so nobody else holds a key that acts as the grantee's within the collective); **the grant counts only once its grantee signed a signature act naming it** (type 1), accepting it, a grant by founding terms included (reading, F128, replacing D6's "the service shows its acceptance by receiving"). An act signed with the grant key names the grant as its `binding` (Identity: a scoped key, which this MIP judges) and its signer is the collective: it is the collective's own act, on the grantee's strand of the collective's actions chain, which cites the grant in `objects` as the decision it acts under (rule 35b); it is judged at the latest link of the collective's identity chain among its grant and the decisions it cites (reading). It is backed (rule 44) only within the grant's reach (field 6, or, for a grant naming no area, acts no area reaches), only while the grant has not ended for it, and never for a decision: a grant key signs no record, grant or revocation, and no act of it places a member's signature or adopts another act for the collective (reading). A grant survives the collective's rotations; it ends only by a revocation, a departure emptying its area, a fork or a closing (reading). **A reinstatement** (field 7) **is a clone of the ended grant** (G2, F128): its fields 0 to 6 repeat that grant's, its key (field 9) is its own, the grantee accepts it again, and it takes nothing on.

**A payee's grant in a deal** (F129, H4). Where a deal names a split service, each payee grants the service a grant key of its own, written in the deal's terms: **the grant names "this agreement" by null** (field 2, scope 1), as founding terms name "this collective" (S1, D6), so it is written before the deal and the deal names it: **field 14 lists one grant per payee**. The grant is a person's grant (rule 38), signed by the payee with its own key, and public; **it counts only through the deal: once a deal its grantor signed lists it in field 14 and exists, every party having signed it** ("signing the deal signs them", Nobody, allegedly); before, it backs nothing. **The split service signs to accept each grant**, as every grantee does (F128). Every grant field 14 lists names the same grantee, the one split service, and is signed by a distinct party; a deal breaking this carries none of them (reading, F129). *"Feels the cleanest" (Nobody, allegedly): the mechanic is the collective's founding grant, and the service is no party to the deal, so leaving it stays a revocation, never a renegotiation.* **Each payee can revoke its own grant** (type 10, an everyday act, public, rule 38); the other payees' grants stand.

**What a payee's grant key may sign** (F129, H5). **Only receipts for money coming into the deal**: a Finance receipt (type 2) whose payee (field 3) is the grantor, and which is a purchase naming the deal's claim (field 9: the deal, or a version of it), or a payment following the deal itself (field 5); **never a receipt whose payer (field 2) is the split service itself, nor a split's payout** (a receipt carrying a batch, field 8, or naming a split in field 5 or in `objects`), and no other act. *"If it's a grant, it should use the grant key. Worth noting that whoever uses it still signs from under its own identity but via grant key" (Nobody, allegedly).* So a payout without the payee's own receipt stays the service's open obligation (rule 29): the service cannot sign "Ben received his payout" in Ben's name. The grant names the grantee's identity, which signed to accept it, so every act of the key is traceable to who used it. *Readings (to confirm): "payments on its offer" are receipts following the deal itself, or purchases of its work, which name its claim anyway; a receipt following a standing offer (type 6), whose format is open, is refused rather than guessed.*

**Revocation (type 10, F128).** A decision of the grantor, signed with its own key and citing the collective's chain as an action does (reading), naming the grant whose key it removes; for a grant within an area, completed by that area's holders' signature acts, judged by that area alone (rule 38a, Q29). It names the grant, never a place on the grantee's strand (C6). It is a decision ending powers: **an act signed with the grant key that the revocation's history holds was done within the grant's powers, and binds; any other, racing it or after it, is void** (the tie rule, G1). *"Yes, it's a grant key, scoped" (Nobody, allegedly).*

## Agreements

1. **Proposal and signatures.** An agreement begins as terms. Each party signs with a signature act naming the terms. Founding terms exist once every party has signed them, in a deal (F107) and in a collective's founding agreement alike (Q11): their signing rule (field 4) is every party. A clone comes into force by its mark (rule 45). A party is bound by an agreement only through its own signature act: a rule decides when an agreement or a clone comes into force among those who signed, never who owes. An obligation in the terms of a party who has not signed is shown as unsigned, never as open (F74).
2. **cMIPs.** Every agreement carries the hashes of the cMIPs it uses, at most one per task. An agreement that does not is invalid. It may also name extensions (Production); a client that does not implement all of them cannot sign it.
3. **Commitments of identities.** An agreement names identity hashes, not keys. After a rotation, the party's new key signs for the same agreement.
4. **Positions** are agreements that confer income without ownership.
4a. **What you sign is what you saw.** *Client conformance.* Before any terms, clone or grant is signed, the client MUST be able to show its text as plain text, SHOULD do so by default, and MUST show every bidirectional control visibly in that view (Text MIP, rule 5a).
5. **Agreement chains.** Every act on an agreement names the chain and the act it follows. Two acts naming the same predecessor are a fork, except signatures naming the act they sign, which are parallel consents; where the agreement lets parties act independently, its concurrency rule settles it; otherwise the status quo stands. An act on an agreement chain that is void or disputed under Identity's rotation rules confers nothing: no stake, no obligation, no consent (F57), save a member's signature act that a collective placed before its line registering that member's rotation, which stays valid for the collective, as "Made before, made after" says (C5); its status under Identity is unchanged.

## Keepers

6. **Named by the agreement.** Keepers are chosen by the parties when they sign, so neither side can later substitute a friendly one; in a collective, the keepers change only with every member's signature (rule 46a, F121); a deal's keepers change only with every party (rule 45b).
7. **Records.** A keeper records the deal and the acts around it as they arrive, in its operator's sequence, and signs each record.
8. **Several keepers.** An agreement may name several keepers and a rule for what counts as recorded: any one, a threshold, or all. Only the named keepers' records count as testimony.
9. **Copies.** Records are signed and hash-linked, so they can be copied anywhere and verified wherever they sit. If a keeper disappears, its records survive in every copy.
10. **Keepers check homes.** Before recording an act, a keeper checks the signer's identity chain at its homes, and records any rotation it sees.
11. **What a rotation cannot erase.** A record counts against a rotation only if it sits before the keeper's own record of that rotation, in the keeper's sequence. Such an act, even outside the kept ancestry or disowned, becomes a visible dispute rather than void (Identity).
11a. **A record counts only alongside the act it names.** A keeper record counts, for that purpose, only if the verifier holds the act it names, that act is valid as an act, and the keeper is one named by an agreement the act's `objects` names, or, for an act of a collective placed against the collective's own line, one named by the collective's agreement as "Made before, made after" says (C4). A record of an act nobody can produce changes no verdict (F58). *A keeper can delay recording a rotation while recording a thief's acts first. That is trust in keepers, accepted and stated: choose keepers as carefully as homes.*
12. **Keepers are always sealed.** *A keeper only has to prove that an act existed before a rotation. For that it needs the act id and the signer, both on the act's outside, and a check of the signer's home, never the content.* A client MUST NOT deliver an act's content key to a party because it is a named keeper; a keeper that must also judge content is named as an arbitrator or verifier as well. *This is a rule for clients: key deliveries are private, so no one else could check it.*
12a. **Judging content is someone else's job.** Where a third party must judge content (escrow and milestones, arbitration, regulated verification), the agreement names an arbitrator or verifier, and the parties deliver it the keys by ordinary key delivery.

## Stakes and works

13. **Stake rule.** Any act that changes the size of a stake requires its holder's signature. A signature on an agreement is consent to everything it defines, including pre-authorised changes such as abandonment outcomes. Clients SHOULD make such terms prominent.
14. **Transfer.** By default, a stake is sold or given by a transfer signed by the seller and completed by the buyer's signature. The transfer names both the work's chain and the agreement's chain in `objects` (Envelope). Other holders are notified, not asked. The new holder inherits the full position, and the transfer chain on the agreement is what names the current holder of each stake; a split service MUST follow it (F73).
15. **Binding a work.** A work is bound to its creators only by an explicit work claim: a sole creator alone; several creators by one creator's claim completed by signature acts from all the others, and bound only when all are present. A publication is a neutral carrier: carrying or quoting a work, or pointing to a web resource, claims nothing. The core records claims and their order, never legitimacy. Order is not evidence of authorship; a claim cMIP MAY carry a pre-publication commitment. Where claims from different identities conflict, priority can only be decided where they meet: by anchoring, or by a keeper that recorded both. Otherwise they remain openly contested.
15a. **Stakes are written in millionths,** summing to 1,000,000. Any leftover from dividing goes to the first party listed in the terms.
15b. **A sale is tied to the seller's agreement, not to the work's stakes.** The core cannot tie a sale of a work to that work's stakes: it ties a sale to the agreement of whoever sells, and makes a sale outside the claiming agreement visible to those it concerns. A publication or standing offer for a work with an existing claim, not signed under that claim's agreement, is a valid act that visibly contradicts a signed agreement: the claim's holders contest it, a keeper has the claim on record, and the seller's own split shows what they took. *The bits and bolts are all in the core: work claims with order, contests with standing, keeper records, the published split, the publication's `for` field, and the payer's claim. A client that refuses to sell a claimed work under a plan that does not pay the claim's holders, or a reputation service that reads contests, can be built on them, and adoption decides (F72).* *Client conformance.* A client SHOULD show such a publication or offer as outside the claiming agreement. **After the fork of a collective, the original's stake passes to the successors in the shares the fork act gives them (rule 47a, F121): a successor's sale is under the claiming agreement, as a holder of that stake, never outside it.**
16. **Publication stakes.** A publisher may hold a stake in its publication, never in the work.
17. **Public domain.** The owners may release a work by a release act (type 5, F121), at once, or at a future point on the time reference, its content key delivered then by an identity the act names (F124, N11): **the claim ends**: nobody earns from the work as its owner any more, and no sale of it is outside a claim; **its history stays named**: the stakes it ends and the claims naming its creators; **its content key is published**: anyone may carry or sell copies. It needs every direct owner's signature, departed holders of a stake in the work included, a collective owner signing by its own rules across the lanes of every layer a release touches (F124, N7), unless the release rule says otherwise (field 24, set at founding, changed only by a clone every owner signs, N8); and it is permanent. **A claim the release does not name in its history is shown beside it**: made after it, or a competing claim, openly contested (rule 15, N12), which the release cannot end. *"A work that belongs to no one but traces its history" (Nobody, allegedly).* Its legal effect lies outside the core.

## Splits

How a split service runs is defined by the split cMIP its owners' agreement names. Law keeps only the vocabulary and the guarantees below.

### Authority

18. **Named by grant key.** The owners' agreement names the split service through a grant, with any limits the owners choose (for example how long it may hold money, or which rails it may use), handing the service a grant key (F128): **in a collective, one grant of the collective** (field 14, one hash); **in a deal, one grant per payee**, each naming "this agreement" by null and signed by the payee, the service holding one grant key per payee, which signs only receipts for money coming into the deal (field 14, a list; "Grant (type 9)", F129, H4, H5). The grant is revocable at any time under the agreement's rule; in a deal, each payee revokes its own. **Two models, chosen per collective** (F124, P2): a split service, two flows (payer to owners' pointer, service to holders); or payer-side splitting, one flow, the payer's wallet paying each holder's own pointer by the stakes of the work's claim, with no intermediary to bypass, each holder's own vault protecting its share. **The model is chosen by naming a split service (field 14) or not** (F125, D3); no field says which. **Where an agreement names a split service, the payee pointer of the identity its publications are made for (the owners' collective, the sole owner, or, in a deal, each payee whose own grant field 14 lists, F129) counts, for Law, only if every address in it also appears in the split service's own signed pointer in force** (F121, F123), **and every entry of its vault in force in the service's own signed vault** (F124, P2), or both in those of a service its chain of judgment names to take over (rule 34a, reading 4): one service carrying every address and every vault entry. An address is a rail, a rail Module and the address bytes it reads (Finance, payee pointer). That is how money reaches the service (Finance, F46, F68). *Client conformance:* a Law client, and any wallet reading Law, checks the pointer before paying, and shows a pointer that bypasses the split service as such, never as an ordinary pointer. *Cost, stated (F121, as F68):* a wallet that reads only Finance cannot check it, and pays the pointer as it stands; payers who want the protection use wallets that read Law. *"Make it legible" (Nobody, allegedly, F123): a bypass needs the service to sign, publicly, for an address that is not its own: collusion in plain view. No format changes. Set aside: a "receiver" field (anyone can write any name next to their own address); a pointer delegating to another identity's pointer (cleanest, but a change to Finance and every wallet); making every pointer change need every stake holder (every new rail would need everyone); sending every buyer's claim to every owner (it reveals buyers).* *Cost, stated for payer-side splitting (P2): several payments, partial payment if one fails, owners' pointers visible to the buyer, and a wallet that reads Law.*
19. **What it receives:** the incoming payment (an ordinary Finance payment, for which it signs the receipt), any payer's claim for it, the split plan from the agreement, and the evidence for role shares: the referral the payer signed in its claim, a relay's signed delivery record, a record signed by the identity running a service, and, for a rail Module, the receipt or claim naming it (F116, F119). *A Module is a specification: it signs nothing (F112, F116).*

### Duties

20. **Publish a split.** For every incoming receipt, and for every payer's claim that shows money arrived without one, the service publishes a split, signed with its own key, naming that receipt or claim and listing every payout. **It delivers the split to every holder it pays, departed or not, and names each fee and who received it** (F121, Q9; "Split plan and split"). A split not delivered to a holder it pays is shown so.
21. **Conservation.** A split's payouts MUST sum exactly to the amount received, in the unit's smallest part. The split cMIP declares its remainder rule; where it declares none, leftovers go to the first party listed in the terms. A split that does not sum exactly is invalid.
22. **Role shares with evidence.** Each role payout names who filled the role and the proof, which MUST be an act signed by someone other than the split service and the payee: the payer's client for a referral (Finance, the claim's referral field), the relay for delivery, the identity running a service for its use (F116). A referral the service names on its own earns nothing. **A rail Module's role share is the one exception** (F119): its proof is the receipt or claim naming that rail Module in its field 0 (Finance rule 10b, F116), and it counts even when the split service signs it, provided that rail Module is one the payee's own payee pointer or vault names, where the payment was paid (Finance rule 12a, F115); the payee is the identity the payment was made to, whose pointer leads to the split service (rule 18). A receipt or claim naming a rail Module the payee's pointer and vault never named is evidence of nothing, and counts for nothing as a receipt either (Finance rule 12a). *The service cannot invent a Module: what it may name is fixed by the payee, publicly, and the Module's own rule checks the rail's proof. Requiring a third party's signature here would leave only the payer's claim, which payers publish by choice, so most uses would go unrecorded.* *Cost, stated (F119): where the payee's pointer or vault names two rail Modules for one rail, whoever issues the invoice, here the split service, chooses which of them earns; the pointer shows it publicly, and the payee's agreement with the service can constrain it.* Anyone can check the evidence afterwards. A role share nobody fills follows the option the plan chose: to the owners pro rata, held open as an obligation, or to a named party (F75).
23. **Pay out.** Each payout is paid as a simple Finance payment to the current holder of the stake, or the named receiver, with its own receipt naming the split.
24. **Keep what it cannot deliver** as open obligations, visible until paid. Amounts below a rail's minimum are held until they add up to enough (F50).
24a. **Payout fees.** The split plan says who bears each payout's rail fee: each receiver by default, or the split service as part of its cut; and it states a maximum fee per payout. A payout is discharged when its receipt shows the amount minus a fee no greater than that maximum; a shortfall beyond it is an open obligation of the service (F64).
25. **Batching.** The grant may allow the service to accumulate and pay out periodically or above a threshold, measured on the agreement's time reference. Every incoming receipt must still be matched by a published split, so accumulation is visible. Payouts paid with one rail payment name the same batch in their receipts (Finance).

### Limits

26. A split service MUST NOT pay anyone outside the plan's stakes as currently held, roles as evidenced, and named receivers, and MUST NOT change the plan: only the owners can, by cloning their agreement.
27. **Fees.** A fee is one total with an agreed split among the modules that earn it. The service's own fee appears in the plan like any other cut. No split can include a fee without the signature of whoever bears its cost; on a creator-side fee, that is the creator. Every split names the modules it ran under, so an omitted declared fee is visible, and each fee it takes as a payout naming its module and receiver (Q9).
28. **Attention.** A split may follow plays, views or any metric a module reports. The metric, its module and the resulting payouts are named, so it is legible like any other; the metric's evidence is a record signed by the identity running the service that measured it, never by the split service, and never by a Module, which signs nothing (F116).

### Failure and switching

29. Every incoming receipt, and every payer's claim showing money arrived, without a matching split, and every payout without a receipt, is an open obligation of the service, naming exactly one receiver and one agreement.
30. The owners switch services by revoking the grant, naming a new one, and having the identity publications are made for publish a new version of its payee pointer. In a deal, each payee revokes its own grant, and the parties clone the deal, every party signing, its field 14 listing the payees' grants to the new service (F129, H4). The old service still owes payouts on everything it received before.
31. **The trust that remains.** *Between receiving and paying out, the service holds other people's money. MOR cannot prevent theft, but makes it provable from the receipts and the payers' claims: hiding income requires the payer's silence or collusion. Owners who want less exposure choose services that pay out immediately, or payer-side splitting cMIPs, where capable clients pay each owner's pointer directly and no service's honesty is needed.*

## Offers, conditions and time

32. **Standing offers, accepted by performance.** Owners publish terms; anyone accepts by paying them. The deal is complete when the offer's side delivers the content key or, for a publication whose key is public, signs a delivery confirmation: that proves payment was verified and honoured. A payment that receives neither, such as one arriving after a withdrawal, creates an obligation to refund it. A refund owed on a payment whose payer is not named is owed to whoever signs with the key the payment committed to as its payer, by a payment claim carrying that key's signature and naming where to be paid (Finance rule 10a, F113), never to whoever presents the rail proof, which others on the route may hold too; a payment that committed to no key leaves its refund unclaimable by anyone. The obligation stays open and visible until claimed and discharged, and a seller who refuses a valid claim is visibly in the wrong. The offer's terms MAY say how long a refund stays claimable, on the time reference; the core sets no lapse (F80). Paid access to a live stream, which has no work hash until it ends, is sold by a standing offer, not a publication.
32a. **A purchase names the claim it pays under** (F126). Buying a work under a Law claim is not pure money, since the work's claim and offer are Law: a payment for a standing offer, or for a publication carrying a work some agreement claims, is a purchase only if its receipt or claim names the claim it pays under (Finance receipt and claim field 9): the claiming agreement, and the line at which the payer's client read it current, a version of that agreement, or the fork, closing or release that last changed who is paid for the work. The payer's client therefore reads the work's Law claim; a Finance-only wallet cannot make a valid purchase. **A payment naming no claim, or a line that carries none of that agreement's claims, is no purchase: money received for nothing, owed back to the payer as a refund** (Finance rule 10a: the payer named, or whoever signs with the key the payment committed to; with neither, an open, visible debt nobody can claim). **Which claim the payment buys under is settled where the payment truly takes place** (F128, W4): *"to not make it actionable, the burden is passed to the step where the action truly takes place" (Nobody, allegedly).* **On a request rail** (the payee's side commits to each payment before it is made: the payment cMIP's commitment, which an invoice carries), **the claim a purchase names is the one the seller's request committed to**, so a buyer cannot pay a version the seller has left: the payment is a purchase under it, whatever the claim became after. **On a push rail** (the payer pays an address with no request), **each holder of the work's stake in the claim named settles on its own chain**: a receipt that holder records before its own signature on the act superseding the claim (a version of the claiming agreement changing who is paid, a release, a fork, a closing) is a sale under the old claim; one recorded after it, its own history holding that signature, is not; **the payment is a purchase only if every holder's receipt is a sale; otherwise every holder refunds what it received**, and the buyer buys again under the current claim; until every holder has signed its receipt, the payment is **unrecorded**. A push payment to several holders is one rail payment: their receipts carry its one rail proof (reading). *"The clock is when each holder records, the only one MOR has, and it belongs to the one answering for it." A receipt on another of the holder's sequences than its signature is not after it (reading): each holder answers for its own devices.* **Where the seller is a collective, a payment becomes a sale once the collective's actions chain records it** (F127, W2): an act in the collective's name that counts (done, on its chain, not void at an ending), signed with its own key or with a grant key of its, its split service's among them (reading 6, adjusted, F128), that is the payment's receipt, acknowledges it, or is a receipt for the same rail proof; on a request rail, the claim being the one the request committed to; on a push rail, where the claim named was current as that act's agreement in force reads it, and before the collective's own release where the release is current. **A payment the original's chain never recorded before the fork or closing that superseded its claim is no purchase, and is refunded**; a sale in the fork's history stays a purchase; one recorded concurrently with the fork falls under the tie rule (the fork wins; refunded). Until the seller's chain records it, the payment is **unrecorded**: a sale once recorded. *The payment is the buyer's act; the sale is the collective's, on its chain, where the fork's line can place it.* *W4 had a superseded claim no collective's chain settled shown as undetermined; F128 withdraws that state: no payment is undetermined.* *"Records it" and "the claim current at an act", confirmed (readings 4 and 6, F127): the latest version, in force for it, that changed who is paid for the work.* *Client conformance (F126):* a split service whose grant has ended issues no payment request, so on rails where the payee's side issues each request (invoices), a payment to a superseded claim cannot happen. *Cost, stated: a wallet that reads only Finance can pay a claimed work's publication, but its payment is no purchase; the money is owed back to it.* *"The work carries a law act, why can the finance only wallet act?" (Nobody, allegedly).*
33. **Time.** An agreement that needs deadlines names its time reference. "Past its terms" is judged against that reference only. Showing that something did *not* happen before a deadline needs evidence: an anchoring cMIP or a named keeper. Without either, the answer is undetermined.
34. **Conditions.** An agreement may make an obligation or a payout depend on a condition, evaluated by the condition cMIP it names.
34a. **The chain of judgment** (F121). For each judge it names, an agreement may name, in its terms (field 21), the ones that take over, in order, when it answers "unknown" or does not act within its period: for a judicial task, other specifications; for a keeper's operator, an arbitrator or verifier, or the abandonment authority, other identities; for the split service, other grants. Everyone signs the chain with the terms: every party in a deal, every member in a collective, as for the whole judicial tier (rule 46a). **When a judge answers "unknown", the next in its chain is asked, and the first answer that is not "unknown" is the chain's answer; no new signature is needed when it passes.** A condition cMIP's "unknown" and a time reference's "undetermined" are such answers; "pending" is not (Tasks). Where every one answers "unknown", the chain's answer is "unknown". *A client that does not hold a judge's specification shows the question as unknown, as for any act it cannot judge; that is the client's ignorance, not the judge's answer, and never passes the question down the chain: otherwise two clients holding different specifications would reach different answers.* **A judge that does not act passes the question on too** (Q7): each link names a period; once the judge has been asked and has not acted within it, as the agreement's time reference judges it, the next may act; where the time reference answers "undetermined", so is the chain's answer. **The period runs from a signed request to the judge by someone with standing** (rule 57a), **and an answer the judge gives after it counts for nothing in that question** (F124, N6): "if the next judge is triggered, the previous judge is void". *"It's a chain of judgment" (Nobody, allegedly): a judge that fails is replaced by the one everyone already signed, so a dead judge traps nobody.*

## Grants and collectives

35. **A collective is a full identity,** with its own genesis, homes, signing key and safety key. It uses the Identity MIP unchanged: it signs its own receipts, sets its own payee pointers, and rotates. Its genesis declares its founding agreement (a Law declaration in the declarations slot), and the agreement carries its key grammar.
35a. **A collective's act is done only once sealed to every member and on its chain** (F126, F128). An act in a collective's name, signed with its key or with a grant key of its (F128), is done, and binds the collective, only once it is sealed to every member, every party of the agreement in force for it among its recipients (Envelope, `to`) or the act public, and it is on the collective's chain, citing its head as its signer knew it (rule 35b). Before that, even signed, it is planning, and binds no one, its signer included: it counts for nothing in Law (an area's signatures on it complete nothing), and an obligation it carries binds no one. **Where an act is held is never a condition** (F128): an act is valid by its signatures, seals and citations wherever a verifier holds it; **relays are transport**, where a collective's clients publish and look first, client conformance or a cMIP's (the relay transport cMIP), never the core. **A verifier states where it found each act as information only; no validity rests on it** (reading 9, adjusted, F128). *A counterparty is safe once a later act of the collective cites its act.* *Client conformance:* the collective's client drafts the act, signs it, seals it to every member, and publishes it where the collective's clients look. *"Powers can be segmented, but information should run free; it's a continuous collective assembly where matters are exposed. It only bothers cheaters" (Nobody, allegedly).* *Cost, stated: a collective keeps no secret from its own members. What a collective plans can stay with some members for a while; once done, it is a fact that touches everyone in it.* **Records too** (W3, F127), **and forks and closings** (W5, F128): one rule for every act in the collective's name, no exception by type; a record not done is no line ("Record (type 17)"), a fork or closing not done takes no effect (rule 47a). **Negotiation messages are the one kind not held to it** (W6, F128): they are talk, binding nothing, on neither chain, and need not be sealed to every member, planning being allowed to stay secret (F126); the deal they lead to is signed, and that signature is an action, which must be done. **The collective's identity-chain acts (genesis, rotations) are done by Identity's own rules**, public and at its homes (reading 8, adjusted, F128: no relays). *F126 had "done" need the act's outside on one of the relays the terms named (field 25), "one is enough"; F128 withdraws it (field 25).*
35b. **Two chains** (F127). A collective keeps **a decisions chain** (its genesis, rotations and records, which write its clones and register who joins and leaves, its grants and revocations (F128), and its fork or closing), each citing the previous decision and the action head it saw, by what it already names: a record by its own sequence and kept tips, a rotation by its kept tips (Identity), a grant or revocation by its own sequence and its citations in `objects`, as an action cites (reading, F128), a fork or closing by its chain act and tips; decisions keep their formats (reading 1, confirmed); and **an actions chain**, every other act signed with its key or a grant key of its (reading 2, adjusted, F128): debts, payments, publications, releases, sales, and the acts of its grantees' strands; negotiation messages are on neither chain (W6), nor Identity's own everyday acts. **An action names, in its inside's `objects`, the collective's identity as chain, once for each act it cites: the decision it acts under (a record of the collective, or a link of its identity chain, under the action's own key or an earlier one), and each head of another device it knows, so that the next act joins the heads where several devices signed at once**; its own sequence's previous act counts as cited. These entries come after those the act's type defines, all on that one chain (Law's formats allow them there: signature, release, resignation, declaration, terms). **An act within its signer's powers counts for the collective as soon as it is done and cites the collective's head as the signer knew it**: it is judged under what its decisions leave in force (the chain's declarations, records carried forward under earlier keys, and under its own key the records its history holds and those before them, rule 37c), and a record it never cites decides nothing for it, save by the tie rule ("Made before, made after", 7). **An action citing no decision, or naming on the collective's chain an act that is not on it, is on no chain of the collective, and counts for nothing.** **An act signed with a grant key cites, among its decisions, its grant** (F128): the grantee's strand is like a device of the collective, limited to the grant's scope. **An action is judged under the decisions its history holds** (reading 3, confirmed). *"If I act within my powers in a collective I act for the collective" (Nobody, allegedly). The chain's role is to make acts unmissable, not to confirm them. Client conformance: the collective's client cites its latest decision and every head it holds.* *Identity's own everyday acts (a witness act, routes, an encryption key) carry no objects (Identity) and are on neither chain (reading 2, confirmed).*
36. **Key grammar.** The founding agreement says how the collective's keys are held and activated, according to the members' taste: one holder, a threshold (any k of n members jointly produce one ordinary signature, for example with FROST threshold schnorr), or a custodian holding the key under the collective's grant. The safety key is held by one holder, a custodian, or as shares: split so that any k of n members can rebuild it. At rotation, k members bring their shares to one offline device, which rebuilds the key, signs once and forgets it. *For that moment one device holds the whole key; that is the price, and it holds the next key too, before dealing its shares. Nothing can prove a device forgot a key.* The Module that does it MUST deal the shares of the next key verifiably, and have them checked against the committed key before that key is relied on, so that the members' shares rebuild it: otherwise the device's holder could end up the only one able to rotate the collective (F97). *Verifiable dealing stops sole control, not a copy; a copy kept is the price above, stated.* **A key grammar MUST leave a way to rotate that survives the loss of any one key holder** (F96): a threshold below the member count, or a named recovery path (a custodian holding a share under grant, or an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority, which the abandonment clause names). A key holder is anyone holding the whole key or a share of it: a member, a custodian, the holder of an escrowed share. **Where one person holds the safety key, the agreement MUST name a successor (a seat successor in that holder's succession plan) and an escrowed share released to them under its succession or abandonment clause;** a single custodian likewise needs a recovery path held by another. A grammar without such a way is invalid. Two partners may choose "both", provided they name where a further share sits and who releases it; whether the path works is the members' risk, stated (F77). *For a single holder, one construction: the key is split so that any two of four shares rebuild it; the holder keeps two and signs alone, the successor and a keeper hold one each, useless alone. When the named authority declares the holder absent, the keeper releases its share and the successor rotates the collective to a new key; the succession clone passes the seat.* **That succession clone also carries a succession plan for the successor, naming their own seat successor, signed by them in the same clone** (Flaw B17, rules 44c, 48c), so the key always has a named successor, and the clone meets this rule. *The successor signs the clone to join anyway (Q11), so the plan they add is signed by them; the escrowed share stays where the key grammar's recovery path puts it, released by the same clause.* Where members want individual consent to show, the constitution gives acts of the collective to areas (rule 36a).
36a. **Areas: rules by layer, powers of members** (F103). A collective's founding agreement may divide its acts and its operational fields into areas, each held by some of its members: by whole layer (Identity, Envelope and Text, Finance, Law, Production) or by act type within a specification. **An act of the collective that an area reaches counts only with valid signature acts naming it (rule 5), by holders of that area in the agreement in force, each of whom signed it or a version it descends from by complete clones, and whose voice there remains for that act ("Made before, made after", rules 37a, 37b), meeting the area's threshold as rule 44d counts it; the agreement in force for that act is the one the collective's identity chain declared at the chain act its signing key is bound to (its binding), or, where the act comes after a record made since that declaration, the clone such a record put in force, as rule 37c picks it** (F100, F106, F109). An act of an area that stands frozen counts for nothing (rule 37b). An act no area reaches counts on the collective's own signature, as any identity's act does, unless its specification is unadopted (below). *So when members change, the rotation that declares the clone also fences off the old rules: whatever the old key signs afterwards is void under Identity, whoever signs it. Members' own identities do not rotate when they leave a collective; without this, former members could sign under the old rules at any later time, and no one could tell.* *An area says whose consent counts; the key grammar says who can physically produce the collective's signature. A holder who should act alone without holding the key acts under a grant issued within the area (rule 38a), with the grant key it hands them (F128).* *Layers and areas are one construct (Q10): a rule for a whole layer, a lane, is an area that reaches that layer, held by the members who decide on it; an area may also reach single act types, and operational fields, with one set of rules for holding it, granting within it and changing it.* **In a collective whose agreement in force has any area, an act whose `spec` is a specification that agreement names neither for a task (field 2) nor as an extension (field 15) counts for nothing** (Q16), unless an area reaches it by a kind naming its type, `[1, spec, type]`, and then counts as that area decides. To count, the specification must first be adopted, under the powers its adoption needs (rule 44c): for an operational task, that task's lane; as an extension, the Production lane with the lane of each layer it declares. An act of a MIP is never such an act. *Otherwise an unlisted specification would be a way around every lane: whoever holds the collective's key could act under a specification nobody adopted, around the lane's holders. A rail is not adopted this way: it is named by the collective's own pointer or vault (Finance rule 12a, F115). With adoption in the lane's own hands, waiting costs little.* An area reaching a layer reaches every act of that layer ("Layer"): the MIP's own acts, the acts of the cMIPs the agreement in force names for that layer's tasks, and, for Production, the acts of the extensions it names (F106). An act that belongs to two layers or more, of a specification named for tasks of each, is reached by the lane of each, and counts only when each lane's holders meet its threshold, as a clone across two areas does (Q7, R4). **A lane decides what the collective adopts for its layer** (F106): which cMIP fills each of that layer's tasks, so the holders of the Finance lane adopt a new payment cMIP alone, the rails beneath it being named by the collective's payee pointer, an act of that lane, or its vault (F115); and, for the Production lane, which extensions the collective adopts. **The tasks of the judicial tier (9 condition evaluation, 10 time reference, 11 anchoring) stay judicial, outside the Law lane** (Q3, Q15): never the Law lane's holders nor any other area changes them; they change only with every member's signature (rule 46a, F121), a version that also changes the constitution included, which needs the constitutional change rule too (rule 44c.1, F122). The Law lane adopts the cMIPs for Law's other tasks (8 split, 12 grant limits, 13 work claims). **A specification adopted for a judicial task is adopted for no other task, and is no extension** (Q20, Q25, "Areas"), so R4 never joins a judge to a lane. *Each judicial task can decide who wins a dispute, and one lane holder picking a friendlier judge after the fact is worse than a majority doing it.* **An extension acting on another layer needs that layer's lane too,** in the same clone, all at once (Q7): its specification declares the layers it acts on (Production, specification field 10), so a client knows whose approval to ask (rule 44b); **dropping an extension needs the same approvals as adopting it**: the Production lane and the lane of each layer it declares (Q18). *"Modules and cMIPs should be able to be governed on the layer they sit."* The declaration is not trusted for enforcement: in a collective, a verifier applies an extension's rules only to acts of Production and of the layers whose power its adoption met, so an extension that under-declares binds nothing beyond the lanes whose holders approved it. Nothing an extension does relaxes the core (Production 8c): the MIPs' own acts stay in their own layers, whatever an extension adds around them.
36b. **Every constitutional voice is covered** (F105). Every party whose signature a collective's constitutional change rule is counted among (every party under the default or a threshold, the named parties under a rule naming parties) MUST be covered by the agreement's abandonment clause: the clause allows outcome 0, removing a voice, and its authority can declare that party absent, so a named authority does not cover itself. Coverage is judged under the clause in force, one version for everyone (rule 46a, F121). A founding agreement or clone of a collective that leaves such a party uncovered is invalid, as a key grammar without a way to rotate is. *The constitution is the tier that can repair every other (reassign an area, revoke a grant, refit the key grammar), so it alone must never freeze; it freezes only through the loss of someone whose signature it needs. A member without constitutional power needs no clause: their loss is repaired from above. This is F96's promise, a dead member never freezes the collective, carried from the keys to the rules.*
37. **Changing members** means a rotation to new keys plus a clone of the founding agreement; the rotation declares the complete clone as the agreement the collective lives under, naming the signature acts that complete it, and places each at its own position; the clone counts as complete there only with those ("Collectives in the identity chain", "Made before, made after", Flaw M). Membership is constitutional, so the clone needs the constitutional change rule (rule 44c): every party whose voice remains at the rotation, by default; a member whose resignation (rule 37a) or declaration with outcome 0 (rule 53) the collective registered on a line before the rotation is no longer counted. The rotation is a line by its kept tips: a change of members, or of an area's holders, that it declares takes effect for the collective's acts there. A clone that takes a party out of the parties takes it out of every area's holders too, and an area it leaves with no holder is written with an empty list of holders and stands frozen until a constitutional clone gives it a holder (rule 37b, Q21). A constitutional clone that removes a party who did not sign it, where the constitutional change rule allows that, takes that party's voice only: the party keeps every stake it holds (rule 46), as a departed holder, under the collective's current rules and judges (rule 46b, F121) (Q6). Any other change to the constitutional tier (the change rules, the key grammar, the areas) is likewise declared by a rotation before it applies to the collective's acts. Each agreement the chain declares after the founding one MUST be a clone of the agreement in force at the rotation (the one declared before it, or recorded since, rule 37c), its parent (field 11) naming it, complete with the signature acts the declaration names, and MUST change the constitutional tier; otherwise the collective's acts that need member signatures count for nothing. *A rotation declares only a constitutional clone (B5): a clone changing only the judicial or operational tier is written by a record. And it declares only a direct clone of the agreement in force (B6): every other clone is recorded at once (rule 37c), so there is never a clone in between, and one that nothing recorded would have no line at which its completeness was judged (A2).* A rotation declaring nothing new carries the agreement in force forward (Flaw B1, "Collectives in the identity chain"). *Clones that change only the judicial or operational tier need no rotation (Q8): they change neither who holds the collective's keys nor who decides, so there is no old key to fence off; they are written on the collective's record by a record act instead (rule 37c). The collective's key resembles a safety key more than a signing key in that regard.* A departing member hands over nothing: the remaining members rotate to keys the departing member never held, and re-split the safety key among the members who stay or join.
37a. **A member can always leave alone** (Q6). A party to a collective's agreement gives up its voice by a resignation act (type 16), without anyone else's signature. **The resignation takes effect for the collective at the collective's line registering it** (record field 3, F109), which the collective's client draws in its next act: from that line on, the party's signature acts count toward no change rule and no area of that agreement or of any agreement descending from it; a signature of the party placed before the line stays valid, and still counts toward what it signed; for that clone or act alone, its signer still counts among the voices that remain, so the departure never lowers the number the rule asks for ("Made before, made after", rule 44d, Q23, C2). The party keeps its stake, and stays bound by what it signed; it is then a departed holder (rule 46b, F121). The remaining members then rotate the collective's keys (rule 37) and clone it without the departed party, which takes it out of every area it held too (Q21), and records it, with its stake in the collective itself (field 7), in the departed members entry (field 22). Meanwhile, from the line on, each rule, and each area the party held, is counted among the voices that remain (rule 44d, flaw C); an area left with no holder stands frozen from that line until a constitutional clone gives it a holder, as in rule 37b, every grant in it ending at that line (G2, F128), to be reinstated, where wanted, by cloning it at the refit. A resignation counts like a loss (F96, F105): where the key grammar's recovery path is an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority, the authority may release it on a resignation as on a declaration of absence. *Leaving needs no permission, and lets nobody remove anybody else; the collective that delays drawing the line only delays itself. Absence remains the abandonment clause's business, under the clause the member signed.*
37b. **Leaving an area** (Q13). Giving up an area changes the constitution at that level: it is a constitutional clone (rule 44c), declared by a rotation (rule 37). It comes about in one of three ways: the holder leaving nominates a replacement, and the constitutional change rule approves it or not; the constitutional change rule proposes a replacement, and the holder leaving approves it; or the constitutional change rule redraws that area's rules entirely. The replacement signs the clone too (rule 45, Q11). Until one of them completes, the holder keeps the area. *Mechanically the first two are one clone, signed by the leaving holder and meeting the constitutional change rule, started from either side; the third needs no signature of the holder, since an area's holders and rules change by a constitutional change (Q5).*
**Stepping down at once.** A holder may instead give up an area at once, alone, by a resignation naming that area (type 16, field 1): *"I'm done, won't do anything anymore."* It takes effect for the collective at the collective's line registering it (record field 3, F109): from that line on, the holder's signature acts count toward nothing in that area, in that agreement or any descending from it; a signature of the holder placed before the line stays valid, and counts as rule 44d says (Q23, C2). The holder keeps the rest of its voice, and its stake. The collective then makes the constitutional change for that area by one of the three ways. **Meanwhile, where other holders of the area remain, they carry on** (Q17), counted as rule 44d says: the area's threshold stands where enough of them remain, and all of them together meet it where fewer remain than it asks for; grants issued within the area keep backing acts. **An area with no holder remaining is frozen** until that change: its acts made after the line that froze it count for nothing (Q17), **and every grant issued within it ends for good** (Q22). The line that freezes the area is the collective's line registering the departure of its last holder whose voice there remained (a resignation, a stepping down, or a declaration removing that holder's voice, rule 53), or the rotation declaring a clone that leaves the area with no holder (A1). **A departure that leaves an area held leaves its grants in force; only a revocation ends them** (G2, F128). **A departure that empties an area ends every grant in it: a decision ending powers** (G2, F128): an act signed with such a grant's key that the line's history holds was done within the grant's powers, and binds; one racing it (neither citing the other) is void, the ending winning (the tie rule); every act after it is void. **No suspension, and no undetermined act**: C8's undetermined state for grants is withdrawn. *"An empty area should be treated as an emergency. End it. The copy still exists, cloning it takes two seconds if need of reinstating" (Nobody, allegedly).* A void act binds the collective only where the collective itself, by an act of its own key that counts, acknowledges, pays on or imports it, adopting it (rule 40, A6; reading). *Client conformance:* a member's client shows an empty area as an emergency. The change ends the freeze once a constitutional clone is declared that changes that area's entry of field 19, the entry with its id (Q32), so that it has a holder whose voice remains (Q21). **At the refit, the new holders may reinstate any ended grant by cloning it** (Flaw N, G2): a **reinstatement**, a grant (type 9) whose field 7 names the ended grant and whose fields 0 to 6 are that grant's, with a grant key of its own (field 9), signed by the collective and completed by signature acts of the refitted area's holders, meeting the area's own rule, its threshold as for any grant within it (rule 38a, Q27), and accepted by the grantee. It is a new decision, judged at its own place like any act the area reaches (rule 36a): it counts only when made after the line at which the new holders took the area, since before it the area has no holder to complete it. One reinstatement for each grant, never one for several, and nothing is reinstated automatically (Q22). A signature act naming the ended grant itself reinstates nothing: it is placed at that grant (C1), before the freeze, where the new holders held nothing. A reinstated grant backs acts as a grant within the refitted area, and only within that area's reach (rule 44), from its own place on; **it takes nothing on**: the acts under the ended grant stay as the ending left them (G2). *F109 had grants that a freeze ended sealed or reinstated at the refit, the grantee's acts the collective never placed undetermined meanwhile, and a reinstatement taking them on (C8, Flaw H). With grant keys, the grantee's acts are on the collective's own chain, so the line that emptied the area places them as it places every act, and nothing is left to decide later.* *Stepping down thus works as resigning does (rule 37a): neither can be played against the other, and one holder cannot stop the co-holders' work at will, nor keep an area frozen by withholding the signature its refit needs. A grant is the area's, not one holder's: a grant made within the area before the stepping down, even one to the holder who stepped down, keeps backing acts while the area runs, until its remaining holders revoke it (rule 38a).* *"If her departure freezes a layer, the collective will organize quickly to fix it." Payments to the collective's payee pointers still arrive on their rails; only the collective's own acts in that area wait.*
37c. **Every complete clone is written on the collective's record at once** (F106, flaw D). A clone that changes the constitutional tier is declared by a rotation (rule 37). Every other complete clone of a collective's agreement is written, at once, by a record act (type 17) that the collective signs in its own sequence. For an act the collective's key signs, the agreement in force is the one the chain declares at the act's binding, or, where a valid record made since that declaration counts for it, under the same binding or an earlier one that a rotation declaring nothing new carried forward (Flaw B1), the clone named by the latest such record, the one furthest along the agreement chain; for a record itself, only the records before it count (B2); for an action, the records under an earlier binding carried forward, and, under its own, the records its history holds and those before them (F127, rule 35b): a record it never cites decides nothing for it, a concurrent one included, save by the tie rule ("Made before, made after", 7). **Records on concurrent lines are a fork of the agreement chain, settled by the agreement's own concurrency rule** (A4, Q38): where two valid records of different clones of the same parent, neither before the other, both count for an act (a record after both, or an action whose history holds both), it is judged under the clone that the parent's concurrency rule (field 10) selects; where the parent has no concurrency rule, under the parent itself, the earlier version staying in force, until a clone of either branch, recorded after both lines, resolves it, as rule 47 says (B11). *The concurrency rule is a judicial clause every member signed (rule 46a, F121); where the collective's records were drawn concurrently, which of them its key holders drew first does not overrule it. Records drawn one after the other are no fork: the second names a parent no longer in force, and puts nothing in force.* A record of a clone counts only if that clone is complete, judged with the signature acts it names (A2), and its parent is the agreement in force for the record itself, or one of the clones a fork leaves for it (B11), so records follow the agreement chain one clone at a time; a rotation's declaration of a new agreement supersedes every record before it, and a rotation declaring nothing new supersedes none (Flaw B1). *MOR has no clock, so an operational clone that nothing writes on the collective's record could not tell which of the collective's acts it governs: a payment cMIP the Finance lane adopted would not govern the collective's acts until the next rotation, and acts of a cMIP adopted for a task would fall in no lane, counting on the collective's own signature, without the lane's holders (flaw D). The record fixes the moment in the collective's own sequence, with the everyday key; only clones that change who decides need the safety key's ceremony (Q8).* *Until the record exists, the collective's acts are judged under the agreement before the clone: a delay, visible, which the key grammar's holders could already cause by withholding the collective's signature from any act.*
38. **Grants** delegate authority with limits: signing new deals, managing specific existing ones, or acting for an identity or collective (posting, publishing, spending up to a limit). **A collective's grant hands its grantee a grant key** (F128): an agent acting for a collective works under a grant, holding a key of the collective scoped to it, never the collective's own keys; what it signs with the grant key is the collective's own act, within the grant's reach, on the grantee's strand of the collective's actions chain, citing the grant (grant field 9, rule 35b). The grant is a decision naming the grantee's own identity, which signs to accept it. Grants are revocable at any time, by a revocation, a decision ending powers, which removes the key (type 10). A grant is authority to act in the grantor's name without a stake, the employer-and-employee kind; it confers no voice in the agreement. *Cost, stated (F128): a stolen grant key acts within its scope until revoked; the collective's identity gains a scoped kind of key (Identity, "Scoped keys").* **Every identity grants keys the same way** (F128, extended; H2 answered): a person's grant hands its grantee a key of the person, and what the grantee signs with it is the person's own act, citing the grant on the person's chain (the person's identity as chain). **A person's grant and its revocation are everyday acts, signed with the person's own signing key: adding or removing a grant key needs no rotation and no safety key.** They are public (reading: "scoped and visible"; a person has no members to seal them to, and everyone who checks the grantee's acts must read them); a private one backs nothing. A rotation of the grantor judges its grant like any act signed with the key it replaces (Identity rules 15 to 17): a grant the rotation does not keep is void, and its key with it, so a thief who stole the everyday key and granted itself a key loses it at the owner's next rotation. A person's grant reaches the kinds it names (a MIP's layer, or a type of a specification), and, naming none, every act but a decision (reading). *Cost, stated: as with every act of the everyday key, a thief holding it can also revoke the owner's grants until the owner rotates and grants again.* **In a deal, each payee grants the split service its own grant key, scoped to that deal** (F128, extended; F129): the grant names "this agreement" by null, the deal lists it in field 14, one per payee, and it counts once the deal exists; its key signs only receipts for money coming into the deal, never one whose payer is the service, nor a split's payout ("Grant (type 9)", H4, H5).
38a. **A grant never reaches beyond the power of whoever issued it** (F103). The holders of an area may grant within it: the collective signs a grant naming the area (field 5) and the acts it reaches (field 6), within the area's reach, and it counts only with valid signature acts naming it by the area's holders, meeting its threshold as rule 44d counts it, as for any act the area reaches (rule 36a); it is judged by the area it names alone, whichever area reaches grants as acts. **So is a revocation of such a grant** (Q29): it counts only with valid signature acts naming it by the holders of the area the grant names, meeting its threshold as rule 44d counts it, whichever area reaches revocations as acts; and so is a reinstatement (rule 37b, Flaw N). A collective's grant naming no area is judged as any other act of the collective, and reaches only acts that no area of the agreement in force reaches. A grant whose field 6 reaches beyond its area is invalid, a reinstatement aside (rule 37b). *So a member holding an area can act alone there, under a grant to their own identity, and can hire others for it; and neither they nor the one who holds the collective's everyday key can give anyone more than that area.*
39. **Strands** (F128). A collective's actions chain has a strand for each of its devices and for each grant key; a grantee's acts are its strand, each citing its previous act there by `prev` and its grant on the collective's chain. *F109's "each grant opens a branch the grantee appends to" read the grantee's own sequence, which nobody else could see; a grant key's strand is the collective's own, which every member's chain reading follows.*
40. **Revocation** (F128, G1). Revoking a grant removes its key. The revocation names the grant, never a place on its strand (C6). **An act signed with the grant key that the revocation's history holds was done within the grant's powers, and binds; one racing it, neither citing the other, is void, the ending winning (the tie rule); every act after it is void.** A revocation of a grant within an area is judged by that area alone (rule 38a, Q29). **The grantor may adopt a void act of a grant key as its own**: an act of the grantor's own key that counts (for a collective, an act of the collective's own key that counts), acknowledging it, paying on it or importing it, makes it bind (F76, A6, reading, F128; F129, H3 and reading 6). **Another grantee then manages it under its own grant** (scope 1, naming it), with its own grant key (F129, H3). *A collective that took the money cannot later say the deal never happened. The adoption is the grantor's own act, never the grantee's: no act signed with a grant key adopts anything (reading 4, confirmed).*
41. **Import.** The grantor's import act lists acts of an ended grant it adopts into its own chain; its format is still open (a verifier refuses it). **The handover is withdrawn** (F129, H3): a grant key's acts are the grantor's own, on its chain, so there is no branch to move them from; a void one counts only if the grantor adopts it (rule 40), and another grantee then manages it under its own grant. *Adoption already does the job, visibly; a second mechanism would have no purpose of its own.* Type 11 is the import alone.
42. **Acknowledgements.** An acknowledgement of a grant key's act counts as an adoption only when it is an act of the collective's own key that counts (rule 40), wherever it sits (C6).
43. **Fate of acts.** An act of a grant key that a decision ending its grant does not hold (a revocation, a departure emptying its area, a fork, a closing) does not bind the collective: void, unless the collective adopted it (rule 40). **Counterparties: wait until a later act of the collective cites the act before performing** (F128): from then on it is in every ending's history.
44. **Evaluation.** A client evaluating an act signed with a grant key follows its binding to the grant (F128). It binds when: the grant counts (its area's holders met, or the founding terms carry it) and its grantee signed to accept it; the act is signed with the grant's key, cites the grant on the collective's chain, is done (rule 35a), and lies within the grant's reach (rule 38a); and no decision ending the grant fails to hold it (rules 37b, 40, 47a), or, where one does, the collective adopted it (rule 40). An act beyond the grant's reach is not backed by it, whatever its strand. *F109's "live, sealed, ended branch" and C8's undetermined acts are withdrawn: every act of a grant key binds or does not.*

## Tiers

*F103: nobody loses their say by default. Each part of a collective's agreement is changed by the power that owns it, and which power that is follows mechanically from the fields a clone changes, so every verifier reaches the same answer and the mark (rule 45a) can be checked.* **Tiers and areas apply to collectives only** (F103, scope): to a founding agreement carrying a key grammar, and its clones. A deal has none: every clone of it needs every party (rule 45b). *"Deals should be as simple as possible while remaining rich. Collectives is for when things get complicated." Two people who end up doing many deals together can found a collective.*

44b. **What a clone changes.** A clone changes a field when the field's deterministic encoding in the clone differs from the parent's, or the field is present in one and absent in the other. Fields 2, 15 and 20 are compared entry by entry, by task number, by extension and by area id, and each differing entry (added, removed or replaced) is one change. Fields 4 and 11 are never compared, nor field 23: a clone never carries it ("Terms"). **A change of field 16 that only takes, for each party the clone takes out of the parties, the seat part of its succession plan (keys 2 and 3), the plan staying for its stake part, or going where it has none, every other plan as it was, is no change of its own: it goes with the removal, as the party's areas do (Q21)** (F124, M1). Each change is in the tier of its field or entry (rule 44a). An operational change lies:
- for an entry of field 2: in the parent's area reaching that task's layer as a whole, its lane (F106); where no area reaches that layer, in the parent's area whose field references name that task, `[1, task]`; where none does, in no area, under the clone rule (B4); and, where the specification the clone names for that task is also named in the clone for a task of another layer, in that layer's lane too (R4); a clone naming one specification for a judicial task and for another task is invalid, whatever its mark (Q20);
- for an entry of field 15: in the parent's area reaching Production, and in the area reaching each layer that the extension added or dropped declares in its specification (Production, field 10): dropping an extension needs the same powers as adopting it (Q18); a clone naming as an extension a specification it names as a judge is invalid, whatever its mark (Q25);
- for an entry of field 20: in the area whose id it carries;
- otherwise: in the parent's area whose field references name that field.

The part of a change lying in a layer that no area of the parent reaches, and any other operational change, lies in no area.

44c. **Which powers a clone needs,** from its changes alone:
1. if any change is constitutional: the constitutional change rule, `[0]`; and, where some change is also judicial, the judicial tier's rule, `[4]`, too (F122, revising F120): the version stays a draft until both are met, its mark naming both. *F120 had the constitutional change rule alone, because rule 46a once kept the old judge for anyone who did not sign; F121 removed that, and F122 keeps two members of three from changing a judge by adding a one-word change to the constitution. The constitutional change rule still changes every other tier alone; a judge needs every member as well.*
2. otherwise, the power of each area in which some change lies, `[2, area]`, one for each such area (Q7); the clone rule, `[1]`, if some operational change, or part of one, lies in no area, or if the clone changes nothing; and the judicial tier's rule, `[4]`, if some change is judicial (rule 46a, F121). So a clone that changes an area and something outside every area needs both, its mark naming both (Q12).
3. a clone that executes a party's succession plan may instead name that plan, `[3, party]`, alone, where rule 48c allows a seat to pass automatically (Q14), provided it changes nothing but what the plan gives (Flaw B14): the party out of the parties (field 0), its seat successors in; the party out of the holders of every area it held (Q21), nothing else in the areas changed; **the seat successors in the party's place in every holding of the key grammar (field 12), every threshold unchanged**; and **the executed plan dropped** from field 16, every other plan as it was; where the party held the safety key alone (`[0, party]`), **a succession plan for the seat successor in the executed plan's place**, naming at least one seat successor of theirs, as rule 36 requires (Flaw B17): the seat successor signs the clone as the plan's signer, so that plan is signed by them in the same clone. *A key grammar's holdings name only parties, and in most collectives every member holds a share of the safety key, so a seat could never pass without the keys; a plan left in field 16 for someone no longer a party would make the clone invalid. The thresholds stay as the members chose them (F96); the rotation declaring the clone re-deals the keys (rule 48c). What this leaves open, a verifier refuses rather than guesses ("Open in this draft").*

A clone needing several powers comes into force only when each is met, all at once or not at all (Q7). *The clone rule does not reach into an area, and one area's holders do not reach into another: an area's power is exclusive, held by its holders or by the narrower rule that governs it. Its holders, and the rule they act by, change only by a constitutional change (Q5).*

44d. **Counting a power.** The constitutional change rule is counted among the parent's parties, under the parent's field 18, or every party when the parent has none; the judicial tier's rule among the parent's parties, every one of them (F121); the clone rule among the parent's parties, under its field 5; an area's power among that area's holders in the parent, at least its threshold of them. In each, only parties whose voice remains are counted, judged on the collective's own sequence at the act, or, for a clone, at the record or rotation that puts it in force ("Made before, made after", F109): a party whose resignation (rule 37a) or declaration with outcome 0 (rule 53) the collective registered on a line before it is left out, and so, for one area, is a holder whose stepping down from it (rule 37b) the collective registered on such a line, under every parent descending from the agreement in which it lost that voice. **A party is named again only by a constitutional clone** made after the version in which it lost that voice, naming it as a party or as that area's holder, which it signed by a signature act the collective did not place before the line registering its departure; it counts again under that clone and its descendants (B10). *An ordinary clone that only copies the list of parties or of an area's holders restores nobody: otherwise a holder who stepped down would count again by signing a change of the area's words.* **A party that lost its voice still counts, for a clone or an act, through a valid signature act on it placed before the collective's line registering its departure** ("Made before, made after", F109, Q23, Q28): for that clone or act alone, it counts as a voice that remains, so the rule is met or not as written, its number counted with that voice among the others, never lowered by the departure; it counts so even where the record or rotation putting the clone in force does not name that signature among those completing it, and is then a voice that did not sign there (B3); and a declaration never undoes a clone that a record or rotation put in force before the line registering it, whatever version of the clause or of the agreement it names. *"Her position at time of signing is valid. She was still acting. Whether it gets approved now depends on collective rules." Under three of three, a member who signed a clone and then resigned still leaves it needing both others: three voices count for it, and three signatures are needed; a rotation naming only the two others' signatures does not meet it (B3). Under every party, it needs everyone who has not left, the early signature standing among them. Under F109 "before she left" reads "placed before the collective's line": the record came first, or the collective acknowledged her signature as it arrived (C2).* **When fewer voices remain than a rule's number asks for, all the remaining voices together meet it; where enough remain, the number stands as written** (F105, flaw C). This holds for every number an agreement states, and for the judicial tier's "every party": the constitutional change rule, the clone rule, an area's threshold, one a clone left above the area's holders included (Q26), and an abandonment authority that is a threshold of the other parties (rule 49). When no voice remains, nothing meets the rule: an area left without a holder, or listing none (Q21), stands frozen, as in rule 37b, until a constitutional clone gives it a holder. *A number can outlast the voices it counts: "three of three" would still ask for three after one voice is removed, and F105 would unfreeze nothing. Read this way, a number never freezes a rule, it matches what "every party" already does, and it keeps the founders' number wherever enough voices remain. A lone survivor still holds the keys and the constitution only under F96 and F105: the key grammar's thresholds are cryptographic, and this rule never lowers them.*

44e. **Protections still hold.** Whatever power brings a clone into force, it cannot reduce a stake without its holder's signature (rule 46), a departed holder's included (rule 46b), nor change the judicial tier without every member, a version changing the constitution included (rules 44c.1, 46a; F122).

*Stated cost (F103): by default a member who is present but unwanted cannot be removed; the others can leave, each alone (rule 37a), or use the abandonment clause where it applies. A group that wants expulsion writes a constitutional change rule below everyone at founding, where every founder signs it; even then, removal takes the voice, never the stake (Q6). A member who dies or vanishes never freezes the constitution: the abandonment clause every collective carries can remove their voice (rule 36b, F105).*

## Changing agreements

45. **Clone, never modify.** A clone is new terms naming the parent and the parent's latest act. Its mark (field 4) names each power its changes require (rule 44c, or rule 45b for a deal) and, for each, the parties whose signatures meet it (rule 44d, or rule 45b for a deal). It is a draft until every party its mark names has a valid signature act naming it, every party it adds and every party it makes a holder of an area it did not hold has one too, every holder whose share of a stake it drops or lowers has one too, member or not (rule 46, F124 N5), every departed holder whose own stake plan it changes has one too (M1; reading 2, confirmed, F125), every holder of every stake the parent defines has one too where it changes the release rule (N8), and the parent exists; a draft changes nothing. *Nobody is added to a collective, or given an area, without their approval (Q11, Q13): a party added is bound only by its own signature (rule 1), and until it signs, the clone that adds it changes nothing.* A complete clone closes the parent; obligations before that point settle under the parent.
45a. **A false mark makes a clone invalid** (F104). A clone is invalid, whatever signatures it gathers, if its mark names other powers than those its changes require, or names, for a power, a signer who is not among the parties that power is counted among, or too few of them to meet it (as rule 44d counts them). A mark naming a succession plan, `[3, party]`, is checked as rule 48c says. Signature acts by parties the mark does not name bind those parties (rule 1), but do not bring the clone into force. *The mark names exactly the required powers, never "one at least as strong": powers are not ranked against each other (an area held by one member is neither stronger nor weaker than a clone rule of two of three), so "stronger" could not be computed. It names identities, not signature acts: the signature acts name the clone, so the clone cannot name them. Every version thus says on its face who brought it into force and under which power, and any verifier checks it from the parent and the clone alone. Founding terms' field 4 already said the same thing: which signatures make them exist.*
45b. **A deal changes only with everyone's signature** (F107). In a deal the signing rule and the clone rule are every party (terms fields 4 and 5): a deal exists when all its parties have signed it, and a clone of it completes only when all the parent's parties whose voice remains have signed it. Its mark names the clone rule, `[1]`, and those parties. Majorities, areas and tiers belong to collectives; rules 44a to 44e, 46a, 46b, 47 and 47a apply to collectives only. *Cost, stated and accepted: one silent party blocks every change to a deal, however large: "one person can force everything until all agree. That's deal making in everyday life." The ways out: the abandonment clause, whose declaration is not a clone, can remove an absent party's voice where the deal provides it (rule 53); succession plans; and, for many parties or a long relationship, a collective.*
46. A clone can never reduce a stake without its holder's signature, unless the agreement pre-authorised it.
46a. **Protected clauses: the judicial tier** (collectives only; F121, replacing F71's per-member versions). **The judicial tier changes only with the signature of every member whose voice remains, and there is one version of it for everyone**: the abandonment clause, the succession plans, the concurrency rule, the keepers, the split service, the time reference, the arbitrators or verifiers (field 13), the condition, time reference and anchoring cMIPs (field 2, tasks 9, 10 and 11) (Q3, Q15), and the chain of judgment (field 21). A clone changing any of them names the judicial tier's rule, `[4]`, in its mark, with every party of the parent whose voice remains, counted as rule 44d says; a version that also changes the constitution names the constitutional change rule beside it, and needs both (rule 44c.1, F122). **A clause naming only the member a version removes goes with the removal** (F124, M1): only the seat part of their succession plan, which needs no judicial power (rule 44b); the plan for their stake stays with the stake, as a departed holder's plan (field 16 may carry it; it names stake successors only), and no other judicial clause changes that way. A departed holder's stake plan changes only with that holder's signature too (reading 2, confirmed, F125). Every member, and every departed holder (rule 46b), is judged by the version in force. *"It's the joint signature that matters" (Nobody, allegedly). Under F71 a member who did not sign a change kept the version they signed, so two members could hold different judges, and where one joined after a change the other never signed, no judge was signed by both: nothing said who decides between them. With one version for everyone, every dispute has one judge both signed. Arbitrators, the condition cMIP and the anchoring cMIP can each decide who wins a dispute, so a majority still cannot pick a friendlier judge after the fact (Q3).* *Costs, stated and answered (F121): one member can hold a change back ("leverage is part of life"); a judge that dies cannot trap the collective, the chain of judgment taking over (rule 34a); and a holdout makes a standoff: "if a group within the collective wants change A but another group refuses it, it creates a standoff, which is good. Communication becomes the only channel to solve it, with a collective fork as an option to settle it" (rule 47a).* In a deal no clone completes without everyone, so the question never arises (F107).
46b. **Departed holders** (collectives only; F121). **A collective's departed members entry (terms field 22) records only who left, without voice; their stake is in the collective's stakes (field 7), which decide what every holder is paid** (F124, N5). A departed holder has **no control and no veto**: it is counted in no change rule and no area, and the collective's current rules and judges apply to it. **Its stake never shrinks without its signature** (rule 46): a clone that lowers its share in field 7, or drops it, is a draft until that holder signs it (rule 45), as for every holder. **The stake is a share of all the collective's income**, not only of what was made while it was a member. **Every term applies equally to every stake, member or departed:** a term may not treat a departed holder's stake worse than a member's of the same size; a fee raised for the departed is raised for the members; and a fee paid to a service the members own is public. **The stakes decide every holder's money** (field 7, Q8, F124 N5), and **every payout matches its stake exactly**, every fee alike for every stake ("Split plan and split", rule 26, N10): equal treatment is what following the stakes with uniform fees produces, not a separate test, and any deviation, either way, breaks the plan. *A member who wants to give income to another transfers part of a stake (N10).* **Every split is delivered to every holder it pays and names each fee and who received it** (rule 20, Q9), so a fee to a service the members own is seen by everyone it is taken from. **Unpaid shares stay open debts** (Finance rule 16) until the holder can receive, for instance after setting up on a new rail the collective chose. *"A person who keeps stake in a collective accepts that they have zero control over decisions. If I think the collective won't survive, the incentive to sell the stake is there. It's a stake in a collective as a whole; it simply defines his share of income" (Nobody, allegedly). A rail swap is the collective's decision: "to get paid, past members have to accept the decision and set up accordingly if they wish to get paid". Equal treatment, from company law, makes any creative accounting "legible and poetically public".* *The core checks the split, not the split plan, whose format (field 8) is still open.*
47. **Forks of records** (collectives only). Where a change rule of a collective (a clone rule, or an area's power) lets parties act independently, the agreement must define how forks are settled. If it does not, the parent stays in force until a clone of either branch, written on the collective's record after both lines (a record that each line of the fork is before), resolves it (B11): that clone comes into force there, and the other branch is not in force. A clone of a branch is a clone of that branch's latest clone, as records follow the agreement chain one clone at a time (rule 37c): where a branch moved on, a clone of its first clone resolves nothing, and where a branch forked again, no clone resolves the fork through it. Two clones recorded on concurrent lines are such a fork, settled the same way (rule 37c, Q38). *A clone names one parent (field 11), so "a clone naming both branches" could not be written; recording a clone of one branch after both lines shows the collective saw the fork and chose, with no new format. "Its latest clone" was found by the ordering simulation: read as a clone of the branch's first clone, a resolution would silently drop a clone already recorded on that branch. A clone of one branch recorded after only one of the lines resolves nothing, and two clones of the branches recorded on concurrent lines leave the fork as it was.*
47a. **How a collective ends: four shapes** (collectives only; F121). *"A fork forces a whole lot of decisions to be taken again" (Nobody, allegedly).*
- **A. A group splits off, with the powers to do so.** An ordinary membership change (rule 37): the collective re-signs, by a constitutional clone and a rotation, and keeps its identity; the members who leave become departed holders, recorded with their stake in the departed members entry (field 22, rule 46b), keeping it under equal treatment, the pointer check and no veto; they may found a collective of their own. Members without the powers can still leave one by one (rule 37a) and found something new together.
- **B. The fork proper**, where both groups want changes and disagree. **Each side first founds its successor**, a new collective (its own genesis and keys) whose founding terms have that side's members as parties and name the original in **"forked from"** (field 23), a back-link only, never a parent (F124, N4). **A fork act** (type 19) **names the successors by their identities, and, signed by the members on its sides with their own identities under the constitutional change rule, closes the original in Law** (N1): every member by default, a silent member being the abandonment clause's business; where the founders set a lower rule, the fork may complete without a member, who is "forced to either fork or leave": **a member who signs no side has no seat in any successor, and is a departed holder of each at their percentage**. From the fork's line (N2), anything the original's keys sign counts for nothing in Law, whatever its key grammar's holders do; a rotation of the original is optional cleanup. *Two fork acts signed concurrently on the same agreement are concurrent acts on its chain (rule 5): with no concurrency rule, the status quo stands, and neither closes it (N4). A later fork of a successor is a new fork, in sequence.* Once the fork is complete:
   - **Ownership.** The fork act transfers the original's stake in each work, as a stake transfer, to the successors, in the shares it names (field 5), and by default in proportion to the stakes of the members on each side in the original itself (field 7, Q8), divided in millionths, leftovers to the first side (rule 15a); **where the original carries no stakes in itself, each member counts alike** (N3: "no agreement means equal weight"). Each successor then owns its share outright and runs it: pointer, rails, split service. The work's fingerprint never changes.
   - **Departed holders keep their percentage in every successor**, and their stake follows into the successors' future works, a stake in each collective as a whole: each successor's founding terms keep every departed holder of the original, and every member who signed no side, at the same share of all its income, in its stake in itself (field 7) and its departed members entry (field 22); the members divide the rest. *Example: four at 25%, one of them departed; sides taking two thirds and one third: the departed holds 25% of each, the others 37.5% in one or 75% in the other.* *"If too many departed members still own shares, the incentive is to create an entirely new collective instead of forking."*
   - **Debts.** **The fork cites the original's action head, and hands out every obligation in that history, each to one successor or to several jointly, or it does not take effect** (F124 N13, F127; "Fork (type 19)"); **the successor collective signs for each debt assigned to it**, by its own signature act on the fork, so members are never made personal debtors of what the collective owed. **A complete fork is never undone over a debt**: a debt published after it was in its history, and handed out, or outside it, and void (the tie rule); **no successor owes a debt the fork did not hand it** (F127, withdrawing F125 D1's joint liability). **A later fork of a successor hands out, in turn, what an earlier fork handed it**, where its own signature on that fork lies in its history (F125 reading 4, as adjusted in F126). **An obligation signed by a collective binds it once done, like any act** (rule 35a, F126, F128): sealed to every member, and on its chain; a debt sealed to the creditor alone binds no one, its signer included, so hiding gains nothing. **N13's "a collective's obligation binds only once its outside is public" and D2's "the verifier found it on a relay" are withdrawn** (F128, "if the two chains solve it, gone"): publishing the debt is the creditor's choice and protection (client conformance), and no validity rests on where an act is stored. *The cost N13 stated, anyone seeing how many debts a collective has, goes with it.*
   - **Grants.** **Every grant of the original ends at the fork, the old split service's included** (F124, N14): **every grant key ends at the fork's line** (F128), as when a departure empties an area (rule 37b); each successor grants, with a grant key of its own, what it wants. An act of a grant key in the history the fork cites, or that the original adopted before the fork, binds, as a debt; **any other is missing from the ending's history, and void** (the tie rule, F127): its counterparty deals again with a successor.
   - **Money after it.** **Payment follows the work's current claim** (N14): the work carries nothing and never changes; its claim names the successors as owners, so routing follows to their own pointers and split services. **The fork act withdraws the original's open offers**: a wallet reading Law sees them withdrawn, and pays a successor's offer instead. **A payment that reaches the old split service after the fork** (a wallet not reading Law paying a withdrawn offer, a push rail that cannot refuse) **names no claim, or the original's superseded claim, and the original's actions chain never recorded it before the fork: it is no purchase, and is owed back to the payer as a refund** (rule 32a, F126, F127 W2; the old service's grant key ended at the fork's line, F128; reading 9, which forwarded it to the successors, and question E3 withdrawn); a sale the original's chain recorded in the fork's history stays a purchase. *Client conformance:* the old split service, its grant ended, issues no payment request. *Cost, stated: a wallet reading only Finance does not see the withdrawal, and could still pay an on-chain address of a dead service; that money is owed back to it.* *"The work is the point of payment… why does it need the old one?" (Nobody, allegedly).*
   - **Defaults.** Whatever the fork act does not settle falls to these defaults.
- **C. Actively abandoned**, where everyone agrees the collective has ended but its works still earn: a plain collective, nothing new. It keeps its rules, changes rails, renews offers and pays every holder until winding it down makes sense. *"Plain collective enough, it's only how it is used by stakeholders that changes."*
- **D. Dissolution**, where the collective dies: its works are sold (stake transfers, rule 14) or released to the public domain (rule 17), then it closes: **a closing act** (type 20), signed under the constitutional change rule like the fork act, **ends a collective that holds nothing and owes nothing**; after its line, the collective's keys count for nothing in Law (F124, N9). **A collective cannot close while it owes anything** (F125, D5): one that cannot pay stays open, abandoned, its debts visible, until each is paid or its creditor releases it (rule 47b).

*"A fork closes the original" holds for shape B only. "Then maybe it is best no group keeps the original hash": a lone holdout keeping the identity, its history and its pointers while everyone else leaves was the cost that closing the original removes. Leaving costs the vote and the right to act, never a stake unless it is sold.*
47b. **The creditor's release** (F125; a Finance act since F126). **A creditor may end an obligation owed to it without full payment** by a creditor's release (Finance type 4), for instance against stakes or a partial payment. **Only the creditor signs it**: no rule, majority or court can discharge a debt for it, and a release signed by anyone else ends nothing. **A collective creditor signs it by its Finance lane alone** (F126, E2): forgiving a sum only gives up money, and the release is a Finance act, which that lane reaches (rule 36a). **A release given against future terms** (a share of future income, stakes) **is also a deal: a Law agreement of its own, signed by the collective's Law lane, which the release names among what it was given against** (Finance release field 1); the agreement binds on its own, and the release counts whatever it names, its field 1 a record only, never checked (reading 3, adjusted, F126). *Plain forgiveness needs the Finance lane; forgiveness traded for future terms needs both lanes, each for its own act.* Once it counts, the obligation is owed by nobody, the debtor or any successor owing it (rule 47a); it ends the whole debt, whatever was paid toward it (reading 1, confirmed): to forgive part, the debtor signs a new obligation for the rest and the creditor releases the old one; it counts wherever a verifier holds it, published or not, even before the debt it ends binds (reading 2, confirmed; E1); **a closing checks that every debt is paid or released** (rule 47a, D). *"That 10% is what changed everything, that's a complex deal that continues in the future. That is law" (Nobody, allegedly).* *Bankruptcy, walked through (Nobody, allegedly, 3 October 2026): a collective that cannot pay keeps its debts open and visible and cannot close; its assets can go to its creditors by sale or by stake transfers, debt turned into ownership; its members are not personally liable (N13); what was missing was discharge, and MOR has no court: the creditor's release is the creditor's own word.*
48. **Exit.** Agreements are always in core format, so activity on a fading cMIP moves by cloning the agreement onto another.

## Succession

48a. **The core gives the language, not the answers.** A party may attach a succession plan to an agreement, as part of its signed terms: stake successors, each with a share of the party's stakes (summing exactly), and seat successors, each with a voting weight, entering automatically or with the members' approval. Stake and seat successors may be the same identity or different ones. Changing a plan takes a clone, which needs every member's signature, the party's included (rule 46a, F121).
48b. **Triggers.** A plan executes when the party triggers it while alive, or when the authority named by the abandonment clause declares the party absent. *Death is a complex issue; the core leaves it to the parties, their named authorities and verifying modules.*
48c. **Execution.** Succession is a clone: the new terms carry the stakes and seats the plan gives, and any position attached to a seat is defined at that moment. In a collective, a change of seats changes the parties, which is constitutional. **A seat passes automatically** (plan key 3, value 0) **only if every party whose voice remains signed that version of the succession plan** (Q14): each of them signed a version of the agreement, in the chain up to the parent, whose field 16 carries that party's plan exactly as the clone executes it. The clone's mark then names the plan, `[3, party]`, with the seat successors entering as its signers, and it comes into force on the trigger (rule 48b) and their signatures, with no other power, provided it changes nothing but what the plan gives, as rule 44c says: the party out and its seat successors in, among the parties and in its place in every holding of the key grammar, every threshold unchanged; the party out of every area it held (Q21); the executed plan dropped (Flaw B14), and, where the party held the safety key alone, a plan for the successor in its place, naming their own successor, signed by the successor in that clone (Flaw B17, rule 36). *Consent given in advance, by everyone still counted (rule 13); a plan that some members cloned without the others is not that version, so it cannot slip a stranger into the constitution through a judicial clause. Since F121 a plan changes only with every member, so a plan some members clone without the others never comes into force at all.* Otherwise the seat successor is a nomination, approved under the constitutional change rule (rule 44c), counted among the voices that remain once the absent party's voice is removed (rule 53, F105), as in rule 37b; the successor signs the clone too (rule 45). A stake passes by succession as planned: it is property, not a seat. **A seat passed by succession carries membership, never the areas its holder held** (Q19): the seat carries the party's voice in the constitution and the change rules; the clone that completes the succession, automatic or by nomination, takes the party out of the holders of every area it held (Q21), and each such area is refitted as rule 37b says: its remaining holders carry on meanwhile (Q17); an area left with no holder is written with an empty list of holders and stands frozen, its grants ending (Q22, G2) and reinstated, where wanted, by cloning at the refit, until a constitutional clone gives it a holder, which may be the same clone where the successor is a nomination; an area left with fewer holders than its number keeps that number, met by all who remain (Q26). *An area is a job the collective gave a person; a plan is the party's own, and letting it hand out that job would reach into the constitution. The successor is free to be nominated for it.* In a deal, the clone needs every party whose voice remains (rule 45b). For a collective, the members then rotate and re-split the safety key to include the new key holders, using the grammar's recovery path where a share is gone for good: the succession clone changes the parties, so a rotation declares it (rule 37), and that rotation re-deals the collective's keys to include the successor (F96, F105, B14).
48d. **Fallback.** Where no plan exists, the identity-level succession declared in a rotation (Identity) applies, and otherwise the abandonment clause.

## Abandonment

49. **Clause.** Signed into the agreement, it names who decides absence and which outcomes may follow. Every collective carries one that covers each party with constitutional power (rule 36b, F105); a deal may carry one or not. *In a deal, a party's death leaves the agreement running as written, with succession plans to step in, and locks nobody out of an identity.* The authority MUST be an identity: the keeper's operator, a threshold of the other parties (counted as rule 44d says, so it never asks for more of them than remain; in a collective, among the voices that remain at the collective's line registering the declaration, or at the recovery rotation where the declaration takes effect there (rule 53, C7), the same place every other departure is judged, Q37), or a named third party. **In a deal, the authority is one identity: a party, a keeper's operator, or a collective, never a threshold of the other parties** (Flaw B19); parties who need to decide together form a collective, and name it. Where a threshold of the other parties needs more than one of them, one signs the declaration and the others add signature acts naming it, as for terms; it counts once the required number have signed (B15, "Abandonment declaration"), placed at the collective's line, or named by the recovery rotation where the declaration takes effect there (Flaw B18). An anchoring cMIP is named as the time reference the authority judges absence against, never as the authority. Absence means absence from duty: no act by the party on the agreement, for a period measured on the agreement's time reference.
50. **Liveness.** A party with nothing else to sign shows presence with a liveness act.
51. **Declaration.** A signed act by the authority the clause names, stating the agreement, the clause, the party and the outcome (type 13, B12). The core checks that it is signed by that authority (for a threshold of the other parties, by one of them, completed by the others' signature acts until the number is met, B15), that the outcome is one the clause allows, and that field 1 names the version the party declared absent signed last ("Abandonment declaration"); the clause applied is the one in force, of the agreement it names, for every party (rule 46a, F121; reading 7, corrected: a party whose voice was removed before a judicial change is judged by the clause in force, as every departed holder is).
52. **Contest.** A party who believes they were wrongly declared absent contests it with a contest act.
53. **Outcomes,** combinable: voice removed (F105): the party is no longer counted in any change rule or area of the agreement, or of any agreement descending from it (rule 44d), in a collective from the collective's line registering the declaration, or from the recovery rotation where the declared party is the one who would draw that line, the rotation that removes them (C7, B16), and in a deal from the declaration on ("Made before, made after"); a clone put in force before then, by a record or rotation of a collective before its line, or in a deal by signatures a keeper recorded before recording the declaration, is never undone, whatever version of the clause or of the agreement the declaration names (Q28); and in a collective the remaining members rotate its keys and clone it without the party (rule 37), the recovery path standing in for a share that is gone; stake redistributed among remaining holders in proportion; stake transferred to parties named or defined by role; obligations redirected or held; agreement closed. *A declaration is not a clone: it needs no change rule, only the authority the party signed. Removing a voice never takes the stake; that takes outcome 1 or 2, which the party also signed.*

## Non-performance

*The core never enforces; it makes default impossible to hide.*

54. **Atomic where possible.** Payment is acceptance; delivery released by payment cannot be defaulted on.
55. **Failure attribution.** Finance's receipts and payment claims name the hop and agreement that failed; a split's missing payout names the receiver and agreement. Law defines what follows: refund, redirection, contest.
56. **Negotiation record** (F118). Two sides negotiate by negotiation messages (type 18), locked and signed, each naming the previous message in the thread and acknowledging the latest one received from the other side. The record is provably complete up to the last acknowledged message: a message acknowledged by the other side fixes, by the previous messages each names, every message of the thread before it, and shows the other side received them all. Only negotiation messages make the record: a text act, a reaction or any other act naming the thread is no part of it, carries no acknowledgement (Envelope rule 4a, F110), and proves nothing about its completeness; a negotiation message whose previous is not a message of the same thread is no part of the record either. Two messages naming the same previous are a fork, visible and provable (Envelope).
57. **Disclosure by choice.** Either party may publish the record, by publishing the keys of its messages (Envelope: going public later).
57a. **Standing.** A contest counts only when signed by someone with standing: a party to the agreement, a holder of a stake the contested act affects, or a keeper, arbitrator or verifier the agreement names. A contest never changes any act's validity, status or selection; it is shown alongside what it names (F69).

*Reputation, arbitration and courts build on this record from outside the core.*

## Tasks

- **Split.** A cMIP accepts an amount received and a split plan, and produces payouts that sum exactly, with its declared remainder rule and the evidence for each role share.
- **Condition evaluation.** A cMIP accepts a condition and the acts it refers to, and produces true, false, pending or unknown.
- **Time reference.** A cMIP accepts a point on its reference (for example a block height) and an act, and produces whether the act is before, after, or undetermined.
- **Anchoring.** A cMIP accepts an act id and produces a proof that it existed at a point on a time reference. *Anchoring is Law's tool for Law's purposes (deadlines, claim priority); Identity does not depend on it (F63).*
- **Grant limits.** A cMIP defines how a grant's limits are expressed and checked.
- **Work claims.** A cMIP MAY define pre-publication commitments carried by a claim.

## Reasoning

- **Terms plus signatures.** *One signer per act (F17) makes every consent a separate, visible act. An agreement is complete exactly when its rule is met, a party refuses simply by not signing, and nobody owes anything they did not sign for.*
- **Identities, not keys.** *Keys change; promises should not. Naming identities means a rotation never breaks a deal.*
- **Stakes and seats can go to different people.** *A stake is property: passing it changes only who is paid. A seat is trust: the other members chose to share control with that person. A plan can give the stake to an heir and the seat to someone trusted for the job, paid through a position.*
- **Collectives are identities.** *A collective that receives money must sign receipts and rotate like anyone else. Giving it its own keys, held as its members choose, keeps one Identity MIP for everyone. The price is that a threshold signature looks like one signature; where individual consent matters, an area can require visible member signatures. And every grammar leaves a way through that survives the loss of any one key holder, so a dead member never freezes the key, not even a sole holder, whose successor holds an escrowed share: the rule is about the exit, not the number (F96). A collective's member signatures are judged under the rules its own chain declared when the key signed, so leaving is final: a former member's signature never again counts on what the collective signs after its line registering the departure (F100, F109), though it can still complete what the collective signed before it (C1).*
- **Keepers stamp sealed envelopes, and only real ones.** *Proving an act existed before a rotation needs its id and signer, not its content. Keepers that never read keep private deals private; judging content is left to arbitrators and verifiers the parties choose and deliver keys to. A record counts only alongside the act it names, so a bought record of nothing changes nothing.*
- **Protected clauses, one version for everyone** (F121). *A stake cannot be reduced without its holder's signature; but the clause that decides absence, the keeper who judges it, the concurrency rule and the split service could move the stake in two steps, and an arbitrator, a condition cMIP or an anchoring cMIP chosen after the fact could decide who wins. So in a collective they change only with every member's signature. Earlier drafts kept, for each party, the version that party signed (F71); that left two members with no judge they both signed. One version, signed by all, gives every dispute one judge; a holdout makes a standoff, which talking or a fork of the collective settles.*
- **A chain of judgment** (F121). *Unanimity could trap a collective whose judge disappears. Naming in advance who takes over, signed by everyone, replaces a failing judge with no new signature.*
- **A departed holder is paid, never consulted** (F121). *A stake is a share of income, not a say. Whoever keeps a stake after leaving accepts the collective's decisions, and is protected by what company law protects: the stake never shrinks without them, and no term treats it worse than a member's. Keeping their old judges, as F71 did, would have left a split service keeping two sets of terms for one payment.*
- **A fork closes the original, under the constitutional rule, each side founding its successor first** (F121, F124 N1, N4). *When a collective splits, no side keeps its identity, so a lone holdout cannot keep the name, history and pointers while everyone else leaves; every side starts anew, and what was made together still pays everyone who made it. A fork is a constitutional decision: where the founders allowed less than everyone, a member who signs no side keeps their money, as a departed holder of each successor, and loses only the seat. The members sign with their own identities, so no key holder can block the fork by refusing to rotate; the fork act names the successors, so none has to claim descent.*
- **Every debt goes somewhere** (F124, N13; F125; F126; F127). *A fork cites the collective's history and hands out every obligation in it, each successor signing for its own, or it does not take effect; what lies outside that history is void, the ending winning (D1's joint liability withdrawn); a collective's acts are done only once sealed to every member and on its chain, so hiding a debt gains nothing; a collective that owes cannot close, and a debt ends only by payment or its creditor's release, a Finance act.*
- **Two chains** (F127). *Hidden debt, late publishing, the missing device, the stray payment and W2 had one cause: MOR has no shared clock, acts are ordered only where one cites another, and a collective's history may branch, so an act could live on a branch nobody builds on. A collective's decisions and its actions are kept on two chains that cite each other: an act within its signer's powers counts once done, citing the head as its signer knew it, and becomes the new head; a decision cites the head it saw. The chain makes acts unmissable; it does not confirm them. Where two acts signed at the same moment never saw each other, only a decision ending powers against an action using them matters, and the ending wins. "A collective has two chains. One is the collective public actions, the other is the decisions by all members within the collective" (Nobody, allegedly).*
- **One thing is to act, one thing is to read** (F126). *Powers can be segmented among members; information runs free among them. What a collective plans can stay with some members for a while; once done, it is a fact touching everyone in it, sealed to every member and on its chain. Negotiation is talk, and may stay secret; the deal it leads to is done like any act (W6).*
- **An act is valid where it is held** (F128). *Naming relays made an act's validity depend on infrastructure: an operator vanishing or turning hostile could freeze a collective, even its decision to change relays; readers checking one relay could be shown less; an offline verifier could decide nothing. The two chains and the tie rule already answer "before"; a relay is transport.*
- **A grant is a key** (F128). *A grantee who signed with a personal key pointing to a grant acted outside the collective's chains, where no ending could place its acts: G1 and G2 tripped over that. A grant key makes the grantee's acts a strand of the collective's own actions chain, so a revocation, an emptied area, a fork or a closing places them like every other act, and the tie rule settles the rest. "Maybe what is missing is a mechanic in the grant" (Nobody, allegedly).*
- **The burden is where the action takes place** (F128, W4). *A buyer can only guess the current version; the seller knows it. On a request rail the seller's request fixes the claim; on a push rail each holder's own record settles it, the only clock MOR has, belonging to the one answering for it. Old versions stay readable forever; they must not be actionable.*
- **Buying is not pure money** (F126). *A work's claim and offer are Law, so a purchase names the claim it pays under, and a payment that names none is no purchase: the money goes back to whoever paid. A stray payment exists only because a rail moves money without reading Law.*
- **Payment follows the claim** (F124, N14). *The work is the point of payment; the fork changes only who owns it, so the money follows to the new owners' own pointers, and nothing keeps the old service alive.*
- **A judge and the constitution together need both rules** (F122). *Otherwise two members of three could change a judge by adding a one-word change to the constitution.*
- **The split service vouches for the owners' pointer** (F123). *A pointer names rails, never an identity, so the check is that the service's own signed pointer carries every address: a bypass needs the service to sign, publicly, for an address not its own.*
- **Splits are seen by everyone they pay** (F121, Q9). *The core cannot know who owns a service; it can make sure every holder sees every fee taken before their share, and who received it.*
- **A release ends a claim, never a history** (F121, F124 N7). *A work given to everyone still says who made it and who owned it; every direct owner signs, since a majority releasing would give away the minority's value. Where the owner is a collective, the work is its asset, and releasing it is a business decision taken by its own rules, which every holder of its income feels alike.*
- **Payouts follow stakes, exactly** (F124, N10). *Equal treatment is not a separate test: a split that pays every stake exactly, every fee alike, treats every holder alike; any deviation, either way, is visible.*
- **Deals are everyone's; majorities belong to collectives** (F107). *A deal is as simple as possible while remaining rich: nobody's terms change without their signature, so a party never has to read a rule to know whether they can be outvoted. The price is that one silent party blocks every change, as in everyday deal making. Where many parties or a long relationship need majorities, areas and tiers, they found a collective.*
- **The constitution never freezes** (F105). *The constitution can repair every other tier, so it alone must survive the loss of anyone whose signature it needs. Every such member is covered by an abandonment clause they signed, which can remove their voice, never their stake.*
- **Leaving needs no permission; removal never takes the stake** (Q6). *Anyone can walk out of a collective alone, keeping what they own. A constitution may allow removing a member from the board; it can never force them to give up their shares: "Kicked from the board does not mean being forced to give up shares."*
- **Lanes are computed from the collective's own list, and govern it** (F106). *What the core sees of a cMIP is the result of a task, and the task belongs to a layer. Reading the lane from which task the collective adopted the cMIP for, never from the cMIP's own label, lets every verifier agree; and letting the lane's holders adopt it lets a new rail join the Finance lane without a constitutional change. Extensions belong to no task, so they sit in Production; one that reaches into another layer needs that layer's holders too, and binds nothing they did not approve.*
- **A number never freezes a rule** (F105, flaw C). *When fewer voices remain than a rule asks for, all of them together meet it: the same as "every party" already does, and the founders' number wherever enough remain.*
- **Before and after are the collective's** (F109). *With no clock, "before" means before a named act in one named chain. A member may write from any number of devices, forget some, and nobody else can see them all; every patch that asked which of a member's lines an act named opened another gap (Flaws E to L). The collective's own sequence is one every member signed up to and every verifier can follow, so what was done in the collective's name is before or after an event by where it sits there. A departure takes effect at the line the collective draws; a member's signature sits at the act it signs, or where the collective acknowledged it; a grantee's act, signed with its grant key, sits on the collective's own chain (F128).* **What others relied on stands** (Flaw F). *A completed clone stays complete, a paid publication stays paid, a buyer the collective acknowledged or paid is protected, a year of receipts a member signed for the collective survives a declaration of absence. A line drawn without a branch costs the collective that drew it, never a member's forgotten phone.* **Only the collective's acts place** (Flaw G, A2). *An acknowledgement by a friend carries no place in the collective's sequence; a record names exactly the signatures it puts in force, and so does the rotation declaring a constitutional clone (Flaw M), so nothing completes either late and nobody who already left is counted. A reinstatement is the collective's own act, made after the new holders took the area (Flaw N).* **A declaration takes effect at the same line** (Q28, C7). *A voice removed by someone else ends at the collective's line: what a record or rotation already put in force stays, whichever version of the clause the declaration names, and what the absent member signed for the collective before it keeps its signature.*
- **Absence in a deal is judged by one identity** (B19). *A deal has no sequence of its own, so nothing could place several signatures against its line. Parties who must decide together are already a group with a say of its own: they form a collective, which has a sequence, and name it.*
- **A signature speaks for the moment it was made** (Q23). *A member who signed and then left was still acting when they signed; their signature stands, and the rule asks for exactly what it asked before they left, neither less nor more.*
- **Nobody is left holding an area they no longer have, and no hire outlives an emptied area** (Q21, Q22, G2). *A clone that removes a member takes them off their areas, and an area may wait, empty and frozen, for the collective to refit it, keeping the founders' number for the refit (Q26). An empty area is an emergency: its grants end at the line that emptied it, which places every act of their keys like any act of the collective; the new holders clone, one by one and visibly, the hires they want back, by the area's own rule (Q27), and nothing is left undetermined (F128, withdrawing C8).*
- **A judge is never chosen by one lane** (Q15). *The time reference, the condition cMIP and the anchoring cMIP can decide who wins a dispute; they stay judicial, as every member signed them, whoever runs Law's other tasks. And a judge never handles what it judges (Q20): a specification chosen as a judge serves no other task and is no extension, however it is named and in a deal as in a collective (Q24, Q25), which removes a conflict of interest and keeps every lane out of the choice of judges.*
- **Nothing passes around the lanes** (Q16, R4). *A specification the collective never listed would let whoever holds its key act around every lane, so in a collective with areas it counts for nothing until a lane adopts it. One that serves two layers is named for the second only with the power over both layers' tasks, and each of its acts counts only when the holders of each lane reaching it have signed it, as their lane's rule asks (rules 36a, 44b).*
- **Every change is on the record at once** (F106, flaw D, F109). *With no clock, the collective's own sequence is what tells which version governed an act, and who still had a say. A rotation is the safety key's ceremony, kept for changes to who decides; every other change, and every departure, is written with the everyday key, at once.*
- **Nobody is added, and nobody is held** (Q11, Q13). *A collective exists only when every founder signed it, and nobody joins it or takes an area without signing. A holder can step down at once; the area waits, frozen, for the collective to refit it, rather than running on without anyone answerable for it.*
- **A split service holds authority, not discretion.** *It is named by grant, bound to the plan, and every incoming receipt or payer's claim must be matched by a split. It can still steal, but never invisibly, and never by naming its own sybil as the referrer: role-share evidence is signed by someone on the other side.*
- **Shares follow stakes, holders follow transfers.** *A plan written in holder names would go stale at the first sale. Written in stakes, it pays whoever the agreement's transfer chain names, and two services compute the same answer.*
- **Leftovers to the first listed party.** *Signatures may arrive in any order; the order in the terms is fixed. So remainders follow the terms, and every client computes the same result.*
- **Keepers are chosen up front.** *Testimony is only trusted if nobody could pick a friendly witness after the fact.*
- **Many ways to split, one vocabulary.** *A paying client may pay each owner directly; a service may receive and forward at once; or it may pool and let owners withdraw, with every unwithdrawn share an open obligation rather than a number in a private database. Each is a competing split cMIP on the same vocabulary.*
- **Splits belong to owners.** *Finance delivers money to a pointer; the owners' agreement decides who gets what. Each payout is a simple payment with its own receipt, so a missing one is as visible as a broken route.*
- **Exact sums, declared remainders, bounded fees.** *Rounding is where money quietly disappears; an unbounded fee line is where it disappears loudly. The plan states the maximum, and the payer's claim fixes what arrived.*
- **Role shares pay for help that is only known at payment time.** *A reposter, a relay or a module earns when its part in a sale is evidenced by someone other than the party paying it, so rewards attach to settlements, never to attention, and never to the service's say-so.*
- **A stake pays through whoever sells.** *The core cannot tie a sale to a work's stakes without judging which claim is the claim, and a rule that tried would be evaded by re-encoding the file. What it can do is make a sale outside the claiming agreement visible, and give builders every piece needed to refuse or rate such sales. The core is as little intrusive as possible, and carries the bits and bolts for sound solutions on top.*
- **Clone, never modify.** *Nothing in MOR is updated. A clone that meets its rule is the only way terms change, and the parent's history stays intact.*
- **Nobody loses their say by default.** *Protecting the abandonment clause guarded one route to removing a party's voice; rewriting the clone rule was another, unguarded (F103). So the rules that decide who decides form their own tier, changed by their own rule, which is everyone unless the founders, all of them, agreed otherwise. What judges the members changes only with all of them (F121). Everything else is operational, and can be handed to the members who run it, as areas: they decide there without asking the rest, and can hire, but never give more than they hold.*
- **The tier follows from the bytes.** *Which power a clone needs is read from which fields it changes, never from what it says about itself, so two verifiers cannot disagree, and a clone cannot slip a constitutional change past as an operational one.*
- **Every version says who brought it into force** (F104). *A field every agreement carries should never be free text that looks binding and is not. A clone's field 4 is a claim anyone can check: this power, these people. A false claim sinks the clone.*
- **A grantor cannot unsay a deal it was paid on.** *Voiding a deal the collective acknowledged or collected on would leave the counterparty with a contest and nothing else. So a revocation never cuts off what the collective itself adopted, wherever its act sits (C6). Deals the collective never touched are the counterparty's risk, which is why a counterparty waits until a later act of the collective cites its deal before performing.*
- **Default made visible.** *The core cannot force anyone to pay or deliver. It can make sure nobody can hide that they did not.*
