## Purpose

Law is the language of verifiable agreements between identities. It defines:

- how an agreement is proposed, signed, recorded and changed (by cloning, never by editing);
- keepers, the notaries that record deals as they happen, so a rotation cannot erase what someone relied on;
- stakes in works and publications, how they are bound, transferred and released;
- splits: how money that reached the owners' payee pointer is divided, exactly, including module fees and shares assigned to roles;
- offers accepted by payment, conditions, and time;
- grants and collectives: acting for others, within limits;
- abandonment, and what happens when someone disappears from a deal;
- non-performance: the core never enforces, but it makes default impossible to hide.

Finance ends when money reaches a payee pointer. Law says who owns what, how it is shared, and who failed when something breaks.

## Dependencies

Identity, Envelope, Text and Finance. Law is optional per client, but all or nothing: a client implements it fully, or shows its acts as unknown (core, client conformance). A client that acts on agreements implements Law (F11). No rule of a lower layer depends on Law: where Law needs a lower layer to carry something, it is a field there (the publication's `for`, Envelope).

## Definitions

- **Terms.** An act proposing an agreement: its parties, text, cMIPs, keepers and rules.
- **Signature.** An act by one party naming terms (or a clone, a transfer, a release) as agreed. Under one signer per act, every agreement is terms plus one signature act per party.
- **Agreement.** Terms together with the signatures its rules require. Its id is the id of its terms act. An agreement commits identities, not keys: a later rotation changes the pen, never the promise. A party is bound only through its own signature act.
- **Agreement chain.** The acts on one agreement (signatures, clones, records, transfers, payouts, contests), each naming the chain and the act it follows in `objects` (Envelope).
- **Position.** An agreement that confers income without ownership.
- **Keeper.** A relay, operated by an identity, named by an agreement to record the acts around a deal as they arrive, and to sign what it recorded: a notary stamping sealed envelopes. A keeper records act ids and signers, never content. A record counts only alongside the act it names.
- **Arbitrator or verifier.** A third party named by an agreement to judge content (escrow and milestones, arbitration, regulated checks). Unlike a keeper, it receives the keys of the acts it must judge, by ordinary key delivery.
- **Stake.** A share in a work or a publication, defined by an agreement, identified by the agreement and its index in the stakes list. Shares in each always add up to 100%. The current holder of a stake is whoever the transfer chain on the agreement names.
- **Work claim.** An act binding a work's hash to its creators.
- **Split plan.** The part of an agreement that says how incoming payments are divided: fixed shares on stakes, role shares, fees, a maximum fee per payout, and what happens to unfilled roles.
- **Split service.** An identity that receives payments on behalf of a work's owners, divides each one according to their agreement, and pays every party out. It can be a company, a collective, a friend running software, or the owners themselves; a sole creator is simply their own split service.
- **Split.** A split service's signed act dividing one incoming payment into payouts.
- **Payout.** One receiver's part of a split, paid as a simple Finance payment with its own receipt.
- **Role share.** A share assigned to a role rather than a named party, filled when the payment happens: for example the reposter who led to the sale, the relay that served the file, the modules the payment ran through. Its evidence is an act signed by a party other than the split service and the payee.
- **Standing offer.** Published terms that anyone accepts by paying.
- **Time reference.** A source of time an agreement names, such as a block height. Only it decides "before", "after" and "past its terms" for that agreement.
- **Collective.** Several identities acting together as one identity. A collective is a full identity (Identity MIP), with its own genesis, homes, signing key and safety key; its founding agreement says how its members hold and use those keys.
- **Key grammar.** The part of a collective's founding agreement that says how its keys are held and activated, and the way to rotate that needs less than every member.
- **Deal.** An agreement that founds no collective (its terms carry no key grammar). A deal exists when every party has signed it, and changes only with every party's signature (F107).
- **Tier.** In a collective's founding agreement and its clones, each field of the terms belongs to one of three tiers ("Tiers"). **Constitutional:** who decides: the parties (membership), the change rules, the key grammar, the areas, the constitution's words. **Judicial:** who judges and by what: the protected clauses. **Operational:** everything else. Deals have no tiers (F103, scope; F107).
- **Change rules.** The rules a collective's clone must meet, each covering part of the terms: the **constitutional change rule** (terms field 18; every party when none was agreed); the **clone rule** (field 5), for the judicial tier and operational matters outside every area; and the **power** over each area. In a deal, the signing rule and the clone rule are every party.
- **Area.** A part of a collective's operational tier that the constitution gives to some of its members, the area's **holders**: acts of the collective, by whole layer or by act type, and operational fields of its terms. Its holders alone decide there, and may grant within it (Q5); its holders, and the rule they act by, change only by a constitutional change, and a holder may step down from it at once (rule 37b, Q13). An area with no holder whose voice remains stands frozen; a clone may list none at all (Q21). An area reaching a whole layer is also called that layer's **lane**: lanes and areas are one construct (Q10). A lane also decides which cMIPs the collective adopts for that layer's tasks, and the Production lane which extensions it adopts (F106).
- **Voice.** A party's say in an agreement: counting toward its change rules and, in a collective, the areas it holds. A party loses its voice only by its own resignation (rule 37a), by an abandonment declaration (rule 53), or by a constitutional clone that removes it (rule 37); it loses its voice in one area by stepping down from it (rule 37b), or by a constitutional clone that replaces it there (Q13); it never loses its stake that way (Q6).
- **Record.** The act by which a collective writes a complete clone of its agreement in its own sequence, so that the collective's later acts are judged under it (type 17, rule 37c, F106).
- **Power.** The right to bring a change into force: the constitutional change rule, the clone rule, or an area's holders meeting its threshold.
- **Mark.** A clone's terms field 4: which of the parent's change rules it claims to meet, and the parties whose signatures meet it (F104).
- **Layer.** One of MOR's layers (core, "Structure"): Identity; Envelope and Text; Finance; Law; Production. An act whose `spec` is a MIP belongs to that MIP's layer. An act whose `spec` is a cMIP belongs to the layer of each task the collective's agreement in force (rule 37c) names that cMIP for (terms field 2), the task's layer being that of the MIP defining it (Production, table of tasks); an act whose `spec` is an extension the agreement names (field 15) belongs to Production (F106). An act whose `spec` is a specification the agreement in force names neither for a task nor as an extension belongs to no layer; in a collective with areas it counts for nothing, unless an area reaches it by a kind naming its type (rule 36a, Q16). Production defines no tasks and no act types of its own: specifications and releases are Envelope publications.
- **Extension.** A cMIP an agreement names outside the listed tasks (field 15), which adds rules and never relaxes the core's (Production). Its specification declares the layers it acts on (Production, specification field 10, Q18); its own acts belong to Production.
- **Grant.** A delegation of authority, with limits, from an identity or collective to another identity. A grant never reaches beyond the power of whoever issued it.
- **Contest.** A signed objection, by a party with standing, to another act. It changes nothing about any act's validity, status or selection; it makes disagreement visible. *Draft 3 called this a dispute; the word is now reserved for the status Identity gives an act after a rotation (F69).*
- **Standing.** Who may contest an act: a party to its agreement, a holder of a stake it affects, or a keeper, arbitrator or verifier the agreement names.
- **Protected clause.** In a collective, a clause that can move a party's stake or voice, or decide who wins a dispute, without that party's later signature: the abandonment clause, the succession plan, the fork rule, the keepers, the split service, the time reference, the arbitrators or verifiers, and the condition and anchoring cMIPs (Q3). Together they are the judicial tier; the split service is operational in kind, and protected as a stated exception, since it can move money. A deal needs none: no clone of it completes without every party (F107).

## Act formats

All acts are Envelope MIP acts, private by default, visible to the agreement's parties and keepers unless they choose otherwise. Terms (type 0) and signatures (type 1) have exact formats, in the fields a collective needs (draft 6), with the tiers' fields (draft 7); grants have exact formats for the fields that tie them to an area (fields 5 and 6, draft 7), and resignations and records exact formats (draft 7); the fields still marked open, and the other acts, are sketched, and exact formats follow once the structure is settled. *A client that meets terms using a field whose format is still open refuses them rather than accepting them unchecked (Envelope rule 11, fail closed).*

| Type | Name | Signed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Terms | the proposer |
| 1 | Signature | each party (or grantee, or collective member) |
| 2 | Keeper record | a keeper's operator |
| 3 | Work claim | a sole creator; or one creator, completed by the other creators' signature acts |
| 4 | Stake transfer | the seller; completed by the buyer's signature; names the work's chain and the agreement's chain |
| 5 | Public domain release | a holder; completed by the required signatures |
| 6 | Standing offer | the owners under their agreement's rule, or a grantee |
| 7 | Delivery confirmation | the offer's side, for a publication whose key is public |
| 8 | Split | the split service, with its own key |
| 9 | Grant | the grantor; for a collective granting within an area, completed by its holders' signature acts |
| 10 | Revocation | the grantor, naming the act that seals the branch |
| 11 | Import or handover | the collective |
| 12 | Liveness | a party |
| 13 | Abandonment declaration | the authority the clause names, always an identity |
| 14 | Contest | anyone with standing |
| 15 | Module fee terms | a module's creator |
| 16 | Resignation | the member giving up their voice, or stepping down from one area |
| 17 | Record | the collective, with its signing key |

### Terms (type 0)

```cddl
terms-payload = {
  0 => [+ hash],            ; parties: identity or collective hashes
  1 => tstr,                ; the terms' words, as canonical text; in a collective, the constitution's and general words (each area's own: field 20)
  2 => { * uint => hash },  ; cMIPs, at most one per task (task number => cMIP hash); empty if it uses no task
  ? 15 => [+ hash],         ; extensions: cMIPs outside the listed tasks, which add rules and never relax the core's; in a collective, adopted in the Production lane (F106)
  ? 16 => [+ succession-plan] ; succession plans of parties, part of the signed terms
  ? 3 => keepers,           ; named keepers and the rule for what counts as recorded
  4 => rule / mark,         ; founding terms: signing rule, which signatures make the agreement exist: every party, in a deal (F107) and in a collective (Q11)
                            ;   a clone: its mark, which change rules of the parent brought it into force, and by whom (F104)
  5 => rule,                ; clone rule: in a collective, the judicial tier and operational matters outside every area; every party in a deal (F107)
  ? 18 => rule,             ; constitutional change rule; absent: every party (F103) (only with field 12)
  ? 19 => [+ area],         ; operational areas, powers of members (only with field 12)
  ? 20 => { + uint => tstr }, ; each area's own words, as canonical text, by area index (only with field 19)
  ? 6 => [ hash, any ],     ; time reference: the task cMIP and its parameters; a naming for task 10 (Q25) [Q31]
  ? 7 => stakes,            ; stakes defined, in works or publications (format open)
  ? 8 => split-plan,        ; how incoming payments are divided (format open)
  ? 9 => abandonment,       ; the abandonment clause
  ? 10 => fork-rule,        ; how forks are settled, where parties may act independently (format open)
  ? 11 => hash,             ; parent: the agreement this one clones (with its latest act in objects)
  ? 12 => key-grammar,      ; present if this agreement founds a collective
  ? 13 => [+ hash],         ; arbitrators or verifiers named, who receive keys to judge content
  ? 14 => hash,             ; the split service's grant, where incoming payments go to one
  ? 17 => refund-terms      ; for standing offers: how long a refund stays claimable, on the time reference (format open)
}

key-grammar = {
  0 => holding,             ; how the signing key is held
  1 => holding,             ; how the safety key is held, usually stricter
  ? 3 => recovery           ; a way to rotate that needs less than every member, required when holding 1 names every member
}                           ; key 2 (draft 6: listed act types) is retired and never reused: areas reach acts (field 19)
holding  = [ 0, holder: hash ]                        ; one holder
         / [ 1, threshold: uint, members: [+ hash] ]  ; any k of these members: jointly for the signing key; by shares for the safety key
         / [ 2, custodian: hash, grant: hash ]        ; a custodian under the collective's grant
recovery = [ 0, custodian: hash, grant: hash ]        ; a custodian holds a share under grant
         / [ 1, authority: hash ]                     ; an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority

succession-plan = {
  0 => hash,                               ; the party whose succession this is
  ? 1 => [+ [ successor: hash, share: uint ]],   ; stake successors, shares in millionths of the party's stakes
  ? 2 => [+ [ successor: hash, weight: uint ]],  ; seat successors, with their voting weight
  ? 3 => uint                              ; seat entry: 0 automatic where every voice signed this version of the plan, otherwise as 1 (Q14);
                                           ;   1 a nomination, approved under the power a change of parties needs (rule 44c)
}

abandonment = {
  0 => [ 0, hash ]          ; the authority: a named identity (a keeper's operator, or a third party)
     / [ 1, uint ],         ;   or a threshold of the other parties
  1 => [+ uint],            ; outcomes allowed (rule 53), ascending: 0 voice removed (F105), 1 stake redistributed,
                            ;   2 stake transferred to parties named or defined by role, 3 obligations redirected or held, 4 agreement closed
  ? 2 => uint               ; the period of absence, on the agreement's time reference (only with one)
}

keepers   = [ [+ hash], rule ]                      ; keeper operators, and any one / threshold / all
rule      = [ 0 ] / [ 1, uint ] / [ 2, [+ hash] ]   ; all parties / threshold / named parties
stakes    = [+ [ object: hash, [+ [ holder: hash, share: uint ]] ] ]   ; shares in millionths, summing to 1,000,000; leftovers to the first party listed; each stake is identified by its index here

area = {
  0 => tstr,                ; its name, as canonical text, shown before signing
  1 => [* hash],            ; holders: parties; empty only in a clone, the area then standing frozen (Q21)
  2 => uint,                ; how many of the holders decide together: at least 1, and at most the number of holders,
                            ;   except in a clone that keeps the number the parent's entry for that area has, which
                            ;   may then exceed the holders, counted as rule 44d says (Q26)
  ? 3 => [+ kind],          ; the acts of the collective it reaches
  ? 4 => [+ field-ref]      ; the operational fields of the terms it reaches
}                           ; an area reaches at least one act kind or one field; it is identified by its index in field 19 [Q32]
kind = [ 0, layer: uint ]                 ; every act of this layer ("Layer", F106): 0 Identity, 1 Envelope and Text, 2 Finance, 3 Law, 4 Production
     / [ 1, spec: hash, type: uint ]      ; every act of this type in this specification
field-ref = [ 0, field: uint ]            ; a whole operational field: 7 stakes, 8 split plan, 17 refund terms
          / [ 1, task: uint ]             ; one entry of field 2: the cMIP for this task, where the task is operational

mark  = [+ [ power, signers: [+ hash] ] ] ; each change rule claimed, and the parties whose signatures meet it (Q7)
                                          ;   ascending by the power's deterministic encoding, no power twice
power = [ 0 ]                             ; the parent's constitutional change rule (its field 18, else every party)
      / [ 1 ]                             ; the parent's clone rule (its field 5)
      / [ 2, area: uint ]                 ; the power over the parent's area of this index
      / [ 3, party: hash ]                ; the succession plan of this party, signed in advance by every voice (rule 48c, Q14);
                                          ;   its signers are the seat successors entering
```

*A rule's threshold is between 1 and the number of parties; named parties are parties. In a key grammar, members of a holding are parties. An area's holders are distinct parties; a clone that takes a party out of field 0 takes it out of every area's holders too, and founding terms never list an area without holders (Q21). A clone that takes a holder off an area need not lower its number: a number left above the holders that remain is met by all of them (rule 44d, Q26), and the refit sets it again; a clone that changes an area's number sets it within its holders. The signers of each power in a mark are distinct. A threshold is written as the founders chose it; how it counts once voices are removed is rule 44d.*

**A clone** is terms with a parent (field 11). Its inside names, in `objects`, the parent's chain and the act of that chain it follows: `[[parent, act]]`. Founding terms name no chain. A clone's field 4 is a mark, founding terms' field 4 a rule; terms whose field 4 has the other form are invalid.

**Founding terms exist only when every party has signed them,** a deal's and a collective's alike (F107, Q11): founding terms whose field 4 is not `[0]` are invalid. *A person is never added to a collective without their approval: a founder who has not signed leaves the collective unfounded, and a member added later joins only by signing the clone that adds them (rule 45).*

**A deal** (terms without field 12) has every party as its clone rule too: any deal whose field 5 is not `[0]` is invalid, and so are terms of a deal carrying field 18, 19 or 20 (F107). *The fields are kept rather than dropped so that every agreement has one format, and a deal says on its face what it needs.*

**A judge never handles what it judges** (Q20), in every agreement, a deal's as a collective's (Q24). A judge is a specification named in field 2 for a judicial task (9 condition evaluation, 10 time reference, 11 anchoring), or named in field 6 as the time reference, which is a naming for task 10 (Q25). A judge is named for no other task, judicial or not, and is not named as an extension (field 15): terms that do either are invalid, founding terms and clones alike, whatever a clone's mark says. *"It removes a slight conflict of interest." An extension fills no task, but it adds rules to acts; one that is also a judge would handle what it judges, so every way a specification can be named is covered (Q25). In a deal every party signs every cMIP, so no lane can slip a judge in; the reason is the judge's, not the lanes' (Q24). It also keeps R4 away from the judges: no specification serves a judicial task and a lane's task at once, so no lane's holders ever have a say over a judge, and no change to a judge reaches a lane's acts.*

**Areas** (field 19) appear only in terms that carry a key grammar (field 12) (F103, scope), and area words (field 20) only for an area that exists. No two areas reach the same act or the same field (Q5): two kinds overlap when some act could belong to both, as computed from the six MIP hashes and the terms' own fields 2 and 15 ("Layer"). So `[0, layer]` overlaps `[1, spec, type]` when `spec` is a MIP of that layer, a cMIP the terms name for a task of that layer, or, for Production, an extension they name; and a lane overlaps a field reference to a task of its own layer, since the lane already decides that task's cMIP (rule 44b). The one exception is a specification the terms name for tasks of two layers or more, never a judicial task (Q20): its acts belong to each of those layers, and are reached by the lane of each, which then decide them together (rule 36a, R4). A field reference names an operational field or task ("Tiers"). Genesis and rotations are never in an area's reach: the key grammar governs them; nor are records (type 17), judged by the clone they name. Terms breaking any of these are invalid.

*Which layer an act belongs to is computed from its own `spec` field, the six MIP hashes and the collective's own terms, never from a label the cMIP's author chose (F106): a collective that gives Finance to some members gives them the acts of every payment and conversion cMIP it adopted, by its own list. A new rail module falls in the right lane once the Finance lane adopts it, with no constitutional change. An act of a specification the terms name nowhere falls in no lane; in a collective with areas it counts for nothing, unless a `[1, spec, type]` kind of some area names it (Q16), since otherwise an unlisted specification would be a way around every lane. A specification named for tasks of two layers belongs to both; where the two lanes are held by different areas, each lane's holders must sign its acts, as for a clone across two areas (Q7), and naming it for the second layer needs both lanes' power (rule 44b), so neither lane's holders can bind the other's acts alone (R4).*

*Cost, stated (F106): a collective cannot give its releases to one member and its other publications to another by lane alone, since both are Envelope publications; a `[1, spec, type]` kind still names a single type.*

### Signature (type 1)

```cddl
signature-payload = {
  0 => hash                 ; the act signed: terms, a clone, an act of a collective an area reaches, or a grant within an area
}
```

The inside names, in `objects`, the act signed as both chain and predecessor: `[[signed, signed]]`. *A signature follows the act it signs.* Signatures naming one act are parallel consents to it, never a fork among themselves: rule 5's forks are between acts that change an agreement. A signature counts only while valid under Identity's rules (rule 5).

### Resignation (type 16)

```cddl
resignation-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the agreement of the collective in which the member gives up their voice
  ? 1 => uint,              ; stepping down: the area given up, by its index in that agreement; absent: the whole voice
  2 => [* kept-tip]         ; the latest act of every other sequence the signer keeps (Flaw E)
}

kept-tip = [ act: hash, position: uint, summary: hash ]   ; as in Identity: the act's id, its position, and the running summary including it
```

Signed by a party to that agreement, with its own key. Like a signature, its inside names the agreement as both chain and predecessor, `[[agreement, agreement]]`, and it is never a fork. Field 2 names the signer's other sequences, so that which of its acts came before can be told ("Made before, made after"). Without field 1 it gives up the signer's voice in that agreement and in every agreement descending from it by clones (rule 37a). With field 1 it gives up only the signer's place among that area's holders, in that agreement and its descendants, until a constitutional clone refits the area (rule 37b); the signer must hold that area there. Only a party to an agreement founding a collective may resign or step down; a deal is left only as its terms provide.

### Record (type 17)

```cddl
record-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the complete clone of the collective's agreement written on its record
  1 => [* kept-tip]         ; the latest act of every other sequence the collective keeps (Flaw E)
}
```

Signed by the collective, with its signing key, as an everyday act in its own sequence. Its inside names the clone as both chain and predecessor, `[[clone, clone]]`, as a signature does. Field 1 names the collective's other sequences ("Made before, made after"). A record counts as acknowledging every signature act naming its clone by a party the clone's mark names (Flaw F). It counts only if the clone it names is complete, judged with the signature acts it acknowledges, and its parent is the agreement in force for the record act itself (rule 37c): records follow the agreement chain one clone at a time, never skipping or forking it. A record of a clone that changes the constitutional tier counts for nothing: only a rotation declares such a clone (rule 37). No area reaches a record, as none reaches a rotation: it is judged by the clone it names alone, whose own mark already carries the consent it needed, so the holders of a Law lane cannot hold up another area's change. *The key grammar's holders produce the collective's signature on it, as on every act of the collective; they could already withhold that signature from any area's acts, so the record gives them no new power.*

### Made before, made after

*MOR has no clock, and an identity may keep several sequences at once, one per device (Envelope, "Sequences"). A resignation, a stepping down and a record each draw a line in their signer's acts, so each says where the line runs, as a rotation does (Identity, "Kept ancestry").* A resignation, a stepping down (type 16) and a record (type 17) each name, in their payload, the latest act of every sequence their signer keeps other than the one they are in (whose latest act is their `prev`), with its position and the running summary including it, as a rotation names its kept tips (Flaw E). An act of the same signer **counts as made before** such an act when it precedes it in that act's own sequence, or lies in the ancestry of a tip it names, proved by position and running summary as for a rotation's kept ancestry. **Every other act of that signer counts as made after it**, an act in a sequence it does not name included, whenever that act was really made. **Only what can be placed in time protects such an act** (Flaw G): **where a keeper named by an agreement its `objects` names recorded it before recording the resignation, stepping down or record (rules 11, 11a)** [Flaw J], it counts as made before, and is shown as disputed (Flaw F); and **the act that puts a clone in force, the record naming it (type 17) or the rotation declaring it (rule 37), counts as acknowledging every signature act naming that clone by a party its mark names** (Flaw F), so that each such signature counts as made before, and a clone once on the collective's record, or declared, never falls back to a draft because one of its signers later left a line out. **An acknowledgement by any other identity (Envelope) carries no place in time: it keeps the act visible, shown as disputed, and has no effect** (Flaw G): the act still counts as made after. *Shown as disputed means shown as Identity shows an acknowledged act outside a rotation's kept ancestry: every client shows that its signer's own line placed it after, and who vouched for it. Where a keeper placed it, the act counts; where only an acknowledgement vouches for it, it confers nothing, as rule 5 already says of an act disputed after a rotation. An acknowledgement carries no date, so it could be made after the line was drawn: a member who left a phone out of a resignation on purpose could otherwise keep signing from it, with a friend acknowledging each signature (Flaw G). A keeper's record can be placed: it comes before or after the keeper's record of the line. A sequence left out still costs the signer: an honest signature in a forgotten line that no keeper recorded first counts as made after, so it no longer counts, and an act of the collective in a line its record forgot is judged under the recorded version. What others had recorded by a keeper stands, as it does after a rotation (Identity; rule 11). Clients SHOULD track every sequence their owner keeps, on any device, and warn before signing one of these acts if one is missing, as before a rotation (Identity).* *Cost, stated (Flaw G): a buyer or counterparty who only acknowledged an act is not protected by that alone against a line its signer drew; deals that need protection name a keeper, as counterparties already ask for an import before performing (rule 43).*

**A declaration removing a voice draws its line the same way** (Q28). An abandonment declaration with outcome 0 (rule 53) removes the party's voice from then on, and names none of the party's sequences: every signature act of that party counts as made after it, except one a keeper named by an agreement its `objects` names recorded before recording the declaration [Flaw J], and one that the record or rotation putting a clone in force acknowledges (above). So a declaration never undoes a clone that a record or rotation put in force, whatever version of the clause, and whichever version of the agreement, it names; any other signature of the absent party no longer counts, since nothing places it before the declaration [Flaw L].

### Collectives in the identity chain

A collective's genesis declares its founding agreement in the declarations slot (Identity): `[LAW, 0, agreement]`, where kind 0 is **the agreement the collective lives under** and the value its id. A rotation declares, the same way, each complete clone that changes the constitutional tier, membership included (rule 37); only such clones need the collective's keys to rotate (Q8). Every other complete clone is written by a record act in the collective's own sequence (rule 37c, F106). For an act the collective's key signs, the agreement in force is the latest declaration of kind 0 at the chain act its key is bound to, or, where the act counts as made after a valid record made under that binding, the clone named by the latest such record (rule 37c); its areas are the ones that act is judged by.

### Split plan and split (type 8)

```cddl
split-plan = {
  0 => [+ share-rule],      ; fixed shares and role shares
  1 => hash,                ; split cMIP, which declares the remainder rule
  ? 2 => [+ fee],           ; module fees, each consented by whoever bears it
  ? 3 => unfilled,          ; what happens to a role share nobody fills
  ? 4 => [+ uint],          ; indexes of shares taken off the top, before the rest is divided
  ? 5 => uint,              ; who bears payout rail fees: 0 each receiver (default), 1 the split service
  ? 6 => amount / uint      ; maximum fee per payout the service may deduct: absolute, or in millionths of the payout
}
share-rule = [ 0, stake: uint, part: uint ]         ; a stake, by its index in the terms; paid to its current holder
           / [ 1, role: tstr, part: uint ]          ; a role, filled at payment time
           / [ 2, receiver: hash, part: uint ]      ; a named receiver that holds no stake (a service, a position)
fee        = [ module: hash, part: uint, bearer: hash ]
unfilled   = [ 0 ] / [ 1 ] / [ 2, party: hash ]     ; to the owners pro rata / held open / to a named party

split-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; the receipt or payer's claim that brought the money to the owners' payee pointer
  1 => [+ payout],          ; each payout, summing exactly to the amount received
  2 => hash                 ; the split cMIP used
}
payout = [ receiver: hash, amount, ? role: tstr, ? evidence: hash, ? fee: amount ]
; evidence: an act signed by someone other than the split service and the payee; fee: the rail fee deducted, within the plan's maximum
```

Each payout is then paid as a simple Finance payment, with its own receipt naming the split. A fee taken off the top, and an app share for the client that brought the sale (a role share), are both expressed in the plan.

### Grant (type 9)

```cddl
grant-payload = {
  0 => hash,                ; grantee
  1 => uint,                ; scope: 0 sign new deals, 1 manage named existing ones, 2 act for an identity (post, publish, spend)
  ? 2 => [+ hash],          ; the agreements concerned, for scope 1
  ? 3 => any,               ; limits: per-deal cap, total, spending, as the grant's cMIP defines
  ? 4 => hash,              ; the cMIP defining the limits
  ? 5 => uint,              ; for a collective: the area whose power issues it, by index in the agreement in force at the grant's binding
  ? 6 => [+ kind]           ; the acts it reaches; required with field 5, and within that area's reach
}
```

A publication made under a scope-2 grant carries the grantor in its `for` field (Envelope), so payment reaches the grantor whatever client pays. A grant with field 5 is signed by the collective and completed by signature acts of the area's holders (rule 38a). Each kind in field 6 must lie within the area's reach: every act it could reach, computed as for overlapping kinds from the agreement in force ("Areas"), is an act the area reaches; otherwise the grant is invalid.

## Agreements

1. **Proposal and signatures.** An agreement begins as terms. Each party signs with a signature act naming the terms. Founding terms exist once every party has signed them, in a deal (F107) and in a collective's founding agreement alike (Q11): their signing rule (field 4) is every party. A clone comes into force by its mark (rule 45). A party is bound by an agreement only through its own signature act: a rule decides when an agreement or a clone comes into force among those who signed, never who owes. An obligation in the terms of a party who has not signed is shown as unsigned, never as open (F74).
2. **cMIPs.** Every agreement carries the hashes of the cMIPs it uses, at most one per task. An agreement that does not is invalid. It may also name extensions (Production); a client that does not implement all of them cannot sign it.
3. **Commitments of identities.** An agreement names identity hashes, not keys. After a rotation, the party's new key signs for the same agreement.
4. **Positions** are agreements that confer income without ownership.
4a. **What you sign is what you saw.** *Client conformance.* Before any terms, clone or grant is signed, the client MUST be able to show its text as plain text, SHOULD do so by default, and MUST show every bidirectional control visibly in that view (Text MIP, rule 5a).
5. **Agreement chains.** Every act on an agreement names the chain and the act it follows. Two acts naming the same predecessor are a fork, except signatures naming the act they sign, which are parallel consents; where the agreement lets parties act independently, its fork rule settles it; otherwise the status quo stands. An act on an agreement chain that is void or disputed under Identity's rotation rules confers nothing: no stake, no obligation, no consent (F57).

## Keepers

6. **Named by the agreement.** Keepers are chosen by the parties when they sign, so neither side can later substitute a friendly one; in a collective, for a party who has not signed a clone, the keepers stay those that party signed (rule 46a); a deal's keepers change only with every party (rule 45b).
7. **Records.** A keeper records the deal and the acts around it as they arrive, in its operator's sequence, and signs each record.
8. **Several keepers.** An agreement may name several keepers and a rule for what counts as recorded: any one, a threshold, or all. Only the named keepers' records count as testimony.
9. **Copies.** Records are signed and hash-linked, so they can be copied anywhere and verified wherever they sit. If a keeper disappears, its records survive in every copy.
10. **Keepers check homes.** Before recording an act, a keeper checks the signer's identity chain at its homes, and records any rotation it sees.
11. **What a rotation cannot erase.** A record counts against a rotation only if it sits before the keeper's own record of that rotation, in the keeper's sequence. Such an act, even outside the kept ancestry or disowned, becomes a visible dispute rather than void (Identity).
11a. **A record counts only alongside the act it names.** A keeper record counts, for that purpose, only if the verifier holds the act it names, that act is valid as an act, and the keeper is one named by an agreement the act's `objects` names. A record of an act nobody can produce changes no verdict (F58). *A keeper can delay recording a rotation while recording a thief's acts first. That is trust in keepers, accepted and stated: choose keepers as carefully as homes.*
12. **Keepers are always sealed.** *A keeper only has to prove that an act existed before a rotation. For that it needs the act id and the signer, both on the act's outside, and a check of the signer's home, never the content.* A client MUST NOT deliver an act's content key to a party because it is a named keeper; a keeper that must also judge content is named as an arbitrator or verifier as well. *This is a rule for clients: key deliveries are private, so no one else could check it.*
12a. **Judging content is someone else's job.** Where a third party must judge content (escrow and milestones, arbitration, regulated verification), the agreement names an arbitrator or verifier, and the parties deliver it the keys by ordinary key delivery.

## Stakes and works

13. **Stake rule.** Any act that changes the size of a stake requires its holder's signature. A signature on an agreement is consent to everything it defines, including pre-authorised changes such as abandonment outcomes. Clients SHOULD make such terms prominent.
14. **Transfer.** By default, a stake is sold or given by a transfer signed by the seller and completed by the buyer's signature. The transfer names both the work's chain and the agreement's chain in `objects` (Envelope). Other holders are notified, not asked. The new holder inherits the full position, and the transfer chain on the agreement is what names the current holder of each stake; a split service MUST follow it (F73).
15. **Binding a work.** A work is bound to its creators only by an explicit work claim: a sole creator alone; several creators by one creator's claim completed by signature acts from all the others, and bound only when all are present. A publication is a neutral carrier: carrying or quoting a work, or pointing to a web resource, claims nothing. The core records claims and their order, never legitimacy. Order is not evidence of authorship; a claim cMIP MAY carry a pre-publication commitment. Where claims from different identities conflict, priority can only be decided where they meet: by anchoring, or by a keeper that recorded both. Otherwise they remain openly contested.
15a. **Stakes are written in millionths,** summing to 1,000,000. Any leftover from dividing goes to the first party listed in the terms.
15b. **A sale is tied to the seller's agreement, not to the work's stakes.** The core cannot tie a sale of a work to that work's stakes: it ties a sale to the agreement of whoever sells, and makes a sale outside the claiming agreement visible to those it concerns. A publication or standing offer for a work with an existing claim, not signed under that claim's agreement, is a valid act that visibly contradicts a signed agreement: the claim's holders contest it, a keeper has the claim on record, and the seller's own split shows what they took. *The bits and bolts are all in the core: work claims with order, contests with standing, keeper records, the published split, the publication's `for` field, and the payer's claim. A client that refuses to sell a claimed work under a plan that does not pay the claim's holders, or a reputation service that reads contests, can be built on them, and adoption decides (F72).* *Client conformance.* A client SHOULD show such a publication or offer as outside the claiming agreement.
16. **Publication stakes.** A publisher may hold a stake in its publication, never in the work.
17. **Public domain.** The owners may release a work, now or at a future point on a named time reference. It needs every holder's signature unless pre-authorised, and it is permanent. Its legal effect lies outside the core.

## Splits

How a split service runs is defined by the split cMIP its owners' agreement names. Law keeps only the vocabulary and the guarantees below.

### Authority

18. **Named by grant.** The owners' agreement names the split service through a grant, with any limits the owners choose (for example how long it may hold money, or which rails it may use). The payee pointer of the identity publications are made for (the owners' collective, or the sole owner) points to it, so money actually reaches it (Finance, F46, F68). The grant is revocable at any time under the agreement's rule.
19. **What it receives:** the incoming payment (an ordinary Finance payment, for which it signs the receipt), any payer's claim for it, the split plan from the agreement, and the evidence for role shares: the referral the payer signed in its claim, a relay's signed delivery record, a module's signed use record.

### Duties

20. **Publish a split.** For every incoming receipt, and for every payer's claim that shows money arrived without one, the service publishes a split, signed with its own key, naming that receipt or claim and listing every payout.
21. **Conservation.** A split's payouts MUST sum exactly to the amount received, in the unit's smallest part. The split cMIP declares its remainder rule; where it declares none, leftovers go to the first party listed in the terms. A split that does not sum exactly is invalid.
22. **Role shares with evidence.** Each role payout names who filled the role and the proof, which MUST be an act signed by someone other than the split service and the payee: the payer's client for a referral (Finance, the claim's referral field), the relay for delivery, the module's own signed use record. A referral the service names on its own earns nothing. Anyone can check the evidence afterwards. A role share nobody fills follows the option the plan chose: to the owners pro rata, held open as an obligation, or to a named party (F75).
23. **Pay out.** Each payout is paid as a simple Finance payment to the current holder of the stake, or the named receiver, with its own receipt naming the split.
24. **Keep what it cannot deliver** as open obligations, visible until paid. Amounts below a rail's minimum are held until they add up to enough (F50).
24a. **Payout fees.** The split plan says who bears each payout's rail fee: each receiver by default, or the split service as part of its cut; and it states a maximum fee per payout. A payout is discharged when its receipt shows the amount minus a fee no greater than that maximum; a shortfall beyond it is an open obligation of the service (F64).
25. **Batching.** The grant may allow the service to accumulate and pay out periodically or above a threshold, measured on the agreement's time reference. Every incoming receipt must still be matched by a published split, so accumulation is visible. Payouts paid with one rail payment name the same batch in their receipts (Finance).

### Limits

26. A split service MUST NOT pay anyone outside the plan's stakes as currently held, roles as evidenced, and named receivers, and MUST NOT change the plan: only the owners can, by cloning their agreement.
27. **Fees.** A fee is one total with an agreed split among the modules that earn it. The service's own fee appears in the plan like any other cut. No split can include a fee without the signature of whoever bears its cost; on a creator-side fee, that is the creator. Every split names the modules it ran under, so an omitted declared fee is visible.
28. **Attention.** A split may follow plays, views or any metric a module reports. The metric, its module and the resulting payouts are named, so it is legible like any other; the metric's evidence is signed by the module, not by the service.

### Failure and switching

29. Every incoming receipt, and every payer's claim showing money arrived, without a matching split, and every payout without a receipt, is an open obligation of the service, naming exactly one receiver and one agreement.
30. The owners switch services by revoking the grant, naming a new one, and having the identity publications are made for publish a new version of its payee pointer. The old service still owes payouts on everything it received before.
31. **The trust that remains.** *Between receiving and paying out, the service holds other people's money. MOR cannot prevent theft, but makes it provable from the receipts and the payers' claims: hiding income requires the payer's silence or collusion. Owners who want less exposure choose services that pay out immediately, or payer-side splitting cMIPs, where capable clients pay each owner's pointer directly and no service's honesty is needed.*

## Offers, conditions and time

32. **Standing offers, accepted by performance.** Owners publish terms; anyone accepts by paying them. The deal is complete when the offer's side delivers the content key or, for a publication whose key is public, signs a delivery confirmation: that proves payment was verified and honoured. A payment that receives neither, such as one arriving after a withdrawal, creates an obligation to refund it. A refund owed on a payment whose payer is not named is owed to whoever presents the rail proof of that payment, by a payment claim naming where to be paid (Finance); the obligation stays open and visible until claimed and discharged, and a seller who refuses a valid claim is visibly in the wrong. The offer's terms MAY say how long a refund stays claimable, on the time reference; the core sets no lapse (F80). Paid access to a live stream, which has no work hash until it ends, is sold by a standing offer, not a publication.
33. **Time.** An agreement that needs deadlines names its time reference. "Past its terms" is judged against that reference only. Showing that something did *not* happen before a deadline needs evidence: an anchoring cMIP or a named keeper. Without either, the answer is undetermined.
34. **Conditions.** An agreement may make an obligation or a payout depend on a condition, evaluated by the condition cMIP it names.

## Grants and collectives

35. **A collective is a full identity,** with its own genesis, homes, signing key and safety key. It uses the Identity MIP unchanged: it signs its own receipts, sets its own payee pointers, and rotates. Its genesis declares its founding agreement (a Law declaration in the declarations slot), and the agreement carries its key grammar.
36. **Key grammar.** The founding agreement says how the collective's keys are held and activated, according to the members' taste: one holder, a threshold (any k of n members jointly produce one ordinary signature, for example with FROST threshold schnorr), or a custodian holding the key under the collective's grant. The safety key is held by one holder, a custodian, or as shares: split so that any k of n members can rebuild it. At rotation, k members bring their shares to one offline device, which rebuilds the key, signs once and forgets it. *For that moment one device holds the whole key; that is the price, and it holds the next key too, before dealing its shares. Nothing can prove a device forgot a key.* The Module that does it MUST deal the shares of the next key verifiably, and have them checked against the committed key before that key is relied on, so that the members' shares rebuild it: otherwise the device's holder could end up the only one able to rotate the collective (F97). *Verifiable dealing stops sole control, not a copy; a copy kept is the price above, stated.* **A key grammar MUST leave a way to rotate that survives the loss of any one key holder** (F96): a threshold below the member count, or a named recovery path (a custodian holding a share under grant, or an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority, which the abandonment clause names). A key holder is anyone holding the whole key or a share of it: a member, a custodian, the holder of an escrowed share. **Where one person holds the safety key, the agreement MUST name a successor (a seat successor in that holder's succession plan) and an escrowed share released to them under its succession or abandonment clause;** a single custodian likewise needs a recovery path held by another. A grammar without such a way is invalid. Two partners may choose "both", provided they name where a further share sits and who releases it; whether the path works is the members' risk, stated (F77). *For a single holder, one construction: the key is split so that any two of four shares rebuild it; the holder keeps two and signs alone, the successor and a keeper hold one each, useless alone. When the named authority declares the holder absent, the keeper releases its share and the successor rotates the collective to a new key; the succession clone passes the seat.* Where members want individual consent to show, the constitution gives acts of the collective to areas (rule 36a).
36a. **Areas: rules by layer, powers of members** (F103). A collective's founding agreement may divide its acts and its operational fields into areas, each held by some of its members: by whole layer (Identity, Envelope and Text, Finance, Law, Production) or by act type within a specification. **An act of the collective that an area reaches counts only with valid signature acts naming it, by holders of that area in the agreement in force, each of whom signed it or a version it descends from by complete clones, and whose voice there remained when they signed it (rules 37a, 37b), meeting the area's threshold as rule 44d counts it; the agreement in force is the one the collective's chain declares at the act's binding, or the one recorded after it (rule 37c)** (F100, F106). An act of an area that stands frozen counts for nothing (rule 37b). An act no area reaches counts on the collective's own signature, as any identity's act does, unless its specification is unadopted (below). *So when members change, the rotation that declares the clone also fences off the old rules: whatever the old key signs afterwards is void under Identity, whoever signs it. Members' own identities do not rotate when they leave a collective; without this, former members could sign under the old rules at any later time, and no one could tell.* *An area says whose consent counts; the key grammar says who can physically produce the collective's signature. A holder who should act alone without holding the key acts under a grant issued within the area (rule 38a), with their own key.* *Layers and areas are one construct (Q10): a rule for a whole layer, a lane, is an area that reaches that layer, held by the members who decide on it; an area may also reach single act types, and operational fields, with one set of rules for holding it, granting within it and changing it.* **In a collective whose agreement in force has any area, an act whose `spec` is a specification that agreement names neither for a task (field 2) nor as an extension (field 15) counts for nothing** (Q16), unless an area reaches it by a kind naming its type, `[1, spec, type]`, and then counts as that area decides. To count, the specification must first be adopted, under the powers its adoption needs (rule 44c): for an operational task, that task's lane; as an extension, the Production lane with the lane of each layer it declares. An act of a MIP is never such an act. *Otherwise an unlisted specification would be a way around every lane: whoever holds the collective's key could pay through a rail nobody adopted, around the treasurer. With adoption in the lane's own hands, waiting costs little.* An area reaching a layer reaches every act of that layer ("Layer"): the MIP's own acts, the acts of the cMIPs the agreement in force names for that layer's tasks, and, for Production, the acts of the extensions it names (F106). An act that belongs to two layers or more, of a specification named for tasks of each, is reached by the lane of each, and counts only when each lane's holders meet its threshold, as a clone across two areas does (Q7, R4). **A lane decides what the collective adopts for its layer** (F106): which cMIP fills each of that layer's tasks, so a treasurer holding Finance adopts a new payment cMIP alone; and, for the Production lane, which extensions the collective adopts. **The tasks of the judicial tier (9 condition evaluation, 10 time reference, 11 anchoring) stay judicial, outside the Law lane** (Q3, Q15): the Law lane's holders cannot change them; they change only under the clone rule, and for each member only with that member's signature (rule 46a). The Law lane adopts the cMIPs for Law's other tasks (8 split, 12 grant limits, 13 work claims). **A specification adopted for a judicial task is adopted for no other task, and is no extension** (Q20, Q25, "Areas"), so R4 never joins a judge to a lane. *Each judicial task can decide who wins a dispute, and one lane holder picking a friendlier judge after the fact is worse than a majority doing it.* **An extension acting on another layer needs that layer's lane too,** in the same clone, all at once (Q7): its specification declares the layers it acts on (Production, specification field 10), so a client knows whose approval to ask (rule 44b); **dropping an extension needs the same approvals as adopting it**: the Production lane and the lane of each layer it declares (Q18). *"Modules and cMIPs should be able to be governed on the layer they sit."* The declaration is not trusted for enforcement: in a collective, a verifier applies an extension's rules only to acts of Production and of the layers whose power its adoption met, so an extension that under-declares binds nothing beyond the lanes whose holders approved it. Nothing an extension does relaxes the core (Production 8c): the MIPs' own acts stay in their own layers, whatever an extension adds around them.
36b. **Every constitutional voice is covered** (F105). Every party whose signature a collective's constitutional change rule is counted among (every party under the default or a threshold, the named parties under a rule naming parties) MUST be covered by the agreement's abandonment clause: the clause allows outcome 0, removing a voice, and its authority can declare that party absent, so a named authority does not cover itself. Coverage is judged for each party under the version of the clause that party signed (rule 46a). A founding agreement or clone of a collective that leaves such a party uncovered is invalid, as a key grammar without a way to rotate is. *The constitution is the tier that can repair every other (reassign an area, revoke a grant, refit the key grammar), so it alone must never freeze; it freezes only through the loss of someone whose signature it needs. A member without constitutional power needs no clause: their loss is repaired from above. This is F96's promise, a dead member never freezes the collective, carried from the keys to the rules.*
37. **Changing members** means a rotation to new keys plus a clone of the founding agreement; the rotation declares the complete clone as the agreement the collective lives under, and counts as acknowledging the signature acts that completed it ("Made before, made after", Flaw F). Membership is constitutional, so the clone needs the constitutional change rule (rule 44c): every party whose voice remains, by default; a member who resigned (rule 37a) or was declared absent with outcome 0 (rule 53) is no longer counted. A clone that takes a party out of the parties takes it out of every area's holders too, and an area it leaves with no holder is written with an empty list of holders and stands frozen until a constitutional clone gives it a holder (rule 37b, Q21). A constitutional clone that removes a party who did not sign it, where the constitutional change rule allows that, takes that party's voice only: the party keeps every stake it holds (rule 46), and its protected clauses stay as it signed them (rule 46a) (Q6). Any other change to the constitutional tier (the change rules, the key grammar, the areas) is likewise declared by a rotation before it applies to the collective's acts. Each agreement the chain declares after the founding one MUST descend from the agreement in force at the rotation (the one declared before it, or recorded since, rule 37c) through complete clones, of which only the last changes the constitutional tier; otherwise the collective's acts that need member signatures count for nothing. *Clones that change only the judicial or operational tier need no rotation (Q8): they change neither who holds the collective's keys nor who decides, so there is no old key to fence off; they are written on the collective's record by a record act instead (rule 37c). The collective's key resembles a safety key more than a signing key in that regard.* A departing member hands over nothing: the remaining members rotate to keys the departing member never held, and re-split the safety key among the members who stay or join.
37a. **A member can always leave alone** (Q6). A party to a collective's agreement gives up its voice by a resignation act (type 16), without anyone else's signature. From then on its signature acts count toward no change rule and no area of that agreement or of any agreement descending from it: none that counts as made after the resignation ("Made before, made after", Flaw E, Flaw F) counts; one made before it stays valid, and still counts toward what it signed, its signer counting as a voice for that clone or act alone (rule 44d, Q23). The party keeps its stake, and stays bound by what it signed. The remaining members then rotate the collective's keys (rule 37) and clone it without the departed party, which takes it out of every area it held too (Q21). Meanwhile each rule, and each area the party held, is counted among the voices that remain (rule 44d, flaw C); an area left with no holder stands frozen, as in rule 37b, its grants ending (Q22) and sealed or reinstated at the refit (Flaw H), until a constitutional clone gives it a holder. A resignation counts like a loss (F96, F105): where the key grammar's recovery path is an escrowed share released by the abandonment authority, the authority may release it on a resignation as on a declaration of absence. *Leaving needs no permission, and lets nobody remove anybody else. Absence remains the abandonment clause's business, under the clause the member signed.*
37b. **Leaving an area** (Q13). Giving up an area changes the constitution at that level: it is a constitutional clone (rule 44c), declared by a rotation (rule 37). It comes about in one of three ways: the holder leaving nominates a replacement, and the constitutional change rule approves it or not; the constitutional change rule proposes a replacement, and the holder leaving approves it; or the constitutional change rule redraws that area's rules entirely. The replacement signs the clone too (rule 45, Q11). Until one of them completes, the holder keeps the area. *Mechanically the first two are one clone, signed by the leaving holder and meeting the constitutional change rule, started from either side; the third needs no signature of the holder, since an area's holders and rules change by a constitutional change (Q5).*
**Stepping down at once.** A holder may instead give up an area at once, alone, by a resignation naming that area (type 16, field 1): *"I'm done, won't do anything anymore."* From then on its signature acts count toward nothing in that area, in that agreement or any descending from it: none that counts as made after the stepping down ("Made before, made after", Flaw E, Flaw F) counts there; one made before it stays valid, and counts as rule 44d says (Q23). The holder keeps the rest of its voice, and its stake. The collective then makes the constitutional change for that area by one of the three ways. **Meanwhile, where other holders of the area remain, they carry on** (Q17), counted as rule 44d says: the area's threshold stands where enough of them remain, and all of them together meet it where fewer remain than it asks for; grants issued within the area keep backing acts. **An area with no holder remaining is frozen** until that change: its acts count for nothing (Q17), **and every grant issued within it ends for good** (Q22): it backs no act made after the freeze. The act that freezes the area is the last holder's resignation or stepping down, the declaration removing that holder's voice (rule 53), or the rotation declaring a clone that leaves the area with no holder. A freeze names no act of a grant's branch, so **the grantee's acts on the branch after the last one known to precede the freeze are undetermined** (Flaw H) until the area is refitted and its new holders seal or reinstate the grant. An act on the branch is known to precede the freeze where a keeper named by an agreement its `objects` names recorded it before recording the act that froze the area, as for a resignation ("Made before, made after", Flaw G), and so is every act before it on the branch [Q30]; those acts stand as made under a live grant. The change ends the freeze once a constitutional clone is declared that changes that area's entry of field 19 so that it has a holder whose voice remains (Q21). **At the refit, the new holders either seal each ended grant or reinstate it** (Flaw H). **Sealing** is the existing seal: a revocation (type 10) naming the last act of the branch that counts, never before a deal the collective acknowledged or was paid on (rule 40); the branch is then a sealed branch, its acts judged by rules 41 to 44 (imported, handed over, protected by rule 40, or not binding), and every act after the seal counts for nothing. A revocation of a grant within an area is judged, like the grant, by the area it names alone, meeting its threshold as rule 44d counts it (rule 38a) [Q29]. **Reinstating**: the holders of the refitted area may reinstate any grant that ended, as their own, by signature acts naming that grant, meeting the area's own rule, its threshold as for any grant within it (rule 38a, Q27): one signature act by each for each grant reinstated, never one for several, and nothing is reinstated automatically (Q22). A reinstated grant backs acts again from then on, as a grant within the refitted area, and only within that area's reach (rule 44); acts under it while it stood ended still count for nothing [Flaw I]. *Stepping down thus works as resigning does (rule 37a): neither can be played against the other, and one holder cannot stop the co-holders' work at will, nor keep an area frozen by withholding the signature its refit needs. A grant is the area's, not one holder's: a grant made within the area before the stepping down, even one to the holder who stepped down, keeps backing acts while the area runs, until its remaining holders revoke it (rule 38a).* *A frozen area has nobody left to answer for its grants, so they end; the new holder chooses which hires to take on, each one visibly, and draws the line on the others by the seal the collective already uses, so a deal the collective was paid on is never cut off (Flaw H). Until then, a counterparty who dealt with the grantee and wants to be sure its deal is placed before the freeze names a keeper, as for every line drawn by another identity (Flaw G).* *"If her departure freezes a layer, the collective will organize quickly to fix it." Payments to the collective's payee pointers still arrive on their rails; only the collective's own acts in that area wait.*
37c. **Every complete clone is written on the collective's record at once** (F106, flaw D). A clone that changes the constitutional tier is declared by a rotation (rule 37). Every other complete clone of a collective's agreement is written, at once, by a record act (type 17) that the collective signs in its own sequence. For an act the collective's key signs, the agreement in force is the one the chain declares at the act's binding, or, where the act counts as made after a valid record made under the same binding ("Made before, made after", Flaw E), the clone named by the latest such record, the one furthest along the agreement chain; an act of the collective in a sequence the record does not name counts as made before it only where a named keeper recorded it first, an acknowledgement by another identity keeping it visible without effect (Flaw F, Flaw G). A record counts only if its clone is complete and its parent is the agreement in force for the record itself, so records follow the agreement chain one clone at a time; a rotation's declaration supersedes every record before it. *MOR has no clock, so an operational clone that nothing writes on the collective's record could not tell which of the collective's acts it governs: a payment cMIP the treasurer adopted would fall in no lane until the next rotation, and its receipts would count on the collective's own signature, without the treasurer (flaw D). The record fixes the moment in the collective's own sequence, with the everyday key; only clones that change who decides need the safety key's ceremony (Q8).* *Until the record exists, the collective's acts are judged under the agreement before the clone: a delay, visible, which the key grammar's holders could already cause by withholding the collective's signature from any act.*
38. **Grants** delegate authority with limits: signing new deals, managing specific existing ones, or acting for an identity or collective (posting, publishing, spending up to a limit). An agent acting for a collective works under a grant, without holding the collective's keys; members import what they approve. A grant is its own act; acts under it name it in `refs`, and a publication under it names the grantor in `for` (Envelope). Grants are revocable at any time. A grant is authority to act in the grantor's name without a stake, the employer-and-employee kind; it confers no voice in the agreement.
38a. **A grant never reaches beyond the power of whoever issued it** (F103). The holders of an area may grant within it: the collective signs a grant naming the area (field 5) and the acts it reaches (field 6), within the area's reach, and it counts only with valid signature acts naming it by the area's holders, meeting its threshold as rule 44d counts it, as for any act the area reaches (rule 36a); it is judged by the area it names alone, whichever area reaches grants as acts. A collective's grant naming no area is judged as any other act of the collective, and reaches only acts that no area of the agreement in force reaches. A grant whose field 6 reaches beyond its area is invalid. *So a member holding an area can act alone there, under a grant to their own identity, and can hire others for it; and neither they nor the one who holds the collective's everyday key can give anyone more than that area.*
39. **Branches.** A collective's chain is a tree; each grant opens a branch the grantee appends to.
40. **Seal.** Revoking a grant seals its branch at the act the revocation names. The seal act MUST be at or after the last act on the branch that the grantor itself acknowledged or paid on: a receipt or split signed by the grantor that names a deal on that branch counts as payment. A revocation naming an earlier seal is invalid (F76). *A grantor who took the money cannot later say the deal never happened.*
41. **Import and handover.** The collective's import act lists which acts of a sealed branch it accepts into its own chain; a handover assigns acts to another grant's branch. Imports can also be made routinely.
42. **Acknowledgements.** An acknowledgement counts against a seal if the collective's own chain recorded it before the seal act [Flaw K], or if the grantor acknowledged or paid on the deal itself (rule 40).
43. **Fate of acts.** An act in a sealed branch that is neither imported nor handed over does not bind the collective: void if unacknowledged, a visible dispute if acknowledged. **Counterparties: require import before performing.** Deals the grantor never touched can be sealed out.
44. **Evaluation.** A client evaluating an act made under a grant follows the pointer to its branch. Live branch: binding. Sealed branch: binding only if imported or handed over, or protected by rule 40. Ended branch, of a grant within an area that froze (rule 37b): binding up to the last act known to precede the freeze, undetermined after it until the grant is sealed or reinstated (Flaw H). An act beyond the grant's reach (rule 38a) is not backed by it, whatever its branch. A publication whose `for` names a grantor without a grant a Law client can check MAY be refused by that client and is shown as unbacked; a Finance-only wallet still pays the `for` identity, which is the only party that could have been harmed by the lie.

## Tiers

*F103: nobody loses their say by default. Each part of a collective's agreement is changed by the power that owns it, and which power that is follows mechanically from the fields a clone changes, so every verifier reaches the same answer and the mark (rule 45a) can be checked.* **Tiers and areas apply to collectives only** (F103, scope): to a founding agreement carrying a key grammar, and its clones. A deal has none: every clone of it needs every party (rule 45b). *"Deals should be as simple as possible while remaining rich. Collectives is for when things get complicated." Two people who end up doing many deals together can found a collective.*

44a. **Every field belongs to one tier.**

| 2 | cMIPs, per task | Tasks 9 (condition evaluation), 10 (time reference) and 11 (anchoring): judicial, outside the Law lane (Q3, Q15). Every other task: operational, in the lane of the task's layer where an area holds it (F106), and of each other layer the same specification serves (R4). A specification named for a judicial task, or in field 6, is named for no other task and as no extension (Q20, Q25) |
| 3 | Keepers | Judicial |

An area's field references (area key 4) name only operational fields and tasks: fields 7, 8 and 17, and tasks of field 2 other than 9, 10 and 11. *Field 4 belongs to no tier (Q9): in a clone it is a mark of fact, written fresh each time; founding terms are signed by everyone (Q11), and the tiers govern only later changes. Who signs what for the collective is its areas, which are constitutional. Each area carries its own words, changed by its holders with it, so the words never go stale and no area's holder rewrites the constitution's (Q2).*

44b. **What a clone changes.** A clone changes a field when the field's deterministic encoding in the clone differs from the parent's, or the field is present in one and absent in the other. Fields 2, 15 and 20 are compared entry by entry, by task number, by extension and by area index, and each differing entry (added, removed or replaced) is one change. Fields 4 and 11 are never compared. Each change is in the tier of its field or entry (rule 44a). An operational change lies:
- for an entry of field 2: in the parent's area reaching that task's layer as a whole, its lane (F106); and, where the specification the clone names for that task is also named in the clone for a task of another layer, in that layer's lane too (R4); a clone naming one specification for a judicial task and for another task is invalid, whatever its mark (Q20);
- for an entry of field 15: in the parent's area reaching Production, and in the area reaching each layer that the extension added or dropped declares in its specification (Production, field 10): dropping an extension needs the same powers as adopting it (Q18); a clone naming as an extension a specification it names as a judge is invalid, whatever its mark (Q25);
- for an entry of field 20: in the area whose index it carries;
- otherwise: in the parent's area whose field references name that field or task.

The part of a change lying in a layer that no area of the parent reaches, and any other operational change, lies in no area.

44c. **Which powers a clone needs,** from its changes alone:
1. if any change is constitutional: the constitutional change rule, `[0]`, alone, which may change every tier;
2. otherwise, the power of each area in which some change lies, `[2, area]`, one for each such area (Q7); and the clone rule, `[1]`, if some change lies in no area (a judicial change, or an operational change, or part of one, outside every area), or if the clone changes nothing. So a clone that changes an area and something outside every area needs both, its mark naming both (Q12).
3. a clone that executes a party's succession plan and changes nothing else, apart from taking that party out of the holders of every area it held (Q21), may instead name that plan, `[3, party]`, alone, where rule 48c allows a seat to pass automatically (Q14).

A clone needing several powers comes into force only when each is met, all at once or not at all (Q7). *The clone rule does not reach into an area, and one area's holders do not reach into another: an area's power is exclusive, held by its holders or by the narrower rule that governs it. Its holders, and the rule they act by, change only by a constitutional change (Q5).*

44d. **Counting a power.** The constitutional change rule is counted among the parent's parties, under the parent's field 18, or every party when the parent has none; the clone rule among the parent's parties, under its field 5; an area's power among that area's holders in the parent, at least its threshold of them. In each, only parties whose voice remains are counted: a party that resigned (rule 37a) or was declared absent with outcome 0 (rule 53) is left out, and so, for one area, is a holder that stepped down from it (rule 37b), under every parent descending from the agreement in which it lost that voice; a party that a later version it signed names again, as a party or as a holder, counts again under that version. **A party that resigned or stepped down still counts, for a clone or an act, through a valid signature act on it made before it did so** ("Made before, made after"): for that clone or act alone, it counts as a voice that remains, so the rule is met or not as written, its number counted with that voice among the others, never lowered by the departure (Q23). **A party declared absent with outcome 0 still counts likewise, for a clone or an act, through a valid signature act on it that a named keeper recorded before recording the declaration, or, for a clone, that the record or rotation putting it in force acknowledges** ("Made before, made after", Q28): for that clone or act alone it counts as a voice that remains, as above, and a declaration never undoes a clone a record or rotation put in force, whatever version it names. *"Her position at time of signing is valid. She was still acting. Whether it gets approved now depends on collective rules." Under three of three, a member who signed a clone and then resigned still leaves it needing both others: three voices count for it, and three signatures are needed. Under every party, it needs everyone who has not left, the early signature standing among them.* **When fewer voices remain than a rule's number asks for, all the remaining voices together meet it; where enough remain, the number stands as written** (F105, flaw C). This holds for every number an agreement states: the constitutional change rule, the clone rule, an area's threshold, one a clone left above the area's holders included (Q26), and an abandonment authority that is a threshold of the other parties (rule 49). When no voice remains, nothing meets the rule: an area left without a holder, or listing none (Q21), stands frozen, as in rule 37b, until a constitutional clone gives it a holder. *A number can outlast the voices it counts: "three of three" would still ask for three after one voice is removed, and F105 would unfreeze nothing. Read this way, a number never freezes a rule, it matches what "every party" already does, and it keeps the founders' number wherever enough voices remain. A lone survivor still holds the keys and the constitution only under F96 and F105: the key grammar's thresholds are cryptographic, and this rule never lowers them.*

44e. **Protections still hold.** Whatever power brings a clone into force, it cannot reduce a stake without its holder's signature (rule 46), nor change a judicial clause for a party who did not sign it (rule 46a).

*Stated cost (F103): by default a member who is present but unwanted cannot be removed; the others can leave, each alone (rule 37a), or use the abandonment clause where it applies. A group that wants expulsion writes a constitutional change rule below everyone at founding, where every founder signs it; even then, removal takes the voice, never the stake (Q6). A member who dies or vanishes never freezes the constitution: the abandonment clause every collective carries can remove their voice (rule 36b, F105).*

## Changing agreements

45. **Clone, never modify.** A clone is new terms naming the parent and the parent's latest act. Its mark (field 4) names each power its changes require (rule 44c, or rule 45b for a deal) and, for each, the parties whose signatures meet it (rule 44d, or rule 45b for a deal). It is a draft until every party its mark names has a valid signature act naming it, every party it adds and every party it makes a holder of an area it did not hold has one too, and the parent exists; a draft changes nothing. *Nobody is added to a collective, or given an area, without their approval (Q11, Q13): a party added is bound only by its own signature (rule 1), and until it signs, the clone that adds it changes nothing.* A complete clone closes the parent; obligations before that point settle under the parent.
45a. **A false mark makes a clone invalid** (F104). A clone is invalid, whatever signatures it gathers, if its mark names other powers than those its changes require, or names, for a power, a signer who is not among the parties that power is counted among, or too few of them to meet it (as rule 44d counts them). A mark naming a succession plan, `[3, party]`, is checked as rule 48c says. Signature acts by parties the mark does not name bind those parties (rule 1) and bring their protected clauses forward (rule 46a), but do not bring the clone into force. *The mark names exactly the required powers, never "one at least as strong": powers are not ranked against each other (an area held by one member is neither stronger nor weaker than a clone rule of two of three), so "stronger" could not be computed. It names identities, not signature acts: the signature acts name the clone, so the clone cannot name them. Every version thus says on its face who brought it into force and under which power, and any verifier checks it from the parent and the clone alone. Founding terms' field 4 already said the same thing: which signatures make them exist.*
45b. **A deal changes only with everyone's signature** (F107). In a deal the signing rule and the clone rule are every party (terms fields 4 and 5): a deal exists when all its parties have signed it, and a clone of it completes only when all the parent's parties whose voice remains have signed it. Its mark names the clone rule, `[1]`, and those parties. Majorities, areas and tiers belong to collectives; rules 44a to 44e, 46a and 47 apply to collectives only. *Cost, stated and accepted: one silent party blocks every change to a deal, however large: "one person can force everything until all agree. That's deal making in everyday life." The ways out: the abandonment clause, whose declaration is not a clone, can remove an absent party's voice where the deal provides it (rule 53); succession plans; and, for many parties or a long relationship, a collective.*
46. A clone can never reduce a stake without its holder's signature, unless the agreement pre-authorised it.
46a. **Protected clauses: the judicial tier** (collectives only). A clone that a party has not signed cannot change, for that party, any protected clause: the abandonment clause, the succession plan, the fork rule, the keepers, the split service, the time reference, the arbitrators or verifiers (field 13), and the condition, time reference and anchoring cMIPs (field 2, tasks 9, 10 and 11) (Q3, Q15). For that party, each such clause keeps the last version that party signed. An abandonment declaration against a party is judged under the clause version that party signed (F71). *A majority may change the keeper for themselves; the minority's stake is judged under the keeper and clause they agreed to. Per party, not blanket, so that one member cannot freeze every governance change forever. Arbitrators, the condition cMIP and the anchoring cMIP can each decide who wins a dispute, so a majority cannot pick a friendlier judge after the fact (Q3). In a deal no clone completes without everyone, so the question never arises (F107).*
47. **Forks** (collectives only). Where a change rule of a collective (a clone rule, or an area's power) lets parties act independently, the agreement must define how forks are settled. If it does not, the parent stays in force until a clone naming both branches resolves it.
48. **Exit.** Agreements are always in core format, so activity on a fading cMIP moves by cloning the agreement onto another.

## Succession

48a. **The core gives the language, not the answers.** A party may attach a succession plan to an agreement, as part of its signed terms: stake successors, each with a share of the party's stakes (summing exactly), and seat successors, each with a voting weight, entering automatically or with the members' approval. Stake and seat successors may be the same identity or different ones. Changing a plan takes a clone, which for the party concerned needs that party's signature (rule 46a).
48b. **Triggers.** A plan executes when the party triggers it while alive, or when the authority named by the abandonment clause declares the party absent. *Death is a complex issue; the core leaves it to the parties, their named authorities and verifying modules.*
48c. **Execution.** Succession is a clone: the new terms carry the stakes and seats the plan gives, and any position attached to a seat is defined at that moment. In a collective, a change of seats changes the parties, which is constitutional. **A seat passes automatically** (plan key 3, value 0) **only if every party whose voice remains signed that version of the succession plan** (Q14): each of them signed a version of the agreement, in the chain up to the parent, whose field 16 carries that party's plan exactly as the clone executes it. The clone's mark then names the plan, `[3, party]`, with the seat successors entering as its signers, and it comes into force on the trigger (rule 48b) and their signatures, with no other power, provided it changes nothing but what the plan gives, and takes the party out of every area it held (Q21). *Consent given in advance, by everyone still counted (rule 13); a plan that some members cloned without the others is not that version, so it cannot slip a stranger into the constitution through a judicial clause.* Otherwise the seat successor is a nomination, approved under the constitutional change rule (rule 44c), counted among the voices that remain once the absent party's voice is removed (rule 53, F105), as in rule 37b; the successor signs the clone too (rule 45). A stake passes by succession as planned: it is property, not a seat. **A seat passed by succession carries membership, never the areas its holder held** (Q19): the seat carries the party's voice in the constitution and the change rules; the clone that completes the succession, automatic or by nomination, takes the party out of the holders of every area it held (Q21), and each such area is refitted as rule 37b says: its remaining holders carry on meanwhile (Q17); an area left with no holder is written with an empty list of holders and stands frozen, its grants ending (Q22) and sealed or reinstated at the refit (Flaw H), until a constitutional clone gives it a holder, which may be the same clone where the successor is a nomination; an area left with fewer holders than its number keeps that number, met by all who remain (Q26). *An area is a job the collective gave a person; a plan is the party's own, and letting it hand out that job would reach into the constitution. The successor is free to be nominated for it.* In a deal, the clone needs every party whose voice remains (rule 45b). For a collective, the members then rotate and re-split the safety key to include the new key holders, using the grammar's recovery path where a share is gone for good.
48d. **Fallback.** Where no plan exists, the identity-level succession declared in a rotation (Identity) applies, and otherwise the abandonment clause.

## Abandonment

49. **Clause.** Signed into the agreement, it names who decides absence and which outcomes may follow. Every collective carries one that covers each party with constitutional power (rule 36b, F105); a deal may carry one or not. *In a deal, a party's death leaves the agreement running as written, with succession plans to step in, and locks nobody out of an identity.* The authority MUST be an identity: the keeper's operator, a threshold of the other parties (counted as rule 44d says, so it never asks for more of them than remain), or a named third party. An anchoring cMIP is named as the time reference the authority judges absence against, never as the authority. Absence means absence from duty: no act by the party on the agreement, for a period measured on the agreement's time reference.
50. **Liveness.** A party with nothing else to sign shows presence with a liveness act.
51. **Declaration.** A signed act by the authority the clause names, stating the agreement, the clause, the party and the outcome. The core checks that it is signed by that authority, that the outcome is one the clause allows, and that the clause is the version the party declared absent signed (rule 46a).
52. **Contest.** A party who believes they were wrongly declared absent contests it with a contest act.
53. **Outcomes,** combinable: voice removed (F105): from the declaration on, the party is no longer counted in any change rule or area of the agreement, or of any agreement descending from it (rule 44d); a clone that a record or rotation put in force, or whose signature by the party a named keeper recorded before recording the declaration, is never undone, whatever version of the clause or of the agreement the declaration names ("Made before, made after", Q28); and in a collective the remaining members rotate its keys and clone it without the party (rule 37), the recovery path standing in for a share that is gone; stake redistributed among remaining holders in proportion; stake transferred to parties named or defined by role; obligations redirected or held; agreement closed. *A declaration is not a clone: it needs no change rule, only the authority the party signed. Removing a voice never takes the stake; that takes outcome 1 or 2, which the party also signed.*

## Non-performance

*The core never enforces; it makes default impossible to hide.*

54. **Atomic where possible.** Payment is acceptance; delivery released by payment cannot be defaulted on.
55. **Failure attribution.** Finance's receipts and payment claims name the hop and agreement that failed; a split's missing payout names the receiver and agreement. Law defines what follows: refund, redirection, contest.
56. **Negotiation record.** Messages between parties are locked and signed, each naming the previous message in the thread and acknowledging the latest one received from the other side. The record is provably complete up to the last acknowledged message.
57. **Disclosure by choice.** Either party may publish the record, by publishing the keys of its messages (Envelope: going public later).
57a. **Standing.** A contest counts only when signed by someone with standing: a party to the agreement, a holder of a stake the contested act affects, or a keeper, arbitrator or verifier the agreement names. A contest never changes any act's validity, status or selection; it is shown alongside what it names (F69).

*Reputation, arbitration and courts build on this record from outside the core.*

## Tasks

- **Split.** A cMIP accepts an amount received and a split plan, and produces payouts that sum exactly, with its declared remainder rule and the evidence for each role share.
- **Condition evaluation.** A cMIP accepts a condition and the acts it refers to, and produces true, false, pending or unknown.
- **Time reference.** A cMIP accepts a point on its reference (for example a block height) and an act, and produces whether the act is before, after, or undetermined.
- **Anchoring.** A cMIP accepts an act id and produces a proof that it existed at a point on a time reference. *Anchoring is Law's tool for Law's purposes (deadlines, claim priority); Identity does not depend on it (F63).*
- **Grant limits.** A cMIP defines how a grant's limits are expressed and checked.
- **Work claims.** A cMIP MAY define pre-publication commitments carried by a claim.

## Reasoning

- **Terms plus signatures.** *One signer per act (F17) makes every consent a separate, visible act. An agreement is complete exactly when its rule is met, a party refuses simply by not signing, and nobody owes anything they did not sign for.*
- **Identities, not keys.** *Keys change; promises should not. Naming identities means a rotation never breaks a deal.*
- **Stakes and seats can go to different people.** *A stake is property: passing it changes only who is paid. A seat is trust: the other members chose to share control with that person. A plan can give the stake to an heir and the seat to someone trusted for the job, paid through a position.*
- **Collectives are identities.** *A collective that receives money must sign receipts and rotate like anyone else. Giving it its own keys, held as its members choose, keeps one Identity MIP for everyone. The price is that a threshold signature looks like one signature; where individual consent matters, an area can require visible member signatures. And every grammar leaves a way through that survives the loss of any one key holder, so a dead member never freezes the key, not even a sole holder, whose successor holds an escrowed share: the rule is about the exit, not the number (F96). A collective's member signatures are judged under the rules its own chain declared when the key signed, so leaving is final: a former member's signature never again counts for it (F100).*
- **Keepers stamp sealed envelopes, and only real ones.** *Proving an act existed before a rotation needs its id and signer, not its content. Keepers that never read keep private deals private; judging content is left to arbitrators and verifiers the parties choose and deliver keys to. A record counts only alongside the act it names, so a bought record of nothing changes nothing.*
- **Protected clauses.** *A stake cannot be reduced without its holder's signature; but the clause that decides absence, the keeper who judges it, the fork rule and the split service could move the stake in two steps, and an arbitrator, a condition cMIP or an anchoring cMIP chosen after the fact could decide who wins. So in a collective, for each party, those clauses stay as that party signed them, whatever a majority clones for itself.*
- **Deals are everyone's; majorities belong to collectives** (F107). *A deal is as simple as possible while remaining rich: nobody's terms change without their signature, so a party never has to read a rule to know whether they can be outvoted. The price is that one silent party blocks every change, as in everyday deal making. Where many parties or a long relationship need majorities, areas and tiers, they found a collective.*
- **The constitution never freezes** (F105). *The constitution can repair every other tier, so it alone must survive the loss of anyone whose signature it needs. Every such member is covered by an abandonment clause they signed, which can remove their voice, never their stake.*
- **Leaving needs no permission; removal never takes the stake** (Q6). *Anyone can walk out of a collective alone, keeping what they own. A constitution may allow removing a member from the board; it can never force them to give up their shares: "Kicked from the board does not mean being forced to give up shares."*
- **Lanes are computed from the collective's own list, and govern it** (F106). *What the core sees of a cMIP is the result of a task, and the task belongs to a layer. Reading the lane from which task the collective adopted the cMIP for, never from the cMIP's own label, lets every verifier agree; and letting the lane's holders adopt it lets a new rail join the Finance lane without a constitutional change. Extensions belong to no task, so they sit in Production; one that reaches into another layer needs that layer's holders too, and binds nothing they did not approve.*
- **A number never freezes a rule** (F105, flaw C). *When fewer voices remain than a rule asks for, all of them together meet it: the same as "every party" already does, and the founders' number wherever enough remain.*
- **Before and after are drawn by the signer, over every line it keeps** (Flaw E). *With no clock, a resignation, a stepping down or a record can only say what came before it by naming it. An identity may write from several devices at once, so each of these acts names the latest act of every line, as a rotation does, and nothing outside them can slip in as earlier.* **What others relied on stands** (Flaw F). *A line the signer forgot costs the signer, never those who had its acts recorded by a keeper, as after a rotation; and a clone on the record stays in force, since the record vouches for the signatures that completed it.* **Only what can be placed in time protects** (Flaw G). *An acknowledgement carries no date, so it could be made after the line was drawn, by a friend, for a member who kept signing from a phone left out on purpose. A keeper's record, the collective's record and a rotation each sit at a known place; an acknowledgement alone keeps the act in view and changes nothing.* **A declaration draws the same line** (Q28). *A voice removed by someone else ends from then on: what a record or rotation already put in force stays, whichever version of the clause the declaration names.*
- **A signature speaks for the moment it was made** (Q23). *A member who signed and then left was still acting when they signed; their signature stands, and the rule asks for exactly what it asked before they left, neither less nor more.*
- **Nobody is left holding an area they no longer have, and no hire outlives the area's freeze** (Q21, Q22, Flaw H). *A clone that removes a member takes them off their areas, and an area may wait, empty and frozen, for the collective to refit it, keeping the founders' number for the refit (Q26). Its grants end with the freeze; the new holders take on, one by one and visibly, the hires they want to keep, by the area's own rule (Q27), and seal the others where the last counted deal lies, never before a deal the collective was paid on. A freeze names no act of a branch, so until then what came after it is undetermined, not guessed.*
- **A judge is never chosen by one lane** (Q15). *The time reference, the condition cMIP and the anchoring cMIP can decide who wins a dispute; they stay judicial, as each member signed them, whoever runs Law's other tasks. And a judge never handles what it judges (Q20): a specification chosen as a judge serves no other task and is no extension, however it is named and in a deal as in a collective (Q24, Q25), which removes a conflict of interest and keeps every lane out of the choice of judges.*
- **Nothing passes around the lanes** (Q16, R4). *A specification the collective never listed would let whoever holds its key act around every lane, so in a collective with areas it counts for nothing until a lane adopts it; one that serves two layers answers to both.*
- **Every change is on the record at once** (F106, flaw D). *With no clock, the collective's own sequence is what tells which version governed an act. A rotation is the safety key's ceremony, kept for changes to who decides; every other change is written with the everyday key, at once.*
- **Nobody is added, and nobody is held** (Q11, Q13). *A collective exists only when every founder signed it, and nobody joins it or takes an area without signing. A holder can step down at once; the area waits, frozen, for the collective to refit it, rather than running on without anyone answerable for it.*
- **A split service holds authority, not discretion.** *It is named by grant, bound to the plan, and every incoming receipt or payer's claim must be matched by a split. It can still steal, but never invisibly, and never by naming its own sybil as the referrer: role-share evidence is signed by someone on the other side.*
- **Shares follow stakes, holders follow transfers.** *A plan written in holder names would go stale at the first sale. Written in stakes, it pays whoever the agreement's transfer chain names, and two services compute the same answer.*
- **Leftovers to the first listed party.** *Signatures may arrive in any order; the order in the terms is fixed. So remainders follow the terms, and every client computes the same result.*
- **Keepers are chosen up front.** *Testimony is only trusted if nobody could pick a friendly witness after the fact.*
- **Many ways to split, one vocabulary.** *A paying client may pay each owner directly; a service may receive and forward at once; or it may pool and let owners withdraw, with every unwithdrawn share an open obligation rather than a number in a private database. Each is a competing split cMIP on the same vocabulary.*
- **Splits belong to owners.** *Finance delivers money to a pointer; the owners' agreement decides who gets what. Each payout is a simple payment with its own receipt, so a missing one is as visible as a broken route.*
- **Exact sums, declared remainders, bounded fees.** *Rounding is where money quietly disappears; an unbounded fee line is where it disappears loudly. The plan states the maximum, and the payer's claim fixes what arrived.*
- **Role shares pay for help that is only known at payment time.** *A reposter, a relay or a module earns when its part in a sale is evidenced by someone other than the party paying it, so rewards attach to settlements, never to attention, and never to the service's say-so.*
- **A stake pays through whoever sells.** *The core cannot tie a sale to a work's stakes without judging which claim is the claim, and a rule that tried would be evaded by re-encoding the file. What it can do is make a sale outside the claiming agreement visible, and give builders every piece needed to refuse or rate such sales. The core is as little intrusive as possible, and carries the bits and bolts for sound solutions on top.*
- **Clone, never modify.** *Nothing in MOR is updated. A clone that meets its rule is the only way terms change, and the parent's history stays intact.*
- **Nobody loses their say by default.** *Protecting the abandonment clause guarded one route to removing a party's voice; rewriting the clone rule was another, unguarded (F103). So the rules that decide who decides form their own tier, changed by their own rule, which is everyone unless the founders, all of them, agreed otherwise. What judges a party stays as that party signed it. Everything else is operational, and can be handed to the members who run it, as areas: they decide there without asking the rest, and can hire, but never give more than they hold.*
- **The tier follows from the bytes.** *Which power a clone needs is read from which fields it changes, never from what it says about itself, so two verifiers cannot disagree, and a clone cannot slip a constitutional change past as an operational one.*
- **Every version says who brought it into force** (F104). *A field every agreement carries should never be free text that looks binding and is not. A clone's field 4 is a claim anyone can check: this power, these people. A false claim sinks the clone.*
- **A grantor cannot unsay a deal it was paid on.** *Sealing a branch before a deal the grantor acknowledged or collected on would leave the counterparty with a contest and nothing else. So the seal stops there. Deals the grantor never touched are the counterparty's risk, which is why import comes before performance.*
- **Default made visible.** *The core cannot force anyone to pay or deliver. It can make sure nobody can hide that they did not.*

### Flaws

- **[Flaw I] A reinstatement draws no line on the grant's branch either.** *Plain words:* Flaw H found that a freeze names no act of a grant's branch, so nothing tells a deal made before the freeze from one made during it. A reinstatement has the same gap: it is a signature act naming the grant, and names no act of the branch. Rule 37b says the reinstated grant backs acts "from then on", and that acts made while it stood ended count for nothing; but the grantee's acts after the last one known to precede the freeze cannot be sorted into "during the freeze" and "after the reinstatement", so no verifier can apply that sentence. *Options:* (1) a reinstatement takes on the whole branch: every act on it counts as under a live grant, those made during the freeze included, the new holders choosing it knowingly; holders who want a line seal the grant instead (rule 40), import what they accept, and issue a fresh grant to the same grantee; (2) a reinstatement also names the last act of the branch it takes on, as a seal does, with rule 40's floor; acts after that one count from then on, which still cannot keep out an act the grantee made during the freeze and showed nobody; (3) a reinstatement seals the old branch at a named act and opens a new branch that starts from the reinstatement itself, so later acts must name it; this needs a new field or act. *Lean:* (1): it needs no new format, every verifier can apply it, and the line the holders may want is already available by sealing and granting anew. The text keeps rule 37b's sentence, marked.
- **[Flaw J] A keeper can place only acts that name an agreement.** *Plain words:* after Flaw G, a keeper's record is the only thing that places a member's act before their resignation (or a grantee's act before a freeze). Rule 11a lets a record count only where the keeper is named by an agreement the act's own `objects` names. A signature on a clone names the clone, an agreement, so its keepers can place it. But a signature on an act an area reaches (a publication, a receipt) names that act, not an agreement, and a publication under a grant names no agreement either: no keeper can place them, so they can never be protected, and freeze suite step 3.7 says one can. *Options:* (1) a signature act, and an act under a grant, can also be placed by the keepers of the agreement in force for the act it signs or the grant it acts under, the collective's own; (2) as written: only acts naming an agreement can be placed, and signatures on area acts in a forgotten line are lost, a cost on the signer, stated; step 3.7 changes to a signature on a clone; (3) the area act itself, once a keeper places it, places every signature naming it. *Lean:* (1): the collective's keepers are those every member signed, so nobody picks a friendly witness, and it gives Flaw G's cost ("deals that need protection name a keeper") a way to be met inside a collective. The text is unchanged, marked where it relies on rule 11a.
- **[Flaw K] A revocation names none of the collective's other sequences.** *Plain words:* rule 42 counts a counterparty's acknowledgement against a seal "if the collective's own chain recorded it before the seal act". A collective may keep several sequences (Flaw E), and a revocation, unlike a record, names none of them, so "before the seal act" is undefined across them. Flaw H makes seals routine at every refit. *Options:* (1) a revocation names the latest act of every other sequence the collective keeps, as a record does (Flaw E); (2) drop "before the seal act": an acknowledgement the collective recorded anywhere counts as the collective acknowledging the deal (rule 40), so the seal can never fall before it; (3) as written, a stated cost. *Lean:* (2): it needs no new field, every verifier can apply it, and it only protects counterparties further; a collective that recorded an acknowledgement has taken note of the deal. The text is unchanged.
- **[Flaw L] A declaration reaches back over every act of the collective the absent member signed.** *Plain words:* Q28 says a declaration removes a voice "from then on", and never undoes a clone a record or rotation put in force. Written mechanically, as the fifth pass leaned, every other signature of the absent party counts as made after the declaration, since a declaration names none of their sequences. That is right for clones, which are on the record at once. But a member's signatures on the collective's everyday acts in their area (a treasurer's on a year of receipts, a release manager's on every publication) are put in force by no record, and no keeper can place them (Flaw J). So a treasurer declared absent would take back, with their voice, every receipt they ever completed under the version the declaration names and those after it: a year of the collective's accounts falling to nothing, which "from then on" does not mean. *Options:* (1) in a collective, a declaration removes the voice for the collective's acts from the rotation that writes it (rule 53 already has the remaining members rotate and clone without the party): an act of the collective counts as made before or after that rotation by the rotation's kept tips, as every act of the collective already does, and the absent member's signature on an act made before it still counts; (2) the declaration itself names the latest act of each of the collective's sequences, as a record does; (3) as written, a stated cost: a collective whose acts must survive a member's absence has a keeper record them. *Lean:* (1): it uses a line the collective already draws in its own chain, with tips every verifier can check, and asks nothing new of the authority, who may know nothing of the collective's sequences. The text writes Q28 as decided, marked where it reaches these acts.

### Questions

- **[Q29] Who signs the seal of a grant within an area?** *Plain words:* Flaw H says the new holders seal each ended grant by a revocation. A revocation is an act of the collective; where the collective's Law lane belongs to someone else, that lane reaches revocations as acts, and would decide the seal instead of the area that issued the grant. Rule 38a settles this for grants ("judged by the area it names alone"), not for their revocations. *Options:* (1) a revocation of a grant within an area is judged by that area alone, at its threshold, as the grant is (rule 38a) and as Q27 decided for reinstating; (2) as any act of the collective, by whichever area reaches revocations. *Lean:* (1), written in rule 37b, marked: the area that hires is the area that ends the hire.
- **[Q30] What places a grantee's act before a freeze?** *Plain words:* Flaw H makes the grantee's acts after "the last one known to precede the freeze" undetermined, without saying how an act is known to precede it. *Options:* (1) as for a resignation (Flaw G): a keeper named by an agreement the act names recorded it before recording the act that froze the area, and every act before it on the branch with it; (2) as (1), and also every act the collective acknowledged or paid on, which a seal can never cut off anyway (rule 40). *Lean:* (1), written in rule 37b, marked: it is the one line Flaw G accepts, and (2) changes nothing at the refit, where rule 40 already protects those deals.
- **[Q31] The time reference is named in two places.** *Plain words:* terms field 6 names "the time reference: the task cMIP and its parameters", and field 2 may also name a cMIP for task 10, the time reference. Nothing says they must agree, so an agreement could carry two time references, while rule 2 allows one cMIP per task and "only it decides before and after" (Definitions). *Options:* (1) field 6 is the only place: terms naming task 10 in field 2 are invalid; (2) where both are present they name the same specification, field 6 adding its parameters; (3) field 6 carries only the parameters, the cMIP being field 2's task 10. *Lean:* (2): one check, no format change, and existing terms that use either place stay valid. The text treats field 6 as a naming for task 10 (Q25) and is otherwise unchanged.
- **[Q32] How is an area followed from one version to the next?** *Plain words:* an area is identified by its index in field 19. A constitutional clone that removes or reorders areas renumbers the rest, while grants (field 5), area words (field 20), a refit (rule 37b) and the number a clone keeps (Q26) all follow an area by its index.
